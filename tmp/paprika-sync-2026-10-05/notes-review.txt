### Basmati Rice => basmati-rice
PAPRIKA:
Washing the rice removes surface starch and prevents the rice from becoming a sticky clump.
The 1:1.5 ratio (1 cup rice to 1.5 cups water) is ideal for most standard rice cookers.
MISE:

## Chef's Note

Basmati can be cooked plain or with a small amount of spice. Choose the version to fit the meal: plain rice leaves room for a richly seasoned main; cumin and whole spices make the rice more prominent. The useful lesson is controlling water and steam. One ratio does not cover every rice brand, soaking method, cooker, and pan.

## Directions

1. **Rinse and drain:** Rinse rice in several changes of cool water until much of the loose starch has washed away. Drain thoroughly. Do not soak for this written method.
2. **Heat fat and optional spices:** In a 2–3 quart heavy saucepan, heat the ghee, butter, or oil over medium heat. If using spices, add them and stir for 20–30 seconds until fragrant. For plain rice, proceed directly to the next step.
3. **Add rice and water:** Stir in drained rice briefly to coat. Add the measured water and salt and bring to a gentle boil. Stir once, cover tightly, and lower heat to maintain a gentle simmer.
4. **Cook and check:** Simmer about 15–18 minutes. Check near the end that water is absorbed and taste a grain for an almost tender center. ## Other equipment or soaked rice

For a rice cooker, use its basmati/white-rice guidance and measuring system, including its water level; do not combine its markings with the stovetop water quantity. Bloom optional spices separately and add them with the rice. If choosing to soak, follow the rice producer's soaked-rice method rather than subtracting an arbitrary two tablespoons of water. These are separate methods with different timing.

## Chef's Note

This Southern-inspired home-style chicken keeps the buttermilk soak and bakes a cornflake-and-Parmesan coating instead of deep-frying. **Kasuri Methi** is essential for the restaurant aroma.
**Kashmiri Chili Powder **provides the red color without excessive heat; do not substitute with full Cayenne or it will be too spicy.
If the sauce is too thick, splash in a **little extra water** or chicken stock.
MISE:

## Chef's Note

This restaurant-style tikka masala combines browned chicken with a generous tomato-and-cream sauce. A little char adds flavor, but the chicken should not be black all over. Have the sauce ready first, cook the chicken in manageable batches, and crush the dried fenugreek leaves into the finished curry for their distinctive aroma.

## Directions

1. **Marinate:** Trim and cut the chicken into pieces about **1 1/2 inches / 4 cm** across. Whisk the yogurt, marinade garlic and ginger, marinade lemon juice, marinade salt, Kashmiri chile and marinade garam masala in a bowl. Fold in chicken, cover and refrigerate for **at least 4 hours**, or overnight up to 24 hours. Wash hands, board and tools after handling the raw chicken. Keep the separate rack oil out of the marinade.
2. **Prepare the sauce and pans:** Begin about **75 minutes before the chicken's four-hour marinade ends**. Dice the onion, measure all sauce ingredients and set aside the finishing ingredients. Prepare the number of foil-lined metal pans and broiler-safe racks needed for your batch: two for the full recipe, one for half or quarter. Lightly brush the racks in use with the listed rack oil, dividing it between them. Keep the chicken refrigerated while making the sauce. Set out a clean covered platter and clean tongs for cooked chicken.
3. **Cook the onion, paste and spices:** Heat the onion butter and oil in the heavy pot over medium heat. Add onion and **half the initial sauce salt**. Cook for **15–20 minutes**, stirring regularly, until soft and golden. Add tomato paste and stir for **2 minutes**, until a shade darker. Lower the heat; stir in the sauce garlic and ginger for **30–60 seconds**, then coriander, cumin, turmeric and paprika for **15–20 seconds**. Promptly add passata before the ground spices scorch.
4. **Finish the sauce base:** Stir in the measured sauce sugar and the remaining initial sauce salt. Bring to a gentle simmer, allowing a few minutes to heat, then cook **uncovered for 15–20 minutes**, stirring the bottom regularly. Use the water reserve a little at a time only if it becomes too thick to stir easily. The base should be thick and aromatic but still flow from a spoon. Turn off the heat and let bubbling stop. If blending, keep the immersion-blender head submerged and blend carefully until smooth, following the appliance's hot-food instructions; stop the motor before lifting it. Cover the pot off the heat while broiling. During the last ten minutes of the sauce simmer, preheat the broiler to HIGH according to the oven's instructions; do not load chicken until the sauce base is finished and the chicken has marinated four hours.
5. **Load the first batch:** Arrange **up to about 450 g chicken** on the first rack (half the full recipe; all the chicken for a half or quarter recipe) in one layer with spaces between the pieces. Lift it from the marinade, letting excess drip back into the bowl; retain a thin coating rather than wiping it clean. Keep the remaining raw chicken and marinade refrigerated. Place the top of the food about **5–6 inches below the broiler element**. Broil only one tray at a time.
6. **Broil through, then repeat:** Broil the thighs for about **12–18 minutes total**, turning after **6–8 minutes** and rotating the tray if the element browns unevenly. Watch throughout. Start temperature checks early: insert a clean instant-read probe from the side into the center of several large pieces, avoiding the rack. Every checked center must reach **165°F / 74°C** before removal; take ready pieces out as needed while thicker ones finish. If the surface darkens first, move the tray lower or use the oven finish in troubleshooting. Transfer with clean tongs to the clean platter and cover loosely. If raw chicken remains, load the second prepared rack and broil the same way. Discard any raw marinade once the last batch is loaded. Keep raw utensils away from the cooked platter. Allow a few minutes for loading and temperature checks between the two rounds; do not leave the broiler to prepare another dish. Discard the scorched tray drippings, keeping only the juices that collect on the clean cooked-chicken platter.
7. **Combine and finish:** Turn off the broiler. Reheat the sauce base, stirring, until bubbling throughout. Stir in the cream and bring back to a gentle simmer, using any remaining measured water only if necessary. Add all the cooked chicken and its clean-platter juices; stir gently for about **2–4 minutes**, until the whole curry is hot and chicken centers reach **165°F / 74°C**. Turn off the heat. Taste with a piece of chicken, then add the reserved salt only as needed. Use small amounts of the optional finishing sugar or lemon only if the sauce needs them. Stir in the finishing butter, crush the measured fenugreek leaves between your palms into the pot, and add the finishing garam masala. Rest **5 minutes** before serving.
8. **Serve:** Divide the chicken evenly among the servings and spoon over the sauce, roughly **1 1/4–1 1/2 cups curry per person**. Serve with plain basmati or naan, and small spoonfuls of cucumber raita if wanted. The full basmati recipe provides six rice sides; if also serving naan, start with smaller rice portions and one bread each.

### Chocolate Soufflé => chocolate-souffle
PAPRIKA:
Soufflé can be assembled up to 30 minutes before baking. Keep, covered with an inverted large bowl (do not let bowl touch soufflé), at room temperature.
MISE:

## Chef's Note

This classic dessert requires careful technique. The secret to a tall, even rise is the thumb-run: after filling the dish, you must run your thumb around the inside rim to create a small channel. This prevents the batter from sticking to the edges, allowing it to rise straight up like a top hat. We use a 2:1 ratio of whites to yolks to ensure a light, airy texture while maintaining deep chocolate intensity. Serve straight from the oven - the magic is in the fleeting moment before deflation.

## Directions

1.  **The Ladder:** Preheat oven to 375°F. Generously butter a 6-cup soufflé dish. Coat with 1 tbsp sugar, knocking out the excess.
2.  **The Base:** Melt chocolate in a heatproof bowl over simmering water. Stir until smooth. Remove from heat and whisk in the egg yolks - the mixture will stiffen, which is expected.
3.  **The Meringue:** In a clean bowl, beat egg whites with a pinch of salt until soft peaks form. Gradually add 1/3 cup sugar, beating until the whites are stiff and glossy.
4.  **The Lighten:** Stir about 1 cup of the meringue into the stiff chocolate base to loosen it.
5.  **The Fold:** Gently fold the remaining meringue into the chocolate using a "cut and fold" motion to preserve the air bubbles.
6.  **The Thumb-Run:** Spoon the batter into the dish. **Run your thumb** around the inside edge of the dish to create a shallow groove.
7.  **Bake:** Bake in the middle of the oven for 24-26 minutes until puffed and crusted on top but still having a slight "jiggle" in the center.
8.  **Serve:** Dust with powdered sugar and serve immediately with lightly sweetened whipped cream.

### Dashi (Japanese Sea Stock) => dashi-japanese-sea-stock

MISE:

## Chef's Note

Dashi is the foundation of Japanese cuisine. Remove kombu just before water boils - boiling releases bitter, slimy compounds that cloud the stock. This "First Dashi" (Ichiban Dashi) creates clean, ocean-fresh umami essential for miso soup and noodle broths.

## Directions

1.  **Steep:** Place cold water and kombu in a large saucepan. Let sit for 10 minutes to begin the extraction.

5.  **Strain:** Pour through a fine-mesh sieve or coffee filter into a clean bowl. Do not squeeze the flakes, as this will cloud the stock.
6.  **Store:** Use immediately or refrigerate for up to 4 days.

## Chef's Note

Baingan bharta often begins with fire-roasted eggplant. This pressure-cooked version builds its flavor from golden onion, cumin, garlic and warm spices. This Mexican-inspired salmon dinner pairs sweet lime-honey glaze with cumin-scented beans and corn, fresh spinach and toasted pepitas. ## Directions

1. **Prepare everything and heat the oven:** Heat the oven to 400°F / 205°C with a middle rack and a second rack positioned so the fish surface will sit 7–9 inches below the broiler. ### Middle Eastern-Style Grilled Chicken Kabobs => middle-eastern-style-grilled-chicken-kabobs
PAPRIKA:
Note: I've specified boneless skinless chicken thighs for this recipe; they're juicier and more flavorful than chicken breasts, and they work better in this type of yogurt and lemon-based marinade. They're also much easier to cook without drying out.
MISE:

## Chef's Note

This home-style version is adapted from Jennifer Segal's Middle Eastern-inspired chicken kabobs, with garlic, lemon, yogurt and a restrained pinch of cinnamon. The yogurt is a marinade rather than a serving sauce. Thin onion layers and evenly cut chicken help the skewers finish together; a thin coating develops browned patches while excess marinade can stick and scorch.

## Directions

1. **Prepare and coat:** Cut the peeled onion into one-inch squares and separate into single or thin paired layers. Cut chicken into about thirty-six pieces roughly 1 1/2 inches across, keeping thickness similar. In a bowl large enough to hold everything, whisk yogurt, olive oil, lemon juice and zest, garlic, paprika, cumin, cinnamon, the desired red pepper flakes, salt and black pepper. Add chicken and onion and turn to coat evenly. Cover and refrigerate for eight to twenty-four hours.
2. **Prepare the grill:** Near the end of marinating, heat a gas grill with the lid closed, then keep a medium-high cooking zone and a low or unlit zone. For charcoal, allow coals to establish and arrange them on one side, leaving the other side cooler. Follow the grill's lighting instructions. Set out a clean serving platter and utensils; the raw-marinated tray is not the serving platter.
3. **Thread and drain:** Divide chicken among twelve 10–12-inch skewers, about three pieces each. Distribute onion in thin layers between the chicken and at both ends, keeping the whole food-bearing length over the cooking zone. Do not thicken the onion stacks to use it up; use an additional onion-only skewer if needed and count it in the grill capacity. All this onion has contacted raw chicken and must be cooked; it cannot become a raw garnish. Thread without tightly compressing or wrapping pieces into thick bundles. Let excess marinade drain back into its bowl so a thin film remains, then discard the residue. Keep six skewers refrigerated while the first six cook, unless the grill is large enough for the whole batch with a clear cooler zone. Lightly oil clean grates with the measured teaspoon neutral oil using a folded paper towel held in long tongs.
4. **Grill the first round:** Lay six skewers over the cooking zone without touching. Close the lid between turns and turn about every three minutes once the first surface releases, using a broad spatula to help if needed. Cook for about twelve to fifteen minutes, moving skewers to the cooler zone if the coating browns too quickly. 5. **Check and repeat:** Begin checking around twelve minutes. Probe the largest pieces on each skewer from the side, avoiding the hot metal, and continue until each reaches at least 165°F / 74°C. Check any unusually thick pieces separately. Transfer fully cooked skewers to the clean platter and cover loosely during the short second round. Cook the remaining six the same way, keeping the same thermometer endpoint. A tight cover softens the coating, and the first round will not retain the same surface texture as chicken served straight from the grill.
6. **Rest and finish:** Rest the final round for five minutes, then serve two skewers per person with the chopped parsley and lemon wedges. For six plates, add one-and-a-half four-side batches of Lemon and Herb Instant Couscous, starting with 375 g dry couscous, plus vegetables or salad. Alternatively, warm six pita breads and add a vegetable side. These are extra components and preparation time. The onion in each portion is a seasoning vegetable, about 40 g raw weight, not a full vegetable side.

PAPRIKA:
Tips for Making the Best Pumpkin Cheesecake Cookies

Chilling your cookie dough is a must! It's almost impossible to fill these cookies if the dough isn't chilled. I find they're easiest to roll out when they've been in the fridge for about an hour.

Keep the cream-cheese filling and assembled unbaked cookies chilled whenever you pause; the dough's one-hour handling chill is not a room-temperature storage allowance. Limit the baked cookies' combined cooling and serving time out of refrigeration to 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.

### Royal Icing => royal-icing
PAPRIKA:
Eggs are easier to separate when cold. Egg whites whip up easier when closer to room temperature. I recommend separating them in advance if you have the time.

You can whip egg whites by hand, but it’ll require a lot of elbow grease to get it done.

Do not rush the recipe and dump all the sugar into the egg whites at once. You lose volume if you add the sugar too quickly.

Whipping the egg whites at a low speed allows the protein in the whites to break down better. This helps the whipped egg whites become more elastic and become more voluminous.

Make sure the bowl you whip your whites in is super clean. Any residue water or fat will prevent the egg whites from becoming fluffy.

While I prefer using gel food coloring, you can use what you have on hand. Remember that gel food coloring is more concentrated, so you can add less, whereas liquid food coloring may thin out the frosting if you add too much to it.

If your icing has started to harden, add a drop or two of water to bring it back to your desired consistency. However, avoid making the icing too thin as it’ll lead to air bubbles.

For the best flavor, use real vanilla extract instead of artificial vanilla.

When using royal icing to decorate cookies, pipe the icing first, then flood it. Outlining means piping a thin outline of the cookie with thicker icing and flooding means filling the middle with thinner icing. Piping an outline first keeps the icing from sliding off the cookies.

Add a few drops of water to the icing and stir with a spatula for a flooding consistency. Continue adding water until you achieve the desired consistency.
MISE:

## Chef's Note

Royal icing is the traditional coating for decorated sugar cookies, gingerbread houses, and wedding cakes because it dries to a hard, glossy finish. Beating egg whites on low speed until frothy before adding sugar breaks down protein chains without creating large air bubbles, producing smooth icing that doesn't crack. Use thick icing for borders; thin it with water to "15-second consistency" (where a ribbon of icing disappears back into the bowl in 15 seconds) for flooding centers.

## Directions

1.  **Froth:** In a stand mixer, beat egg whites on medium-low for 1 minute until bubbly but not peaking.
2.  **Incorporate:** Switch to low speed. Slowly add the sifted sugar and vanilla.
3.  **Emulsify:** Once combined, increase speed to medium-high. Beat for 2-3 minutes until the icing is stiff, glossy, and holds a Crunch peak.
4.  **Dye:** Divide into small bowls. Stir in gel food coloring (The Finishing Touch).
5.  **Consistency:** For flooding, add water 1/2 tsp at a time until a ribbon of icing disappears back into the bowl in exactly 15 seconds.
6.  **Store:** Keep bowls tightly covered with plastic wrap - this icing sets instantly when exposed to air.

## Chef's Note

Saag refers broadly to leafy greens, while palak means spinach. This spinach-only version keeps a chopped texture, with cream and fenugreek leaves rounding out the onion and spices. Drain the greens well but leave them moist, and brown only two faces of the paneer before its brief warm soak.

## Directions

2. **Blanch and chop the spinach:** Divide the washed spinach into about four loose batches. Stir in garam masala and half the lemon juice; taste, then add the remaining lemon and reserved seasoning salt only as needed. The lemon should brighten the spinach without making the cream sauce sharply sour.
7. **Optional chile ghee and serve:** In a small pan, melt the separate measured chile-ghee portion over low heat. Take off the heat, stir in the Kashmiri chile powder for about 5–10 seconds, and immediately spoon over the saag or offer separately. If it blackens or smells bitter, discard it. Divide the cheese and greens evenly among the servings, with rice or bread alongside.

For a smooth sauce, blend all the cooled, drained spinach with the full measured sauce-water allowance before step 5. Add no second reserve automatically. ## Chef's Note

Whisking flour into cold milk before heating creates a light, stable base that won't separate when cheese is added - an easier technique than making a traditional butter roux. Boiling pasta for exactly 8 minutes (very al dente) ensures it holds its shape against the heavy cheese sauce instead of turning mushy. Mustard powder adds savory depth that enhances the sharp cheddar without tasting overtly mustardy.

## Directions

1.  **Boil:** Cook pasta in salted water for 8 minutes until **very al dente**. Drain and set aside.
2.  **The Base:** In a large saucepan, whisk together one third of the measured milk and the flour until no lumps remain. Stir in the remaining milk.
3.  **Thicken:** Cook over medium heat, whisking constantly, for 3-4 minutes until the milk thickens to the consistency of heavy cream.
4.  **Emulsify:** Reduce heat to **low**. Add the cheese one handful at a time, stirring until completely molten and glossy.
5.  **Season:** Stir in the salt, mustard powder, and butter.
6.  **Bind:** Return the hot pasta to the saucepan. Toss vigorously until every noodle is saturated in the cheese silk.
7.  **Serve:** Serve immediately while hot. (Note: if baking, top with breadcrumbs and broil for 2 minutes).
