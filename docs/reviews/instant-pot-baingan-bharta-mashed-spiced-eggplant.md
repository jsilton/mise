## Concrete direct method and appliance conditions

Use one **6-quart electric pressure cooker whose applicable instructions permit the stated 375 ml added thin water and 60 ml oil**. This is a compatibility requirement for a complete method, not permission to use an arbitrary cooker or to reduce its minimum water. The [manufacturer FAQ](https://instantpot.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions) lists 375 ml for its usual six-quart models, requires deglazing and warns about thicker sauces. The document also advises against exceeding 60 ml oil and explicitly prohibits quick release for oily/thick food. These passages were directly retrieved and read during this revision; the previously inaccessible full US/regional PDF manuals are not claimed as read.

The onion/cumin base cooks in all the measured oil. Garlic/ginger/ground spices bloom briefly at lower heat. **Cancel sauté, add all 375 ml water and fully scrape the base clean before loading vegetables.** This is a thin watery base rather than a concentrated stirred tomato paste. Put eggplant above it, then fresh chopped tomatoes and all their juices on top. Do not stir that layer down before pressure. The public steps replace, rather than retain alongside, the earlier tomato sauté.

[Amy and Jacky's primary spaghetti sauce](https://www.pressurecookrecipes.com/instant-pot-spaghetti-sauce/) is evidence for complete deglazing followed by an unmixed tomato layer; it is not a validated eggplant formula. That changed tomato placement gives up the source's brief tomato/oil contact, but the tomatoes still cook with the eggplant through pressure and substantial reduction. This is a smaller identity change than discarding juices or cooking the vegetable separately. Do not describe layering as a guarantee against every burn warning.

The FAQ's extra-cup guidance for thicker sauces is not ignored: the written method specifically does not seal a thick stirred tomato sauce. A version that first reduces tomatoes to paste and stirs them into the bottom would be a different liquid/setup decision. Neither the original 60 ml nor a casual caveat to 'use more if needed' is left in the main pressure method. No tomato paste, canned tomato sauce or crushed-tomato substitution is offered as equivalent here.

**Release is fully natural.** After the seven-minute high-pressure period, turn Keep Warm off and leave the valve sealed until the cooker is entirely depressurized. Budget 20–30 minutes or longer. Do not use a timed natural rest followed by a quick-release remainder, and do not begin quick release to see whether it sputters. Root identified the manufacturer restriction; this supersedes both the source's quick-release direction and the earlier options discussion. The softer mash tolerates the continued cooking during natural release. Opening requires the actual pressure indicator/model conditions, not merely thirty elapsed minutes.

**Fixed scaling remains essential.** The chosen water minimum must not halve when portions halve; increasing the full 60 ml oil exceeds the referenced threshold, and a larger vegetable load can exceed the pressure maximum. The recipe stays a fixed four-portion batch. The uncompressed raw food and liquid must fit below the actual pressure maximum and clear of lid fittings. With no insert/trivet there is more usable volume than the held basket design, but the cooker label alone does not waive the loaded-volume check.

## Cooking, yield and full one-cook schedule

The source-based seven-minute high-pressure starting point now operates directly on wetted vegetables again rather than through an unvalidated basket. The selected cube size, net quantity and new natural release still make this an adaptation, not a source-exact tested method. Check center and edge cubes for ready collapse after full release. If firm, gently simmer in the retained liquid before mashing. No second pressure cycle or quick release is prescribed as a rescue.

Uncovered reduction is necessary because **315 ml extra added water** joins the vegetable juices. Plan **20–35 minutes, possibly longer**, stirring/scraping and reducing heat as the mash thickens. A glossy spoonful with no separate watery rim is the endpoint. Do not drain liquid to accelerate it. No measured evaporation rate was found for this particular cooker/food load, so the range remains an editorial planning allowance, not a guarantee. Natural release retains more moisture than immediate venting; this revision does not reuse the original source's two-to-three-minute optional reduction.

A representative complete path, with ordinary preparation rather than premade masala:

- 0–20: peel/weigh/cut vegetables and measure ingredients.
- 20–38: heat oil, cumin/onion 8–12 minutes, brief aromatics, full deglazing and vegetable loading.
- 38–53: build pressure, about 10–15 minutes.
- 53–60: seven minutes high pressure.
- 60–90: full natural release, around 20–30 minutes but longer if needed.
- 90–125: check/mash, uncovered reduction and final salt/cilantro/lemon.

**125 minutes total, roughly 60–70 active** is therefore an honest planning value. Quick conditions may finish near 110 minutes; long release or wetter vegetables can extend beyond 125. The time fields and public learning prose now include release, reduction and sequential work. There is no parallel skillet masala to subtract from elapsed time and no oven requirement. Do not promise the original thirty-minute convenience.

Final **four vegetable-main portions, approximately 4–6 cups total** is deliberately a broad yield estimate; retained juices, varying produce and reduction alter final volume. Do not insist on the first proposal's four measured one-cup portions. Starting edible vegetables total 1.4 kg, with oil and aromatics retained; four substantial vegetable servings are plausible. No specific protein/calorie completeness is claimed.
