# Japanese Cucumber Sunomono: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Replaced the wildly variable two-English/four-regular cucumber equivalence with 400 g usable cucumber for four small side portions. Defined thin 1–2 mm slices with a safe mandoline alternative.
- Retained 3 tbsp rice vinegar, specifying unseasoned; reduced sugar from 2 tbsp to 1 tbsp for a brighter side and reduced dressing salt to a measured 1/4 tsp Diamond Crystal. Draining salt is separate and named by type. These choices require tasting, not an authenticity claim.
- Removed optional dashi powder, which undermined vegan labeling and added unmeasured sodium. Kept optional ginger/shichimi and measured sesame, accounting for every ingredient.
- Corrected total from 10 to 25 minutes, included the salt-and-drain stage, and removed the contradictory overnight-marinade tag and blanket claim that longer holding improves it. Make-ahead preparation now keeps dressing separate.
- Compared with the reviewed smashed cucumber salad and Korean oi muchim. Retained as a meaningful variation in cut, vinegar, seasoning, and texture, not a duplicate of either.
- The primary Japanese Cooking 101 source confirms the preparation family; its seasoning contains soy sauce and differs from this inherited amazu-style formula. Removed unverified nutrition and rigid cultural generalizations.

## Kitchen-test questions

Check four small portions, cucumber thickness, water release, vinegar/sugar balance with actual vinegar acidity, salt retention after draining, and texture after refrigerated holding. No physical kitchen test recorded.
