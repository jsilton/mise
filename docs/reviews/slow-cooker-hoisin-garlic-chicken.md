# Slow-Cooker Hoisin Garlic Chicken: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Individual findings and proportion decisions

Read the complete original and compared it with slow-cooker General Tso and the reviewed honey-sesame preparation. Kept this garlic-led, balsamic/tomato, coarsely pulled and reduction-finished dish distinct from the ginger-chile/stronger rice-vinegar/starch-finished adaptation. Balsamic marks it as Chinese-inspired home cooking, not a claim of traditional Chinese origin.

Reduced raw thigh from 1.13 kg for four to 900 g for six modest mains with separate starch/vegetable components. Retained 60 ml hoisin and 1 tbsp tomato paste; added 120 ml water for an explicit initial sauce; used six minced garlic cloves, removed redundant garlic powder, lowered optional chile to one-quarter teaspoon, and defined two scallions. Set balsamic to 15 ml divided between cook and finish. Actual hoisin salt/sugar and thigh liquid release matter more than a fixed reduction by half.

Replaced fixed two-hour HIGH cooking and unchecked shredding with thawing, 165°F and pulling tenderness, 4–5-hour LOW/2–3-hour HIGH ranges, cooker fit, fat skimming, roughly 250–300 ml sauce with an early-stop salinity cue, coarse pulling and gentle final warming. Total planned LOW time is 5 hr 30 min, including prep and reduction. Removed syrup guarantees and unverified nutrition. No ingredient is introduced only in the method; rice/broccoli remain separate optional components.
