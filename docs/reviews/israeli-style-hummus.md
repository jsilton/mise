## Proportions, yield, and ingredient accounting

The original contains 1 cup dried chickpeas, 1 cup tahini, 1/4 cup lemon juice, four unpeeled garlic cloves, 2 tsp baking soda split between stages, unspecified kosher salt, cumin, and two unspecified-size ice cubes. It uses another 1/4 cup water and paprika or za'atar that are absent from the ingredient list. The method also tells the cook to drain before calling for cooking liquid without reserving it.

The proposal preserves the tahini-heavy profile and original chickpea, lemon, garlic, cumin, and soda framework. Dry chickpeas become an explicit 200 g starting weight; tahini becomes 240 g, matching the reviewed sauce's 120 g per half-cup convention. This is editorial measurement normalization, not a weighed kitchen yield. Approximately 480–500 g cooked, drained chickpeas is a provisional substitution target, not a promise that every 200 g dried batch yields exactly that amount. Can weights include liquid, so the alternative explicitly uses drained weight. No recipe step substitutes one cup cooked chickpeas for one cup dried.

The rough mass budget is about 480–500 g hydrated drained chickpeas + 240 g tahini + up to 60 g initial lemon minus straining losses + roughly 60–120 g blending water, before optional lemon, seasoning, or extra thinning. That suggests roughly 0.85–0.95 kg puree and a plausible 3 1/2–4 cup target, but volume depends on hydration and entrained air. The original 4 cups is plausible as an approximation, not a verified measurement. State yield as approximate and separate it from portion count. Remove unverified nutrition because its serving unit was undefined and because additions and salt are now specified differently.

Water is listed by purpose; two ice cubes become adjustable measured cool water. Drainage uses a fine strainer to retain disintegrating beans, and fresh water replaces the awkward unreserved cooking liquid. This also avoids concentrating residual soda in the final adjustment. Oil and the formerly unlisted paprika have quantities. Every optional ingredient has a destination. The full batch's garnish is divided proportionally when serving part of it.

## Technique, time, and science

The original twelve-hour total covers only its soak. The proposal retains twelve hours but refrigerates the soak, gives a 60–120-minute simmer range plus heat-up time, overlaps the garlic infusion, and displays the upper planning allowance of 14 hours 30 minutes. This is a conservative scheduling choice; older beans can exceed it. The cue is a creamy center under a fork, not a clock or translucent skin. Hardware capacity and duty-cycle limits are explicit.

Remove the assertion that ice causes a temperature shock that emulsifies hummus and the claim of the smoothest hummus on earth. Neither is established here. Keep observed sesame-paste thickening/thinning rather than an elaborate phase model. No molecular claim that waiting ten minutes neutralizes previously formed garlic pungency is made. Garlic is broken down in lemon before straining, and the processor is rinsed so papery fragments do not get returned to the food.

Primary practitioner evidence: Lopez-Alt reports comparisons supporting acidic garlic processing, alkaline chickpea softening, and cooking beyond intact-bean tenderness; his blender method uses hot puree. Those findings inform the review but do not establish an ice-shock mechanism or test this exact formula. Our warmer-food processor handling, fresh-water adjustment, range, yield estimate, and canned-bean option are editorial development.

## Source and cultural accuracy

The direct Bon Appetit page credits Michael Solomonov and documents his named preparation. The proposal preserves that attribution, labels this an adaptation, and uses broad Middle East origin plus Israeli/Middle Eastern cuisine tags. It does not assign a sole national birthplace or claim a particular country's cooks always use a fixed tahini ratio. No family history or invented tradition was present or added.

## Troubleshooting and substitutions

The recipe now distinguishes firm centers from simple insufficient dilution, avoids treating long blending as a universal repair, explains cold thickening, and gives conditional remedies for runniness and soapy bitterness. Rancid tahini is replaced; lemon is not presented as a repair for it. Canned chickpeas are allowed as an explicitly different starting material; the soda and soak are omitted and the beans are cooked to the same center cue. The 20–60-minute simmer/40–80-minute total is an editorial estimate needing a can-brand comparison. Tahini has no identical sesame-free substitution for this preparation; another seed butter produces a different dip.

## Storage and composition

Use general government cooked-leftover guidance: shallow covered containers at or below 40°F / 4°C, refrigerate within two hours or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, and use within 3–4 days. This is conservative general guidance, not a hummus-specific challenge study. Previously cooked bean storage days count toward the window; blending with lemon does not restart it. No week-long life is imported from a culinary source. No untested freezer texture or mandatory reheating claim is added.

Pairings now earn their place: warm flexible pita carries the spread, fattoush contributes crisp vegetables and tartness, and shawarma adds spiced browned chicken. About 1/4 cup hummus per person is an editorial side portion: five need 1 1/4 cups, not the whole batch. The separate shawarma recipe already includes a tahini drizzle; that is an additional sesame element and can be offered sparingly when hummus shares the plate. This review does not claim a completed meal audit or alter the chicken recipe.

## Kitchen-test questions

1. Weigh the actual one-cup dry chickpea measure, hydrated cooked yield, drained mass, finished mass, and finished cups. Record cultivar/age and how many grams remain after passing through the chosen strainer.
2. Compare at least two tahini brands at 240 g; record total blending water, lemon, salt, warm texture, and next-day texture. Is the 3 1/2–4 cup estimate and seven/eight half-cup portion description accurate?
3. Time the refrigerated soak and simmer in the specified pot with the actual burner. Does 1 tsp soda in six cups cooking water leave noticeable flavor after thorough draining? Does a brief warm-water rinse improve flavor without losing too much soft chickpea?
4. Test the processor's ability to chop four cloves in 60 ml lemon juice and then strain enough juice. Does its small bowl work better? Record garlic size, retained solids, juice recovery, and pungency immediately and after storage.
5. Compare 480–500 g drained canned chickpeas with the dried batch; record brand, starting firmness, necessary simmer duration without soda, water additions, and flavor. Do not label the shortcut equivalent before this comparison.
6. Taste 1/4-cup hummus alongside the actual shawarma, pita, and fattoush. Is sesame richness excessive with the chicken's full tahini drizzle? Measure quantities and diner response before changing a composed meal.
7. Repeat the final written version with another cook and record equipment, elapsed and active time, finished yield, sensory outcome, and failures before any reproducibility or kitchen-tested claim.

## Root evaluation and integrated corrections

Accepted the richer 200 g dry chickpea/240 g tahini identity and rejected inventing a duplicate or treating this as the existing tahini sauce. Independently checked the accessible Bon Appetit, Serious Eats and FoodSafety.gov pages. The independent challenge exposed a small-load appliance gap: the final version requires a mini chopper/small insert for garlic in 60 ml lemon and a larger processor bowl for the finished puree, plus a named 4-quart cooking pot. Soak setup adds five minutes: final total 14 hr 35 min, prep 20 min, about 30 minutes hands-on; the cooked-bean option allows 45–85 minutes. Cooked substitution weight is explicitly before its additional simmer.

Refined mass reasoning: approximately 0.82–0.92 kg base puree before optional adjustments, seasoning and serving oil is a plausible budget from estimated drained chickpeas, tahini, recovered lemon and water. It is not a measured volume conversion; 3 1/2–4 cups remains approximate. The published recipe makes no kitchen-tested claim. No ice-shock mechanism, sole national origin or week-long preservation claim was retained.
