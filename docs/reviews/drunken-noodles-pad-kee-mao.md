# Pad Kee Mao (Drunken Noodles): individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

Reviewed the full original, including unspecified oil, marinade oil missing from ingredients, ginger/scallion appearing only in the method, basil added too early, and chicken removed at an unverifiable 90% endpoint. Reconciled every ingredient: omitted the stray ginger/scallion and the unnecessary wine, measured 45 ml oil across two chicken batches, vegetables and four noodle batches, and separated chicken/sauce water and soy.

Raised chicken from 340 to 450 g and the dried-noodle alternative from 225 to 300 g for four main portions. Default is now 800 g fresh wide noodles, with dried explicitly a practical alternative rather than an exact conversion. Retained shallot/baby-corn composition, now weighed at 100/150 g. Sauce has 30 ml oyster, 15 ml fish, 15 ml light soy, 5 ml dark soy, 30 ml water and 2 tsp sugar; this is a considered increase from the small original formula, with first-portion tasting and brand variation acknowledged. Exact salt balance still requires cooking.

Added chicken 165°F checks, four final batches, package-specific noodle hydration and a late basil finish. Removed the aggressive scraping/MAXIMUM HEAT/wok-hei guarantee. Holy basil is the default with explicit aroma-changing substitutes. The full one-cook schedule is about 55 minutes, not a 15-minute cook for four finished servings; dried soaking can extend it. Removed extra rice pairing and unverified nutrition. Leftover quality is now poor rather than good.

## Source and relationship review

Pailin's primary recipe text retrieved via the indexed comment-page route supports holy basil, separate protein cooking, portion organization and short noodle contact before a late herb finish. Her vegetables, sauce and optional aromatics differ; this retains our site's baby-corn/shallot version rather than copying her complete recipe. Pad see ew remains distinct through soy-led seasoning, Chinese broccoli and eggs, without this chile/basil profile. Drunken Noodles Night needs its actual batch schedule and a separate decision about edamame; it is not a completed meal review merely because this main is reviewed.
