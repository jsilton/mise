The substitution uses the listed optional cayenne early instead of at the finish, preventing either a lost ingredient or a second dose. ## Aloo gobi: individual findings

The original is a repository-authored, tomato-first, staged-potato recipe, first added in April 2026; no saved family author or external immediate source was recovered. Keep that distinction instead of relabeling it an exact Swasthi or Hari recipe. The proposed three tablespoons oil, cumin plus crushed whole coriander and ground coriander, garlic, ginger, serranos, turmeric, tomato, lemon and cilantro preserve its identity. Moving onion before garlic, delaying garam masala, covering the vegetable stages and abandoning the compulsory crisp/crumbling claims are coherent technique corrections, not a conversion to a different creamy curry.

The default 680 g potatoes plus 680 g cauliflower is substantial: about 340 g core vegetables per main serving, or 227 g per generous side, before tomato/onion. Four mains with an accompaniment or six larger vegetable sides are plausible. Three tablespoons oil remain intact. The final 1 tsp fine salt plus optional 1/2 tsp reserve gives approximately 6–9 g across roughly 1.6–1.7 kg unsalted vegetables/aromatics. That staged range is reasonable and leaves tasting room; the primary comparisons support reconsidering the earlier unusually low cap without proving an exact gram-normalized dose. This is a new seasoning decision, not a same-volume kosher conversion. Fresh serranos and cayenne are not reliably equivalent heat doses; the explicit omit-all option is the dependable very-mild path.

A deep, lidded 12-inch pan of 5–6 quarts can accommodate the raw vegetable volume and gentle folding. The potato-only stage, followed by smaller cauliflower florets, creates a usable transition check. Tomatoes can affect softening; the author preserves this tomato-first identity and compensates with covered cooking, longer time and a measured water reserve rather than claiming time alone proves doneness. Raising time from 35 minutes to a 65–80-minute range is warranted. Twenty minutes prep plus onion/aromatics, tomato reduction, potato head start, reheating with cauliflower, covered finish and uncovered drying plausibly centers in that range. Upper bounds, slow cutting or additional potato cooking can exceed 80 minutes, as stated. About 40–50 active minutes is plausible; the covered stages are not entirely unattended.

[Swasthi Shreekanth's first aloo-gobi method](https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/aloo-gobi-recipe/) supports staged vegetables, covered gentle cooking and moisture checks; her tomatoes go late, so it does not establish the proposal's early-tomato clock. [Hari Ghotra's method](https://www.harighotra.co.uk/aloo-gobi-recipe) directly provides an early tomato masala, potatoes before cauliflower, covered cooking and gentle turning. Her cuts, seasonings and no-added-water approach differ, and her numbered cook stages exceed her headline clock; use her method as a comparison, not exact timing validation. Both pages were directly readable. [Dassana Amit's current page](https://www.vegrecipesofindia.com/aloo-gobi-recipe-punjabialoo-gobi/) now presents a wetter cashew/cream variation; the proposal appropriately does not use that as proof of its dry-style formula.

## Coconut chickpea curry: individual findings

[Jessica Hylton's current primary recipe](https://jessicainthekitchen.com/coconut-chickpea-curry-recipe/) is directly readable and matches the saved core formula and method. Its chickpea wording is ambiguous about package versus drained mass, so do not call 454 g a proven historical drained amount. Retaining the current repository's two cans and defining an approximately 500 g drained expectation is transparent. Its old vegan-and-GF slug is history of this same recipe, not a newly discovered live duplicate to count separately.

The full-fat coconut milk, two tablespoons coconut oil and generous 1½ tablespoons garam masala remain. They define a rich, aromatic sauce; there is no reason to cut them for dietary goals. A 400 ml working milk volume is a small standardized measure, distinct from recovering an exact equivalence to the archive's 383 g. Canned tomatoes retain all their juice; rinsed beans do not donate an unlisted can of liquid. Coconut flour is optional and mixed with part of the already measured milk, so no extra milk is hidden. Lime is measured separately and added gradually. The chili-flake addition is optional editorial heat control, not a recovered original ingredient. Salt, cilantro and water reserve have clear destinations.

The approximately five-cup finished yield is plausible after tomato reduction and simmering, not measured: bean displacement plus the remaining sauce determines volume, rather than adding every ingredient's loose measuring-cup volume. Four portions contain about 125 g cooked drained chickpeas each, with sauce. Forty-five minutes is reasonable for 15 minutes preparation, 10–12 minutes moist onion/tomato cooking, a brief spice stir, heating, 10–12 minutes gentle simmering and finishing. Moist tomato cooking need not become dry spice frying; the draft correctly avoids that claim. The full recipe's 4-quart, 9–10-inch pot provides ample capacity and useful reduction area. The flour is cooked with the curry, not sprinkled onto finished bowls.

Correcting the Thai/Thailand assertions is supported: this formula uses garam masala and an Indian-style curry powder and has verified authorship from Jessica Hylton. It should be presented as her adaptation, not a newly discovered traditional regional dish. Chana masala is genuinely different: no coconut milk, a separate tomato/whole-cumin/amchur system and mashed chickpeas. These do not warrant consolidation.

## Accompaniments and meal boundary

Basmati rice and naan are alternatives, not automatically two full side batches. The reviewed rice makes six side portions in forty minutes; the reviewed naan makes twelve breads and takes up to three hours from dough. Recipe time does not include either. Aloo gobi already contains a substantial potato allocation, making a small rice portion appropriate. Raita is a small cool spoonful; choosing it introduces dairy alongside an otherwise vegan curry. Coconut rice would repeat the main's coconut richness, while cilantro-lime rice repeats its lime finish; replacing default suggestions with plain basmati or bread is a culinary composition choice, not a claim those foods cannot accompany curry. Do chua is a separate Vietnamese condiment and cannot be assumed to fit a 45-minute dinner when made from scratch.

Indian Dal Night remains **unreviewed**. Its current four full components, extra naan suggestion, 30-minute active claim and crispy-aloo wording do not become correct merely because aloo gobi gains an individual review. Saffron-cardamom rice is still unreviewed. This two-recipe batch must not certify or silently rewrite that meal; retain a concrete follow-up in the record.
