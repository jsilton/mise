# Garlic-Ginger Stir-Fry Sauce: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read complete original and all inbound references. It was called garlic sauce with no garlic, used an unheated starch mixture as if it were cooked, described starch as dissolved, and claimed one cup despite ingredient volumes exceeding that. Added measured garlic/ginger and neutral oil, reduced soy/dark-soy concentration, clarified unsalted liquid and oyster/mushroom alternatives, and standardized the final cooked yield to 240 ml. One teaspoon slurry starch gives a light body; cold dispersion, remixing and a brief simmer are explicit. Vinegar/sesame finish, five-minute cooking and ten-minute preparation replace the seven-minute raw-mixture shortcut.

A 120 ml half batch is a required component of the retained 600 g Garlic-Ginger Eggplant. Its default vegetarian version uses mushroom sauce. This is not an all-purpose substitute for Pad Thai sauce or a Sicilian fried-eggplant technique: removed those unrelated incoming references. Uses with other food begin by coating to taste, not a universal quantity or automatic double seasoning. No nutrition claim remains. Federal cooling/reheating rules do not establish a shelf-stable garlic preserve; it is a refrigerated cooked sauce, with the original storage clock retained after reuse.
