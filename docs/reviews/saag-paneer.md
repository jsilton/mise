## Amounts, yield and substitutions

Fresh spinach becomes an explicit **680 g trimmed net raw weight**, excluding tough stems, rather than an ambiguous bunch purchase weight. The dish has approximately 100 g paneer and 170 g raw trimmed spinach per diner, plus onion/cream/fat. Four mains require a starch alongside; no exact finished-cup or cooked-spinach weight is invented. Cooking and squeezing lose variable water and some soluble material, so fresh weight is not a finished yield guarantee.

The old 10–12 oz frozen alternative is not established as equivalent to 680 g raw greens. Choose **450 g frozen chopped spinach before cooking/draining**, a full, practical greens option near the inherited 16 oz quantity. This is an editorial alternative, not an exact conversion or complete restoration of the old formula: it keeps the modern cheese and cream. Freeze processing and retained package liquid affect yield; the recipe explicitly says drained amount/texture differs. Cook according to the actual package before cooling and squeezing; no thaw-only safety assumption. The substitution is expressed per 680 g fresh so it remains usable when scaling.

The onion becomes 150 g peeled, a chosen usable net mass rather than claiming every medium onion weighs this. Garlic remains four cloves, ginger one tablespoon. Jalapeño becomes optional for mild diners; same measured serrano option retains its hotter difference rather than pretending one to two whole peppers equal one tablespoon.

Salt is now **1/2 tsp fine initially plus up to 1/4 tsp reserve in the sauce**, and **1/2 tsp fine in 720 ml soak water**, discarded. These are explicit editorial doses, not a spoon conversion from the old unspecified kosher salt. Absorption from the soak is unknown and is not all counted as retained seasoning. Initial sauce salt plus optional reserve allows adjustment for different paneer salinity and drained green yield. No unlisted blanching salt.

Cream stays exactly 80 ml. Lemon becomes **one tablespoon maximum, half first then taste**; the original half-fruit juice varied. Reserve **60 ml water** for correcting spinach consistency instead of unlimited added cream. These allowances are not automatically added. Optional smooth sauce uses this full same reserve for blending, not an extra quarter cup. Optional chile ghee conserves its one tablespoon fat and half teaspoon powder but blooms off heat to reduce scorching. No automatic cream, salt or garnish additions remain.

## Sequence, geometry and sensory checks

A 6-quart pot with three litres blanching water accommodates four roughly 170 g loads; return water to the boil between batches rather than forcing 680 g buoyant raw leaves into one small pan. An explicit cold bath is two litres cold water plus 500 g ice; it must remain cold. Drain and squeeze until no longer freely dripping, leaving tender moist chopped greens. The bone-dry rule and guarantee of permanently green glossy sauce are removed. Ice cooling stops this cooking stage; it does not lock color against all later heat or acid.

A deep 12-inch nonstick skillet (at least 3 quarts) has ample final cooked capacity and browns paneer in two approximately 200 g rounds. One third of the three main tablespoons ghee goes to those rounds, divided equally; two thirds remains for cumin/onion. Brown two broad faces 2–3 minutes each, not most/all sides. Nonstick and properly dried cheese make the small frying-fat allocation plausible; no generic protein internal-temperature rule is imported for this cheese. Very fragile fresh paneer may skip frying with an explicit loss of browned flavor.

Hot drinking water is measured 720 ml with its own salt. Each round goes into the snug bowl; after the final round, the 10–20 minute soak overlaps masala cooking. It is not held at a simmer or claimed to reverse severe overfrying. First-round cheese may soak several minutes longer, which is harmless to the planning claim but a texture question. Drain thoroughly and discard soak water before the cheese returns to the dish.

Onion gets 8–10 minutes to soften and become golden at edges, followed by 1–2 minutes aromatics, 20 seconds powdered spice, 3–5 minutes spinach, 1–2 minutes cream and 3–5 minutes gentle cheese warming. Off-heat garam masala/lemon remain late additions; acid dose is tasted to avoid dominating the cream. Final paneer center must be hot, greens moist without a puddle. Burnt spice fat is discarded rather than hidden. Base moisture can be corrected before cheese goes in, reducing its unnecessary cooking.

Half batch: smaller deep 10-inch pan and smaller blanching pot, same cube size/endpoints. Larger: additional uncrowded paneer rounds, spinach loads and greater final-pan capacity or separate full batches. Ghee distribution is proportional; numeric 1+2 tablespoons is explicitly anchored to the original batch. Recipe headings and substitution wording do not imply exact non-scaling process quantities.

## Time and practicality

About 60–70 minutes with already washed leaves is an editorial planning estimate: overlapping water heat/prep 15–20, batch blanch/cool/chop 10–15, pan heat/two paneer rounds 10–14, masala/greens 13–18, cream/cheese/finish 6–9. Faster edges overlap; all upper endpoints can exceed 70. Active 45–55 minutes, plus washing gritty bunches if needed. The old 40-minute headline omitted realistic water heating, all faces of the cheese and an ice/chopping stage. The dinner separately budgets rice/raita instead of hiding them in this total.

Fresh default is a deliberate choice preserving the current dish. Frozen can reduce blanching logistics but still needs its package cooking, cooling and draining time. Creamy saag is not made generically lower-fat; butter is a same-amount substitution with lower heat. Fenugreek leaves stay prominent; omitting them changes aroma, and seed powder is not an equal substitute. No automatically vegan tofu swap is claimed while retaining ghee and cream.
