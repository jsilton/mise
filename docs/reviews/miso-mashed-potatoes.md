The source page has no identified author, cookbook or family attribution to preserve beyond its ingredient/method identity.

Define the two pounds of potatoes as 900 g peeled and trimmed, retaining the approximate original batch size and making shopping weight roughly 1.05 kg. Four portions are explicitly generous starch portions (225 g net raw potato each), with six smaller servings possible alongside another starch. No exact cups or cooked mass are promised. The formula's 57 g butter is retained: there is no evidence that four tablespoons is wrong for this potato quantity, and erroneous inherited nutrition is not a culinary reason to reduce it.

Retain the two tablespoons white miso, defined as 36 g using the primary Hikari label's 18 g tablespoon as a reference; this is a specified working quantity, not a universal volumetric density. Hikari's current white product is a rice/soybean miso and labels 820 mg sodium per 18 g serving. That single brand demonstrates why miso is substantial seasoning, but does not establish all brands' sodium content. Require plain miso without added fish dashi. Change the unconditional gluten-free tag to gluten-free-option and tell cooks to verify the product. Soy and dairy remain. No fish-free claim is made for arbitrary instant miso soup products.

The original unspecified salted cooking water is now 2 L or enough to cover by one inch, with 8 g salt (0.4% at the measured water volume). Most water drains; this is not eight grams of salt necessarily eaten. Miso supplies finishing salt, so additional one-eighth teaspoon fine salt is optional only after tasting. Keep original half-cup dairy as 120 ml whole milk instead of the vague milk-or-cream default. Up to 60 ml further milk is an explicit texture reserve, not a mandatory increase. Heavy cream remains an explained alternative with more fat and less water. The original half-teaspoon garlic powder was never used in its method; it now disperses in the warm miso dairy. Retain garlic powder deliberately rather than replacing it with raw garlic that would need its own cooking. Quantify the optional garnish as two tablespoons washed scallion greens, used after final seasoning and stored separately for later portions.

Correct the scientific teaching: brief drying removes surface water, not an enemy of all creaminess. Warm dairy maintains serving temperature and facilitates gentle combination; cold additions do not by themselves guarantee irreversible glue. Full potato tenderness and limited mechanical work are the important texture controls. Miso is dispersed before folding, avoiding salty clumps that would demand extra stirring. Avoid saying it completely “dissolves”; some pastes retain fine particles. The ricer/masher choice stays practical and no blender is introduced.

The original 30 minutes omitted pot heating, realistic peeling and final draining/mashing. The proposed approximately 55-minute total includes one-inch pieces, heating, an estimated 18–22-minute simmer, warm-dairy work during simmering and the final mash. It is a planning estimate, not a timed result. Bigger pieces or a slow burner can extend it. Optional thirty-minute hot holding requires a heatproof bowl and measured 140°F minimum rather than an unspecified warm setting. Refrigeration, shallow cooling, three-to-four-day use and 165°F reheating are sourced. Milk for reheating is a condition-dependent leftover adjustment, not a hidden addition to the fresh recipe. Garnish is separated when saving portions.

Remove the inherited 190-calorie/15 g carbohydrate/110 mg sodium nutrition, which cannot be trusted for this generous potato/miso serving. Keep the underlying butter formula rather than correcting it to match those numbers. Mark hearty for a generous potato side with butter/milk; that is meal-planning metadata, not a nutritional measurement.
