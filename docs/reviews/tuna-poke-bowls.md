## Formula, identity and ingredient accounting

Retain this as **Tuna Poke Bowls with Chile and Cucumber Salad** at its original slug. Its defining composition is chile-soy tuna with Korean oi muchim, edamame, avocado and nori for four. The mild shoyu recipe uses fresh cucumber and sugar for three larger bowls; the bar has multiple seafood choices and controlled self-service. Shared tuna/soy/sesame dressing alone is insufficient to treat all three as identical, though future kitchen testing may support a simpler single canonical recipe with tested variations.

Preserve approximately one pound fish as 450 g edible yellowfin, 3 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp sesame oil, 1 tsp vinegar, 1/2 tsp ginger and 1/2 tsp heat. Default sriracha makes the sauce's thickness and label clearer; original chile crisp remains a same-amount alternative with its additional oil/allergens explained. Preserve four cooked cups rice and one avocado. The original rice link incorrectly calls a butter/onion pilaf jasmine rice; replace it with an actual measured shari dependency and a genuine plain-short-grain option.

The unmeasured Korean cucumber link becomes a half recipe of prepared oi muchim, starting with 300 g cucumber and divided across four bowls. This is 75 g starting cucumber per diner, with actual finished yield depending on drainage, and respects the recently reviewed cucumber recipe's fresh-salad identity rather than calling it shelf-stable pickles. This quantity is editorially chosen and its drained yield needs measurement. The soy/garlic/chile/sesame in oi muchim is part of the bowl's overall seasoning; it is neither automatically drained of all flavor nor poured in as a second full sauce.

The original 1/2 cup edamame is represented by 75 g shelled beans, approximately 19 g per bowl. This is a modest topping, not a full vegetable or protein side. Package-directed cooking/chilling replaces the original missing preparation. The garnish placeholders become two scallions, 1 tbsp sesame and one sheet nori. Optional soy/spicy mayo from the original method are now measured at 1 tbsp and 2 tbsp for the table; not compulsory extra drizzles. Unverified baseline nutrition is removed because its portion/formula assumptions cannot be verified. All ingredients are used in method steps; the small edamame amount is preserved rather than inflated silently.

## Raw fish and method corrections

Replace the label-only freshness rule with supplier/species/handling checks and clear limits. Yellowfin has a specific parasite-freezing exemption, which does not mean raw tuna is hazard-free. Original bigeye alternative is not selected as the family default given distinct FDA mercury advice; no claim is made that yellowfin is appropriate raw for children. The chef's note and sourcing paragraph avoid “traditional” claims for the Korean/Hawaiian bowl combination.

Brief 5–10-minute refrigerated dressing is an editorial service suggestion, not an exact curing threshold. Acid cannot rescue mishandled fish. Raw-contact dressing is not a reusable table sauce. Tuna's temperature history matters for histamine; freezing and cooking do not remove histamine once formed. Cold fish remains refrigerated until the final assembly. Fish intended for vulnerable diners should be fully cooked in an appropriate separate meal rather than reassuring them that a sourcing label removes risk.

Rice is prepared with its full soak, cook, rest and finishing sequence, then measured; a full shari batch makes extra. Cucumber salting and bean preparation run in parallel. Ninety minutes replaces the unsupported 40-minute total; 30 minutes applies only when rice, cucumber and beans are already safely prepared. Final fish cutting and seasoning overlap setting out bowls, not prolonged room-temperature rice storage. Nori is added dry at service.
