# Philly Tuna Roll: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Proposal only; no physical test. Complete current/first source and history read with both adjacent rolls and final proposed shari. No repository edits.

## Identity and attribution

Preserve tuna, cream cheese, cucumber and sesame as a distinct roll. Do not silently replace tuna with salmon because many restaurant Philadelphia rolls use it; the source title specifically says tuna. Its exact 1980s brand-origin history is unsupported by the available source record and is omitted. Japanese-American is a style description, not a claim of a verified inventor or place/date. No pre-Astro matching HTML or linked author source was recovered; all three files first appear at fd4f07a8.

The original has nori outside. Sprinkling sesame onto its dry exterior and expecting adhesion is inconsistent with that method. Keep the full tablespoon sesame but place it on the rice before filling, so it stays inside the wrapper. Do not silently flip to an inside-out roll merely to justify the old topping step.

## Ratios, quantities and closure

Retain eight ounces raw tuna and four ounces cream cheese as 230 g and 115 g, rounded practical weights, not a recipe reduction. Standardize an ambiguous half English cucumber to 100 g usable flesh after removing the watery core. That is a selected consistent filling amount, not a claim that all half cucumbers weigh 100 g. One quarter of the filling is about 58 g tuna, 29 g cheese and 25 g cucumber, plus its portion of sesame. Cold block cheese gives a firm strip; whipped tub spread is not treated as equivalent.

Preserve the two prepared cups rice as a weighed 320 g, 80 g per roll. The total of rice, tuna, cheese, cucumber and sesame is roughly 775 g before nori, about 194 g per roll. Four rolls yield 32 pieces at eight each, approximately two main portions or four starters. The original “serves four” would otherwise be misread as four complete dinners despite only two cooked cups rice and half a pound fish. No calorie estimate is adopted.

The original half-sheet/one-inch seam must enclose about 112 g filling plus 80 g rice. This is far larger than the single-strip half-sheet maki example checked in the primary source. Increase wrappers to four 19 x 15 cm three-quarter sheets cut from four full sheets, leaving one centimeter bare. Full-sheet purchase and dry saved offcuts are explicit. This gives additional wrap space while preserving filling and nori-outside identity; the first closure test and possible remove-and-serve-small-excess instruction acknowledge actual capacity still needs a physical test. See docs/reviews/sushi-roll-batch-comparison.md for the geometry estimate and its limits.

Tuna strips are about 6–10 mm thick and can be placed end to end. Cut across visible muscle grain where useful and remove tough connective tissue; the original “always perpendicular to every white line means never chewy” is not supportable. Cheese and cucumber are cut before fish on clean equipment. No invented rice ridge or hard compression is required. Clean, sharp, damp-blade cutting creates a fixed eight-piece yield.

## Sequence, timing and storage

From dry rice, 120 minutes includes shari's 80-minute process, ingredient preparation during its waits and about 40 minutes of final rolling/cutting. Ready rice changes the schedule to about 45 minutes. Frozen tuna thawing is additional; a first cook or slow rice program can take longer. Rice is barely warm only for brief rolling, not held indefinitely at a target body temperature. Fish and cheese come from the refrigerator one roll's portion at a time.

Use-by history and intended raw service matter more than marketing grade. Yellowfin's species-specific parasite exemption is not a universal safety certificate, and home freezing is not prescribed as a substitute for sourcing controls. FDA susceptible-group advice is explicit. Dipping-water vinegar is a nonstick aid, not sanitation. Cold cheese contains milk; fish and sesame remain. No unsupported dietary or nutrition labels are added.
