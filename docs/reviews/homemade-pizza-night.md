## Formula decision and attribution

Kenji López-Alt](https://www.seriouseats.com/jim-laheys-no-knead-pizza-dough-recipe): flour 500 g, water 350 g, fine sea salt 16 g, active-dry yeast 1 g. It is a replacement, not a conversion of the old three cups of flour. Water/flour is 70%; salt/flour is 3.2%; yeast/flour is 0.2%; mixed mass is 867 g. The printed source's spoon salt equivalent is not adopted: weigh the specified mass. This is an assertively salted source formula; taste its interaction with Parmesan and salty optional toppings before claiming an optimal result.

The old 3 cups flour/1.5 cups water is ambiguous and unusually wet under common flour conventions. [King Arthur's flour chart](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/ingredient-weight-chart) uses 120 g per cup, but no chart establishes the original author's actual cup mass. Do not relabel three cups as 500 g. The source-attributed replacement resolves a reproducible trial without falsely recovering intent.

## Process and geometry

The dough has one defined overnight rise near 72°F/22°C, controlled by aeration and expansion; post-rise refrigeration is a separate branch. [Lahey's Bon Appétit method](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/no-knead-pizza-dough) informs the additional relaxed-ball rest and separate cool-pan/hot-stone methods. The draft deliberately uses a bake-only 500°F finish rather than importing that source's broiler timer. [Kenji's home-oven pizza](https://www.seriouseats.com/new-york-style-pizza) informs the 12–15-minute starting bracket; underside and set-center cues control completion. Neither diameter nor fresh/low-moisture cheese is promised the same exact time.

New geometry choice: four roughly 217 g, 10-inch pies, or two roughly 434 g, 14-inch pies. Their total areas are approximately 314 and 308 square inches. The large branch requires a correctly sized pan, peel or stone, not an ordinary half-sheet assumption. All four pizzas require separate oven rounds; pans cool before reuse. Planned total is about 21 hours, mostly fermentation; active work is about 45 minutes excluding preparation of optional toppings.

## Topping and sauce ledger

Preserve all 12 oz mozzarella and 1 oz Parmesan. Four-pie allocation is 3 oz plus 1/4 oz per pie; two-pie allocation is 6 oz plus 1/2 oz. Each pizza uses half its own mozzarella below and half above toppings, not half the entire batch on the first pie. Remove the unsupported moisture-barrier claim about cheese already laid over sauce.

Retain the existing sauce batch: one 28 oz can tomatoes, 2 tbsp oil, three garlic cloves, 1/2 tsp salt and optional 1/4 tsp flakes/basil. Delete the contradictory second two-clove sauce. Use 3 tbsp on each small pie or 6 tbsp on each large one: 3/4 cup total. This selects the former upper bound and scales it by area; the remaining sauce is an explicit leftover. A prepared-sauce substitution omits the complete sauce-making component, preventing double shopping. No exact finished sauce yield is invented.

Optional topping choices remain optional, with prepared/cooked states stated in the exported recipe body. Fresh mozzarella stays the default but is drained/blotted; the same-weight low-moisture option remains. Preserve richness and customization rather than imposing a health-driven reduction. The four-lens judgment favors a coherent family activity with measured dough, sensible equipment and a sparse topping layer; it makes no unearned New York-style or universal child-handling claim.
