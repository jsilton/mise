## Cooking sequence and broiler capacity

The original/current method broils chicken before a 30–40-minute sauce build and describes incomplete cooking with a percentage or visual center. That is not a usable endpoint, particularly across two home broiler batches. The proposal prepares the sauce base first. Each chicken batch reaches 165°F / 74°C before it leaves the oven; the first rests briefly on a clean platter while the second cooks. A brief return to finished cream sauce avoids simmering the first batch through the full second broil. This is a material method change justified by both immediate-completion logistics and quality, not a claim that all traditional two-stage cooking is unsafe.

Two 450 g batches each fit with generous spacing on a 13-by-18-inch metal rack/pan, positioned under the broiler's actual active area. The unused tray is prepared empty; the second chicken portion stays refrigerated until needed. A full rack's area does not guarantee a domestic element heats it evenly, so only one tray cooks at a time and rotation remains available. The cook watches throughout, turns after 6–8 minutes and checks thick centers; 12–18 minutes is a planning range for these thighs, not a measured universal broiler result. Distance is measured from the top of food. Equipment must be broiler-safe, with foil under the rack and no parchment. Oven-door position follows the appliance instructions.

If browned before cooked, move farther from the element or continue immediately at 400°F. Do not hold partial chicken. If cooked but pale, stop rather than force blackening and dry meat. Surface color is separate from center temperature. The breast option begins checks at eight minutes but keeps the same 165°F endpoint.

Keep raw tools away from the cooked platter. Clean-platter juices return to the sauce. The proposal deliberately discards scorched broiler-pan drippings, unlike the archive's blanket addition of pan juices: concentrated burned yogurt/spices can make the full sauce bitter, and pan liquid should not be assumed safe solely from one chicken temperature reading. This loses some roasted liquid flavor; it does not authorize removing the sauce's fat or the clean resting juices. Actual drip loss and flavor are kitchen questions.

Use a 5–6-quart, approximately 10-inch-wide pot for the roughly 2-plus liters of finished food, leaving stirring/blending room. Half uses a 3-quart pot and one batch; quarter uses a 2-quart pot and one small batch. Each smaller recipe prepares only one rack, and all its scaled rack oil goes to that rack. Method loading is capped at about 450 g rather than always halving scaled chicken, and repeat only if raw chicken remains. Larger amounts are separate complete batches to avoid a long queue of cooked meat.

## Sauce texture and science

Allow 15–20 minutes for soft golden onion rather than calling a short medium-heat sauté deep caramelization. Tomato paste darkens before garlic/ginger are briefly cooked; the full fine-spice quantity then gets only 15–20 seconds on reduced heat before passata enters. All the flavoring remains, with less opportunity to scorch.

The tomato base simmers 15–20 minutes until thick and aromatic. Pools of oil are not a necessary marker. Optional immersion blending smooths onion texture; it does not remove tomato acid. No claim is made that yogurt penetrates the center, guarantees tenderness after unlimited marination, or that cold butter creates a permanently stable emulsion. The practical limits are coating thickness, gentle finishing and the food's temperature.

## Portions, time and relationships

Six servings retain the original count: 150 g raw net chicken per diner, likely roughly 110–120 g cooked depending on moisture loss, plus a generous sauce. An estimated 7 1/2–9 cups total supports roughly 1 1/4–1 1/2 cups curry per person. This is a broad working yield, not a measured pot volume or nutrition basis. Sauce is intentionally substantial; it has not been cut merely to make the yield smaller.

The timing plan allows 15 minutes marinade preparation, then four refrigerated hours. Start sauce during the final 75 minutes: approximately 20 minutes chopping/measuring/pan setup, 15–20 onion, 3–4 paste/aromatics, several minutes heating, 15–20 tomato simmer and a few minutes optional blending. Broiler preheating overlaps only the late sauce simmer; chicken does not enter until sauce is ready and marinade time elapsed. Two 12–18-minute broils plus loading/checking and roughly 10–15 minutes reheating cream sauce, combining and resting produce about 5 hours 15 minutes to 5 hours 30 minutes overall. Extra oven finishing can extend it. Already-marinated chicken still needs about 2 hours to 2 hours 15 minutes with this one-cook sequence.

Hands-on work is about 105–120 minutes: roughly 35 preparation, 20–25 onion/paste/spice attention, several minutes sauce stirring/blending, about 30–40 watched broiling/handling, and final sauce/seasoning work. Broiling is not counted as free time for another recipe. This is a project dinner, not a 40-minute weekday curry.
