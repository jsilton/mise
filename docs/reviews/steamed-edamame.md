# Edamame with Sea Salt: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and proportions

Read the complete original and compared Sesame Roasted Edamame. The original title said steamed but only supplied a boil; retained the stable steamed-edamame URL and renamed the public dish Edamame with Sea Salt, with boil as its actual method. Kept 450 g frozen pods and four servings but explicitly named small side portions and the discarded pod weight. Specified unsalted product, practical water/pot capacity, and product-specific cooking requirements. Added water-heating time to the former seven-minute total; now approximately 15 minutes.

Kept the one-teaspoon flaky salt supply but staged it instead of instructing generous automatic salting. Corrected the false claim that fine salt cannot adhere/season. Optional sesame oil and pepper flakes remain measured and used. Added an actual bean-center cue, draining before seasoning, pod disposal and shell-before-bowl guidance. Deleted unverified nutrition, whose yield basis did not distinguish edible beans from pods.

## Relationships and source limits

Boiled pods and roasted shelled beans can be distinct snacks; no consolidation is justified solely by the word edamame. The roasted record was read but remains pending: its shelled/in-pod interchangeability, raw/frozen handling, roast timing and lasting-crispness claims need independent correction. This reviewed side supports several meals; Drunken Noodles Night now has reviewed components, but each other meal still requires its own complete composition review.

Namiko's primary article supports distinguishing frozen product types, tasting the bean and discarding pods. Her 4% salted-water method is different and was not copied into this finishing-salt recipe. Her suggested reductions to some package timings and nutrition/health claims were not adopted. FoodSafety.gov supports prompt chilling and reheating.

Physical tests still needed: pod-to-bean yield, heating time by brand and pot, tenderness at the package endpoint, salt-crystal amounts and four small side portions. No physical kitchen test recorded.
