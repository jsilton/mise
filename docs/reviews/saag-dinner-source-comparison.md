## Primary comparisons and exact scope

- [Swasthi Shreekanth, Saag Paneer](https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/saag-paneer/): read complete ingredient/method and relevant explanatory sections. Light browning on two faces, greens cooked with moisture, cream and a separate chile tempering are relevant. Her mixed greens, tomato/carrot/yogurt and puréed base differ. This is a method comparison, not the inherited recipe or proof of this pan/time/yield.
- [Swasthi Shreekanth, Palak Paneer](https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/palak-paneer-recipe-easy-paneer-recipes-step-by-step-pics/): read full recipe and relevant advice. Distinguishes spinach from broader greens and offers a brief hot-water soak for packaged paneer. Her mostly unfried-paneer soak is not a controlled study proving our post-fry salt bath restores every cheese. She explicitly has not personally established a frozen-spinach conversion. No bitterness, nutrition or acid-extraction mechanism is imported.
- [Dassana Amit, Palak Paneer](https://www.vegrecipesofindia.com/palak-paneer/): read complete recipe card and preparation/variation sections. Blanching, rapid cooling, purée, cream and optional lemon support the culinary choices. Her tomato-containing home recipe and separately described restaurant recipe differ, including dairy and seasoning quantities. A brief blanch is useful preparation here, not a claim of permanent color protection or removal of every contaminant.
- [Birds Eye, frozen vegetable FAQ](https://www.birdseye.com/faq): directly read. Manufacturer says to follow the product's cooking directions and cook thoroughly; its own guidance specifies 165°F. The proposal instructs full package cooking before draining, not merely thawing. This establishes that a frozen package may require cooking, not a universal package time or a recommended ingredient brand.
- [National Center for Home Food Preservation, blanching vegetables](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/freeze-generalinformation/blanching-vegetables/) and [freezing greens](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/vegetable/freezing-greens-including-spinach/): full relevant guidance read. Small loads, prompt cooling and avoiding overblanching are useful handling context. Its two-minute spinach blanch is specifically for home freezing; the curry's approximately one-minute wilt step is not claimed to meet a freezing-preservation process.
- [FoodSafety.gov, Leftovers](https://www.foodsafety.gov/blog/leftovers-gift-keeps-giving): directly read. Supports shallow prompt chilling, two-hour/one-hour hot-weather limits, four-day maximum refrigerator allowance and 165°F reheating. The separate 140°F hot-hold instruction is directly supported by [FoodSafety.gov's Four Steps](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety), whose full hot-holding, package-cooking and chilling sections were read.
- [Dassana Amit, Cucumber Raita](https://www.vegrecipesofindia.com/cucumber-raita-recipe/): full current method and storage read. Grating/draining, cumin, yogurt and cilantro/mint choices support the existing raita; no health claims adopted. Her current main card uses chopped cucumber, so it does not validate our exact squeezed yield.
- [Tilda, basmati methods](https://www.tilda.com/blog/quick-guides/how-to-cook-the-perfect-basmati-rice/): read as context alongside the actual rice and existing review. Method-specific ratios differ; the meal retains the reviewed unsoaked recipe's 400 g/720 ml ratio, halves it for four and uses 250 g/450 ml for five, without importing another source's water amount.

Aarti Sequeira's Food Network saag recipe was also read as a frozen-spinach comparison; it uses a different formula. No attribution or equivalent-yield claim follows from that comparison. Manjula Jain's palak page was searched as a possible provenance lead but its flour/tomato formula is not a match; its inconsistent supplemental prose is not used to establish the proposed soak.
