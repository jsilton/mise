## Main formula, geometry and clock

The full dish retains 397 g paneer, 680 g trimmed fresh spinach, 150 g onion, the main 3 tbsp ghee, 80 ml cream, all cumin/ginger/garlic/coriander/turmeric, full tablespoon dried fenugreek, teaspoon garam masala, lemon and optional extra chile ghee. This gives about 99 g paneer and 170 g raw trimmed greens per diner before aromatics and cooking losses. Four mains with a starch are reasonable. No unmeasured exact final volume or cooked-green weight is asserted.

Plain paneer is distinct from tofu; its milk remains an allergen. Same-amount butter is an explicitly lower-heat substitution, and no dairy-free claim follows from changing only one ingredient. Optional green chile and separately offered chile ghee are usable mild-diner choices without reducing the aromatic spice base. Fine salt is a disclosed editorial starting amount with a reserve, not a same-volume conversion from unidentified kosher salt. The half-teaspoon salt in 720 ml soak water is separately allocated and discarded; its unknown uptake is not falsely counted as fully retained seasoning. Blanching water is unsalted in the new method, so there is no missing blanching salt.

The 6 quart pot with 3 litres boiling water has room for four roughly 170 g spinach loads; heating/recovery and cooling/chopping are actual work. The cold bath is prepared first. Removing freely dripping liquid while leaving greens moist gives an actionable endpoint and avoids the old unqualified bone-dry rule. Ice cooling does not promise permanent green color. The 450 g frozen option is an editorial alternative near the old 16 oz package, with the current cheese/cream formula retained. Following full package cooking before draining is sound; thawing alone is not assumed sufficient.

A deep 12 inch skillet of at least 3 quarts fits the final cooked dish. Two roughly 200 g paneer rounds have ample room; patting dry, modest heat and browning two faces make the one-tablespoon total frying allocation plausible on nonstick. The remaining two tablespoons cook the masala. The 10–20 minute final-round soak overlaps onion/greens; the first round may soak several minutes longer. Soaking is described as a technique, not a guarantee that it reverses severe overfrying. Gentle final heating and an actually hot cheese center are clearer than claiming this already-made cheese is a partially cooked protein.

The 60–70 minute main estimate includes water heat overlapped with prep, four spinach loads/cooling/chopping, two cheese rounds, onion and finish. All upper stage values can exceed 70; the draft acknowledges slower washing and extra rounds. The 45–55 active estimate is plausible for organized work, not measured proof. Halving retains cube dimensions and texture endpoints; bigger batches require more rounds and final capacity. A smaller batch does not simply divide the heating time.

## Complete dinner

Four-person dinner is one full saag, half plain basmati and half raita. Each diner receives roughly 50 g dry rice equivalent and 60 g yogurt/37.5 g cucumber alongside the main. The rice's 200 g/360 ml ratio and its half fat/salt/reserve are conserved. The estimated three cups rice is plausible, and all finished components are divided evenly. The spinach already supplies the vegetable; adding another compulsory vegetable or naan would create an unasked addition. The texture contrast is intentionally gentle: chopped greens, tender browned cheese, separate grains and cool cucumber. The meal does not promise crisp paneer after a soak.

The five-person list enlarges every component:500 g cheese, 850 g greens, 250 g rice/450 ml water and 300 g yogurt/190 g cucumber. It includes every remaining spice, fat, cream, lemon, process bath and salt quantity directly. Main ghee 3.75 tbsp splits 1.25 + 2.5; cream becomes 100 ml. The 75 ml sauce reserve and 900 ml soak are proportional. Rice fat/salt and tiny raita seasonings use disclosed practical rounding. Spoon combinations are explained. No unavailable 1.25 or 5/8 interface control is required.

The process baths reasonably stay at the original volumes with an additional spinach load; they are discarded process fluids, and the plan calls for reboiling and refreshing a warming bath. Two 250 g paneer rounds fit the specified pan in principle, with a third-round fallback if its flat area is smaller. The 2 quart rice pot fits the five-person amount. Extra 10–15 minutes cover the increased handling; that remains an estimate.

The two-burner 95 minute dinner sequence is executable on paper. Spinach is prepared before frying, rice starts after the blanching pot leaves the stove, raita cumin uses the other burner before paneer, and the optional chile ghee follows the off-heat main. Rice's 15–18 minute simmer and full 10 minute rest are counted; its endpoint check interrupts paneer frying only with the skillet briefly off heat. Rice ordinarily waits covered about 20–25 minutes after its rest, not hours. The 67–82 masala window is its faster edge, but the subsequent broad finish window and texture-based extension prevent a forced deadline. No extra oven, simultaneous delicate tempering or instant naan is hidden in this plan.

The 70–80 active estimate is plausible for an efficient cook. Sandy greens and all upper stage lengths can exceed the headline. Prepared service specifically assumes fully cooked refrigerated saag, including its existing ghee/cream; it counts fresh rice/raita and thorough reheating rather than a second full fat dose. The main's separate masala-only option is not confused with the prepared-meal route.
