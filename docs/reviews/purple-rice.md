## Exact formulation and ingredient accounting

Retain **1 1/2 standard cups medium white and 1/2 cup whole-grain aromatic purple**. Standard cups mean 240 ml, not the commonly supplied 180 ml cooker cup. No invented gram conversion is attached to these historically volumetric grains. The purple ingredient is scoped to a whole grain with roughly a 45-minute stovetop package time; glutinous black rice and quick-cooking blends are not interchangeable.

Starting liquid is restored to the latest intended **720 ml total: 270 ml plain water for the purple head start, then 270 ml water plus 180 ml unsalted chicken stock with white rice**. This preserves all 540 ml original water and all 180 ml stock. It differs from the held 690 ml two-pot synthesis, which does not become a baseline accidentally. All purple cooking liquid stays in the pot. All white soaking liquid is added. No visual top-up is added to a full second water allowance, and no strained liquid is replaced blindly.

A **separate 120 ml hot-water correction reserve** is extra, not part of the starting 720 ml. Each drying/firm-grain correction uses two tablespoons/30 ml, shared across both stages and never more than the total reserve. This is intentionally simpler than transferring liquid back and forth from a bowl of soaking white rice. If the first stage needs correction, actual total liquid rises by the amount used; the recipe says so explicitly.

Keep a mild fine-salt range: quarter teaspoon initially, up to another quarter after tasting. This is an editorial seasoning choice against unsalted stock, not a density conversion from the original unspecified kosher half-teaspoon. Initial salt goes into the white soak and then the shared pot. Keep the optional teaspoon coconut oil/butter, folded through at the finish. Vegetable stock or water replaces exactly 180 ml stock; using coconut oil or omitting fat gives the vegan version. Gluten-free-option depends on stock; the public substitution now explicitly tells the cook to use gluten-free stock or water. No unsupported nutrient/antioxidant claim or calculated nutrition is introduced.

## Geometry, transition, timing and endpoints

One tightly lidded **2-quart pot with a 6–7-inch base** is large enough for the finished batch while reducing exposure of the small first stage. One burner plus a normal rinsing/soaking bowl replaces two cooking pots. The initial purple water still forms a shallow layer, so very gentle heat and the twenty-minute check are necessary. **Fixed scaling is now required for this staged formulation.** A quarter batch would start with only 67.5 ml in the same base, changing evaporation and increasing the risk of drying; double/triple batches would yield roughly 10–18 cups in an eight-cup pot. The specified water depth and final capacity therefore apply only to this full batch. Make another batch for more servings rather than inventing unverified pot and liquid rules. Root and the second independent reviewer flagged this limitation, which the earlier draft had missed.

After rinsing/setup, purple simmers alone for about **25–30 minutes**. At twenty minutes, check water and taste: the grain should be swelling with a softened outside and firmer center, not uniformly hard. If still hard throughout near the proposed transition, continue another five minutes before adding white, using measured correction water if dry. This is a partial-doneness cue and an editorial head-start estimate, not a validated equation between package times. Do not wait until purple is completely cooked and then impose another full white cycle.

The white grain receives a **twenty-minute soak in its measured 450 ml stock/water mixture** while purple cooks. It is neither drained nor given an additional water dose. Add everything to the purple pot, mix once, allow about five minutes to regain a gentle boil, then count **15–20 minutes covered simmer**. Only after both grain centers are tender and no free water remains does the ten-minute off-heat rest begin. The white grains now hydrate in colored liquid; the final tint depends on the actual purple grain, not a guaranteed deep-violet shade.

If the combined pot is dry and firm, use small measured corrections and taste both grains. Continuing to cook already-soft white rice changes its texture; the public troubleshooting admits that limit and recommends a longer head start next time, rather than promising an unlimited repair. Wet but tender rice gets brief low uncovered heat rather than vigorous stirring.

**80 minutes elapsed, about 15–20 active** is the planning total. Representative upper normal path: rinse/setup and initial heat 10–15, purple 30, return to simmer five, combined simmer twenty, rest ten: 75–80. Additional head-start checks or hydration can extend it. White soaking overlaps the first stage. PrepTime 10/cookTime 60/totalTime 80 makes room for the ten-minute rest; the old 40-minute convenience claim is withdrawn rather than hidden.
