## Preserve a meaningful separate recipe

Keep this plate/bowl distinct. Jordan's lemon-plus-vinegar miso marinade, split scallion/garlic aromatics, and oyster-seasoned greens have a different flavor and cooking architecture from grapefruit miso with buttered rice and cold toppings. They also differ from a sriracha/soy skillet reduction on cubes with mayonnaise, avocado and edamame. Restore the historically defining citrus/aromatic combination and oyster sauce in an amount that actually seasons its separate greens, while retaining the later useful sear/brief-steam treatment. Remove the unsubstantiated “crunchy” title guarantee; stems should retain a little bite, leaves should soften. Atomic Japanese/Chinese/American tags describe the family combination, without claiming a traditional national dish.

## Ratio decisions and complete ingredient destinations

- **900 g net skinless, boneless fish / four 225 g portions** is a practical metric expression of the original two pounds. These are generous portions. Approximately one-inch thickness is specified independently; the same weight in a thick narrow block cooks differently. This is a deliberate increase from current 680 g and is not concealed as a unit conversion.
- Restore the archived **3 tbsp miso + 3 tbsp honey + 2 tbsp soy + 1 tbsp mirin + 2 tbsp lemon + 2 tbsp unseasoned vinegar**. Before aromatics this is about 195 ml by spoon volume, with actual mixed volume differing. That quantity is reasonable as a shallow-dish marinade for 900 g fish, **not** as a thick 50 ml coat on each fillet. Reserve just 2 tbsp clean before raw contact; the remainder briefly marinates fish, then is drained and thick clumps scraped off. Discard the raw-contact excess. The clean reserve gives only 1 1/2 tsp finishing marinade per fillet. Eating all 195 ml as a reduced sauce would be a very different salt/acid load; this draft does not do that. Exact retained marinade weight and salt uptake are unknown without testing.
- Restore the three garlic cloves and six scallions, with **half for marinade and half for greens**. Remove the later teaspoon ginger from this version to recover that distinctive archived aromatic identity, not because ginger is inherently wrong. Introduce a measured quarter teaspoon pepper, split half fish/half vegetables. Unmeasured supplemental salt is not made mandatory because soy/miso season fish and oyster sauce seasons greens. This is ingredient accounting and final-taste judgment, not generic sodium reduction. Miso/oyster/soy brands vary substantially; no precise absorbed sodium claim is possible.
- **600 g trimmed baby bok choy** is a chosen weighed amount for four 150 g vegetable shares; it is not a universal six-head conversion. Tiny and large heads differ. It increases current one-pound greens by about one third to suit the restored larger fish plate. Use two 300 g batches in a 12-inch lidded skillet. Four teaspoons neutral oil splits two per batch; one teaspoon toasted sesame oil splits half per batch; the separate teaspoon foil oil is additional. Two tablespoons water and two tablespoons oyster sauce split one of each per batch. Restored oyster sauce is six times the later teaspoon, but remains only one tablespoon per 300 g greens, a plausible full-flavored vegetable seasoning. Taste validation with named sauce remains necessary.
- **270 g dry jasmine / 540 ml water** deliberately supplies about four cups cooked, roughly one cup per diner. This interprets neither “1 1/2 cups rice, cooked” as proven dry weight nor as proof that the original intended only 3/8 cup cooked per diner. It is a new explicit dinner allocation, preserving the archived/current jasmine serving intent. Mahatma labels 45 g per quarter cup and gives 1.5 volumes water to one rice, so this chosen quantity corresponds to 1.5 kitchen cups dry plus 2.25 cups water; rinsed rice must drain well. A package method supersedes this starting ratio. No orzo, butter, broth or seasoned shari is smuggled into the rice.

All ingredients have explicit destinations, including the separate oil/water measures and every aromatic split. Legacy nutrition is removed because both the original and revised formulas fail to support the old number.

## Practical sequence, endpoints and schedule

A middle-rack 400°F roast does most of the cooking; only the brief final coloring uses high broil with fish 7–9 inches from the element. Drain the marinade, use foil and metal, and give the last one-to-three minutes undivided attention. Each fillet reaches **145°F/63°C**, replacing 135–140°F. If browned too early, finish gently in the oven, shielded as necessary; if already at temperature, avoid overcooking merely to obtain darker glaze. The JOC source informs thickness, miso burning and rack-distance controls but does not test this exact family marinade or this final endpoint.

The main schedule is **55 minutes**, approximately 30–35 minutes hands-on: start rice/oven, then mix marinade while rice cooks; give fish fifteen refrigerated minutes while trimming/washing/drying greens; roast fish while cooking two greens batches; pause the skillet and finish greens before the watched broil; fluff and divide the rice. Fifteen minutes is an editorial choice inside the archive's ten-to-thirty-minute window, not a measured optimum. This is not an overnight-marination recipe. Rice on one burner and bok choy on the other fit a home stove; no third saucepan is needed for sauce. Thick portions or slow oven/batch recovery extend the estimate.
