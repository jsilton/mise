# Pulled Pork: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test, measured
yield, telemetry, or reproducibility claim.

The review used the current source, its local history, the Group A and braises
pilot records, the recipe standard, and FoodSafety.gov sources. The source gives
United States/Southern context but no author or family attribution. The final
proposal preserves the defining identity: a 4–5 pound bone-in Boston butt with a
smoky-sweet rub, onion, garlic, apple cider vinegar and Worcestershire in the
slow cooker, with no barbecue sauce.

The full formula is preserved: 3 tablespoons brown sugar; 1 tablespoon each
hickory smoked salt and smoked paprika; 1 teaspoon each garlic powder, onion
powder and cumin; 1/2 teaspoon pepper; the stated shoulder, onion, garlic, 1/4
cup vinegar and 1 tablespoon Worcestershire. The rub ingredients all enter step
1; the pot base enters step 2; the shoulder enters step 3; and the cooking juice
is decanted, skimmed and deliberately returned in step 6. The existing decanting
correction is retained. No nutrition fields are carried forward because legacy
values are not credible for the listed pork shoulder and were not recalculated.

The unsupported claims that acid itself breaks down collagen, the bone conducts
heat to the center, and no shoulder can finish under nine hours were removed. The
recipe now uses the stated 10–11 hours only as an estimate and requires both the
whole-pork 145°F / 63°C minimum-temperature check and a fork-pullable texture
check, with a three-minute rest on the board after cooking and before pulling.
A fully thawed shoulder is required for the stated method. A 6-quart cooker is a
fit constraint, not a scaling promise: one whole shoulder must sit below a fully
closed lid; larger or doubled shoulders need separate equipment and a new timing
check. Hickory smoked salt remains exact, without a blind volume conversion.

The same-day planning budget is 11 hours 30 minutes (15 minutes setup, 10–11
hours on LOW, then up to 15 minutes for the required three-minute rest, pulling
and juicing). Optional overnight seasoning remains separate
refrigerator time. Storage replaces the unsafe “all week” wording with prompt
shallow-container cooling, refrigeration at 40°F / 4°C or colder, use within 3–4
days, freezing later portions, and 165°F / 74°C reheating. The proposal directs
reheating on the stovetop, oven, or microwave rather than using the slow cooker
for refrigerator-cold leftovers.

Sources: [FoodSafety.gov cooling](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety),
[cold storage](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts),
[USDA slow-cooker guidance](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo30229/pdf/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo30229.pdf), and
[safe temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures)
support the safety guidance, including not reheating cold leftovers in a slow
cooker. They do not validate the rub, cooker timing, smoke salt composition,
shoulder size, or serving yield.

Relationship comparison: Buttermilk Cornbread (nine side portions) and Classic
Creamy Coleslaw (eight side portions) are reciprocal side relationships and fit
the pork’s eight-serving metadata. Korean Style Tacos explicitly calls for one
pound of cooked pulled pork, so it is an alternate use for part of a batch rather
than a claim that the whole batch makes four taco servings. Slow-Cooker Mac and
Cheese was removed because it also occupies a 6-quart slow cooker; it needs a
second cooker or a separate cooking window. Carnitas Bowl was removed because it
is a separate raw-pork braise-and-broil main rather than an accompaniment or
prepared-pork use. Directly inspected all three meal references: BBQ Pulled Pork Spread, Pulled Pork Spread and Southern BBQ Spread. BBQ Pulled Pork Spread assumes cooked pork for six; root corrected its “eat all week” statement and missing reheating endpoint in this batch; the other two plan eight diners but need finishing time and coordinated sides reviewed. All three remain pending; none is counted as reviewed here. These related menus require a separate component/duplicate decision.

Kitchen tests still needed: 4- and 5-pound bone-in shoulder fit in a 6-quart
cooker; actual time and texture on LOW; rub salt/smoke balance; retained liquid
and the amount returned; eight-serving yield; refrigerated and frozen reheat
quality; and half/double-batch capacity.

Integration check: fixed scaling protects the single 4–5 pound shoulder/6-quart cooker geometry, and manufacturer allowance for that whole-cut size is now explicit. Removed the placeholder “No direct substitution established” entry while retaining the useful smoked-salt variability note. Directly read the archived USDA FSIS PDF, which supports thawing meat before slow cooking and reheating cold leftovers by another method; the earlier game-day citation does not establish the slow-cooker-specific reheating rule. No exact cooked yield is invented.
