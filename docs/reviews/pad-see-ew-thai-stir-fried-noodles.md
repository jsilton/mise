## Findings and formula decisions

Reviewed the original five-serving recipe and its linked cucumber meal. Original 680 g fresh noodles versus 397–454 g dried were not equivalent options, and 795 g chicken plus all noodles/vegetables/eggs returned to one pan despite preliminary batching. Now defaults to 1 kg fresh noodles for five portions, with 375 g dried as a practical alternative explicitly not a precise conversion. Chicken is 750 g (150 g raw per serving), gai lan is measured at 500 g, and three eggs remain. This is a substantial five-portion main, not a promise of a specific adult/child appetite split.

Reconciled all oil: 1 tsp marinade plus 4 tbsp cooking oil, allocated 1 tbsp chicken, 2 tsp vegetables, 1 tsp eggs, and 2 tbsp across five finishes. Added measured broccoli-steaming and sauce water. Sauce is 4 tbsp oyster, 2 tbsp regular soy, 2 tbsp Thai sweet dark soy, 1 tsp sugar, 2 tbsp water and white pepper. Compared with the original, more oyster sauce and less added sugar accompany a larger noodle/vegetable batch. Condiment brands need first-portion tasting; no single ratio has been physically validated. Moved 1 tbsp vinegar to an optional measured table supply instead of calling it crucial in-pan seasoning.

Chicken now stays refrigerated during preparation and reaches 165°F before transfer. Broccoli and eggs cook separately before five final batches; the record explicitly describes this home-stove adaptation and its egg texture difference. Dried hydration follows the package and a center-texture check rather than universal hot-tap soaking. Removed vigorous tossing, smoking-pan instructions and the simplistic wok-hei guarantee. Replaced 40-minute total and excellent leftovers with a 65-minute schedule and next-day quality guidance.
