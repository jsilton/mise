# Sushi Rice (Shari): individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Complete original, reviewed gohan and actual incoming poke dependencies compared. Editorial review only; no kitchen test.

## Formula and method

This is a distinct base from plain gohan: vinegar, sugar, salt and kombu define shari, and its seasoning/handling deserves its own method. Keep the existing master-sushi-rice slug and shorten the display title to Sushi Rice (Shari). No merge with plain rice and no claim of mastery or testing.

The original two cups dry rice lacked a cup system, then used scant two cups water and claimed subtracting two tablespoons accounts for the finishing vinegar. It also called 20 minutes a universal rice-cooker cycle despite a separate soak and cooling requirement. Normalize the same approximate two US cups dry rice to 400 g, with 480 ml cooking water as a 1.2 ml/g covered-pot starting formula, not an exact universal conversion. The primary Japanese source uses 300 g / 360 ml for its sushi-rice cooker recipe; that supports a firmer sushi-rice hydration approach but is not a physical test of this pot. Rice-cooker matched cup/fill-line settings remain an explicit alternative. Do not mix a cooker cup with a US-cup water count or promise cooking-water removal can compensate arbitrarily for every later seasoning volume.

Preserve 5 tbsp unseasoned vinegar, 1 1/2 tbsp sugar and the original full allowance of 1 1/2 tsp fine salt. Salt is staged: start with 1 tsp, then use the remaining half only after tasting. This is an explicit seasoning safeguard because the full roughly 9 g salt plus soy-dressed tuna can be assertive. It is not a silent kosher/fine conversion. Reserve one tablespoon of the measured vinegar to dissolve optional finishing salt, then add that vinegar whether or not salt is added; all five tablespoons are conserved. Initial independent review correctly rejected attempting to dissolve salt in a spoonful of rice.

The original undefined kombu strip becomes about 5 cm square, all in the cooking water; the separate optional extra strip in seasoning was unlisted and removed. Wipe visible grit rather than promising every white surface deposit is definitely mannitol. Remove the claim that any retained kombu necessarily becomes slimy. No required extra ingredients appear in the method.

The covered-pot sequence gives a 30-minute soak, heating, 12–15-minute gentle simmer, ten-minute off-heat rest, a tender-center check and five-to-ten-minute seasoning/steam release. The 80-minute total includes rinse/soak/cook/rest/finish rather than treating fan cooling as an exact two-minute process. It is an estimate with correction time extra. A rice cooker may take longer and may include soaking; adding a second mandatory soak is not automatic. The base retains no false overnight-prep requirement.

## Texture, yield and food safety

Gentle lifting/folding keeps grains intact, and seasoning while hot is a supported practice. Remove the original mislabeling of cut-and-fan as te-zu, the exact body-temperature target and “never refrigerate.” A barely warm portion can be used promptly for poke, but extended warm-counter holding is not a preservation method. The recipe does not establish measured equilibrium pH or an approved acidification process. Flavor vinegar alone cannot certify storage safety.

Finished yield is approximately 5 1/2–6 cups, about 900–1000 g depending on water retained and evaporation; it is not “four servings” with no use context. The 400 g rice + 480 ml water is 880 g before rinsing absorption, evaporation and about 100 g seasoning, so that mass range is a plausible starting estimate rather than an observed result. Main recipes must measure cooked amounts and reserve extras; four-cup poke bowls do not require consuming the full batch. Existing rolls may have different needs and are not newly reviewed by improving their linked base.

All ingredients are plant-based and gluten-free as specified; no dubious nutrition estimate (original ten calories for a rice base) remains. White-rice storage guidance comes from official temperature/leftover sources; chilling may change texture without making “never refrigerate” acceptable. Reheating to 165°F is specified for leftovers; do not return cold rice to a lukewarm rice-cooker mode as a reheating instruction.

Test rice-cooker methods separately with model/cycle recorded. Confirm roll performance, next-day texture and actual reheating workflow before making reproducibility claims.
