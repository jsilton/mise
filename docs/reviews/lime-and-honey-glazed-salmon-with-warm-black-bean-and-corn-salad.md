## Method, equipment, attention and safety

Use a full-size oven, broiler-safe metal half-sheet pan, foil and a thermometer. Seven roughly five-ounce portions fit as two staggered rows only if their real dimensions leave about an inch between pieces; do not assume a small toaster tray can hold them. A deep 12-inch skillet with at least three quarts capacity accommodates roughly two quarts of finished salad with stirring room. A well-seasoned cast-iron surface is specifically identified for the retained dry-char method. This is an editorial equipment choice, not a guarantee that any dry stainless or coated pan behaves identically. Excessive smoke/blackening calls for lower heat and a lighter result, not extra unlisted oil or “char all the kernels.”

Prepare all ingredients first. Corn cooks in two batches before fish enters the oven. Baking then overlaps the less demanding onion/pepper and bean-heating steps. The skillet is turned off and loosely covered before the broiler is switched on. If fish reaches its intermediate 135–140°F first, remove it briefly while finishing vegetables. This is explicitly not the final endpoint: every portion must reach **145°F / 63°C**, checked sideways in the thickest center, after glazing or a gentler oven finish. Separate thin pieces and remove them when ready. Surface gloss or opacity alone does not establish the center temperature.

The honey/lime mixture is initially clean and applied near the end, rather than marinated with fish and later drizzled cold. Any residue in the brushing bowl is discarded; it is not a cold finishing sauce. All glaze put on fish is heated in the final cook. No method asks the cook to reuse raw fish utensils with fresh spinach or avocado. Do not recover blackened sticky deposits from foil as though they were desirable pan juices.

The proposed 7–9-inch food-to-element clearance, foil and optional gentle oven finish follow relevant sweet-coated-salmon controls in Namiko Hirasawa Chen's primary method. Her miso formula and preferred lower fish endpoint are **not** imported; FDA supplies this recipe's 145°F endpoint. Broiler settings and thickness determine actual duration, not a fixed two-minute promise.

The smaller-oven variation retains the same bake-then-glaze approach and refers to actual appliance-approved foil, clearance and pan instructions. Two batches add roughly 20–25 minutes and worsen simultaneous service; the first batch waits longer. It is not presented as equally fast or as universally compatible with a 3–4-inch element distance. Oil and glaze split between batches, and waiting raw fish stays chilled.

## Timing and one-cook practicality

**65 minutes elapsed, approximately 50–55 active**, replaces the nominal 35-minute recipe. Plausible ordinary path:

- 0–25: shuck/cut five ears, chop vegetables and herbs, rinse beans, prepare leaves, portion/check/season fish, measure lime/glaze and oil the pan. Oven heats during this work.
- 25–40/42: heat skillet, two 5–7-minute corn rounds with recovery and transfer.
- About 40–54/57: put fish in oven; cook aromatic vegetables 6–8 minutes, garlic/spices under one minute and complete bean/corn salad 4–6 minutes. Check fish at eight minutes and briefly remove it if it gets ahead.
- About 54–61/64: pause the skillet, heat broiler, spread glaze, watch 1–3-minute browning and verify each fish portion.
- About 60–65/70: divide salad, leaves, fish and toppings; cut avocado at serving.

Sixty-five is a planning estimate within this range, not a guarantee at every upper bound. Slow chopping, a weak burner, thicker fish or a broiler requiring longer heat-up can push toward seventy minutes or beyond. The active estimate includes roughly 25 preparation, 6–8 corn handling, 8–10 vegetable handling, 7–8 oven/glaze checks and 4–5 assembly; unattended oven cooking overlaps salad work. The method specifically removes high-attention broiling from concurrent skillet work.

**Fixed scaling** protects the seven-portion coordinated method: fourteen or twenty-one fillets cannot fit the single half-sheet, and doubled/tripled salad exceeds the stated skillet. Additional complete batches require their own pans and time; an ingredient multiplier alone does not describe that dinner. The independent reviewer flagged this omission before finalization.

For **five diners without scaling**, serve five of the seven planned portions and chill two complete cooked portions separately from greens and garnish. Each person still receives the intended 146 g raw-equivalent fish and one-seventh salad; do not silently spread five portions over seven people or increase their fish by shrinking the side. Mathematically, five-sevenths of the formula would be about 729 g fish, 429 g corn, 357 g drained beans, 171 g spinach and 2 6/7 tbsp honey. These numbers are arithmetic only, not a published alternate batch method. Cans/ears/avocados need actual division by net amount; the recipe intentionally does not expose a generic scaler for this coordinated setup. Smaller batches do not shorten oven preheat, thickness-based cooking or all prep proportionally. For seven or more, preserve the stated pan loading and add batches if needed; ingredient scaling cannot expand a pan.

## Storage, substitutions and remaining verification

FDA supports raw seafood separation, chilled storage, thawing and 145°F cooking. USDA supports shallow-container prompt chilling, 40°F maximum refrigeration, 3–4 days for cooked leftovers and 165°F reheating. The recipe applies those limits separately to cooked fish/salad and fresh toppings. No acid-preservation claim is made. Assembled leaves wilt, avocado browns and seeds soften; these are quality reasons to store parts separately, not a promise that reheated fish reproduces freshly broiled texture.

Frozen corn uses the same **net kernel weight**, thawed/drained/blotted before dry charring; neither the archived 10-oz frozen suggestion nor an unmeasured five-ear equivalence is silently carried over. Skin-on salmon retains edible flesh quantity but changes purchase weight and skin texture. Pure ancho changes spice composition; cayenne is not an equal substitute. Gluten-free suitability requires a compatible spice blend. Dairy-free follows the actual ingredients; honey prevents vegan classification, and this dish is not vegetarian.

It does not establish a fixed two-minute broiler finish or exact sensory yield.
