## Corrections raised during independent review

1. **Resolved — realistic honey-sriracha elapsed time.** The first 55-minute estimate was possible for an efficient cook but tight after cutting fish, the full 20-minute refrigerated marinade, two batches, pan recovery, sauce and assembly. The final default is 60 minutes. This is an editorial allowance, not a measured test. Prepared rice alone does not remove the marinade or batch-cooking time.
2. **Resolved — explicit overlap in Jordan's method.** A complete rice cook/rest followed literally by making the marinade would defeat 55 minutes. Step 2 now starts “While the rice cooks,” so vegetable preparation overlaps marination and vegetable batches overlap roasting. Broiling receives the cook's undivided attention.
3. **Resolved — optional cucumber salad is a replacement with extra work.** Its 25-minute process cannot be represented as fully hidden inside a 20-minute fish marinade while the same cook also prepares beans and other toppings. Honey now calls for starting salting earlier, omitting plain cucumber and allowing 5–10 additional minutes.
4. **Resolved — application-specific lessons.** Removed `gentle-proteins`, whose current lesson is specifically about gently cooking eggs, from all three fish recipes. Temperature, browning where relevant, starch and seasoning are more defensible mappings.
5. **Resolved — sticky rice correction and avocado destination.** The two tablespoons optional corrective hot water are now listed; step 6 explicitly slices avocado. This closes the earlier ingredient/method gaps without altering the normal rice hydration.
6. **Resolved — source scope for reused marinade.** Honey's raw-contact liquid must reach a full boil before contacting cooked fish. Its final FDA Outdoors citation supports both reserving clean marinade and boiling reused marinade in the lower Four Steps section. The broader FoodSafety.gov Four Steps link alone was not the best direct evidence for this exact claim. FDA gives a boil requirement, not a validated one-minute schedule for this formula.
7. **Resolved — final accounting corrections.** List honey's optional sauce-loosening water separately, e.g. “1 tbsp water, reserved only for loosening the finished sauce”; specify teaspoon additions up to this tablespoon. Align its timing text with one minute boiling plus 1–3 minutes simmering: “about 2–4 minutes after reaching a full boil,” with initial heating additional. Both corrections are present in the final draft. These are precision corrections, not a change to normal marinade concentration.

## Sticky Miso Salmon Bowls

**Identity and history pass.** The archive explicitly links NYT Cooking. The later rewrite removed oil and butter from ingredients while still using them, halved grapefruit zest, changed skinless to skin-on and made originally flexible toppings appear obligatory. Restoring the tablespoon glaze oil, tablespoon butter, two teaspoons zest and skinless fillets preserves a meaningful original composition. Root subsequently verified Andy Baraghani through the official NYT Cooking video. The final draft names him, and its record distinguishes root’s direct verification from this reviewer’s inaccessible recipe page and cache-missed video fetch.

**Formula and capacity pass on paper.** Four 170 g net fillets, specified independently as 3/4–1 inch thick, fit a metal half-sheet with space. Roughly 100–120 ml mixed glaze is substantial, about 25–30 ml per fillet; the instructions to spread evenly without piles or puddles matter. This full-glaze recipe should not borrow a scraped-marinade source's exact timing as though tested. Keeping the archived richness is justified; reducing it solely to make a nutrition number smaller would not be.

The 400 g dry short-grain rice and 535 ml cooking water follow the reviewed measured hydration approach; the 30-minute soak is a disclosed adaptation from the archived no-soak method. About 100 g dry rice per person is ample. All butter/scallions go into that rice; cucumber is 75 g each, avocado is quartered, and nori/optional fermented toppings have destinations. It is neither shari nor jasmine nor pilaf.

**Time and endpoint pass with disclosed uncertainty.** A plausible route is 5 minutes rinsing, 30 soaking, about 5 heating, 12–15 simmering, 10 resting and 5 assembling, with glaze/toppings during soaking and broiling during the final rice phases. That supports roughly 70 minutes. Broiler distance, checking from minute four, 145°F center measurement and a gentler oven fallback are important because surface darkness does not establish center doneness. The stated 10–16 minutes is plausible guidance, not validated for every broiler or full glaze load.

Remaining kitchen questions: actual rice yield/tenderness; named miso and grapefruit balance; whether the full coating scorches at the chosen distance before 145°F; actual active time and portion satisfaction. No arbitrary salt or zest adjustment is warranted without that evidence.

## Miso Salmon with Bok Choy

**Family attribution and material choices pass.** The original export explicitly credits Jordan Silton. The changes at `1dc24afe` were an intentional refinement rewrite, not migration corruption. The records accurately disclose that recovering the original lemon/vinegar, honey/miso/soy quantities and split garlic/scallion aromatics is an editorial restoration; neither version is proven family preference by this history alone.

This is materially different from the grapefruit bowl: a loose citrus marinade, oven roast followed by brief broil, oyster-seasoned cooked greens and plain jasmine rice. The original two pounds of fish become 900 g net, a deliberate increase over the later 680 g. Four 225 g raw portions with 150 g trimmed greens and approximately a cup cooked rice are generous but coherent. Six bok choy heads are not treated as a universal weight conversion.

**Marinade allocation is essential and passes.** The spooned formula is about 195 ml before mixing effects and aromatics. Only 30 ml is reserved clean for finishing, about 7.5 ml per fillet. Most raw-contact marinade is drained/discarded, with clumps removed. Baking or reducing all of it onto four fillets would be a materially saltier, wetter dish; the proposal correctly avoids that. Exact retained marinade and sodium absorption remain unknown.

The two 300 g bok choy batches suit a 12-inch skillet. Four teaspoons neutral cooking oil split two per batch; the teaspoon foil oil is separate; two tablespoons each water and oyster sauce split one per batch; the teaspoon sesame oil splits in half. The remaining garlic, scallions and pepper are explicitly divided. Restored oyster sauce is a material increase over the later teaspoon, but one tablespoon per 300 g greens is a defensible full-flavored starting formula. Brand-specific salt balance needs testing.

**Rice and schedule pass.** Mahatma's 45 g quarter-cup measure makes 270 g equal 1.5 dry cups; 540 ml is 2.25 cups water, the stated 1:1.5 volumetric starting ratio. Rinsing/draining and package precedence are explicit. The approximately four-cup finished yield is not a laboratory guarantee. Two burners and overlapping 15-minute marination, roasting and two vegetable batches make about 55 minutes plausible; slow ovens, pan evaporation or thick portions extend it. No overnight acidic marinade is prescribed. Short-grain rice takes a different 65-minute schedule and cannot silently replace jasmine inside this estimate.

Remaining kitchen questions: salt/acid after draining, retained marinade, purchased and trimmed fish/greens yields, scallion size, oyster-sauce brand, actual fillet thickness and timing of the two batches versus roast.

## Honey Sriracha Salmon Bowls

**Source and identity pass.** The primary Claire Cary page and archive agree on reduced-sodium soy, water, plain cucumber, edamame, avocado and half a cup mayonnaise. Restoring those elements is justified. Cubed skillet salmon with a separately reduced sweet-hot sauce has a different identity from both miso fillet recipes; no merge among these three mains is warranted.

**Portions and sauce pass.** Each bowl receives about 170 g raw net fish, 75 g cucumber, 37.5 g edible shelled edamame, one quarter avocado, two tablespoons mayonnaise and roughly half a cup cooked rice. The rice portion is modest but matches the original two cooked cups across four rich bowls. The beans are not the same weight in pods. The 120 g rice/240 ml water ratio is consistent with the same manufacturer's method, although the small batch's evaporation in a 2-quart pot and actual cooked yield deserve a kitchen check. A tightly fitting lid and package caveat are important.

The marinade contains about 150 ml spooned liquid plus garlic before absorption or loss. Restoring water reduces initial concentration but does not remove sodium. Restoring reduced-sodium soy does materially change the source of salt; varying brands and final reduction remain explicit uncertainties. Half a cup mayonnaise is retained rather than silently converted into a tiny drizzle.

Two 340 g batches of one-inch cubes are plausible for a 12-inch skillet. Medium-high heat, draining liquid/clumps, two teaspoons oil per batch, heat recovery and removing burnt residue address household-pan behavior. Five-to-eight minutes of stated face cooking is a starting range, not an instruction to cook all six faces for several minutes each. Fine-tip center checks at 145°F and earlier removal of smaller pieces prevent dependence on surface opacity. Cooked fish stays out while raw-contact marinade boils and reduces, then returns off heat. That protects both sauce safety and fish texture. A later sauce coating necessarily softens browned faces; the draft does not promise permanent crispness.

A plausible one-cook route is fish marinating by minute 10–15, finishing that rest at 30–35 while rice/beans/toppings are prepared, then two batches plus recovery, sauce and assembly near 60. Adding the dressed salad or using a smaller skillet extends it. Remaining kitchen questions are small-batch rice yield, pan/burner differences, oil adequacy, recovered marinade volume and reduction, brand-specific heat/salt and first-batch holding quality.
