## Concrete challenges resolved

1. **Partial chicken waiting:** Original/current tikka broiled incompletely cooked chicken before the long sauce build. Final proposal finishes the sauce base first, fully cooks each chicken batch to 165°F / 74°C and holds only fully cooked chicken briefly on a clean platter. No percentage-cooked or visually underdone endpoint remains. This also avoids simmering the first batch throughout the second broiler round.
2. **Scaled sauce sugar:** Final step 4 uses the measured sauce sugar, replacing the fixed tablespoon in method prose.
3. **Half/quarter batch geometry:** Final steps 5–6 load up to about 450 g per round and repeat only when raw chicken remains. They no longer force two rounds for a scaled half or quarter recipe that the equipment guidance says needs one.
4. **Rack oil and unnecessary cleanup:** Final step 2 prepares two racks for the original batch, one for half/quarter, and applies the listed oil only across racks in use. It no longer strands half the scaled oil on an unused rack. The full final tikka file was reread after this correction.

No additional formula correction was needed for chana after reading its complete draft and evidence record.

## Chana Masala

The draft retains two 15 oz cans, all 3 tbsp fat, whole cumin, 1 tbsp coriander, 2 tsp divided garam masala, 1 tsp turmeric, the original amchur, two serranos, tomato, cilantro and lemon. The rather pronounced spice profile is preserved instead of silently normalized to a different author's formula. The mild option changes chile heat explicitly while retaining aromatic spices.

Printed can weight includes liquid; approximately 250 g drained per can is an operational expectation, not guaranteed net weight. The alternative uses already fully cooked/drained beans at that same per-can weight, with prior soaking/boiling outside this clock. The 250 g peeled onion and tablespoon grated ginger are documented working measures for previously vague produce sizes. Four portions contain about 125 g drained chickpeas each, plus substantial masala; roughly five cups total is plausible but remains an estimate.

All 240 ml original cooking water enters the simmer. A separate 120 ml reserve covers sticking onion or overly thick curry and is shared across corrections, not added by default. Half/quarter pots and scaled reserves account for evaporation rather than cooking small quantities in the full wide pan. A 4-quart, 9–10-inch pot easily holds this amount with stirring room. Larger quantities need more capacity or separate pots.

The ground-spice stage still receives amchur as in the original. Its lemon alternative is now a separate listed allowance used at the end, not fried as liquid with spices. The original finishing lemon and replacement allowance can be combined for tasting but are not both forced into a sharp batch. Both garam masala portions, cilantro, fine salt and its reserve have destinations. Whole slit serranos are removed or identified. Added fine salt, approximately 4.5–6 g before considering canned ingredients, is a disclosed seasoning choice rather than a kosher-density conversion.

The method gives onion 12–15 minutes, tomatoes 8–10 minutes, a full 20-minute covered simmer and a short uncovered mash/finish. This supports about 70–80 minutes from uncut ingredients; the old 35-minute cook headline did not contain its own stages. Roughly 35–40 active minutes is plausible with only intermittent attention during long onion/curry stages, but slow chopping and reductions can exceed it. No claim is made that acidic sauce absolutely prevents all softening; tender beans are simply required before this short curry method. Spoonable coating, not mandatory separated oil or a dry paste, is a clear endpoint.

## Chicken Tikka Masala

The draft retains 900 g net trimmed chicken, the complete yogurt/aromatic/chile marinade, all 2 tbsp onion butter plus 1 tbsp oil, 720 ml passata, 240 ml heavy cream, 1 tbsp finishing butter, full coriander/cumin/turmeric/paprika and fenugreek/garam masala finish. The one-teaspoon rack oil is newly explicit surface preparation; it is not disguised restoration of the removed tablespoon marinade oil. Excess raw marinade is discarded, so neither yield nor salt accounting pretends it is all eaten.

The separate marinade and sauce garlic/ginger/lemon/salt doses are clear. Sauce salt splits between onion and tomato; the finishing reserve is optional after tasting chicken with sauce. The original tablespoon sugar remains, with measured optional extra sugar/lemon for formerly unlisted adjustment instructions. The 120 ml water is an explicitly conditional allowance related to the historical sauce water, not an automatic return to that old dilution. All current richness is preserved. Fenugreek leaves are not swapped for stronger ground seeds, and cayenne or salted chili seasoning is not treated as equal to Kashmiri chile.

Two 450 g loads have generous physical room on individual 13-by-18-inch metal racks. Broiler heat coverage, not just tray dimensions, still governs placement and rotation. Food-to-element distance and appliance-specific door instructions are stated; the racks must be broiler-safe and no parchment is used. The same-size pieces remain important at smaller quantities. Half/quarter use one round; larger totals are separate complete batches rather than an indefinitely long queue of cooked meat.

Every batch is checked at several thick centers for at least 165°F before removal. First-batch cooked chicken rests on a clean platter while remaining raw chicken stays refrigerated. Clean tools/platter remain separate from raw loading tools. Only clean-platter juices return. Discarding scorched tray drips loses some possible roasted flavor but avoids returning burnt yogurt/spices indiscriminately; the author documents that tradeoff. Surface char is not required once the center is done. The immediate 400°F oven fallback finishes pieces that darken too soon rather than parking partially cooked poultry.

The final sauce is reheated before cream and chicken, and the brief 2–4-minute finish is governed by hot food/165°F centers, not forced by time. Breast retains the same endpoint with earlier checks and a shorter useful margin. Slight extra reheating of the first batch remains a texture question, but the sequence is coherent and safer than prolonged incomplete-cooking waits.

A 5–6-quart pot gives ample room for a roughly two-liter-plus curry and submerged immersion blending. Smooth blending changes texture; it does not remove acid. The projected 7 1/2–9 cups is plausible given the substantial tomato/onion/cream sauce and cooked chicken, supporting six 1 1/4–1 1/2-cup portions. Each gets 150 g raw net chicken equivalent. Do not present these as measured finished volume or verified nutrition.

The 5-hour-30-minute plan correctly counts marinade preparation, four refrigerated hours, sauce preparation during the final 75 minutes, two watched broiler rounds, reheating, finishing and five-minute rest. A representative 15-minute marinade preparation means its four hours end around minute 255; sauce starts around minute 180 and is ready before broiling. Roughly 35–45 minutes of batch handling plus reheating/finish/rest place service near the stated upper window. All slow endpoints or an oven fallback can extend it. Already-marinated chicken still needs the sauce and batches; it is not a quick 40-minute dish. The 105–120 active estimate appropriately includes attended broiling rather than using that time for imaginary parallel side dishes.

## Pairings and handling

Rice and bread are alternatives or intentionally smaller combined starch portions. Current naan takes up to three hours and makes twelve; one naan each with less rice is sensible optional advice, not two full starch servings. Raita is a separately made cool accompaniment; its full batch is not silently required. No new dinner or calendar is certified.

Both recipes use prompt shallow chilling, the two-hour/one-hour hot-weather limits, 40°F storage, 3–4 days refrigerated and 165°F reheating. Raw tikka marinating is at most 24 hours without resetting the chicken's previous storage life. No raw marinade is reused. Sauce prepared earlier and any finished curry retain their original storage age; partial broiling is excluded as make-ahead work.
