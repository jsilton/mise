# Grilled Zucchini with Mint and Lemon: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read the complete original and Grilled Chicken Summer Plate. Other zucchini titles concern fritters, soup, baking or noodles, not a second grill/mint side. Kept the technique distinct. Summer Plate remains pending because the full meal and smashed potatoes still need review; a reviewed vegetable alone does not complete it.

Kept 680 g zucchini for four sides, now consistently 3/8-inch planks. Two tablespoons total oil are allocated as 1 1/2 for cooking and 1/2 for finishing, replacing the extra finishing tablespoon and unlisted grate oil. Salt is density-defined and restrained; no automatic second flaky-salt layer. Measured pepper, staged lemon, mint and zest account for all finishes. Smaller firm squash and a fork test govern texture. Removed the claim that maximum heat is universally best, that any heat destroys herb brightness, or that standing always improves the result.

The thirty-five-minute plan allows heating and up to three 6–8-minute stovetop rounds, instead of assuming all four zucchini fit at once in eleven minutes. A large outdoor grill may be faster. Broad skillet browning is a valid alternative to stripes. Texture loss after overcooking or reheating is acknowledged. Removed the automatic relationship to a chicken roast that already includes a full vegetable batch; pairing with the feta spread has a clearer purpose. Legacy nutrition removed.

Direct reference: https://www.loveandlemons.com/grilled-zucchini/ supports even planks, moderate cooking, tender-center cues and a late lemon/herb finish. Its thinner planks and shorter times are not used as a guarantee for this thicker, larger batch. FoodSafety.gov and USDA leftovers guidance are linked.
