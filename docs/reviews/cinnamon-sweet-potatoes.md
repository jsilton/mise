# Sweet Potatoes with Cinnamon: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review; no physical kitchen test or measured nutrition. Initial proposal reasoning is retained below; the final root-evaluation section records the corrections integrated after independent challenge.

Consolidation decision: keep the original slug and display title. Thin orange sweet-potato rounds with butter/honey/cinnamon/lime have a different shape, handling and flavor from skin-on maple/olive-oil wedges (optional smoked paprika), cumin/chili/cilantro/lime wedges, or garlic/paprika fries. They are also a different tuber and preparation from either mashed Yukon Gold dish. The fact that several sweet-potato recipes roast does not make them duplicates. No alias, extraction or base dependency is justified. The neighboring variants retain their own pending defects; reading them is not a substitute for their reviews.

The original approximately two-pound quantity becomes 900 g peeled/trimmed orange-fleshed sweet potato, with about 1.05 kg shopping weight. This clarifies whether peel is included; it is not an assertion that every sweet potato yields the same trim percentage. Keep one-third-inch rounds and specify that unusually broad rounds can be halved. Orange flesh is a necessary definition for the intended moist/tender result; white-fleshed Japanese varieties and purple sweet potatoes are not promised the same water content or schedule.

Retain all original flavor/fat quantities: two tablespoons butter (28 g), two tablespoons honey (42 g), half a teaspoon cinnamon and a tablespoon lime juice (15 ml). There is no demonstrated reason to lower butter or honey simply because inherited nutrition is wrong. The sweetness is intentional and lime balances perception without removing sugar. Define salt at half a teaspoon fine salt and pepper at quarter teaspoon. Remove unquantified cooking spray by lining with temperature-rated parchment; the already-listed butter supplies the potato coating. No extra finishing butter, herbs or garnish is silently added.

The material method change is splitting the glaze: first roast buttered/salted/peppered rounds, then brush honey/cinnamon after about fifteen minutes, and add lime at service. This aims to give the tuber a head start before sugary edges darken, and retain a clear citrus finish. It is a reasoned editorial adjustment, not proof the original all-in glaze cannot work; related primary honey-cinnamon recipes do use an all-in coating successfully at different cuts and oven settings. Retained amounts mean the later application changes exposure and distribution, not total sweetness. Honey is gently warmed only to brushing consistency, not boiled into candy. The mixture is a coating; the original “emulsion” label was unnecessary and unsupported.

Capacity is material: 900 g cut into broad thin discs occupies much more sheet area than the vague one-pan direction implies. Specify two roughly 18-by-13-inch sheets, no overlap, small gaps, upper/lower shelves and switching/rotation when glazing. Do not claim a mathematically verified one-inch gap around every disc. A one-sheet oven requires two baking rounds and extra time; the first batch loses heat. Pairings with chicken/turkey do not prove all meat and potato pans fit simultaneously or share temperature. The proposed outgoing links retain buttermilk chicken and add the reviewed turkey and lemon/shallot beans; remove the original pork link as a convenient default because its 375°F roast does not match this 400°F plan and the untreated recipe has other pending issues. That is not a prohibition on pork and sweet potatoes—an actual composed meal needs its own oven sequence.

Sensory controls separate tender centers, browned surfaces and glaze color. A knife must pass through several thick pieces without a hard core. A glossy brown coating is not a center-cooking test. Expected texture is soft inside with browned patches, not brittle fries; no absolute crispness guarantee is made. Add careful turning with a thin spatula and individual removal if thickness varies. Address too-dark glaze, pale crowded rounds and tearing without pretending added acid or prolonged cooking can reverse burned sugar or broken pieces.

The original 35-minute total had only ten minutes for oven heating, peeling, slicing, glaze and arranging. Revised estimate is fifty minutes including those jobs, a fifteen-minute first roast, the handling/glaze interval and ten-to-fifteen-minute finish. It can reach fifty-five with an extra initial five minutes; tell the cook to follow cues rather than promise a twenty-five-minute finish in all ovens. Two pans bake together by default; consecutive baking adds about thirty minutes. There is no cooling or resting stage omitted from this immediate-service dish. A day-ahead route includes actual cooling/refrigeration and reheating and therefore is not hidden inside the fresh fifty-minute total.

Four side servings are intentionally generous at 225 g net raw tuber per diner; six smaller portions are about 150 g each beside stuffing or another starch. The final cooked weight depends on roast loss and was not measured. A ten-person turkey link does not magically expand this batch or remove the need for more pans. Avoid invented exact cups. The former 140 kcal and 22.5 g carbohydrate figures were removed as unverified; neither was used to reformulate the butter or honey.

Add recipe-specific leftover guidance: shallow prompt refrigeration, three-to-four-day use and 165°F reheating. Cover initially during reheating to warm through without burning glaze, then uncover briefly to dry the surface. State that stored rounds soften. For a cooked-ahead batch, reserve the fresh lime for after reheating rather than repeatedly cooking its finish. The two-hour/one-hour hot-weather limits are included; cooked tubers are not safe to leave on the counter through an afternoon merely because no meat is present.
