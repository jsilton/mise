# Brown Butter-Chocolate Soufflé — individual source and culinary review

Editorial source review, 2026-10-05. Baseline `a2c9e28069faa57c7bb7fe4ac5e7ca02b6462861`. This complete correction has been checked against the current publisher and the recovered pre-migration recipe; it has not been physically kitchen-tested.

## Identity and source decision

The recovered HTML identifies Claire Ptak's Bon Appétit recipe. It contains the older three-egg formula and 475°F bake. The current [publisher page](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/brown-butter-chocolate-souffle), read in full on the review date, carries a February 2025 editorial correction prompted by reader feedback. That correction changes both the formula and baking conditions. Adopt it as one coherent specification, retaining Ptak / Bon Appétit attribution, the chocolate-and-brown-butter identity and the existing recipe URL. Do not treat this as uniform doubling or merely reduce the oven temperature in the old recipe.

The publisher's displayed rating was 3/5 from two ratings at review time. That aggregate does not distinguish old and corrected versions and is not evidence of reproducibility. No individual reader comment or completed kitchen test is claimed.

## Complete allocation audit

The corrected recipe accounts for every listed ingredient and every method-only addition:

| Component       | Amount and allocation                                                                                    |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dish fat        | 4 tbsp unsalted butter, browned and chilled until brushable; all used to coat the dish, none in the base |
| Coating sugar   | About 2 tbsp granulated sugar, additional to the meringue sugar; tap out excess                          |
| Chocolate       | 7 oz / 200 g, 65–70% cacao; melted with the cream                                                        |
| Cream           | 1 cup in the base; separate extra cream for serving                                                      |
| Cocoa           | 1/2 cup / 57 g Dutch-process cocoa, added to the chocolate base                                          |
| Optional rum    | 2 tbsp in the base; omission follows the publisher's optional status                                     |
| Salt            | 1 1/2 tsp Diamond Crystal **or** 3/4 tsp Morton kosher salt, in the base                                 |
| Water           | 1/2 cup hot water in the base; made explicit in the ingredients                                          |
| Eggs            | 6 large eggs at room temperature; all 6 yolks in the base, all 6 whites in the meringue                  |
| Meringue sugar  | 1 cup / 200 g granulated sugar, all beaten with the whites                                               |
| Cream of tartar | 1/2 tsp, in the meringue                                                                                 |
| Serving finish  | Powdered sugar and extra heavy cream; explicit extras, not subtracted from base amounts                  |
| Vessel          | One 1 1/2-quart straight-sided soufflé dish; equipment, not a scalable ingredient                        |

Remove the old extra tablespoon of sugar sprinkled onto the batter: it is absent from the corrected method. The source's granulated sugar and 57 g cocoa replace the obsolete superfine-sugar and cocoa specification. The original cocoa and water were not doubled with the eggs, so a simple multiplier would produce a different batter.

The measured formula uses the publisher's 3/4 tsp Morton kosher-salt branch. Its 1 1/2 tsp Diamond Crystal alternative is retained in substitutions, rather than embedding a second quantity inside a structured ingredient name. Salt names are retained narrowly to preserve the meaning of these two volume alternatives. This is a measurement requirement, not a product endorsement. The [official Morton label](https://www.mortonsalt.com/fr/article/morton-coarse-kosher-salt-nutritional-facts/) states 1/4 tsp = 1.2 g; its branch therefore corresponds to approximately 3.6 g, but the publisher supplies no salt weight, and no new single mass is substituted for both alternatives. Diamond Crystal describes its [light flake structure](https://www.diamondcrystalsalt.com/kosher-salt), which is not interchangeable with dense kosher or fine salt by spoon volume. Keep the explicit warning against using the larger spoon measure for fine salt.

## Method and teaching decisions

Keep the corrected 375°F oven, 35–40-minute bake, middle rack, 8–10 portions, medium peaks and staged incorporation. The first quarter of meringue is mixed completely into the chocolate to lighten it; the remaining three quarters enter in three gentle additions. The original reverse-fold and contradictory stiff-versus-soft cues are removed. Describe the medium peak by its glossy surface and bending tip, not an exact mixer time alone. The base receives cocoa, rum, salt and water before the yolks, as in the corrected publisher method.

[Lauren Weisenthal's Serious Eats recipe](https://www.seriouseats.com/chocolate-souffle-cake-how-to-make-technique-recipe) and the [CIA Egg Cookery guidance, pages 13–14](https://www.ciachef.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/egg-cookery.pdf) independently support lightening a dense base with an initial portion of whites and folding subsequent additions. They use different formulas, peak targets, temperatures and vessels; those other specifications are not imported. The final-fold cue follows the corrected publisher's tolerance of fine streaks while avoiding large unmixed pockets and a dense bottom.

Replace the sugar-ladder explanation with [egg-foam and protein-setting chemistry](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/eggs/eggscience.html), and retain butter and sugar as the flavored dish coating. The leavening lesson is linked for its gas/structure framework, not to suggest chemical leaveners belong in this egg-foam recipe; the temperature lesson supports the center check. Source attribution remains concise, with review status and bibliographic discussion kept out of the public method.

The [FDA egg guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety) supports a minimum 160°F center for egg dishes, immediate service, prompt shallow-container refrigeration, 3–4 days refrigerated storage and 165°F reheating. The measured endpoint is an explicit editorial safety addition to the publisher's visual cue, not a claimed publisher instruction or tested ideal texture temperature. No guarantee is made that every oven reaches it within the time range. Refrigerated leftovers are safe-handling guidance, not a promise that a reheated soufflé regains its original texture.

## Practicality, timing, yield and metadata

This is an entertaining dessert that needs service coordinated with the oven, rather than a make-ahead promise. Keep the 1 1/2-quart vessel and full batch together using fixed scaling: arithmetic ingredient changes cannot preserve fill depth or bake time. This does not assert that all three-egg soufflés fail in that vessel; the publisher's complete correction is the evidence for this recipe's revision.

Allow about 75 minutes from ready, room-temperature ingredients: roughly 35 minutes preparation, including egg separation and a 20-minute butter chill overlapped with base and meringue preparation, plus 35–40 minutes baking. This allows a small margin over the roughly 65–70-minute tightly overlapped schedule. The method starts the oven early, makes the base while the butter chills and starts the meringue halfway through that chill. A stand mixer permits dish coating while whipping finishes; hand mixing, colder eggs, slower chilling and oven preheating can extend preparation. These are planning estimates, not observed kitchen timings. No resting period is added before service.

Remove stale nutrition rather than recalculate unsupported values. Remove the steam method because no steaming stage exists. Retain vegetarian and identify the flour-free formula as gluten-free; allergy-sensitive cooks must still check their purchased chocolate and cocoa. Remove old unrecognized metadata and unrelated dessert-on-dessert pairing links; no separate chocolate-soufflé recipe is merged or altered.
