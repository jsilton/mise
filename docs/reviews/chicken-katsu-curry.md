# Chicken Katsu Curry: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Retained four 170 g breast portions and established an even 1/2-inch cutlet thickness. Increased panko from roughly 60 g to 100 g for dependable coverage; gave dredging flour a weight and retained two eggs.
- Added the missing 30 g sauce flour with 30 g butter for 480 ml stock. Defined onion/carrot/potato/pea weights and separated sauce flour from contaminated breading flour. Sauce now has an actual thickening mechanism instead of an unsupported velvety claim.
- Moved sauce before frying, corrected tiny vegetable cuts and their simmer time, and allowed two chicken batches plus a short rack rest. The hour estimate excludes separately prepared rice, which is explicitly called out.
- Replaced a fixed half-cup oil quantity with a 1/2-inch depth and supply range suitable for a deep 12-inch skillet. Added oil temperature recovery, internal checks for each cutlet, and a finish-in-oven rescue when coating browns too quickly.
- Removed the invented overnight marinade, two-second egg-dip rule, broad Japanese-versus-Indian comparison, and unverified nutrition. Specified a short low-oven hold on a rack rather than trapping steam on paper towels.
- Distinct from Japanese Chicken Curry: this is a separately breaded and fried protein plus vegetable sauce, not stewed chicken. Distinct from Chicken Milanese by panko, sauce, and meal context. One canonical plain-rice pairing replaces two duplicate rice links.
