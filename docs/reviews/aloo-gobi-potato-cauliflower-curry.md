## Ingredient and proportion decisions

- Keep **680 g potatoes**, now explicitly net after trimming, with optional peeling and original 2 cm cubes. Yellow-fleshed all-purpose/firm potatoes are described by useful behavior. Calling a particular cultivar categorically waxy or claiming all floury potatoes cannot work is unnecessary; the substitution explains their crumbling tendency.
- Define **680 g net cauliflower** in 2.5 cm florets. This is a working replacement for a variable medium head, consistent with the original page's roughly equal-vegetable intent. Equal weights are a choice for this batch, not a universal traditional rule. Buying a head by weight alone will not establish edible yield; remove leaves/tough core and weigh florets.
- Define **100 g onion and 150 g tomato** for the half-medium onion and medium tomato. These are transparent working weights, not recovered source precision. They keep a small tomato coating rather than turn the dish into a large-volume gravy.
- Retain all **3 tbsp oil**. Its job includes coating the whole seeds, cooking onion/aromatics and carrying the spices through 1.36 kg potatoes/cauliflower. There is no unsupported fat reduction and no extra oil silently added for a claimed crisp crust.
- Keep **1 tsp each cumin seeds, coriander seeds and ground coriander**, plus 1 tsp turmeric. Crush whole coriander lightly to prevent a hard seed bite. The whole-plus-ground coriander is intentional texture/aroma, not an accounting duplicate. Keep all garlic and ginger. Move the existing half-teaspoon garam masala to the finish to limit prolonged aroma loss; no additional dose.
- Keep two serranos as the standard fragrant hot version and explicitly distinguish the milder alternative. The old half-teaspoon red-chile substitution is not guaranteed milder than any two variable peppers. The new quarter-teaspoon cayenne alternative replaces both serranos and omits the separate finishing cayenne; a fully mild option omits both. The default optional quarter-teaspoon finishing cayenne remains for those using fresh chiles and wanting more heat. This avoids accidental double dosing.
- Replace unspecified half-teaspoon kosher salt with **1 tsp fine salt initially plus up to 1/2 tsp reserved**, approximately 6–9 g in total. Root correctly challenged the first proposal’s 3–4.5 g maximum as an unusually low cap for roughly 1.6–1.7 kg unsalted vegetables and aromatics. Swasthi uses 1/2–3/4 tsp salt for 1 1/2 cups potato and only 180 g cauliflower, plus onion/tomato: substantially less core vegetable than this 1,360 g batch. Her source does not weigh the potatoes, so an exact gram-normalized conversion is not claimed, but a rough threefold core-vegetable comparison would give about 1 1/2–2 1/4 tsp of her salt. Hari uses 1 tsp with two unweighed potatoes and a small cauliflower; those counts likewise are not a precise scaling basis. The revised 1–1 1/2 tsp fine-salt allowance is therefore a conservative staged choice, around 0.35–0.55% of the unsalted food, rather than an arbitrary multiplication or conversion from kosher salt. Taste after cooking and use the reserve only as needed. This is a material formula correction to preserve savory flavor; no physical salt preference was tested.
- Retain the original **120 ml cooking water**, split into 60 ml with potatoes and 60 ml with cauliflower. Add a separately listed **60 ml maximum corrective hot water** so the cook can rescue drying before tenderness without an unaccounted splash. It is not added automatically and is shared across all recovery steps. Finish uncovered after tenderness to remove excess moisture.
- Keep one tablespoon lemon and two tablespoons cilantro, now clearly used at the end. The alternative canned tomato is the same weight including juice; its acidity and retained liquid can differ.

No invented nutrition survives. This is **four substantial vegetable mains or six generous sides**, not four small cups. The raw ingredients total roughly 1.8–1.9 kg including the cooking water; evaporation may leave roughly 1.5–1.7 kg, but these are accounting estimates, not observed yield. Main shares therefore plausibly approach 375–425 g; six side shares approach 250–280 g. The public recipe divides the actual batch rather than guaranteeing an unsupported cup conversion. It already includes 680 g potatoes, so a composed meal should not automatically give every diner a full rice allocation too.

## Pan geometry, sequence and complete timing

The original generic large skillet is insufficient guidance. This substantial batch needs a **deep 12-inch pan, about 5–6 quarts, with a fitting lid**. Loose raw florets, potato cubes and aromatics occupy several litres and need room for gentle turning. A shallow 12-inch frying pan can spill; a narrow Dutch oven retains more steam and may need longer drying. This is a covered vegetable cook, so a pristine single layer is not promised after cauliflower enters. A doubled batch needs another pan, not a taller pile and the same timer.

Keep the original one-pan tomato-first identity. Whole seeds get 20–30 seconds in the oil; onions soften 6–8 minutes before the shorter garlic/ginger/chile stage. Ground turmeric/coriander get about 20 seconds before moist tomato, not a mandatory scorching 45 seconds. Tomato cooks 5–7 minutes to a thick moist coating. Potatoes turn 1–2 minutes, then cook covered 12–18 minutes with 60 ml water until the outside is tender but the center still offers resistance. Cauliflower then joins with the other 60 ml water; recover the simmer and cook covered 15–20 minutes, checking both vegetables. The final uncovered 3–5 minutes removes free liquid. Garam masala/optional cayenne get 30 seconds, then lemon/cilantro go off heat.

Potato-first is supported by both consulted Indian cooks; the actual clock is an estimate for this larger measured batch. Tomato-first can slow potato softening relative to Swasthi's late-tomato version, so its shorter clock is not copied. Hari's recipe also has an early masala, ten-minute covered potato head start and 25–30 minutes with cauliflower, but her 4 cm potato cut and unspecified head size differ. A lid, maintained low simmer and actual knife test matter more than either borrowed number.

On paper, pan heating/seeds 1–2 min +onion 6–8 +aromatics 0.5–1 +tomato/spice 5–7.5 +potato coat/recovery 2–3 +potato 12–18 +cauliflower heat recovery 1–2 +cauliflower 15–20 +finish 4–6 gives roughly 47–67 minutes on the stove depending on recovery/endpoints. The ordinary planning range is 45–60 cook with **80 minutes total** including 20 prep; slow/hard potatoes can extend it. The draft transparently says 65–80 typical and includes recovery time in its upper allowance rather than repeating 35. About 40–50 minutes is active:20 knife/measure plus roughly 15 watched saute work and 5–15 checks, turns and finish. The covered intervals permit other small tasks, but do not make the whole recipe passive.

The endpoint is tender potato centers, recognizable tender florets and a clinging masala, **not crispy edges**. A truly crisp roasted/fried version would require another considered method, batches and different oil/contact accounting. It is not added as an effortless variation. Gentle turning protects soft edges; a few broken florets do not make the whole dish a creamy sauce.
