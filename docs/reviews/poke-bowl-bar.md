# Poke Bowl Bar: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial proposal; no kitchen test. Full original and adjacent tuna/shoyu recipes, rice components, cucumber salads, edamame and direct meal relationships read before this decision.

## Why this is a distinct serving format

The original bar intends diners to choose among tuna, salmon and scallops, two rices and many toppings. That is a meaningful host workflow distinct from one fixed tuna bowl. Preserve the seafood choices rather than quietly replacing them all with cooked salmon or tofu. Its flawed quantities and safety claims need rewriting, but that does not itself establish duplication. The shortened display title **Poke Bowl Bar** preserves the original slug and explains choice in the description and method.

## Portions and conservation

The original fish section lists 1.5 lb tuna plus 1 lb each salmon and scallops with “choose 1–2,” so four diners might get 1–2.5 lb with no dressing adjustment. Set one total 680 g edible seafood for four, either one choice or split between two. This preserves the original full tuna allotment and is an explicit new allocation for salmon/scallop choices, not a recovered original equivalent. Two choices means 340 g each if split equally, not buying 680 g of both. Approximately 170 g seafood per person is a generous bar portion.

Retain the original 3 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tsp vinegar, 1/2 tsp ginger and 1/2 tsp heat. There was one marinade recipe despite instructions to choose two; now divide that clean dressing proportionally between fish bowls, making one mild and one spicy if desired. The original 1 tsp lime in fish dressing remains; add a separately accounted 1 tsp for the original avocado-lime instruction, and measure its previously unspecified optional salt at 1/8 tsp. No promise is made that lime sterilizes or instantly cures fish.

The original two cooked cups shari plus two cooked cups white rice are four cups total, yet its assembly only allocates half a cup shari and ignores white rice. Proposal preserves four total cooked cups and permits one kind or two cups of each, using one cup per diner across choices. Do not default to a full master batch plus a full white-rice batch for four portions. If both are cooked fresh, another pot/cooker or longer schedule is required. Default one-rice schedule is explicit.

Keep avocado, shelled edamame (150 g approximately one cup), fresh diced cucumber (150 g approximately one cup), shredded carrot (50 g approximately half cup) and red cabbage (50 g approximately half cup). The original method's radishes were absent from ingredients; remove that stray instruction and actually use the listed cabbage. Oi muchim becomes one half recipe starting with 300 g cucumber, divided across four bowls, rather than an unlimited pickle bowl. No fixed post-draining cup yield is promised. Combined fresh and dressed cucumbers are deliberately retained as separate bar choices, not presented as both being essential to a traditional dish.

The original garnishes contain 2 tbsp each black/white seeds plus another teaspoon in the marinade section, a total 4 tbsp plus 1 tsp (~39 g) for four bowls. Reduce to 1 tbsp each (~18 g) as an explicit optional-garnish balance decision: sesame oil is already in the fish dressing and oi muchim. No unallocated marinade sesame remains. Root may preserve a larger garnish allowance if that richness is preferred; the change is not safety-mandated. Measure one nori sheet, two scallions and the optional herbs, furikake, soy, spicy mayo and wasabi. Remove the unlisted final extra sesame-oil drizzle because the recipe already measures dressing oil. All remaining ingredients are used.

## Handling, timing and culture

Remove the false sashimi-grade freshness guarantee, rejection of supermarkets while endorsing the same claim at fish counters, wild-is-safer implication, freshest-Friday/Saturday invention, and 5–10-minute acid-to-mush certainty. Species-specific sourcing matters: wild salmon requires supplier parasite-destruction freezing for intended raw use; applicable aquaculture exemptions require conditions, not just “farmed”; yellowfin and scallop-only adductor products have specific exemptions that do not remove other hazards. Domestic freezer settings and casual fishmonger reassurance are not a documented process. Freezing does not eliminate all germs; raw service stays a voluntary risk and is inappropriate for groups FDA identifies.

Small bowls nested in ice, refrigerator refills, separate utensils, clean/raw work separation and excluding meltwater provide a concrete service arrangement. Temperature is measured, not inferred from being near ice. Do not top up an old serving bowl with new fish, and do not use raw-contact marinade at a communal garnish station. Default 95 minutes from one fresh shari batch includes vegetables, oi muchim and package-directed edamame cooking/chilling while rice works; about 40 minutes only from all prepared components. Overnight thawing is extra. Initial two-minute assembly is not the complete workflow.

Poke is not defined as marinated fish over rice; the many-topping bar is a contemporary Hawaiian-inspired bowl arrangement, with Japanese/Korean components named accurately. No authentic origin story, specific inventor, universal traditional fish choice or nutritional claims remain.
