## Formula and accounting

Retain **680 g beef** (practical expression of 1.5 lb), specified as net trimmed boneless top sirloin. This is the default naturally tender steak, sliced across the grain about 1/4 inch thick and 2 inches long. Flank remains a disclosed somewhat firmer alternative without alkaline treatment. No marinade was present in the source, and this proposal does not silently add one or promise restaurant-velvet texture. Tough stew cubes are not equivalent.

Define the variable five cups broccoli as **450 g trimmed edible broccoli**, with consistent small florets and peeled stem slices. This is a chosen usable batch, not an exact conversion of every cook's loosely packed cups. Four portions each receive 170 g raw beef allocation and 112.5 g prepared raw broccoli; cooked yields need weighing. Plain rice is a separate accompaniment; no extra broccoli side is needed by default. The linked short-grain rice is specifically that reviewed rice product/method, not a generic 15-minute jasmine-rice promise; it takes 65 minutes and can set the dinner's overall clock.

Preserve all substantive original sauce amounts: **4 tbsp regular soy, 2 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil, 120 ml stock/water and 2 tsp cornstarch**. Specify unsalted stock and unseasoned vinegar to control unintended salt/sugar, not to impose a generic low-sodium formula. White pepper now explicitly enters the sauce; the original ingredient was never used. Toasted sesame oil finishes the thickened sauce rather than being exposed to the searing heat. Three garlic cloves and one tablespoon ginger remain. Define two variable scallions as 40 g and separate their white/green destinations. Unmeasured sesame seeds become an optional teaspoon, preserving rather than silently removing the garnish.

The water-based sauce inputs total approximately 225 ml before sesame oil; roughly 5–6 g cornstarch produces a light sauce rather than an intentionally stiff gel. Oyster sauce also contributes thickness and brand-dependent salt/sugar. The selected sauce is strongly savory but proportionate to over a kilogram of meat and broccoli. Do not guarantee sodium values from unspecified brands or remove sauce ingredients merely to hit a target. Avoid reducing it excessively. Reserve two tablespoons water for small corrections; it does not erase salt already present.

**Cooking oil is 3 tbsp, not a hidden extra splash.** The original two tablespoons could not unambiguously fund two one-tablespoon beef sears plus a remaining tablespoon for broccoli. New explicit allocation is:

- Two broccoli batches × 1 1/2 tsp = 3 tsp.
- Three beef batches × 1 1/2 tsp = 4 1/2 tsp.
- Aromatics = 1 1/2 tsp.
- Total = 9 tsp = 3 tbsp.

Separate steaming water is 60 ml, two 30 ml uses. The two final combinations use the already allocated sauce, with no new oil. All original ingredients and every newly measured water/oil allowance have destinations. No legacy nutrition survives.

## Method and timing

The source demanded smoking oil and said a burned smell proved correct heat. Remove these instructions. A shimmering film, an audible sizzle, release after browning and unburned garlic are actionable; deliberate oil burning is not a goal. A 12-inch heavy skillet or 14-inch wok is explicit. The method uses two approximately 225 g broccoli batches, three approximately 225 g beef batches, and two final sauce combinations. Merely batching the sear and then dumping everything into a crowded pan would not solve the household-capacity problem.

Broccoli first browns, then briefly steams to soften its center, and excess water evaporates before it leaves the pan. Color alone is not doneness. Beef cooks separately to a measured endpoint; it is not left partly raw for an uncertain final thirty-second toss. The final sauce is re-stirred before heating because starch settles. Half is temporarily removed to a heatproof cup so each half of the cooked food can be coated without forced heaping. All beef juices are divided and returned. At least three minutes elapse after the last beef batch reaches 145°F/63°C before serving; inserting a fine-tip probe sideways into representative thicker slices is explicit.

**55 minutes**, with 25 preparation and approximately 30 cooking/service, replaces the impossible twenty-minute promise. Cooking comprises two 5–6-minute vegetable rounds, three 2–4-minute meat rounds, reheating the pan, aromatics/sauce and two short final combinations. The beef rest normally overlaps sauce preparation and finishing. Slower pans or thicker stems can take about an hour overall. Rice, optional freezer firming and extra sides are additional. These estimates are editorial; timing and thermometer access still need a kitchen trial.

- https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures — 145°F/63°C plus three-minute rest for whole-cut beef and 165°F/74°C for leftovers. This is a conservative application to slices of a whole steak, not a claim that every three-millimeter surface is easy to measure.
- https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety and https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts — separate raw and ready-to-eat equipment, prompt refrigeration at 40°F/4°C or below, two-hour/one-hour-above-90°F limits, cooked-meat leftovers 3–4 days.
