# Chili-Garlic Eggplant and Tofu: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read entire original, including the nonsensical instruction to rest a cup of chicken even though the recipe used tofu and optional chicken broth. Removed that injected template sentence and unlisted salt/pepper/cilantro. Specified 600 g eggplant and 400 g drained tofu. Rather than asking for an undefined block, a medium eggplant, an unmeasured half-inch frying bath and 2/3 cup coating starch, use separate roasting/pan-frying for deliberately different textures: two tablespoons roasting oil, two tofu oil, one teaspoon aromatics oil; sixteen grams tofu starch and one teaspoon sauce starch. The eggplant has no coating-as-impermeable-barrier claim.

Roast 3/4-inch eggplant at 425°F to tender centers, pan-brown dried extra-firm tofu during that roast, and coat briefly at the end. Fifteen-minute pressing is included alongside preparation; 60-minute elapsed plan, including the final sauce and coating, is realistic with an adequately sized skillet. Broth/soy/vinegar/sugar, chili, sesame and optional lime are measured; cilantro/scallions are listed and used. Default vegetable liquid is vegan. One optional bird chili replaces mandatory three and acid is not said to remove capsaicin heat. Broad Chinese-inspired framing replaces an unsupported precise Sichuan identity.

Maggie Zhu supports the separate starch-coated tofu pan technique and brief sauce finish; Jeanine Donofrio supports roasting cubed eggplant to tenderness. This combined recipe is an editorial redesign, not their tested recipe or a claim that roasted eggplant tastes identical to deep-fried. It remains distinct from the lighter vegetable-only garlic side and fermented-bean pork side because tofu is central, its sauce is sweet-sour, and the textures/workflow differ. Test tofu water content, single-layer pan capacity, thin coating release, roast tray load, 1 tsp slurry after coating carryover, sugar/vinegar/chili balance, four main portions with rice, pressing overlap and full timing.
