# Honey-Chipotle Chicken and Lime Rice Bowls: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read the complete source, Cilantro-Lime Rice, the recently reviewed Honey-Lime Chicken Thighs for Tacos and the pickled-onion component. Kept this as a distinct chipotle/adobo/Dijon breast-and-rice bowl rather than merging it into soy/fish-sauce thigh tacos. Mexican-inspired home cooking is explicit; no claim that Dijon is a traditional Mexican marinade or that smoky flavor penetrates deeply after a long soak.

The original six servings had unmeasured rice, greens, tomato, avocado and salt. Defined 900 g chicken as six roughly 150 g cutlets with even half-inch thickness; retained the two-tablespoon chipotle/honey/lime/olive-oil and one-tablespoon Dijon character, four garlic cloves, and added named salt plus two teaspoons separately allocated skillet oil. A mild substitution is bounded and acknowledges brand variability. No uncooked raw-contact marinade becomes a finishing sauce.

The six bowls now include two complete batches of the individually reviewed rice (360 g dry rice, 600 ml starting water and all doubled seasonings), 120 g greens, 450 g tomatoes, 300 g avocado and six lime wedges. This is roughly 3/4–1 cup rice per bowl according to actual yield. The rice is an explicit usesBase dependency and live ingredient link, not a second optional side. Removed automatic corn salad and beans that would enlarge the bowl without a portion plan. Pickled onion is a small optional tomato replacement with extra preparation time stated. The parenthetical two-batch rice yield is an estimate to test, not an asserted measured yield.

Changed the fifty-five-minute claim that hid at least two hours marinating to ninety minutes including thirty minutes cold marination, the full rice/rest workflow, two skillet rounds and meat resting. Longer marination up to two hours is additional rather than mandatory. Moderate heat, excess-coating drainage, non-overlap, batch oil and removal of scorched deposits replace a generic ten-minute grill/skillet equivalence. Each cutlet reaches measured 165°F / 74°C before removal. First-batch holding is acknowledged; an outdoor option only cooks all six at once if grill capacity allows. Added separate storage for hot cooked components and fresh toppings, and removed unverified legacy nutrition.

Evidence: USDA FSIS poultry marinating guidance, indexed text read at https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/poultry-basting-brining-and-marinating ; FoodSafety.gov temperature guidance and USDA FSIS leftovers guidance. Rice formulation evidence is recorded in cilantro-lime-rice.md. These support handling and endpoints; the revised complete bowl formula is editorial development from the original and has not been cooked.

Kitchen questions: six-cutlet geometry and two-round capacity in an actual 12-inch pan, cooking time and browning before 165°F, chipotle-brand heat/salt, whether the sauce coat is balanced at the defined salt amount, 360 g rice's finished yield, six-diner component balance, first-batch temperature at service and the ninety-minute schedule. Do not advance to kitchen-tested without recorded results.
