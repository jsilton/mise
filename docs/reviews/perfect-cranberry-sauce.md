# Whole-Berry Cranberry Sauce: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read and compared both complete cranberry sauce sources. Both cook a 340 g/12 oz bag of cranberries with sugar and citrus for the same holiday condiment role. The juice/zest version and peel-strip version are useful variations, not separate canonical dishes. Retain perfect-cranberry-sauce under its established URL as Whole-Berry Cranberry Sauce, with a measured cranberry-orange variation and a tarter sugar option. The individual consolidation decision is in cranberry-orange-sauce.md.

Changed the default sugar from 200 to 150 g and water from thirty to 180 ml. This is an editorial tart-sweet sauce between the two originals, with the original two-stage berry texture retained but corrected. Explicitly divide 255 g berries for initial cooking and 85 g for a short final simmer. The late fruit is tender before cooling, not raw whole cranberries stirred into an off-heat pot under a promise of crunchy perfection. A two-by-one-inch pith-free peel strip is removed, and the small salt amount is defined. Every ingredient is used.

The orange variation replaces sixty of the 180 ml water with juice and uses one teaspoon zest after heat, omitting the strip. It preserves the citrus-forward idea without two tablespoons of zest or falsely describing zest itself as the acid punch. A 100 g sugar alternative is explicitly tarter and may be looser; it is not asserted to make the same gel. This is a spoonable refrigerator sauce, not a molded jelly or a canning formula. Natural fruit solids and pectin contribute body as it cools; popping is not a precise pectin-release switch.

Corrected role to condiment, yield to roughly two cups/twelve 2–3-tablespoon portions, and time to three hours including a shallow-container chill. The eighteen-to-twenty-minute stove estimate depends on berry state and simmer strength. The original thirty-seven-minute total omitted cooling, while the other record's three-cup yield was implausible for its quantities after evaporation. Refrigeration is prompt rather than waiting indefinitely for a deep hot saucepan to reach room temperature. Replaced unsupported two-week storage with the general three-to-four-day refrigerated prepared-food guidance and explicit no-pantry-storage claim. Removed legacy nutrition and unrelated stew/oatmeal pairings; waffle usage is a small condiment use, not another sauce recipe.

Direct Ocean Spray whole-berry sauce provides a comparison: 340 g berries, 200 g sugar and 240 ml water yield about 2 1/4 cups. The revised lower sugar/water formula and staged texture are independent development from the two repository sources, not Ocean Spray's exact recipe. NCHFP's Jellied Product Ingredients explains fruit/pectin/acid/sugar interactions and lists cranberries among pectin/acid-rich fruit; no canning process is borrowed. USDA leftover guidance supplies the general cooling/storage approach.

Kitchen questions: finished two-cup yield, sweetness with actual roast and rich sides, tenderness of the late berries, fresh/frozen timing, chilled consistency at both sugar levels, orange peel bitterness, strength of the orange variation and real chilling time. No physical kitchen test recorded.
