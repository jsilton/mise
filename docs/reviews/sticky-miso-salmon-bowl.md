## Formula and complete accounting

The original has two cups sushi rice, three tablespoons white miso, two honey, one vegetable oil, one grated ginger, **two teaspoons grapefruit zest plus one tablespoon juice**, four skinless 6–8-ounce portions, four scallions and one tablespoon butter. The `2f2553cd` refinement changed fish to skin-on, halved zest, removed oil/butter from the ingredient list while still using both in directions, and made all cold toppings seem mandatory. Its fixed five-to-seven-minute heavily coated broil did not give a measured endpoint. These are evidenced editorial changes and omissions, not proof that a family intentionally preferred less zest or skin-on salmon.

The proposal deliberately keeps the archived skinless identity at the lower stated fish range: **680 g net / four 170 g portions**, excluding skin and pin bones. The comparable fillets must be 3/4–1 inch thick; weights alone cannot set broiling time. Restore the original tablespoon glaze oil, tablespoon butter and two teaspoons zest; add one separate teaspoon oil for foil as a measured practical allowance. Keep full miso/honey/ginger/juice rather than cutting sweetness or richness. Depending on brands and packing, the combined glaze is roughly 100–120 ml, around 25–30 ml per portion; this is a coating, not an additional cold sauce. Spread evenly and avoid thick piles or puddles. All raw-contact coating cooks with the fish, with no later raw drizzle.

Two cups Japanese-style short-grain becomes **400 g dry rice / 535 ml water**, a close water quantity to the saved 2 1/4 cups (540 ml), now using the reviewed rice approach of thirty-minute soaking, 12–15-minute covered simmer and ten-minute rest. This is an intentional method adaptation, **not evidence that NYT's original no-soak eighteen-minute method was impossible**. It favors the same measured hydration approach as the individually reviewed Plain Short-Grain White Rice and openly increases total dinner time. The cooker's cycle is an alternative, not an automatic extra soak. This preserves the original ample starch allocation of 100 g dry rice per diner; expect roughly 1.3–1.5 cups finished rice each depending on product and packing, not a measured final yield. It is plain rice, not vinegar-seasoned Shari, glutinous rice, long-grain jasmine or pilaf. Butter and all scallions go into the cooked rice. The reserved two tablespoons corrective water is optional and separately accounted.

Cucumber becomes 300 g/four 75 g shares, one large avocado supplies four shares, and two sheets nori are explicitly snipped and used. Optional kimchi is half a cup/four two-tablespoon shares and chile crisp two teaspoons/four half-teaspoon shares. This makes a reproducible default arrangement while keeping the original ability to omit fermented heat. No radish was added to an already complete topping set. Unmeasured additional salt/pepper from the archive is not reinstated as compulsory: miso and optional kimchi/chile crisp already season the bowl, and the clean glaze is tasted before contacting fish. This is an editorial seasoning decision, not a low-sodium claim. Miso brands vary, so no invented sodium calculation is supplied. Legacy nutrition is removed.

## Household cooking and sensory checks

One 3-quart pot and a metal half-sheet pan can handle the stated amount. Foil is broiler-safe, parchment is excluded. The fish surface begins 7–9 inches from the element rather than five inches under an intense broiler. The cook checks from minute four, watches browning, rotates uneven heat and measures each fish center at 145°F/63°C; ten-to-sixteen minutes is a starting interval rather than a guaranteed exact finish. Dark glaze is not a proxy for safe doneness. A gentler 350°F finish with loose shielding preserves an already browned surface if the center lags. Namiko Hirasawa Chen supports the thickness/distance/scorching controls, but her separate recipe removes marinade and uses a lower endpoint; this proposal does **not** claim she tested this heavy grapefruit glaze or its 145°F schedule.

Total **70 minutes** includes five minutes washing rice, thirty-minute soaking, approximately five-minute heating, 12–15-minute simmer and ten-minute rest, with ingredient preparation during soaking and fish during the last rice cooking/rest. Approximately 25–30 minutes hands-on is an estimate; the frontmatter's fifteen-minute prep/twenty-five-minute cook does not include the passive soak. Thicker fish or oven fallback can exceed seventy minutes. Rice should be cohesive and tender, not dry and perfectly separate; cold cucumber, avocado and just-added nori provide contrast to warm fish and rice.
