# Greek-Style Lamb Burgers with Mint Yogurt: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read the complete original, its meal, the pita method, standalone tzatziki, village salad and instant couscous. Compared the neighboring turkey meatball pita: different meat, seasoning, shape and cooking workflow justify separate recipes. No duplicate standalone lamb burger was found among the lamb entries. This is a Greek-inspired herbed burger, not an unsupported authenticity claim.

Retained 680 g lamb for four generous filled pitas, but weighed onion at 60 g rather than allowing an unspecified small onion to dominate and weaken the patties. Garlic and mint are listed separately for meat and sauce. Salt now distinguishes Diamond Crystal from fine salt; pepper, skillet oil and every optional topping have explicit quantities. The two teaspoons cooking oil are divided between two pan rounds; sauce oil is a separate tablespoon. Four equal patties contain 170 g meat each plus aromatics and are shaped about 3/4 inch thick. The default 12-inch skillet holds two with space, not an assumed four-burger batch.

Replaced timer-only medium doneness with measured 160°F / 71°C for every ground-lamb center, entered from the side. The American Lamb Board/Well Seasoned burger page was read for context but its 150°F medium instruction was not adopted; federal ground-meat guidance governs this version. Removed the unsupported claim that oval shape uniquely renders fat, the absolute ban on grated cucumber, and the generic mandatory five-minute ground-meat rest. AMSA's directly read ground-meat texture guidance supports mixing enough for cohesion while avoiding excessive protein extraction; browning and doneness are distinct checks.

The sauce retains the original mint/diced-cucumber identity rather than silently becoming the separate grated-cucumber/dill tzatziki. Thick yogurt, 100 g dried diced cucumber, measured salt/pepper, staged lemon and modest oil provide a spoonable sauce. Start with 2–3 tablespoons in each pita and offer extra separately; do not force the whole roughly 1 1/2-cup sauce yield into four sandwiches. Refrigeration during meat preparation replaces an unsupported mandatory thirty-minute flavor wait. Prepared tzatziki is an explicit replacement, not an additional pairing; its old lamb pairsWith entry was removed. No second sauce recipe is created.

The full recipe includes four pita breads and declares the reviewed warming component through usesBase and working ingredient/method links. Its sixty-minute allowance includes 25 minutes preparation, two 10–14-minute skillet rounds, overlapping oven bread warming and assembly. The first patties' short hold has an acknowledged texture cost. The composed meal omits optional burger feta/tomato/greens because the separate village salad already provides them. Standalone toppings remain measured options. Legacy nutrition removed; no recalculated nutrition claimed.
