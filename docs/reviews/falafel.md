## Quantities and yield

The original says two cups dried chickpeas but only fifteen walnut-size balls. Normalizing to 400 g dry chickpeas follows this collection's reviewed hummus convention of 200 g per cup. Hydration can bring that to approximately 800 g or more before adding 60 g herbs, 120 g peeled onion, garlic, and seasoning. Roughly 1 kg mixture divided into fifteen portions would produce approximately 65–70 g pieces, not small walnut-size falafel. The original 3–4-minute frying instruction therefore has no dependable relationship to its stated yield.

The proposal specifies approximately 25 g pieces, around 1 1/2 inches across, yielding roughly forty, or six main portions with bread, sauce, and vegetables. The exact hydration gain and finished count remain estimates. The key instruction is small consistent weighed portions, not stretching the mixture into an arbitrary count. The 25 g reference is supported by the practitioner recipe discussed below, while the forty-piece mass budget is our arithmetic development, not a measured yield.

Herbs now have 30 g weights each, preserving the original two cups as approximate measures rather than relying on undefined packing. Onion becomes 120 g peeled, and the four garlic cloves are retained. The original tablespoon each of cumin and coriander seeds is fairly assertive but recognizable and is retained; no unrequested blandness correction or spice reduction is made. Toasting was only implicit in the ingredient line; it is now an actual step with cooling and fine grinding. Salt type is named, retaining an initially modest dose and requiring a fried test before additional seasoning. All baking powder and spices are incorporated, and all processor batches recombined before judging texture or seasoning.

Oil is an approximate purchase quantity constrained by actual pot geometry: two inches in an eight-inch cylinder is about 1.65 L, rising to approximately 2.1 L at nine inches. The 1.9 L estimate is consistent with the stated eight-to-nine-inch pot range, but oil depth/headroom govern use. Larger pot width or scaling needs more oil and changes batch throughput; it is not a fixed absorbed ingredient. Remove unverified nutrition because the original fifteen-piece serving unit, fifty-calorie estimate, and oil uptake were not defensible.

## Chickpea preparation, moisture, binder, and science

Distinguish dried weight, cold-soaked uncooked beans, cooked beans, and canned beans explicitly. The same two-cup quantity of canned or cooked beans is not a substitute. No hot quick-soak is offered. Hummus can be made beside falafel only from separately allocated chickpeas: its baking soda, long cooking, and soft puree are the wrong starting state here. This contrast is a real ingredient dependency, not a reason to merge the recipes.

Keep the flour-free formula rather than adding a routine flour rescue. Surface drying includes herbs as well as chickpeas; processor batch capacity and a squeezed-mound checkpoint replace an ambiguous coarse-sand endpoint. Chilling remains for thirty minutes, but the unsupported assertion that starch sets in a refrigerator is removed. No exact molecular binding model or guarantee that baking powder ensures a fluffy result remains. The small original baking-powder dose is preserved without representing it as essential to every falafel recipe; it cannot correct undergrinding, pureeing, or oversized portions.

Lopez-Alt's firsthand comparisons support raw-soaked chickpeas and adequate grinding for flour-free falafel. His published formula differs in aromatics and omits baking powder. That evidence does not validate our entire mixture or establish that every canned-chickpea fritter fails. The proposal limits the incompatibility claim to this formula rather than declaring no canned-chickpea recipe can ever work.

Ottolenghi's direct falafel recipe uses soaked beans, small portions, and a refrigerator rest, but also flour, water, and a different herb/spice formula. It demonstrates that flour and leavening choices vary among practitioner recipes. Its portion reference informs our size decision; its formula and timing are not claimed as a test of our flour-free batch.

## Frying, timing, and safety

Replace the ambiguous ball-or-disc choice with one defined small-ball geometry. Retain 350°F / 175°C oil, use a dedicated frying thermometer and deep pot with headroom, supervise the oil, and lower portions with a dry utensil. Start with one fried test, then about five or six at a time, recovering temperature between batches. The 4–5-minute range is a starting estimate tied to the stated size. Inspect the center rather than treating green herbs, steam, or dark crust as proof of doneness. No invented USDA first-cook internal temperature is assigned to falafel; the 165°F figure appears only in government-backed leftover reheating guidance.

The original twelve-hour total omitted the rest and all day-of work; its ten-minute cook time could cover only a few small batches. The proposed 14 hours 20 minutes includes five-minute soak setup, twelve hours refrigerated soaking, twenty-five-minute ingredient preparation, thirty-minute rest, fifteen-minute shaping, fifteen-minute oil heating, and fifty minutes for the test and remaining batches with recovery. Twenty-four-hour soaking adds twelve hours. The fifty-five or more small pieces produced by smaller scoops would take longer, and a weak burner or tiny processor adds time. A seasoning correction after the fried test can require reshaping and also adds time. This is a planned weekend/entertaining main rather than an automatic weeknight recipe.

A cook cannot safely be instructed to leave hot frying oil unattended while completing a salad. Accompaniments should be prepared before oil heating or by another cook. Serve successive batches promptly; there is no unsupported claim that forty minutes of holding leaves the first pieces identical to fresh-fried ones. Drain on a rack in one layer rather than trapping steam in a hot pile.

Cold soaking is backed by Extension's general dried-bean preparation guidance. Its hot quick-soak and subsequent simmer instructions are not imported into this falafel. Raw mixture is ground on the day of frying and remains covered/refrigerated, avoiding an unverified multiday raw-mixture shelf life. Cooked leftovers follow general three-to-four-day refrigeration guidance and 165°F reheating. The 375°F reheating oven and ten-minute initial check are editorial quality guidance, not a validated time endpoint; actual center temperature governs reheating.

## Substitutions, dietary labels, and composition

The original is already vegan and without added wheat, dairy, egg, or sesame. Tags reflect the falafel alone, with baking-powder label checking where gluten avoidance matters; pita and sesame sauce change the complete meal's allergens. An all-parsley option preserves total herb mass. The ground-spice alternative is an approximate starting amount, not a density equivalence or a toasted-seed claim. No fava-bean, canned-bean, oven, or air-fryer conversion is invented for an untested mixture.

Keep main-dish role but pair with a carrying bread, measured lemon-tahini sauce, and fresh tart vegetables rather than two separate couscous bases. Six portions at two tablespoons sauce use 3/4 cup, within the reviewed approximately one-cup sauce yield. Six breads require more warming rounds than the reviewed four-bread recipe. Fattoush's four generous portions must be scaled for six or supplemented with vegetables in a complete meal review; this falafel review does not relabel the composed meal as reviewed. Hummus remains an optional small dip because chickpea falafel plus a full hummus portion can crowd out vegetables and increase overall richness.

## Kitchen-test questions before stronger claims

1. Weigh dry beans, drained cold-soaked beans, each herb after washing/drying, final mixture, and cooked yield. How many actual 25 g pieces result, and does a 25 g ball measure around 1 1/2 inches?
2. Compare twelve- and twenty-four-hour refrigerated soaking with the actual chickpea brand/age. Record grind time, moisture, shape retention, and tenderness after frying; do not assume larger beans hydrate at the same rate.
3. Photograph a squeezed-mound checkpoint in the actual processor. Record capacity, batch count, pulses, scraping, and whether a slightly finer grind improves cohesion without making paste.
4. Fry one 25 g test at measured 350°F, record four/five-minute center texture, crust, oil drop, and recovery. Repeat across the full batch and record practical pieces-per-pot capacity and elapsed frying time.
5. Taste the retained tablespoon each of toasted cumin/coriander, the initially modest salt, and baking powder. Compare with a no-baking-powder half batch only as a documented experiment; assess interior texture and any bitter/off flavor.
6. Record first-versus-last-batch service texture and leftovers reheated to 165°F; test whether the stated reheating starting check is realistic.
7. Compose an actual six-portion service with six breads, 3/4 cup sauce, and sufficient vegetables. Check portion satisfaction, sauce coverage, warming equipment, and labor before assigning any meal review status.

## Root evaluation and final integration

Read the full original, proposal, supporting record and independent challenge, and independently opened the Ottolenghi/Tamimi and Lopez-Alt primary pages. Kept the flour-free raw-soaked chickpea formula, herbs and original whole-spice/leavener quantities. No duplicate exists. A 25 g portion is the controlled target; approximately forty to forty-five is an estimated count, while the simple six-main-portion serving attribute remains scalable. The broad diameter is a guide, not a guaranteed conversion.

Raised the initial salt to 6 g with up to 3 g reserved after a fried test. About 3 g in over a kilogram of otherwise unsalted mixture was an unjustifiably mild interpretation of an unnamed kosher salt. The two primary comparisons support considering a higher dose but do not establish our optimal concentration; the cooked test and kitchen comparisons remain necessary. The original quantities are not silently reinterpreted as tested equivalents.

Moved the test before bulk shaping, turns the burner off in place while tasting/adjusting/shaping under supervision, and counts the subsequent reheat. Final schedule is a rounded 14 hr 45 min (45 min preparation, about 90 min oil heating/testing/frying, 12 hr soak and 30 min chill), with stage ranges and slower equipment stated. It does not claim an exact untested elapsed optimum. Added a cut-open hydration cue, even processor allocations and successive-batch serving guidance with real sauce/bread/salad amounts. Frying-oil quantity now stays tied to pot depth rather than multiplying as an absorbed ingredient in the scaler.

The public note connects water uptake during soaking with moist-center cooking and surface drying during frying, without asserting a cold starch-setting mechanism or guaranteeing that every cooked-chickpea fritter must fail. All counts, salt preference, oil recovery, holding texture and kitchen timings still require physical validation. No new meal or calendar review is claimed.
