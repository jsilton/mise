## Ingredient accounting, portions and capacity

Specified 1360 g net bone-in, skin-on cooking pieces, excluding back/giblets: two breast halves, two thighs, two drumsticks and two wings. This retains a cut-up bird rather than quietly converting to boneless thighs. Shopping weight for a whole bird must exceed the listed net pieces. Four mixed portions are reasonable with the planned mash and vegetable, but no cooked yield is claimed; one wing cannot count as a full portion. Sharing breast meat makes distribution explicit. A larger family needs more substantial pieces, not a fiction that all eight pieces are equal.

The 475 ml buttermilk amount remains, with a snug container or bag instead of claiming it submerges a cut bird in any large bowl. Defined 12 g fine salt in the marinade, 1 tbsp hot sauce, 1 tbsp lemon juice, 75 g onion, three garlic cloves and five thyme sprigs. Twelve grams is about 2.5% of the buttermilk mass and roughly 0.65% of chicken plus buttermilk, before other ingredients; these are formulation comparisons, not estimates of absorbed salt. Cereal, cheese, hot sauce and cultured dairy all contribute variable salt, so no automatic extra coating salt was added. Choose unenhanced chicken. The lemon juice is measured instead of relying on half a lemon's variable output; all aromatics are stripped off after marination, not inadvertently breaded.

Replaced the ambiguous two cups of finely crushed cornflakes with 150 g plain flakes weighed before crushing. That quantity, combined with 45 g finely grated Parmesan, provides working crumbs for eight irregular pieces; excess raw-contact crumbs are explicitly discarded, not all represented as eaten. Small crumbs rather than powder should attach while retaining texture. Retained two teaspoons chopped thyme and set black pepper at half a teaspoon. Added two measured tablespoons oil: one teaspoon on the rack, five teaspoons distributed through crumbs. This is an editorial coating formula, not experimentally established pickup or a promise that the skin beneath becomes crisp. No unlisted spray, egg, flour, gravy or finish remains.

An 18-by-13-inch rimmed sheet pan and oven-safe rack are the base equipment, with roughly one-inch gaps. A broad set of breast halves may not fit that rack; a second pan is explicitly required when needed. Larger batches get two shelves with position swapping and possible extra cooking time. Ordinary cooling racks are not assumed oven-safe. This is more honest than asserting that every eight-piece chicken fits a generic large pan.

## Sequence, timing and sensory controls

The original sixty-minute total omitted at least three hours of marination and a five-minute rest. Proposed total is 265 minutes: 10 minutes initial prep + 180 minutes minimum refrigerated marination + 15 minutes coating/preheating + 55 minutes baking + 5 minutes rest. Prep metadata is 25 minutes and cook metadata is 55 minutes; a 12-hour soak adds nine hours beyond the minimum. Seventy-five minutes after marination includes coating, heating, baking and rest. All are planning allowances, not observed results. A cold oven needing longer than the coating interval or larger/two-pan batches extends the total.

Kept 400°F baking but start individual thermometer checks at thirty minutes. Each piece must reach 165°F / 74°C away from bone; large breasts get more than one spot checked. The thirty-five-to-fifty-five-minute range is provisional. Wings/breasts can leave before legs, and legs may cook further for texture without defining a higher temperature as a safety minimum. Removing pieces uses a separate clean rack; an uncovered five-minute final rest limits trapped steam. Meal service must account for earlier pieces cooling; this is not a prolonged holding method.

Draining marinade to a thin film, stripping aromatic pieces, pressing small crumbs, oiling crumbs and leaving skin-side up without turning address adhesion and wet pockets. There is deliberately no post-marination pat-dry instruction that removes the only binder. Loose foil shields an overbrowning coating while the interior finishes, with its crispness cost disclosed. Burned crumbs and overcooked breast cannot be repaired by extra resting or sauce. The rack separates chicken from drippings; it cannot guarantee an equally crisp bottom or crisp skin beneath a wet crust.

Removed the unsupported buttermilk natural-enzymes mechanism. The recipe now teaches the observable tang and binding film, without replacing that claim with an absolute acid-tenderizing or bacteria-killing promise. The paper reviewed below does not isolate a natural enzyme action or establish a three-hour treatment for this chicken. The original three-hour minimum is retained as a practical recipe choice, with a bounded three-to-twelve-hour quality window; it is not a safety threshold or a scientifically determined optimum.

## Sources and limits

- https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/chicken-farm-table was accessed and supports 165°F for all individual chicken parts, raw refrigeration for one to two days, cooked storage three to four days and reheating to 165°F. It also rejects rinsing poultry. Its roasting chart uses 350°F, so it does not validate this 400°F coated formula or the proposed timing.
- https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/poultry-basting-brining-and-marinating was initially unavailable through direct open but its primary-source search result supplied the full relevant marination section: covered refrigerated marinating for up to two days, food-safe containers, and no uncooked reuse of raw-contact marinade. This draft chooses disposal. The three-to-twelve-hour window is the recipe's narrower editorial choice.
- https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety was accessed and supports separating raw/cooked equipment, 40°F refrigeration, shallow prompt cooling, the two-hour/one-hour-above-90°F limits and measured hot holding. Oven reheating at 350°F with checks from twenty minutes is a culinary estimate, not the agency's tested schedule.
- https://www.saltfatacidheat.com/buttermilkmarinated-roast-chicken was accessed on the author's site. It provides a useful whole-bird comparison for about two cups buttermilk in a snug bag and a plain-yogurt alternative. Its salt level, whole-bird geometry, uncoated surface and day-long soak differ. The present coating, salt dose, yogurt-water dilution and timings are not attributed to Nosrat. The source's clear-juice endpoint was not adopted.
- https://www.wkkellogg.com/recipes/double-coated-chicken-with-kellogg-s-corn-flake-crumbs-recipe was accessed. It uses 1 3/4 cups prepared cornflake crumbs with 3 lb chicken and added fat, a useful gross coverage comparison. Its egg/flour batter and 350°F direct-pan method differ. The formula here is not a reproduction, and the source's rinsing/clear-juice advice was not adopted. The associated UK original https://www.kelloggs.co.uk/en_GB/recipes/double-coated-chicken.html measures 150 g flakes for four unspecified chicken pieces with double coating; it supports a weighed-cereal approach, not validation of this eight-piece batch.
- Primary research: Augustynska-Prejsnar et al., Animals 2021, 11, 3282, https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/11/11/3282 . Publisher direct access failed and PMC showed a browser check; the study methods and findings were consulted through its full-text PDF mirror https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/2f24/af6b415f400b1a391e4fb4e632ac6875d8cd.pdf . It studied breast meat from older laying hens and fermented dairy under controlled conditions, and reported texture benefits. It does not establish that commercial buttermilk's natural enzymes explain this recipe, prove its soak duration or justify a household microbial-safety claim. No such extrapolation appears on the proposed recipe.

## Substitutions and storage

Yogurt plus measured water supplies a practical dairy alternative with the dilution explicitly untested; homemade acidified milk is not called identical. Certified gluten-free flakes preserve the cornflake identity; no default gluten-free claim is made because common brands contain barley malt. The dairy remains. Equal-weight bone-in thighs are a distinct all-dark-meat option; the recipe does not promise identical time for boneless breasts. Panko was not offered as a thoughtless equivalent to the defining cereal crust.

Refrigerated marinade handling preserves the original raw-storage clock; it is not an extra two days after aging chicken. Used marinade, aromatic pieces and raw-contact breading are discarded. Shallow prompt leftover storage and the cooked-poultry reheat endpoint follow agency guidance. Rack reheating is a texture proposal with an endpoint, not a fixed twenty-minute assurance, and residual sogginess is acknowledged.
