# Jamaican Rice and Peas (Kidney Beans): individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Corrected dried/canned equivalence: 200 g dry beans are not equivalent to the original 1 1/2 cups canned beans. Specified 500 g drained canned beans as a practical cooked-yield alternative; exact dry yield remains a test question.
- Added an explicit full 30-minute rolling boil in fresh water after soaking, followed by further cooking to complete tenderness. The original immediate reduction to a low simmer did not communicate the FDA kidney-bean lectin control. Canned beans skip this stage because they are already cooked.
- Corrected the liquid arithmetic to 400 ml coconut milk plus 320 ml cooked-bean liquid/water = 720 ml. The previous can-plus-cup-plus-quarter-cup instruction varied with can size and contradicted its exact-three-cup claim. Short covered infusion limits evaporation before rice enters.
- Weighed rice as 360 g and described six to eight side portions. Named and staged salt for canned-bean variability; kept thyme, scallion, garlic, allspice, pepper and optional coconut oil accounted for in the method.
- Removed the guarantee of no heat from an intact Scotch bonnet and supplied omission as the mild option. Avoid breaking it during stirring and remove before fluffing. The optional whole-chile instruction deliberately remains whole when ingredient quantities are scaled; do not cut it to satisfy a fractional count.
- Corrected total from 8 hr 40 min to about 10 hr 30 min with dried beans, versus about 55 minutes with canned. Removed unverified nutrition and the claim that full-fat milk is the only workable option.
- Distinct from coconut jasmine rice by cooked legumes, savory aromatics, chile, and the two-stage process. Other coconut-rice records were compared; this is not a duplicate.

Verify dried-bean yield and age-dependent time, rice hydration after the infusion, actual six-to-eight-portion yield, canned-bean seasoning, chile variability, and pot/burner settings. No physical kitchen test recorded.
