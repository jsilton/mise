# Plain Short-Grain White Rice (Gohan): individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Read both complete source recipes: Steamed White Rice (Short Grain) and Japanese Short-Grain Rice (Gohan). Both are unseasoned, rinsed, absorption-cooked short-grain rice with a ten-minute rest; their small water and soak differences do not justify duplicate recipes. Retained the widely linked steamed-white-rice slug; a separate consolidation record preserves the other source.
- Replaced ambiguous two-cup amounts with 300 g rice and 400 ml water for four side portions. This is less dry rice than the original two US cups; it is a deliberate portion correction. Explained rice-cooker cup versus kitchen cup and volume versus weight rather than claiming a universal ratio.
- Made a 30-minute soak part of the schedule and corrected total time to approximately 65 minutes. Allowed a brief endpoint check rather than forbidding lid lifting even when doneness is uncertain.
- Distinguished Koshihikari from medium-grain Calrose and from glutinous or seasoned sushi rice. Removed misleading claims about total starch content and washing away the starch required for stickiness.
- Replaced towel-wrapped room-temperature keeping and reheating on a keep-warm function with prompt chilling and measured reheating endpoints. Removed unverified nutrition and the unrelated noodle-salad pairing.
- The primary Japanese-rice reference supports cup distinctions, hydration, soaking, and covered cooking; the complete method also reconciles our two existing recipes. It does not establish kitchen-test status for our pot, rice, or serving yield.
