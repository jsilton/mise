# Bok Choy with Garlic and Oyster Sauce: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read full original and both meal references. The old method browned sliced garlic before another 3–4 minutes of searing/steaming, risking burnt garlic. It also called for an implausibly universal 60–90-second stem endpoint, unlisted water/broth, unmeasured fried garnish and a salty 2 tbsp oyster plus 1 tbsp soy for 450 g greens. Specified 600 g trimmed baby heads for four sides, two pan batches, 1 tbsp oil allocated as three teaspoons, separate steam/sauce water, 1 1/2 tbsp oyster and 1 tsp reduced-sodium soy, with measured sesame and optional prepared garnish.

Garlic now follows cooking/draining the greens and precedes the brief sauce finish. Stem knife resistance, head sizing and additional water are explicit. The twenty-five-minute total covers grit removal, draining and both batches. Not a blanching instruction disguised as steaming; not a guaranteed browned/bright-green ninety-second result. Vegan mushroom-sauce alternative is conditional, not an oyster-sauce vegan badge. Kaitlin Leung's source supports cleaning, sizing and avoiding burned aromatics, not this exact covered-pan/oyster formula. Same-family mature bok choy variation separates stems/leaves. Federal leftover guidance is distinct from quality claims.

Mongolian meal now receives a restrained vegetable accompaniment rather than two heavy soy glazes.
