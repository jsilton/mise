# Japanese Chicken Curry: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Added missing oil, garlic, ginger, and a named salt amount. Weighed onion, carrot, potato, and apple to make the six-portion composition reproducible. Clarified thighs as boneless and skinless.
- Changed flour from roughly 30 g (four tablespoons) to 70 g for 960 ml stock plus vegetable moisture, with 70 g butter. The original thin, dark roux could not reliably deliver its promised thick gravy. Golden-to-light-brown cooking replaces a forced dark endpoint; the final flow cue and water adjustment remain essential.
- Retained 3 tbsp Japanese curry powder, reduced garam masala to 1 tsp, and used unsalted stock with measured condiments. Increased soy from 1 tsp to 1 tbsp within that unsalted system. These flavor proportions require tasting in a physical test.
- Removed the implausible deep-onion-paste promise and supplied a staged simmer, smooth roux incorporation, bottom-stirring, vegetable checks, and 165°F chicken endpoint. Thickening is described as starch hydration rather than emulsification.
- Renamed the rice-less ingredient formula Japanese Chicken Curry and explicitly separated six rice portions from it. Removed stock-as-side and extra mashed-potato pairings. Retained as distinct from katsu curry, not a duplicate: the protein is stewed in this sauce.
- Source review supports the roux method and late thickening, not the superiority claims about homemade versus commercial roux or the exact performance of this formula. Removed unverified nutrition.
