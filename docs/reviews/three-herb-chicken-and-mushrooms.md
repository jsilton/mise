# Three-Herb Chicken & Mushrooms: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

Read all ingredients and steps. The original put eight unspecified bone-in thighs in one cold 12-inch skillet and claimed 35 minutes total despite searing, roasting and a separate mushroom sauce. Kept eight small thighs but defined about 175 g each (1.4 kg total), two per serving. Added two searing batches and a rimmed half-sheet roasting pan, with sauce preparation overlapping the roast. Stated 70 minutes elapsed; larger thighs need longer. Kept the 165°F minimum and added a distinct higher dark-meat tenderness preference rather than implying those are the same endpoint.

Mushrooms increased from 227 to 450 g for four servings, with a realistic water-release/browning stage. Rendered fat is measured down to 2 tbsp for sauce, with an explicitly listed oil supplement only as needed. Salt and pepper now have amounts and are actually used. Reduced woody herbs from 2 tbsp each to 2 tsp rosemary and 1 tbsp thyme; moved both into the simmered sauce rather than searing fresh minced herbs against skin and finishing with large raw amounts. Retained 1/4 cup parsley at the end.

Kept 120 ml wine, 240 ml broth and 2 tbsp Dijon; raised flour from 1 to 1.5 tbsp for a lightly thickened sauce, reduced wine first, and moved mustard later. Flour gelatinization and evaporation, not mustard alone, explain the body. Sauce goes around the chicken instead of covering the crisp skin. Removed the mandatory High Note language, unverified nutrition and the second starch pairing.

## Relationships and remaining tests

This skin-on bone-in roast with a light mustard/herb sauce differs from braised coq au vin and boneless chicken Marsala. Existing potato and green-vegetable partners remain suggestions, not required components or reviewed meals. ACS and Exploratorium support browning and starch principles; they do not validate this exact roast or sauce formula.

Kitchen tests: actual thigh weights/yield, first cold-pan versus second warm-pan sear, sheet-pan spacing, tenderness range, sauce reduction/flour balance, mustard salt variation, herb balance, and the 70-minute overlapping workflow. No physical kitchen test recorded.

ThermoWorks culinary testing guidance was also checked for the 175–185°F dark-meat texture preference. Its discussion supports distinguishing tenderness from the minimum safety endpoint; no instant collagen-conversion threshold is claimed here.
