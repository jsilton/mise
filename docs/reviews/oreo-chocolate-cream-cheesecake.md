## Pan, yield and timing

A 9-inch pan has roughly 410 cm² bottom area. Total raw ingredients are about 2.0 kg, with about 1.8 kg above the thin crust. Requiring a 3-inch-deep springform gives headroom for this dense batter; a shallow cake pan is not an interchangeable container. A rimmed sheet catches fat from the seam without a new water bath. Fixed scaling is deliberate: multiplication within the same pan would change depth, endpoint and cooling. It is not a general ban on making another cake.

Sixteen slices follows the source's internal serving-size evidence and yields roughly 120 g per finished slice after some bake loss; no precise baked yield is claimed. Twelve would be very generous rather than impossible. Restoring sixteen is about credible party portions and source evidence, not lowering nutritional density. The imported 90-calorie nutrition is plainly impossible for this formula and is removed instead of recalculated from unmeasured outcomes.

Ten-hour total includes 45–60 minutes to soften cubed refrigerated cheese, 45 minutes setup/preparation including the 10-minute crust bake, 50–70 minutes filled-cake baking, roughly 60 minutes rack cooling and at least 6 hours cold storage. This sums about 9 h 20–9 h 55 before small serving work. The filled-cake upper bake range is extended to allow the actual 160°F center endpoint; 50 minutes is a first check, not a finish promise. Six-hour chilling is a conservative service plan for the roughly 2 kg cake, longer than the source's four hours; actual fully cold center controls readiness and overnight is simplest. The original 300 minutes cannot contain all those stages.

Cheese softening plus batter work stays below the stated two-hour pre-bake counter allowance in a cool kitchen. Eggs come out only for the last 20–30 minutes. No requirement to leave dairy out all morning. After baking, loosen the edge while warm, keep the ring attached for support, cool on a rack about one hour and refrigerate while still somewhat warm if necessary. No indefinite oven-off cooling or wait for a counter-cold center. Warm cooling time does not get another two-hour allowance afterward.

## Technique and safety decisions

Cream cheese must be smooth before eggs; low-speed egg incorporation avoids unnecessary foam. This does not guarantee a crack-free cheesecake. Chocolate is melted separately in short low-power bursts and enters lukewarm/fluid, limiting overheating and immediate hardening against cold cheese. Cream cheese holds water/fat/protein; eggs set the baked filling, and chilling firms it further. Public teaching stays sensory and practical rather than a branded “pattern” or “ultimate dessert” claim.

The center must reach 160°F / 71°C, consistent with FDA consumer egg-dish guidance; a coherent tremble is supplementary texture information. Some baking references suggest lower center values or higher temperatures near the edge; those are not silently substituted for the center minimum here. No raw batter tasting. Check near the endpoint and avoid waiting until the entire hot center is rigid. Cool/refrigerate on the stated prompt schedule and use within 3–4 days. FDA's leftover hot egg-dish reheating sentence is not treated as an instruction to reheat cheesecake: this is a refrigerated dessert served cold, consistent with its source method. Public dairy/egg/wheat/soy cautions reflect actual ingredients.

Only `gentle-proteins` is selected: its protein-setting and 160°F principle applies, even though its worked example is scrambled eggs. It is not a direction to scrape a baking cheesecake. The cold-preparation lesson's central cheese-cutting advice serves firm sushi fillings, the reverse of this softened mixing setup, so it is not attached just because the cake chills.

Substitutions retain full amounts: comparable vanilla-creme cookies make a flavor variation; equal-weight 60–70% cacao chocolate makes a darker version. Plain unsweetened chocolate, mini/thin cookies, whipped spread and lighter cheese are not claimed interchangeable. The drafted vanilla-creme substitute explicitly requires similar unit size to retain 12/20 counts; where available cookies are much lighter, those counts cannot establish the same total mass, so use the stated type/size rather than guessing a new count.
