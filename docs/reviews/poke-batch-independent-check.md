# Independent challenge: three poke recipes and Sushi Rice (Shari)

Reviewed 2026-09-06. Proposal review only; no repository changes or physical cooking/service tests. This check compares all four full original recipes against all four proposals, all four final individual records, sources.md and decision.md, and reads the actual linked Plain Short-Grain White Rice and Oi Muchim recipes plus both direct poke meals. Also read the three roll recipes linked from the rice page to check that the changed rice yield does not imply each roll recipe uses a complete rice batch.

## Decision

Retain all three poke recipes as meaningful variants. Their original compositions support the distinctions; different bowl counts alone would not. The mild shoyu bowl has fresh cucumber, a larger edamame proportion and a lightly sweetened dressing with no mandatory chile. The other tuna bowl incorporates a separately salted and dressed Korean cucumber salad, chile and nori; its rice seasoning and total salty components also differ. The bar adds different seafood choices, lime, a broader vegetable set, divided mild/spicy dressing and communal cold-service requirements. A shared central raw-fish reference could reduce future maintenance, but that is not evidence that these whole recipes are duplicates.

The rice is a reusable seasoned base, distinct from plain gohan. Retaining its existing slug and replacing misleading universal cooker/counter-holding instructions is appropriate. No consolidation redirect is recommended in this batch.

## Findings and exact corrections raised during review

### A. Clarify the shoyu bowl's prepared-shari substitution

The substitution currently says to omit “all of this recipe's rice vinegar, sugar and salt.” That wording can remove the fish dressing's teaspoon of vinegar and half teaspoon of sugar as well as the rice seasoning. The later body section is more precise; make the structured substitution agree.

Exact replacement effect:

> Omit only the three seasoning ingredients under Rice: 2 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar and 1/2 tsp salt. Keep the vinegar and sugar in the tuna dressing. The prepared shari has a stronger seasoning profile and its full batch makes extra.

Also replace timing's “About 85 minutes from freshly cooked plain rice” with “About 85 minutes when making the plain rice from dry.” The present sentence immediately includes the rice's whole 65-minute process, so its starting-state wording contradicts itself even though the arithmetic is defensible.

### B. Remove the assumed cucumber weight-to-cups conversion

Both chile tuna and the bar start a half batch of Oi Muchim with 300 g cucumber and state or imply this yields about 1 1/2 cups after draining. The component gives a raw weight, not a measured dressed cup yield. Its brief salt-and-blot method does not establish this conversion. Do not treat the whole half batch and 1 1/2 measured cups as necessarily equivalent.

Least disruptive replacement in both methods:

> Make a half batch of Oi Muchim, starting with 300 g cucumber and halving all its other ingredients. After salting and dressing, measure 1 1/2 cups for these bowls, lifting slices from excess dressing. Refrigerate any extra separately for use by the next day.

Keep the ingredient's 1 1/2 cups as the served amount and record actual gram/cup yield in testing. Alternatively, root may deliberately choose the entire half batch as the bowl allocation, express it as a half batch instead of cups, and justify that portion change. Do not insert an invented exact gram equivalent for dressed cucumbers.

### C. Give optional shari salt an actual dissolving liquid

A spoonful of cooked rice is not a useful medium for dissolving and evenly distributing up to half a teaspoon of fine salt. The current step risks a concentrated salty pocket. Preserve the original maximum seasoning rather than arbitrarily cutting it.

Change vinegar ingredient to `5 tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar, divided`.

Replace rice step 4:

> While rice cooks, combine 4 tbsp of the vinegar, all the sugar and 1 tsp salt. Warm briefly if needed to dissolve, without boiling. Keep the remaining tablespoon vinegar and half teaspoon salt separate.

In step 5, replace the reserved-salt sentence:

> Taste the seasoned rice. Warm the reserved tablespoon of vinegar and dissolve as much of the reserved salt as needed in it, then fold it evenly through. If no extra salt is wanted, add that tablespoon of vinegar without salt.

All five tablespoons of original vinegar still reach the rice. The final salt range remains a considered flavor choice, not a preservation claim.

### D. State the salmon sourcing decision completely

The bar correctly says wild salmon needs documented freezing and farmed salmon is not automatically exempt. Make the action explicit for either salmon purchase:

> For salmon, obtain supplier documentation of parasite-destruction freezing unless the supplier documents a qualifying aquaculture exemption. A farmed label alone does not establish that exemption. If the supplier cannot confirm the applicable control, choose another suitable seafood or a fully cooked meal.

This strengthens an already sound caution without introducing a home-freezing procedure or suggesting that freezing controls all hazards. Keep the species-specific tuna and shucked scallop-muscle distinctions. An ice-filled tray is a method of keeping seafood cold, not proof of temperature: add to bar step 5, “Check that seafood stays at 40°F / 4°C or below; replenish the ice as needed.” Add a clean food thermometer to equipment if using this instruction; a refrigerator thermometer alone does not measure the food on the bar.

## Portions, sauce and sequence that pass

The two tuna drafts preserve 450 g fish and approximately 65 ml basic dressing, enough to coat the diced fish. A three-bowl shoyu serving contains 150 g fish, about 1 1/3 cups rice, 100 g cucumber and 50 g edamame, plus avocado. Calling those generous bowls is reasonable. The final four-bowl chile version has about 113 g fish, one cup rice, a quarter of the cucumber half batch (75 g raw cucumber before salting/dressing) and about 19 g edamame per bowl, plus avocado. Its beans are a garnish-sized amount, preserved from the original half cup, not a second substantial protein portion. The cucumber and nori distinguish the bowl even with the small chile quantity.

The bar's 680 g TOTAL seafood supplies 170 g per diner across choices. It fixes the original ambiguous instruction that could lead to buying every listed pound amount. Its roughly 70 ml dressing has a lighter dressing-to-fish ratio than the tuna bowls, reasonable with the seasoned cucumber and optional table condiments; no automatic 1.5× sauce increase is required. The bar's original one-pound salmon/scallop choices have been standardized upward when chosen alone; the individual record should identify that deliberate change rather than claim every alternate quantity was preserved. Two tablespoons total sesame seeds is half the original garnish load (plus the original marinade teaspoon); this should also be explained as an editorial choice, not attributed to the original formula.

The bar has roughly 38 g edamame, 38 g fresh cucumber, 25 g combined carrot/cabbage and a quarter of the cucumber half batch per diner (75 g raw cucumber before salting/dressing), plus avocado. Broad optional garnish choice does not require every diner to add all optional soy, furikake, mayo and wasabi. Lifting fish and salad from pooled dressing, keeping the nori dry, and tasting before extra salty condiments are useful controls on this composition. No required radishes or extra finishing oil remain unlisted.

Edamame package-directed cooking is a substantial improvement over assuming frozen beans are ready to eat after thawing. All vegetable preparations occur before raw-fish work. Cook edamame early in the rice cycle and chill it promptly; do not leave cooling until the last five-minute tuna marinade. The three fresh-rice schedules—85, 90 and 95 minutes—include the real nested rice work and allow vegetable/edamame work during soaking and cooking. The five-to-ten-minute refrigerated fish seasoning can overlap rice cooling; it is correctly described as seasoning rather than a safety clock. Refrigerator thawing is separately disclosed. These remain planning estimates requiring a one-cook service test.

Scaling fish, vegetables and dressing proportionally is reasonable for bowl counts, but the linked rice's fixed pot/cooker capacity and the bar's chilled serving area do not scale indefinitely. Add a brief instruction when scaling beyond the stated batch: use a suitable larger pot or separate rice batches, increase cold serving space, and do not assume unchanged time. Buying two seafoods or offering two rices divides the total allocation rather than doubling dinner. Making one full shari batch intentionally leaves extra; the drafts say so and direct prompt separate refrigeration.

## Rice assessment

The original two-kitchen-cup formulation is clarified as 400 g dry rice. Starting water increases from approximately 450 ml after the original two-tablespoon subtraction to 480 ml; this is a modest, explicit covered-pot formulation change. The 30-minute soak, 12–15-minute simmer, ten-minute rest and five-to-ten-minute seasoning/cooling sequence plausibly fits the 80-minute allowance including washing and heating. Cookers remain appliance-specific.

The original 75 ml vinegar and roughly 19 g sugar remain. Fine salt starts at one teaspoon with up to half a teaspoon more; the full original amount is preserved as the maximum. Compared with the culinary reference, this is a less sweet and relatively saltier house formula, so taste it with soy-dressed fish before endorsing the full optional salt. No nutrition calculation can decide that flavor question. Approximately 5 1/2–6 cooked cups is plausible but not measured. A four-cup poke portion leaves extra rather than consuming a whole batch by implication.

Rice hydration must be checked before seasoning. The shari troubleshooting covers hard/dry rice but only tells the cook to “correct” free water. For completeness, add: “If free water remains and grains are still firm, cover and simmer on low a little longer, then rest and check; if grains are already mushy, further mixing will not restore them.” This follows the already-reviewed gohan distinction. Refrigeration changes texture but is preferable to the original blanket prohibition. Vinegar is not a verified acidification process here, and no extended body-temperature hold should return.

The rice page's three linked rolls each specify two cups prepared rice; retaining these links does not mean each needs an entire 400 g dry batch. Those roll recipes remain unreviewed and contain their own raw-fish, timing and other issues (including an unrelated roast-rest instruction in New York Crunch Roll). This batch must not imply that reviewing their rice reviews the rolls themselves.

## Primary evidence, source boundaries and safety decisions

Directly accessed 2026-09-06:

- [FDA Food Code 2022](https://www.fda.gov/media/164194/download), sections 3-402.11–12 and 3-501.13: freezing controls are −20°C/−4°F for seven days; or −35°C/−31°F until solid followed by fifteen hours there; or −35°C until solid then −20°C for twenty-four hours. Specific tuna, scallop-muscle and qualifying aquaculture exemptions apply. Supplier records establish controls; ordinary household freezer capability is not assumed. Vacuum-packed fish labeled to remain frozen is removed from its reduced-oxygen environment before refrigerator thawing. These retail-code criteria inform supplier questions; the drafts do not need to become a home parasite-destruction manual.
- [FDA seafood consumer guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely): supports cold handling, susceptible groups avoiding raw seafood and the fact that freezing does not eliminate every hazard. “Sushi-grade” branding or an exact marinade interval is not presented as a guarantee. Same-day raw service is a conservative recipe choice within any earlier supplier limit, not a guarantee of suitability.
- [FDA histamine guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-issues-final-compliance-policy-guide-scombrotoxin-histamine-forming-fish-and-fishery-products): cold-chain control is essential; later cooking/freezing does not remove histamine already formed. Supplier selection cannot be reduced to the day the fish was caught.
- [Namiko Hirasawa Chen, sushi rice](https://www.justonecookbook.com/how-to-make-sushi-rice/): confirms 150 g per 180 ml rice-cooker cup, equal rice/water volumes for its sushi preparation, soaking and seasoning while hot. It also distinguishes prepared rice quantities from raw cups. This is technique/formula comparison, not proof of our exact cooked yield or house seasoning. Its refrigeration/holding recommendations are not adopted over the proposal's federal handling references.
- [University of Hawaii Nutrition Center, Ahi Poke](https://nutritioncenter.ctahr.hawaii.edu/ahi-poke/): demonstrates poke as seasoned ahi with limu and onion, without requiring a rice bowl. It supports the drafts' restrained cultural distinction. Its own sauce is materially different; do not portray it as the source of these exact dressings, vegetables or raw-service controls.

No vendor has been certified by this review. No claim about a named retailer, freshness guarantee, universal farmed-fish exemption, or home freezer performance is supported. Keep those claims absent. Federal temperature/time guidance is not a sensory shelf-life test for raw tuna or seasoned rice.
