## Formula and accounting decisions

- Preserve two 15 oz cans of chickpeas, 3 tbsp oil or ghee, the whole cumin, the entire 1 tbsp coriander / 2 tsp garam masala / 1 tsp turmeric / 1/2 tsp Kashmiri chile formula, two serranos, 400 g tomatoes, amchur, cilantro and the finishing lemon allowance. Do not reduce the strong turmeric or split garam masala merely to mimic another author's milder formula.
- A can's printed mass includes liquid. Add the operational expectation of about 250 g drained beans per can, consistent with the neighboring curry's shopping language; the total edible bean target is about 500 g. It is not a guarantee that every brand drains to that weight. A cooked-from-dried alternative replaces this cooked weight, with all prior soaking/boiling excluded from the clock.
- Standardize the undefined large onion as 250 g peeled and the variable one-inch ginger as 1 tbsp finely grated. These are chosen working quantities, not recovered historical conversions. Four garlic cloves remain four; no spurious universal gram conversion is necessary.
- Replace unspecified kosher salt with a deliberate 3/4 tsp fine salt initial dose plus 1/4 tsp reserve, about 4.5–6 g added salt. This is a seasoning decision, not an equal-volume salt conversion. Canned ingredients vary in salt; the separate reserve and tasting with an actual chickpea prevent an automatic maximum addition. The comparable primary canned-chickpea formula begins with 3/4 tsp salt; salt crystal type is not established there, so it does not validate an exact gram equivalence.
- Preserve the original 240 ml cooking water. Add a separate conditional 120 ml reserve to address sticking onion or unusually concentrated tomatoes; it is not automatically poured into the curry. Half and quarter amounts scale, and the method refers to the listed reserve rather than a static maximum.
- Keep amchur in the original ground-spice stage. Preserve the original lemon but add it gradually after tasting. If omitting amchur, a separately listed additional tablespoon of lemon replaces it at the finish. The two lemon portions are a maximum tasting allowance, not an instruction to force both into a sour batch. This resolves the original method's risk of frying the liquid substitute with dry spices or losing track of the separate finish.
- All ingredients have destinations. Garam masala is divided equally, the finishing portion gets one minute in hot curry, cilantro is folded in, and whole slit serranos are removed or identified to diners. No unlisted garnish or sugar is introduced.

## Pan, yield and sequence

A 4-quart pot about 9–10 inches wide leaves ample stirring room for roughly 1.5 kg of ingredients before reduction. Half uses a 2-quart pot and quarter a 1-quart pot; larger quantities need larger/wider or separate pots. Vessel changes matter because a quarter batch in the full wide pot would lose water too quickly. Piece sizes and sensory endpoints stay the same rather than halving every time.

The proposed onion time is 12–15 minutes for soft golden onion, rather than promising deep browning in 6–8 minutes. Cumin cooks briefly first, then onion cools the fat. Garlic/ginger follow the onion. The ground-spice stage is 15–20 seconds on low before tomatoes go in; reducing the time from 30 seconds protects the substantial fine powder without removing its full amount. Tomatoes reduce for 8–10 minutes, with a thick moist trail as the cue. Visible oil separation is optional, not proof of readiness or a requirement to keep frying.

Tender canned chickpeas enter the tomato base with the full original water. Covered simmer remains 20 minutes. Mashing about a quarter and a 3–5-minute uncovered finish give control over body. The target is coating and spoonable, not soup or a dry paste. If canned beans are firm, cook them separately until tender first; the short acidic-curry simmer is not a reliable rescue plan.

Roughly five cups finished curry is an estimate from the bean volume, vegetable base and expected reduction, not a measured yield. Four portions of about 1 1/4 cups each are moderate mains with rice or bread; each contains approximately 125 g drained chickpeas before cooking. Serving dal as well makes a multiple-curry plate and calls for smaller portions of each; the pairing is not an instruction to cook three full mains.

## Timing and workload

Original 35-minute cook time cannot contain 20 minutes covered plus the stated aromatic and tomato stages, heating and finishing. Proposed 70–80 minutes includes 20 minutes prep; 12–15 minutes onion; about two minutes cumin, aromatics and ground spices; 8–10 minutes tomatoes; several minutes to heat; 20 minutes covered; 3–5 minutes uncovered; and final seasoning. Hands-on work is about 35–40 minutes, with the covered simmer partly passive. A slow burner, watery tomatoes or firm beans extend this.

The rice link is the reviewed 40-minute unsoaked basmati base, not a chicken-rice dish. Naan is the reviewed 12-bread, up-to-three-hour dough recipe, with 65–75 minutes active work. Neither is silently included in this curry's clock. Dal is likewise separate. No meal review claim is made.
