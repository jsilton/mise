## Source-supported choices and limits

Primary methods were read in full: Swasthi Shreekanth's saffron rice uses unsoaked rinsed basmati and varies water with grain age/type; Dassana Amit's Indian-style saffron rice uses whole spices, ghee and a separate soak. Their ratios and timing differ, so they do not establish a universal basmati formula. The proposal keeps the original unsoaked identity and full cooking liquid while specifying aged white, non-parboiled basmati. Exact milk/onion/whole-spice synthesis remains an editorial formulation, with actual hydration checks.

Sohla El-Waylly describes saffron infusion in warm water or milk and direct addition to sufficiently wet, long-cooking dishes. This supports a fifteen-minute warm infusion and disproves the old insistence that milk is uniquely essential. The recipe does not assert a lab-derived optimum or that cold liquid categorically cannot extract saffron. Burlap & Barrel describes smoked black cardamom as stronger and more resinous than green; this supports distinguishing their flavor roles, not an exact pod conversion. Detailed links and scope are in saffron-dal-source-comparison.md.

## Practical method, capacity and clock

A 2–3-quart heavy pot with a snug lid holds the full batch, allowing room for boiling and a loose finished grain bed. Half uses a narrow 1 1/2–2-quart pan; quarter uses a 1-quart pot. Doubling needs about four quarts; larger quantities use separate batches. Ingredient amounts can scale, but pot width/evaporation and whole-spice counts still require thought. No assumption that a quarter batch in the same large pan shares the full-batch water loss. The associated meal gives practical whole-spice counts for its half and five-eighths batches.

Warm milk first, steep crumbled saffron for at least fifteen minutes while rinsing and cooking onions. This overlaps work rather than tacking an arbitrary wait onto dinner. Bloom whole spices briefly in ghee, then cook onion moderately so small spices do not blacken. Add drained rice for a short coating, then add all liquid and salt. Bring to a gentle boil, cover and use a gentle simmer rather than an automatic lowest setting that might stop cooking.

Remove the parchment-under-lid step and the claim it prevents condensation dripping onto rice. A fitting lid is sufficient here; no loosely draped paper near a burner is needed. Check actual hydration: no hard grain center and no free liquid, then ten minutes off heat covered. Steam holes alone do not prove tenderness. A dry/firm pot receives measured corrective water; wet/tender rice gets gentle venting. Stirring cannot restore mushy grains, and burnt bottom residue is left behind.

**60 minutes elapsed, about 25 active** replaces 35. Approximate sequential upper ordinary path: preparation ten, fat/spices one, onion 10–15, rice coat one, heat liquid five, simmer 15–20 and rest ten: 52–62 minutes. The saffron steep overlaps preparation/onion. Cooking corrections can extend this. Half a batch retains grain thickness, onion endpoint and ten-minute rest; it does not become a thirty-minute side by arithmetic.
