## Portions, geometry and crunch

Preserve eight ounces tuna as 230 g edible trimmed yellowfin and two prepared rice cups as 320 g, four 80 g rice portions. One avocado becomes 150 g edible flesh, a selected medium-avocado portion rather than a fixed yield of every fruit. A half English cucumber becomes 100 g flesh after watery-core removal, explicitly a chosen consistent filling amount. Per roll: about 58 g tuna, 38 g avocado, 25 g cucumber, 80 g rice and one quarter of the original flakes. Total mass is around 815–850 g before nori, depending on flake density, yielding four rolls cut to eight pieces each. Thirty-two pieces are about two main portions or four starters, not a verified complete meal for four.

This is the fullest of the three rolls. Four half-sheets plus one-inch bare seams cannot reasonably be assumed to contain the original generous fish/avocado/cucumber/rice allocation. Propose 19 x 15 cm three-quarter sheets with one-centimeter bare seams, cut from four full sushi sheets. The method trims only the first sheet before testing closure, preserving flexibility for the remaining sheets. A paper capacity screen is in docs/reviews/sushi-roll-batch-comparison.md; actual rolling is still required. Do not claim the wrapper dimension has been proven merely because the recipe now has numbers.

Avocado is cut into 5–6 mm slices, cucumber into slender dry strips and tuna into 6–10 mm strips after tough connective tissue is trimmed. The original unconditional “always across white lines means never stringy” becomes a practical visible-grain cue without a guarantee. The spoon pit-removal instruction avoids requiring the cook to strike a handheld pit with a knife. Only enough hand water to reduce sticking is used; flooding nori promotes tearing.

The undeveloped rice dam is removed. Dry flakes are added just before rolling, not mixed with all wet fillings in advance. Following independent challenge, each completed roll is cut and served before making the next for the best remaining crunch; the procedure no longer promises immediate serving while instructing all four rolls to wait for a batch cut. Four-roll assembly is thus service in rounds, not simultaneous perfectly crisp plates. The unrelated “let roast rest five minutes so juices redistribute” sentence is deleted. Cutting to eight pieces is consistent and no cooked-meat rest logic applies.

## Time and raw-service controls

From dry rice, allow about 120 minutes with the final shari's 80-minute process and approximately 40 minutes of rolling/cutting service, doing station/vegetable work during rice waits. From freshly prepared barely warm rice and purchased flakes, about 45 minutes. A first attempt, homemade crumbs, refrigerated-rice reheating/cooling or fish thawing takes additional time. No 25-minute complete-from-dry claim remains.

Supplier species/handling checks and raw-service intent replace label certainty. Yellowfin's parasite-freezing exemption does not establish universal raw safety. Vacuum-packed frozen fish is removed from reduced oxygen packaging before refrigerator thawing. FDA susceptible-group advice is explicit. Fish comes out in single-roll portions and unused rice is promptly refrigerated separately. Finger-dipping vinegar water is not an acidification or sanitation treatment. Same-day raw service is a conservative plan, not a shelf-life experiment.
