## Safety correction and timing

The original 1–2-minute blanch remains an early texture check, not the stop instruction. FDA warns that washing does not eliminate bacteria and that raw/lightly cooked sprouts are unsuitable for vulnerable groups:
https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely

University of California ANR Publication 8151, page 1, gives a concrete cooked-sprout endpoint of steaming hot or above 165°F / 74°C, especially applicable to mung bean sprouts:
https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2024-02/393187.pdf

The patch requires thorough heating and the measured endpoint, before cooling with clean water/equipment. It does not claim a fixed minute count guarantees pathogen destruction. Longer packaging directions take precedence. The twenty-minute estimate adds realistic water heat-up, cooling and draining to the former ten-minute promise; optional chilling is additional.

FoodSafety.gov supports prompt refrigeration and time-out limits:
https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety

Two days is a conservative quality target. Remove unverified legacy nutrition rather than pretending all blanching salt is eaten. Four small sides remain the intended yield.
