# Greek-Style Turkey Meatball Pitas: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read the complete original and compared the lamb pita, standalone tzatziki and full Turkey Meatballs source (easy-baked-turkey-meatballs). The latter is a larger Italian-seasoned carrot/egg/breadcrumb batch intended for sauce/pasta and remains pending; it is not a duplicate of this spinach/oregano sandwich. The lamb version is also distinct. Renamed the public title to avoid representing oven meatballs as shaved spit-roasted gyro meat; preserved the existing URL.

The original metadata said roast but the method pan-fried twelve supposedly one-inch balls. With a pound of turkey plus vegetables, that stated count and diameter were incompatible. The revised 450 g 93%-lean turkey, 30 g dry chopped spinach, 40 g minced onion and garlic/seasonings make twelve roughly 45 g, 1 1/2-inch balls. Short mixing supplies cohesion without a paste; the original binder-free character is retained with a specific physical-test question. Oven roasting avoids the fragile mixture's repeated skillet rolling and uses one tablespoon oil, one teaspoon on the pan and two on surfaces. It does not promise the same crust as frying.

The one-batch 425°F roast uses a 15–20-minute planning interval and measured 165°F / 74°C centers, with checks beginning at fifteen minutes. Removed the generic ten-minute rest and unsupported guarantee that spinach prevents dryness. AMSA's handling guidance and FoodSafety.gov's poultry endpoint were read directly. Kristina LaRue's spinach/turkey meatballs support a comparable 425°F/~20-minute roast as a reference, but contain binders and a different yield; her color-only finish is not used and her kitchen-tested claim is not transferred. Monique Volz's Greek turkey meatballs were also compared (400°F, different binder/herb mix) rather than copied as this formula.

The sauce now has 180 g thick yogurt, 75 g cucumber before squeezing, one tablespoon initial lemon plus one optional teaspoon, measured dill/garlic powder and salt. This tempers the original two tablespoons lemon against only half a cup yogurt. It provides approximately one cup, with a precise prepared-tzatziki replacement if already available. Tomato, serving cucumber and serving onion have separate quantities and allocation; all four pita breads are part of the main and use the reviewed warming component. Four sandwiches contain three meatballs each. The forty-five-minute plan includes prep, roast, parallel skillet bread warming and assembly. Legacy nutrition removed; no dietary/test claims invented.
