## Geometry screen: evidence versus development

The original methods are nori-outside rolls. They are not inside-out rolls merely because many restaurant versions with similar names use rice outside. Preserve that actual method. The reference hosomaki recipe uses half-sheets with 80 g rice and small single fillings; its tuna quantity corresponds to about 17 g per roll. The reference inside-out spicy roll uses about 57 g fish per roll with rice outside. Our originals put roughly 57–58 g tuna and all rice inside, plus cheese/cucumber or avocado/cucumber/crumbs. They also remove a full inch of usable rice area for a bare seam. That combination deserves a capacity correction.

Proposed common format: four **19 cm wide x 15 cm deep** three-quarter nori rectangles, each containing **80 g prepared rice** and one fourth of its actual filling, with a **1 cm** bare far edge. Buy four full unseasoned sushi sheets; trim the first and test closure before trimming the other three. Do not buy four snack packs or assume four half-sheets can be enlarged. Reserve clean dry offcuts. Eight cuts per roll give 32 pieces consistently.

A paper screen explains why the proposal is plausible but not tested. A 15 cm wrap with 1 cm overlap permits roughly 14 cm circumference, or a circular cross section up to about 4.5 cm diameter. At 19 cm length that idealized cylinder is about 296 ml. Actual wrappers, seam overlap, compression and uneven fillings reduce usable capacity. The rice is about half a US cup (roughly 120 ml before compression) per roll. Spicy filling adds roughly 65–75 ml, Philly roughly 110 ml, and New York roughly 140 ml including its one-quarter flake volume. These rough totals imply outer diameters around 3.6, 3.9 and 4.2 cm respectively. New York has the least spare capacity and must be tested first. These approximations use ingredient masses/volumes and simple geometry, not measured rolled volumes or proof of optimal nori texture.

A full sheet would add wrap room but spread the same rice more thinly or create more nori overlap. An inside-out conversion would move rice outside the wrapper but change the source method. The three-quarter format is a documented general nori option in the primary reference; our exact dimensions/portions are editorial development. If physical trials show awkward closure or excess nori layering, revise the dimension and/or count with a measured test record rather than claiming the current paper screen guarantees success.

## Ingredient conservation and deliberate changes

- All keep approximately eight ounces tuna as 230 g edible yellowfin and their two cooked rice cups as 320 g. The rice mass is practical portion control from the primary rolling reference, not an assertion every cup universally weighs 160 g.
- All preserve the nori-outside identity; wrapper use increases from two full-sheet equivalents to three, purchased as four full sheets and trimmed. This is the major shared formula/geometry adaptation.
- Spicy retains one tablespoon mayonnaise, one teaspoon sriracha, two scallions and the original one drop sesame oil. A proposed 1/8 tsp oil increase was rejected after independent challenge; easier measurement was insufficient justification for an untested aromatic increase.
- Philly retains approximately four ounces cheese as 115 g and the full tablespoon sesame. Cucumber is standardized at 100 g trimmed. Sesame moves inside onto rice for adhesion; it is not discarded or held on dry nori by an unexplained trick.
- New York keeps one-third cup prepared flakes, standardizes avocado at 150 g flesh and cucumber at 100 g trimmed, and uses purchased flakes so no hidden frying oil/time remains. Flake grams are product-dependent; the checked manufacturer labels a tablespoon at 10 g, which is far denser than an assumed “one-third cup is fifteen grams.” Preserve measured volume and weigh it in the kitchen rather than invent a conversion.
- Hand-dipping water is measured at 120 ml with one teaspoon vinegar, a nonstick aid rather than an edible sauce allocation or food-safety treatment. No unlisted soy/mayo drizzle is added at service.

## Portion and timing consequences

All make four rolls / 32 pieces. With about 320 g cooked rice and 230 g fish total, the spicy batch is two light main portions or four starters. The cheese and avocado versions are more substantial but still describe two main portions or four starters, not a complete four-person dinner. No meal-count claim is derived from piece count alone.

Default time includes the actual shari preparation: approximately 115 minutes spicy and 120 minutes Philly/New York. About 40–45 minutes is the prepared-rice rolling workflow, not dry-rice cooking plus service. Rice waits accommodate clean station and vegetable preparation; raw fish is cut near rolling and held in the refrigerator in individual portions. A novice, slower cooker, reheated rice or frozen tuna can add time. New York is explicitly cut and served in rounds as rolls are completed to limit crunch loss.
