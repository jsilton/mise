# Chicken Marsala: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Original 1/4-inch thighs with 6–8-minute searing and no final temperature risked dry edges or an unverified center. Standardized roughly 1/2-inch thickness, two batches, and finishing to 165°F in the sauce before butter.
- Reduced oil from 1/4 cup to 2 tbsp and prosciutto from 113 g to 57 g; retained 680 g chicken, 225 g fresh mushrooms, 180 ml wine, 180 ml stock, and 28 g butter. Oil is divided across batches; stock is unsalted and final salt conditional.
- Removed fresh/dried porcini ambiguity and supplied a measured parsley finish. Flour is a dredging supply, not assumed fully consumed.
- Corrected unrealistic wine-reduction timing and increased total from 45 to 50 minutes, including two batches and mushroom browning. Sauce cooking precedes the off-heat butter finish.
- Removed duplicated frontmatter printed in the article, invented historical origin story, restrictive Marsala purchasing claims, and unverified nutrition. Wine reduction is not called alcohol removal.

## Relationships and composition

Choose one starch (mashed potatoes or egg pasta) and a green vegetable. Mushroom-wine pan sauce makes this distinct from breaded Milanese and baked Parmesan, despite shared chicken. No true duplicate identified in the reviewed group.

## Kitchen-test questions

Test thigh thickness, flour adhesion, wine reduction time, 50-minute workflow, and final sauce volume with two searing batches. Taste salt against different prosciutto and Marsala brands. Test breast substitution separately.
