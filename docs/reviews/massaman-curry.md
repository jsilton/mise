# Massaman Curry: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test or measured yield.

Challenged the saved proposal against the complete current recipe, its creation/history patch and the saved source comparison. The source began as uncredited Markdown; no author or family attribution was recovered. External massaman recipes are technique comparisons, not the origin of this recipe. Removed broad historical/authenticity claims and kept its Thai massaman identity, full-fat coconut milk, whole peanuts, potatoes, onion and long-cooked chuck or pork shoulder.

Preserved 1 1/2 pounds meat; 2 tablespoons oil; 3 tablespoons paste; the 14-ounce can of coconut milk; 1 cup stock; 2 medium potatoes and 1 large onion; 1/2 cup peanuts; 3 garlic cloves and 1 tablespoon ginger; 2 tablespoons fish sauce; 1 tablespoon each starting sugar and tamarind; 1 teaspoon whole cardamom pods and 3–4 dried chiles, both optional. Rejected the earlier proposal's 400 g potatoes, 250 g onion, four-pod replacement and 400 ml can substitution: none was an established equivalent. The original produce counts remain a variable to measure during kitchen testing, not disguised as exact weights. Green cardamom specifies the warming rather than smoky species; pods are measured whole before cracking. Unsalted or lightly salted peanuts remain available; final fish sauce depends on their salt contribution too.

All quantities have destinations: two-thirds of the oil divided across beef browning rounds, the rest for paste/spices; garlic/ginger then coconut milk and stock; half fish sauce in the braise, remainder conditional at finishing; beef and plate juices return; potatoes/onion enter when beef is nearly tender; peanuts/sugar/tamarind finish the sauce; whole pods/chiles removed; cilantro/lime served. Hot water is a conditional braising correction, not an invented default two-cup addition. Optional final sugar/acid adjustments remain taste-led rather than new mandatory formula amounts.

Stir the coconut milk and fry paste in the existing measured oil. A cream cap or visible oil separation is unnecessary; stabilized coconut milk must not be boiled dry waiting for it to split. Tamarind is specified as unsweetened, pourable Thai-style pulp-and-water purée, not a thick highly concentrated product. Lime is a sharper alternative added off heat, beginning at half the tamarind amount and adjusted by taste, not a asserted equal acidity conversion. Pork shoulder remains a long-braising alternative with the same cube size and tenderness endpoint. The incomplete chicken/mushroom substitutions are removed; neither is an equivalent timing/vegetarian conversion of this fish-sauce-and-stock formula.

The main defect was the claimed 15–20-minute chuck braise and 50-minute total. The proposed planning window is now 3–4 hours: 25 minutes preparation; about 20–25 minutes browning and sauce; 90–150 minutes gentle covered beef braise with initial checks at 75; 20–30 minutes vegetables; finishing/reduction and 5 minutes rest. Vegetables can be prepared during the late braise. Tougher beef takes longer. A fork entering beef with slight resistance triggers potatoes; final beef and potato tenderness govern finish. Minimum 145°F beef endpoint and subsequent rest are separate from tenderizing; rest is five minutes. A heavy 4-quart pot is a working choice with headroom required, not measured fit. Smaller/larger batches need suitable vessel geometry, more browning rounds and unchanged cube size, not multiplied cook time. Method quantities use fractions of scaled ingredients.

Four portions retain the source serving count with rice prepared separately. Removed the earlier 6–8-cup yield and 1 1/2–2-cup serving estimates entirely. Read the current Basmati and Coconut Jasmine Rice recipes: plain basmati is a useful default; coconut rice adds richness. No direct meal references were found. Compared current Thai Red Chicken Curry, Panang Curry with Chicken and this batch's red-beef recipe: different protein geometry, paste dose, ground versus whole peanuts, potato/tamarind versus basil/lime, and light versus full-fat sauce are material differences. No consolidation justified. No pending neighbor or meal is certified here.

Evidence: directly read [Bee Yinn Low's massaman](https://rasamalaysia.com/beef-massaman-curry-recipe/), whose beef-first/potatoes-later braise supports sequence but not this formula or exact clock. Do not adopt its erroneous 1.5 lb/750 g equivalence. [Pailin's tamarind article](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tamarind-everything-you-need-to-know/) and [coconut article](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/coconut-milk-101/) were available as primary indexed evidence; direct opens returned 402. They support product distinctions, not taste certification. Shared directly read FoodSafety.gov [cooling](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety), [cold storage](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts) and [temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures) support prompt shallow cooling, 3–4 refrigerated days and 165°F reheating. Removed arbitrary frozen-quality deadline. Legacy nutrition removed without replacement.

Kitchen trial priorities: exact meat/trim, actual medium-potato and large-onion weights, cardamom measure, paste salt/heat, tamarind product strength, pan width/lid, water added, time until beef accepts potatoes, final vegetable integrity, finished quantity, four-portion satisfaction and reheating. Confirm actual active/elapsed time and half/double vessel behavior. These remain physical questions, not passed assertions.
