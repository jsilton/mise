## Source and cultural correction

The original Chef's Note is wrong about its own method: it says yogurt-marinated chicken, fast tenderizing enzymes, and fresh chiles. The chicken never enters yogurt, and the sauce contains cayenne and dried pepper flakes. Removed all three assertions instead of modifying the actual recipe to make the bad paragraph true. The exact original recipe matches a Food & Wine preparation credited to Jimmy Bannos Jr., recovered through the publisher's syndicated AOL listing. The first-party Food & Wine URL and a named adaptation credit are proposed; the source-access limitations are recorded below.

A broad claim about Central Asian and Middle Eastern dairy traditions cannot establish this particular contemporary recipe's regional origin. The proposal names its chef and uses Mediterranean-inspired as a descriptive adaptation label, without claiming an authentic country-specific dish or an invented family history. Sweet paprika remains the pepper type; it is not automatically smoked paprika or a source of added sugar. The display title exposes paprika and preserves the separate chile-yogurt sauce.

## Ratio, yield and ingredient accounting

Retained 790 g net boneless skin-on thigh meat for four substantial main portions, approximately 198 g raw meat/skin each. The old eight-thigh/three-pieces assumption was fragile because boneless skin-on thighs vary in size and are often unavailable as a supermarket cut. The proposal asks for the correct butcher cut or home deboning, with weight measured afterward and extra deboning labor excluded explicitly from the standard time. Skinless thighs are an honest convenience substitution with a different surface result, not silently used while promising crisp skin.

Cut approximately twenty-four similar 1 1/2-inch pieces and distribute across eight metal skewers, about three pieces per skewer and two skewers per diner. Similar thickness and exposed skin matter more than forcing every thigh into three pieces. The source's single rigid thigh count is not retained as a yield law. Actual cooked yield and diner satisfaction require measurement.

Chicken coating oil falls from eight tablespoons to two, while its two tablespoons sweet paprika stay. This should make a coating instead of leaving a large oily bath beneath skin that will render more fat; it is an editorial change motivated by coverage and flare-up control, not a measured minimum or fat-absorption result. Sauce oil stays one tablespoon. A separate teaspoon neutral oil accounts for grate preparation, with any raw-contact oily residue discarded. No oil spray or extra grilling fat is hidden.

Chicken gets six grams fine salt, about 0.76% of its raw net weight, plus half of the listed half-teaspoon pepper. The sauce gets a separate quarter-teaspoon fine salt and remaining pepper, so the cook does not arbitrarily re-season the skewers outside after already salting the coating. Greek yogurt is one cup/240 g, with one tablespoon each lemon juice and olive oil. Sauce yield is roughly a cup plus those additions; a quarter-cup per diner leaves a small extra amount rather than pretending every gram vanishes. The sauce is an integrated component, so another full sauce such as toum is not automatically needed.

Cayenne starts at a quarter-teaspoon with another quarter available to taste; quarter-teaspoon red pepper flakes remain. This is a modest reduction of default heat while retaining the source's two dried-chile forms. The mild option reserves a plain-yogurt portion before chiles, using clean utensils. Lemon wedges have an explicit half-lemon quantity. Removed inherited nutrition, including the implausible seven grams protein per serving for a chicken main. Actual retained oil/coating and skin yield have not been analyzed.

## Practical sequence and equipment

Grill preparation begins first and overlaps prep/brief seasoning. Chicken is coated and held refrigerated for fifteen minutes while a completely separate sauce is mixed, correcting the room-temperature holding instruction without inflating a fifteen-minute seasoning interval into a mandatory overnight tenderizing process. Sauce can be prepared a day ahead. Raw-contact tools never enter the sauce bowl. Skewers keep the skin outside instead of rolled into wet interior layers.

The base grill timing assumes all eight skewers fit on the moderate direct zone without touching while leaving a cooler zone available. This cannot be assumed merely because a grill has eight slots across its total surface. Use four-skewer batches if needed and count the extra approximately fifteen minutes; hold the uncooked half cold. Gas and charcoal setup are both described, with charcoal allowed twenty-five to thirty minutes to establish. Those times are planning allowances dependent on actual equipment and conditions.

Moderate direct heat suits the skin fat and paprika coating better than an undefined very hot sear. Start skin toward the grate where geometry allows and turn every two to three minutes after release, moving to the cooler zone for flares or rapid darkening. Expected skin outcome is brown with some crisp edges, not guaranteed uniform crackling. A thicker folded piece cannot be declared cooked because the outside looks crisp.

Every skewer's largest chicken pieces are checked at 165°F / 74°C with the probe in meat, away from metal; unusually thick pieces get their own check. A clean platter/utensils receive cooked skewers. The five-minute final rest is included without claiming an exact juice-redistribution mechanism. Removing faster skewers and short uncovered service protect texture; no long crisp hold is promised.

The 55-minute total reflects approximately ten minutes initial cutting/coating, fifteen minutes refrigerated seasoning with roughly five minutes sauce work overlapped, ten minutes threading, fifteen minutes cooking and five minutes rest. Prep metadata is 25 minutes, cook is 15. Gas/charcoal preheat overlaps the front of that chain. Home deboning, slow fuel ignition, a second grill batch or simultaneous sides extends it. A twelve-inch heavy skillet gets two roughly 395 g unskewered batches, about twelve to fifteen minutes each, with skin rendering and burned-fat cleanup described. Seventy to eighty minutes overall is a conservative indoor allowance, not a tested reproduction. A small grill pan is not assigned the outdoor full-batch timing.

## Flavor balance, substitutions and relationships

The main has aromatic paprika, salty chicken and fatty skin, balanced by a tart, cool chile sauce and fresh lemon. Garlicky butter pearl couscous is a sensible soft starch under or beside this separately sauced main. Lemon/herb instant couscous offers a brighter alternative; they are different pasta types and are not both mandatory starches. One full four-side batch of either serves the four-person main. A vegetable side is still needed; fattoush adds vegetables and crisp bread but its full batch is generous and contains its own pita. An extra pita serving is optional rather than silently required.

Removed pending toum from this recipe's recommended list because a second garlic/oil sauce adds little necessary function to the already sauced main; its reciprocal historical link can remain a possible variation without this review certifying that sauce. This is not a directive to merge or delete toum. No separate sauce file was created for the small integrated yogurt sauce because it has only this measured use here.

Skinless chicken loses the skin texture but keeps the paprika/yogurt identity. Mild sauce retains lemon and salt without pretending extra yogurt neutralizes already-added chile; diluting makes additional sauce. A skillet gives different contact browning and batch handling. No boneless breast substitution with an identical timer is asserted.
