# Smashed Cucumber Salad (Pai Huang Gua): individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Duplicate comparison and decision

Read smashed-cucumber-salad and sesame-cucumber-salad in full. Both were Chinese-style smashed, salt-drained cucumber with garlic, soy, vinegar, sesame, and optional chili. Their names did not identify different dishes; cucumber sizes and dressing ratios varied without explanation. Retained the better-recognized, more frequently referenced smashed-cucumber-salad URL and consolidated the sesame URL as an alias.

## Findings and corrections

The original cucumber counts were not equivalent and made the salt/acid ratio unpredictable. Set 600 g for four sides. Named the salt and its fine-salt alternative; chose 2 tsp soy, 1.5 tbsp vinegar, 1 tsp sesame oil, and 1 tsp sugar for a restrained dressing. Kept scallion and a measured sesame garnish from the companion version. A brief drain now appears in the total time (25 minutes); removed the claim that salting guarantees permanent crispness and the contradictory four-hour marination instruction.

Removed conflicting unsupported regional exclusivity claims (Sichuan versus northern China). The primary Made With Lau recipe establishes an independently documented Chinese preparation and salt/drain technique, not the exact seasoning formula used here. A rolling pin avoids instructing the reader to strike a knife blade. Added label-aware vegan and gluten-free options; Chinkiang vinegar can be another wheat source besides soy.

## Relationships and composition

This is a fresh acidic side for roast pork, noodles, beef, or dumplings. It is not interchangeable with Japanese sunomono, Korean oi muchim, or pickled cucumber simply because all contain cucumber; those need separate considered comparison. Existing fusion-meal references remain possible, with cuisine context reviewed at meal level.
