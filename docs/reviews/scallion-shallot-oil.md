# Scallion-Shallot Oil with Crisp Alliums: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read entire original and all lo mein references. Original half-cup oil plus three shallots/six scallions was poorly specified for immersion and claimed one-third cup oil supplied four three-tablespoon dinners; that arithmetic is impossible. Set 240 ml initial oil, 100 g shallots and 50 g scallion whites/pale greens in a small deep saucepan, with expected but untested 180–210 ml recovery. This provides about four 45 ml lo mein pan-oil allocations near the lower end. The separate teaspoon chicken coating oil is explicit. Removed the smaller eight-minute shortcut whose two-tablespoon output could not meet a three-tablespoon recipe.

Even cuts, drying, late scallion addition and lifting early-finishing pieces address mixed-allium browning. Oil and solids are strained at pale gold, ginger discarded, solids salted and spread to cool. Fifty-minute total includes cooling; the condiment remains optional rather than a hidden lo mein requirement. Peanut oil was removed from a recipe previously labeled nut-free, and broad cuisine/low-density claims were narrowed.

The original two-week oil refrigeration and three-day room-temperature garnish claims lacked a validated moisture/acidification process. OSU Extension's guidance for other home flavored oils supplies the four-day refrigerated or frozen approach. It is not a test of this fried mixture's water activity; the review deliberately does not infer shelf stability from crispness or straining. Sarah Nevins's primary recipe supports cold-start frying and watching color, but its room-temperature/long storage advice is not adopted. Separate refrigerated solids may soften; same-day texture is the honest preference.

Test whether the specified pan/oil fully wets the measured alliums, staggered fry times, ginger effect, output volume and garnish weight, carryover color, four-day refrigerated quality and small frozen oil portions. No physical kitchen test or shelf-life study recorded.
