## Distinct complete bowl and formula choices

Keep it separate from the two miso dishes. Cubed pan-cooked fish coated with a garlic/soy/honey/sriracha reduction, plus edamame, creamy mayonnaise/avocado and cold cucumber, is a distinct full composition. It is not raw poke, and no regional authenticity claim is necessary. Call it a contemporary recipe adapted from its named author. Its richness stays: no arbitrary honey, mayonnaise or avocado cut.

- **680 g net skinless/boneless salmon** keeps current 1.5 lb and the upper archived four-by-six-ounce range. Each diner gets about 170 g raw fish allocation. Cubes are one inch, with thin tail pieces checked earlier; skin-on purchase weight is not interchangeable with edible net weight.
- Restore **3 tbsp reduced-sodium soy + 3 tbsp sauce water**, retain **2 tbsp honey + 2 tbsp sriracha + 2 tsp minced garlic**. The spooned liquid volume is roughly 150 ml before garlic/packing differences. This is a modest marinade and reduction for 680 g fish, not a large bowl of sauce. Restoring water changes initial concentration and controllability, not the final sodium amount if equally reduced. Reduced-sodium soy is an explicit source restoration; final salt and heat still vary by soy/sriracha brands and how far the sauce reduces. There is no claim that all brands share the same sodium or that reduction removes salt. A separate tablespoon correction water can loosen an over-reduced sauce in teaspoon additions; it is not automatically part of the marinade.
- Retain twenty minutes of refrigerated marination, not the original unexplained room-temperature rest. Drain fish over the bowl, recover loose garlic clumps with the liquid, and keep all raw-contact marinade for immediate cooking after the fish. This avoids silently discarding most of the defining sauce or confusing a raw reserve with a cold topping. Bring it to a full boil before using it; the one-minute boiling instruction is an editorial practical interval, while FDA specifies bringing reused marinade to a boil rather than this exact tested minute. Simmer only until spoon-coating, then fold fish in off heat.
- Add **four teaspoons neutral oil**, two per 340 g fish batch in a 12-inch skillet. This is a measured practical film replacing “a splash,” not a nutritional oil reduction. Adequacy depends on pan material and fish fat and needs cooking verification. Remove blackened deposits between batches and before sauce; slightly browned residue can remain. Fish is cooked through before removal, so final coating does not depend on an uncertain quick rewarm to finish raw centers.
- **120 g dry jasmine with 240 ml water** supplies roughly the original two cooked cups, a modest half-cup rice allocation per bowl. Mahatma's 45 g/quarter-cup label and 1.5:1 volumetric starting ratio ground this two-thirds-cup dry/one-cup water quantity. It is a deliberate approximate yield, not a promise that all rice products expand identically. Full bowls also contain fish, avocado, edamame and mayonnaise; the smaller rice quantity is preserved source composition, not generic carbohydrate restriction. Rice package guidance governs if it differs.
- Restore **300 g plain cucumber** as a practical measured equivalent for the original single cucumber, plus one large avocado. The later full dressed cucumber requirement adds soy, vinegar, garlic, sesame and potential chile crisp to an already glazed, mayonnaise-topped bowl. Plain cucumber preserves the source's fresh cooling role. The current linked salad is retained as a clearly measured **half-batch replacement**, 300 g cucumber for four, never additional to the plain cucumber. Its 25-minute process can require 5–10 extra dinner minutes; it does not disappear inside a twenty-minute marinade.
- Define edamame as **150 g frozen shelled beans**, approximately one cup after cooking, with a separate 750 ml cooking-water measure and package cooking endpoint. This is not 150 g pods, nor the full 450 g pod recipe. Keep **half a cup prepared sriracha mayonnaise**, two tablespoons per bowl, rather than an unmeasured drizzle. Purchased prepared mayo is an acceptable component, not an implicit new condiment recipe. A plain-mayo/sriracha alternative preserves total topping volume while disclosing variable heat. One teaspoon optional toasted sesame seeds accounts for the old unlisted garnish; red pepper flakes are not silently made mandatory.

## Pan behavior, complete timing and sensory endpoints

Use a 12-inch skillet, two 340 g batches with visible space and recovery between loads. Very hot syrupy marinade scorches easily; medium-high heat with adjustment is a practical starting point. Sear the first face two-to-three minutes and finish other faces over three-to-five minutes, checking actual center temperatures at **145°F/63°C** with a fine-tip probe. Remove smaller pieces individually. All six sides do not each need a three-minute cook. The browned faces will soften under sauce; no lasting crisp-crust promise remains. A pale wet pan needs smaller/drier loads, not a demand for violent smoke.

**60 minutes elapsed** is an editorial allowance for one cook: rice starts around minute zero; mixing/cutting/coating fish occupies the next ten-to-fifteen minutes; the twenty-minute refrigerated rest overlaps edamame heating/cooking, cucumber slicing and topping setup; fish starts around minute thirty-five, two batches finish around fifty-one, and sauce plus assembly reaches about sixty. Roughly 30–35 minutes hands-on is plausible. Frontmatter is twenty minutes prep, twenty-five minutes cooking, with the partly overlapping rest explained explicitly. Shorter fifty-five-minute service may work with efficient preparation, but heat recovery, thermometer checks and a dirty-pan correction argue for the sixty-minute default. A smaller skillet or cucumber-salad substitution extends it. Two burners suffice: rice and edamame first, then the vacated edamame burner can hold the fish skillet. All toppings are ready before the sauce finish.
