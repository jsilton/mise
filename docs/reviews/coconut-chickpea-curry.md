## Pot, method and full timing

Specify a **wide 4-quart saucepan or Dutch oven**, roughly 9–10 inches across. The batch occupies around 1.2–1.5 litres as it cooks, leaving useful headroom for stirring without a tiny pot's slow evaporation. A large broad shallow skillet could reduce too aggressively; a tall narrow pot may not thicken in the same window.

Keep the source's 10–12-minute onion/tomato stage, judged by softened onion and a thick moist base, not a guaranteed jam regardless of tomato treatment. Use medium heat and regular stirring. The garlic and ground spices get only 30–60 seconds before the drained chickpeas and coconut milk enter. The flour mixture is prepared before this step so the cook is not whisking it elsewhere while spices scorch. Warm the whole pot to a gentle simmer, then count 10–12 minutes uncovered. This is reheating already cooked beans and melding flavors, not cooking dry pulses. Do not boil hard merely to reduce a watery sauce; first give the base proper time and then use a modest extension if needed.

Restore **45 minutes** from the actual source:15 prep +about 1–2 heat +10–12 base +0.5–1 spice +3–5 heat recovery +10–12 simmer +1–2 finish =roughly 41–47 minutes. The 30-minute cook field is rounded rather than a guarantee in a narrow pot. Active work is about 25–30 minutes, with 15 prep plus cooking/stirring/checking/finish. Any extended reduction adds time. Rice or bread is separate, and incoming prepared beans do not erase their earlier production work.

The finishing lime is off heat and staged after texture correction. Final salt is adjusted after tasting because neither coconut milk nor a can label establishes the complete salt load. The sauce should remain spoonable, not set like paste. The finished bowl does not need another full-fat coconut rice by default to meet its identity.
