## Formula and amount decisions

- Preserve the coconut/almond/curry-powder identity, full-fat richness, original two tablespoons curry powder, teaspoon garam masala, two tablespoons ground almonds, two garlic cloves, and restored half cup peas. No extra frying oil, pre-fried paste, tomatoes, potatoes or chickpeas are imported.
- Define **300 ml coconut milk** from the archived three-quarters-can quantity and the author’s historical clarification on the live source page. On August 12, 2020, a reader asked what size can the three-quarters referred to; Arman replied that his canned coconut milk was 400 ml. Root located this exchange, and this reviewer directly reread it on the live page. Thus 3/4 × 400 ml = 300 ml is corroborated by the author, not merely an inferred common can size. This limited evidence does not validate the proposed vegetable weights, finished yield or revised cooking clock. Stir the whole can before measuring so the 300 ml includes both water and fat.
- Choose **900 g net cauliflower, 300 g net carrots, 150 g onion, 125 g trimmed beans and 75 g peas**. These resolve inherently variable heads/carrots/cups and create a substantial vegetable batch for six with starch. They are working weights, not newly discovered historical weights. Fresh vegetables only for the written baseline; frozen florets are not assigned an unsupported equivalent clock.
- The initial loose volume is plausibly around 12–15 cups: roughly 8–9 cups cut cauliflower plus carrots/onion. This fits the intended half-to-two-thirds range of a six-quart cooker, but packing and shape vary. The method requires actual fill and model checks and does not claim geometry was physically verified. Keep the batch fixed because a blindly multiplied or greatly reduced load changes depth/heating.
- The raw food totals roughly 1.9 kg before optional corrective water. Approximately 7–8 finished cups and six approximately 1 1/4-cup portions are estimates; vegetables soften/collapse and evaporation varies. A full batch offers roughly 258 g raw vegetables per diner, plus coconut/yogurt/almonds. Do not present invented nutritional totals or call this a high-protein main. Main role describes its use as the center of this vegetarian dinner, replacing the erroneous side/main disagreement with the meal.
- Replace unspecified **1 tbsp sea salt** with **1 tsp fine salt initially plus up to 1/2 tsp only after tasting**. This is an intentional seasoning reduction, not a kosher-to-fine conversion. The original could represent roughly 12–18 g depending on salt, whereas the proposed initial amount is roughly 6 g for about 1.9 kg food, with a reserve to about 9 g. Curry powder must be salt-free so that adjustment is meaningful. This low-risk correction is justified by the large, uncertain original salt load, not by legacy nutrition.
- Optional pepper flakes return as **1/4 tsp**, a deliberately mild start instead of the archive's optional full tablespoon (a twelvefold reduction). The main is aromatic rather than hot; more can be offered separately to diners. Optional heat is not claimed to reproduce the original maximum. No default mandatory new condiment.
- Keep the already-present **60 g / 1/4 cup yogurt** and finally use it: off heat, warmed with a little sauce, then folded in. This is a real completion of a later addition, not recovered Arman source behavior. It supplies tang without adding an unlisted cream finish or cutting coconut fat. Plain whole-milk unstrained yogurt keeps the measure meaningful.
- Ground almonds supply nut flavor, fat/protein/fiber and fine particles; they are not described as a starch slurry or a guarantee against splitting. Meal versus finer blanched flour texture is explained. The original quantity is preserved rather than increased to force an untested set.
- A separate **60 ml maximum corrective hot water** is conditional, not automatically added. Extra evaporation/reduction may be needed in an unusually watery batch; lifting vegetables and reducing only sauce avoids destroying all the florets. This is troubleshooting with additional time/pan, not the baseline recipe.

## Method, texture and timing

Carrots are thin 6 mm half-moons at the bottom; cauliflower is approximately 4 cm, neither tiny rice-like fragments nor huge dense stems. Their different structural tenderness is explicitly checked. Beans are added once the slower vegetables are nearly tender and cook about 45–60 minutes. Peas enter for the last 10–15 minutes (inside that bean period), with package instructions overriding the estimate. Warm cannot cook the late vegetables.

The archive's five-hour High schedule supports the upper planning window but not a firm texture promise. Start checking at three hours. The manufacturer cauliflower-soup recipe independently uses three to four hours High before **pureeing**, supporting caution about very soft cauliflower; its much wetter soup is not evidence that this exact batch finishes at three hours. No physical validation is claimed for this model-independent estimate. A slow cooker, thick carrots, fill or frequent lid opening can extend it.

The ordinary path is 25 minutes preparation, roughly 3 3/4–5 hours High including late vegetables, then five minutes off heat/finish. Header 5 h 30 min is an upper planning allowance, not a deadline to keep already-tender vegetables cooking. About 35–45 minutes is active: vegetable prep/sauce 25, staged additions and two or three checks 5–10, finishing/tasting/serving 5–10. Optional sauce reduction adds another 5–10 or more. No automatic Low eight-hour alternative is offered: the original is recorded, but the revised staggered method is written and budgeted for High so it has one actionable schedule rather than an unexamined conversion.

Desired texture is **soft carrots and fork-tender cauliflower**, with some floret edges contributing body, not crisp five-hour vegetables. All contents remain in the curry; no soluble vegetable flavor is routinely discarded. The one-pot convenience remains; a second pan appears only as an explicit rescue for excess liquid.

Yogurt is added only for immediate service. If holding before serving, leave it separate and keep fully cooked curry at least 140°F. Aim for an hour or less for quality; a safe hot hold still softens vegetables. No sensory cue is presented as proof of safe holding, and an off cooker is not treated as indefinitely safe.
