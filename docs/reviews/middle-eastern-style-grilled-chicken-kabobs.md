# Yogurt-Marinated Chicken and Onion Kabobs: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test recorded. Proposal for `src/content/recipes/middle-eastern-style-grilled-chicken-kabobs.md`. Applied after root evaluation and independent challenge; final decisions below supersede initial proposal choices.

## Individual comparison and identity decision

Read both complete original kebab recipes, their proposed revisions, reviewed souvlaki, shawarma-style chicken and Vietnamese lemongrass chicken, plus the complete two couscous preparations, warm pita and fattoush. The key distinction is where the dairy goes. Here, skinless chicken and onion are cooked after an eight-hour garlic/lemon/yogurt marinade; the paprika skin-on kebab puts no yogurt on the meat and includes a separate chile-yogurt serving sauce. The cuts, aromatics, skin treatment, vegetable component, timing and sauce accounting differ enough to retain both. Similar skewers and common paprika do not make them duplicates. No alias or deletion is proposed.

The neighbor comparison supports technique decisions without treating unrelated flavor systems as interchangeable. Souvlaki is lemon/oregano with a much shorter seasoning period; its cut-size and thermometer lessons are relevant, but not a reason to import the same marination limit into this source's dairy-rich formula. Shawarma roasts whole thighs then slices/sears them and includes tahini, so it is structurally different. Vietnamese chicken uses fish sauce, sugar and lemongrass with flat whole-thigh pan/grill cooking; it informed the need to reduce heat as a coating browns, not the flavor ratio or a claim that yogurt and sugar behave identically.

## Quantities, geometry and portions

Retained 1130 g chicken for six portions, now explicitly boneless skinless thighs; the old unqualified thigh wording could lead to bone-in or skin-on shopping. This is about 188 g raw boneless meat per diner, with roughly 40 g onion, before cooking losses. Twelve skewers allow two per portion with about three chicken pieces each, avoiding six enormous sticks being treated as interchangeable with a small-home-grill load. Approximately thirty-six 1 1/2-inch pieces is a cutting guide, not an assertion that all thigh geometry is cubical or that every piece weighs identically.

Defined onion as 250 g peeled weight. One-inch squares are separated into one or two thin layers. Thick onion chunks can char outside while staying raw and can hold meat apart in dense insulated stacks; thin layers retain the alternating chicken/onion presentation with a more plausible shared cook time. The outcome honestly allows a little onion bite instead of promising long-roasted sweetness. Onion is coated with the chicken during the marinade so its seasoning is accounted for. It is not a separate fully cooked vegetable side.

The formula retains one cup/240 g whole-milk Greek yogurt, two tablespoons olive oil, two tablespoons lemon juice, five garlic cloves, two teaspoons sweet paprika, half-teaspoon cumin and an eighth-teaspoon cinnamon. Yogurt is approximately 21% of chicken weight; oil and juice are each roughly 2.5–2.7% by weight. This is enough for a coating, not a claimed immersion brine. The initial proposal reduced zest to two teaspoons; independent challenge and root evaluation rejected that unsupported flavor change. The final recipe retains the original tablespoon, now explicitly finely grated. Default pepper flakes are half a teaspoon, with the other half optional, matching the source's less-hot choice.

Ten grams fine salt is roughly 0.88% of raw chicken or around 0.6% of the combined chicken/onion/dairy mixture before other ingredients. Some marinade is discarded, so these are formulation ratios rather than absorbed-seasoning estimates. Black pepper is half a teaspoon. Added the missing cooking oil, parsley and half-lemon wedges to the ingredient list. All yogurt in the main is raw-contact marinade and is discarded after coating; no ingredient remains invisibly available to make a clean dip. The skillet alternative explicitly changes grate oil from one teaspoon to one tablespoon for three pan batches.

Removed inherited nutrition rather than recalculating from all marinade as though it were eaten. The quarter-cup differences in yogurt brands and meat trimming can change fat and water; actual retained marinade and cooked yield are untested. The main's gluten-free/egg-free labels follow the specified plain ingredients, while its dairy content remains clear. Adding couscous or pita creates a meal that is not gluten-free by default.

## Sequence, timing and capacity

The original fifty-minute total omitted an explicitly required eight-hour marinade and a five-minute rest. The new default is nine hours fifteen minutes: thirty-five minutes preparation split before/after an eight-hour refrigerated marinade, thirty-five minutes for two grilling rounds and changing batches, and five minutes final rest. An eight-to-twenty-four-hour window bounds the overnight option within the raw chicken's existing cold-storage limit. Preheating overlaps the final part of marination, with roughly fifteen minutes for gas or twenty-five to thirty for established charcoal. These are editorial planning allowances, not observed test results.

A small home grill cannot be assumed to hold twelve loaded skewers while maintaining an escape area. The default cooks six at a time and holds the uncooked half refrigerated. A larger grill can do all twelve in about fifteen to twenty minutes only if the actual direct zone fits them without touching and leaves its cooler zone available, reducing the full schedule by roughly fifteen to twenty minutes. Six-skewer capacity itself still needs checking on the user's equipment; smaller grills require more rounds. The instructions do not multiply the recipe's timer with ingredient scaling.

Excess marinade drains away rather than being thickly smeared onto the hot grate. Clean lightly oiled grates, a set surface before turning and a broad spatula address the source-acknowledged sticking. Golden/brown patches are the desired cue; the yogurt does not form a guaranteed crisp shell, and blackened garlic/paprika/dairy are not desirable char. The cook turns about every three minutes, closes the lid between turns and uses the cooler zone if the coating gets ahead of the center.

Temperature is measured from the side in the largest pieces of every skewer, away from the metal. Each checked center must reach 165°F / 74°C, with separate checks for odd thick pieces. The chicken is not pulled at 160°F and assumed to finish during the rest. A clean platter and cooked-food utensils receive the first round, and its short loose cover sacrifices some surface texture while round two cooks. This is a disclosed service compromise, not a claim of identical just-grilled texture across batches. A long or delayed service needs a separate measured holding plan rather than leaving the platter out indefinitely.

The indoor option uses a twelve-inch heavy skillet, unskewered chicken/onion and three batches of roughly 375 g chicken plus one-third of the onion. One teaspoon of the alternative tablespoon oil is used per batch; burned deposits are removed between rounds. Twelve to fifteen minutes per round plus pan recovery/cleanup and handling plausibly adds twenty to thirty minutes to the default two-round outdoor schedule. A grill pan can require smaller batches because ridges and walls reduce usable contact area; it does not inherit the whole outdoor-grill timing. This option is explicitly untested and may have more sticking and a softer coating.

## Composition and substitutions

The six main portions need a genuine vegetable side; roughly 40 g raw onion each is seasoning, not the entire vegetable course. One-and-a-half four-side batches of reviewed Lemon and Herb Instant Couscous starts with 375 g dry couscous and serves six. Six warmed pita breads are an alternative starch, with their own warming time. Fattoush can provide the vegetables plus some crisp bread; consider its included pita before automatically adding a full bread/starch portion on top. No composed-meal review or fast whole-dinner time is claimed.

The main has tang, garlic and warm spice, so a separate toum or yogurt sauce is optional additional food rather than a missing mandatory component. Removed pending toum from the recommended list; this does not change or certify its reciprocal link. Garlic-butter pearl couscous remains a possible alternative but the bright herb couscous and vegetable salad form a clearer default plate. The two couscous recipes differ in product and cooking method; no same-measure substitution is suggested.

Low-fat Greek yogurt is an equal-weight convenience option with less richness and possible thickness variation, not a guaranteed identical marinade. Yellow onion retains geometry with a flavor/color change. No equal-time breast option is offered because smaller lean pieces and a prolonged dairy/lemon treatment need their own practical schedule. Skin-on chicken is also not silently substituted; it creates the other recipe's fat-management and surface questions.
