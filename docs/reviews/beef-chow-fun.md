## Ratio and ingredient decisions

- **450 g net trimmed flank + 680 g fresh noodles + four servings** restores the archived 225:340 beef/noodle ratio while preserving the current full pound of beef. The current 450:450 ratio supplied only about 112 g hydrated noodles per diner; increasing noodles deliberately makes the broad ribbons a substantial part of four moderate main portions. This is not an unannounced calorie cut or a literal unchanged original yield. A portion receives roughly 112 g raw beef allocation, 170 g fresh noodles, 30 g sprouts and 15 g scallions. Cooked total yield needs measuring; hungry diners may want the existing vegetable side.
- Retain **1/4 tsp baking soda for 450 g beef**, approximately 1.1–1.2 g or about 0.25% by meat mass. This is lower per gram than the old 225 g batch and falls within Bill Leung's small-dose no-rinse guidance. The quantity is an editorial choice supported by that technique reference. Add two tablespoons water for distribution/hydration and restore a teaspoon coating oil. Increase starch from one to two teaspoons for the pound of beef; keep current tablespoon each soy and wine in the coating. The twenty-minute refrigerator rest stays from the current recipe, with remaining preparation overlapping it. This duration is an editorial starting choice: the referenced Woks of Life approach and America’s Test Kitchen demonstration use different treatment/rest schedules, and neither directly tests this exact 450 g/quarter-teaspoon/twenty-minute combination. Retain that uncertainty rather than attributing an exact twenty-minute rule to either source. Do not double the soda simply because the archived meat weight doubled; do not make an unsupported “longer is always better” claim.
- **4 tbsp cooking oil** is explicit: 1 tsp for sprouts, 1 1/2 tsp for each of two beef batches (3 tsp), and 2 tsp for each of four final noodle batches (8 tsp), totaling 12 tsp. The separate teaspoon coating oil is additional. A literal doubled archive would use six tablespoons cooking oil plus two teaspoons coating oil; this proposal uses a fresh measured film for each smaller home-pan operation, with fresh noodles often carrying some oil from manufacture, depending on the product. Four tablespoons is a practical starting formula rather than an attempt to reproduce professional free-pouring or a low-fat target. Its adequacy must be tested on the selected pan/noodle brand; retain richness and do not claim a grease-free plate.
- Keep the current **2 tbsp regular soy + 1 tbsp Chinese dark soy + 1 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp sesame oil** for the noodles. Restore two tablespoons finishing Shaoxing wine from the archived ingredient identity, separate from the tablespoon in the beef coating. The overall recipe is not an exact doubled archive: the current soy balance and modest sweetness remain, while added noodle mass should yield a thin flavorful coating rather than a sauce pool. Brand variation and salt balance need tasting. Do not substitute Thai sweet dark soy or kecap manis as though they were the same product.
- Define the original variable scallions as 60 g trimmed, separated into white/green portions. Keep a tablespoon ginger, finely julienned; use 120 g drained mung bean sprouts as a practical expression of the current 4 oz. No onion, oyster sauce, chile or new garnish is silently added. One tablespoon water cooks the sprouts; two tablespoons separate reserved water can loosen firm noodles. All ingredients have explicit destinations, including both soy and wine allocations.

## Noodle handling and household batches

Fresh ho fun is a cooked, hydrated product; its freshness and packaging determine whether it is already flexible or needs warming. A fixed thirty seconds for a whole refrigerator-cold block is unreliable. Follow package instructions; if microwaving is permitted, transfer to a suitable plate, loosely cover and work in short intervals until the ribbons peel apart without force. The method does not microwave arbitrary sealed plastic. Cut sheets to 3/4–1 inch widths. Divide into four portions before high-heat cooking.

The **250 g dried extra-wide alternative** is a chosen four-person quantity, not a literal 680 g fresh = 250 g dried conversion. Hydrated yield and texture vary. Package stir-fry preparation governs soak/boil method; the specific endpoint is flexible and just tender through the center, followed by thorough draining. The almost-cooked state matters because the finishing step is brief and has very little liquid. A generic overnight soak, a single boiling time, or adding brittle noodles to the sauce would not work across brands. If the product needs a longer soak than the prep window, elapsed time grows. The fresh-noodle identity remains the default.

Two 225 g beef batches fit a 12-inch heavy skillet or 14-inch wok. All beef reaches measured 145°F/63°C before removal, using a fine-tip probe sideways into representative thicker slices, and rests at least three minutes before serving. This avoids depending on an uncertain final rewarm to cook partly raw beef. Four final batches each contain 170 g fresh noodles plus a quarter of cooked ingredients, seasoning and juices; these are substantially smaller than dumping the whole kilo-plus family batch into one pan. A wide spatula lifts/folds, with short 15–20-second contacts rather than the original mandatory unattended one-minute char. Heavy smoke or bitter blackened sauce triggers lower heat and cleaning, not praise for wok hei.

Sprouts are separately cooked with the measured oil and a brief covered steam, then evaporation, until steaming hot and softened through their stems. This is an intentional change from a thirty-second just-wilted finish. Merely rinsing or warming sprouts is not the same as thorough cooking. Rewarming them in the noodle batches keeps the final cooking short enough to protect the noodles. No unnecessary public risk checklist is introduced.

## Timing and service

**65 minutes** allows approximately thirty minutes of initial slicing/coating and concurrent refrigerated twenty-minute rest/noodle and vegetable preparation, then about thirty-five minutes for pan warmup, sprouts, two beef batches, dividing cooked ingredients and four 3–4-minute noodle finishes. The quartered sauce includes the sesame oil and must be well mixed before division. All 170 g noodle batches, oil measures and sauce/vegetable shares sum to the complete formula. First portions can be served as they finish; a cook choosing simultaneous service must accept cooling and some softening during the final three rounds. Do not promise restaurant-fresh simultaneous texture from one small home pan.

Fresh noodles that take unusually long to separate, a long dried-noodle soak, optional freezer firming, side-dish cooking or slow pan recovery adds time. A timed trial may justify an adjustment; sixty-five minutes is an editorial allowance, not measured kitchen evidence.
