## Formula, process, timing and scale

Divide all the sugar before starting: two-fifths for the peaches, two-fifths for the dairy and one-fifth for the yolks. At the original size these are 1/2 cup, 1/2 cup and 1/4 cup.

Prepare the machine as its manufacturer requires, often the day before. Its maximum liquid fill is lower than its finished-ice-cream capacity; leave room for air and the reserved fruit.

Use a thermometer and shallow chilling containers. Keep waiting custard and fruit cold when churning more than one batch. Six servings describe portions, not a verified finished volume.

Plan about 9–11 hours with a ready machine: 30 minutes preparation, 30–60 minutes maceration overlapping custard work where possible, at least 4 hours chilling, manufacturer-directed churning and 4–6 hours hardening. About 30–40 minutes is hands-on before churning. Extra batches and freezer-bowl preparation add time.

Freeze promptly after churning and keep frozen until serving; repeated melting and refreezing damages texture. The publisher suggests 1–2 weeks for best texture, not a guaranteed safety deadline.
