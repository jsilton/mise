# Grandmommy’s Turkey Dressing: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review; no physical kitchen test. Initial proposal reasoning is retained below; the final root-evaluation section records corrections applied after independent challenge.

Read the complete existing recipe, CLAUDE.md, docs/RECIPE_STANDARD.md, Corn Bread Dressing, the currently reviewed Buttermilk Cornbread, and the currently reviewed Sausage, Kale and Ciabatta Stuffing, plus the latter's individual review record. This is a complete recipe comparison, not a keyword consolidation decision.

Keep the original slug and title. The named family attribution is material and is now also explicit in `source`; no place, date, person’s biography, inherited brand, or Thanksgiving anecdote is invented. Preserve the source's unsweetened self-rising cornmeal component plus plain flour, whole milk, full stick of butter, and all three eggs in the cornbread. All three eggs are cooked in the first bake; the final dressing has no raw egg binder. “Turkey” describes service, not an omitted raw turkey ingredient. Do not import an egg into the assembly just because other cornbread dressings use one.

The principal formula uncertainty is the original “1 cup Self-rising Corn Meal” plus “1 cup All-Purpose Flour.” Self-rising cornmeal mix is not plain cornmeal: Martha White’s primary product information confirms wheat flour, leaveners and salt in the mix. Specifying unsweetened self-rising white cornmeal mix, with Martha White as an example, is an editorial interpretation of an unnamed family ingredient, not proof of the original brand. The recipe keeps the additional plain flour without manufacturing an extra baking-powder dose. It may yield a more compact bread than a stand-alone cornbread formula; that is acceptable for coarse crumbs if cooked through. Exact rise and mix choice remain important family/kitchen confirmation questions. A rewrite to plain cornmeal with measured leavening would be a developed adaptation and should be named as such, not silently presented as Grandmommy's original. The linked Buttermilk Cornbread uses a different milk/leavener/sweetener/egg/fat system and is deliberately not substituted as the mandatory base here.

Quantified white bread at 180 g before drying, celery at 150 g and onion at 200 g. Kept the full cornbread batch, with its 650–750 g cooled yield clearly identified as an estimate. This puts total bread roughly at 830–930 g before white-bread drying, supporting twelve holiday side portions, not twelve independently validated measured servings. Stock now starts at 720 ml and rises only as needed to 1080 ml, against the original imprecise 2–3 cans of 14 oz each (roughly 830–1240 ml depending on the package). The entire stock used to tenderize vegetables counts within the total. The endpoint is a loose moist mound with no free liquid, not stock poured up to the top of the bread. The upper bound is a reserve, not a compulsory addition. Confirm the actual uptake in a kitchen.

Raw onion and celery originally went straight into a vague casserole, risking harsh onion and crunchy celery. Added a covered stock-simmer stage until tender, without diverting the cornbread butter or silently adding sauté fat. This changes the original raw-fold method and is disclosed in the Chef's Note. Added the omitted greasing butter as two teaspoons, divided between the defined 8-inch square metal bread pan and roughly 3-quart 9-by-13-inch dressing dish. Poultry seasoning starts at two teaspoons, rising to the original tablespoon only after tasting; specified salt-free blend and unsalted stock so salt can be assessed after the salted cornmeal mix is accounted for.

The original 120-minute total omitted complete cornbread cooling. The proposal allows 195 minutes from raw ingredients, including first bake, 45–60 minutes cooling, white-bread drying and vegetable cooking during that cooling, assembly, final baking and rest. “Stale” is replaced with a measured drying cue because old bread is not necessarily dry. The cornbread is cooled but not dried into croutons: the fine, soft mixed-bread texture is part of this preparation. The previous blanket guarantee of interior crunch from “grain structures” is removed. A 90-minute route assumes bread already baked/cooled and white bread dry; it is not the default total.

Specified the casserole center reaches 165°F / 74°C even though it bakes separately from the bird. Brown edges alone do not establish doneness. Wet/dry components stay separate for advance work; no refrigerated overnight hold of mixed unbaked dressing. Added shallow-container leftover cooling, refrigeration limits, three-to-four-day use and 165°F reheating. This follows USDA stuffing guidance, not an inference from the absence of raw eggs at assembly. A shared turkey cavity or alternative low-temperature oven is not silently authorized by this casserole method.

Removed unverified nutrition: the original 90-calorie, 2.5 g fat serving was not a trustworthy calculation for a recipe containing a stick of butter, three eggs and both breads. Changed flavor metadata from sweet to savory/herbaceous because the family formula adds no sweetener. Replaced biscuit/cornmeal-cobbler starch pairings with actual turkey and cranberry recipes. Cranberry provides tart contrast beside the dressing; no compulsory lemon addition alters the family seasoning.

Consolidation decision: retain as its own considered recipe. Corn Bread Dressing uses 700 g all-cornbread pieces, mushrooms, shallots, garlic and fresh herbs, dried to preserve some pieces; this recipe combines coarse cornbread crumbs with white bread, onion/celery and poultry seasoning for a soft texture, with explicit family attribution. Neither matches the ciabatta/milk/sausage/kale/Parmesan formula. Buttermilk Cornbread is a stand-alone lightly sweet bread and a possible component for the other dressing, not a duplicate of a stock-hydrated casserole. No aliases or removal are warranted.

Primary evidence and access: Martha White’s product page was read through current indexed content at https://www.marthawhite.com/product/white-corn-meal-mix/ ; its ingredient list establishes the nature of the commercial mix, not the family’s historical choice. Its direct recipe at https://www.marthawhite.com/recipes/old-south-herbed-cornbread-dressing/ was accessible and provides a related Southern cornbread/white-bread, cooked-aromatics, 9-by-13-inch precedent. Its different bread formula, egg binder and stock ratio are not claimed as validation of this family recipe. USDA FSIS https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/stuffing-and-food-safety returned 403 on direct open; its substantive indexed text was available, including separate baking, 165°F, no refrigerated mixed-uncooked hold, and leftover handling. FoodSafety.gov https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety was directly accessible for refrigeration temperatures and hot-weather limits. King Arthur’s https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/cornbread-recipe and leavener article were read for comparison, not adopted as a replacement family formula.

Kitchen-test questions: confirm the actual family cornmeal product and whether the extra plain flour is intentional; record level-cup mass, first-bake rise/crumb, cooked cornbread weight, cooling time and original flavor memory. Test 180 g white bread and 150/200 g vegetables against the family texture. Record exactly how much stock is absorbed, including pan liquid, and whether a 720 ml start is already too wet with a moist bread. Verify shallow-pan depth, final yield, 165°F timing and edges after the rest. Check whether the stock-simmer adaptation preserves the remembered aromatic character. Test separately refrigerated components as a distinct route. No source or arithmetic review substitutes for those results.

## Root evaluation and final integration

The root reviewer read both complete originals, proposals, linked cornbread and all review/challenge records. Both dressing identities are retained and no consolidation is warranted. The initial proposal above is the decision history; the corrections below describe the final published recipe.

Removed the mandatory three-cup hydration floor: the 1080 ml supply is a reserve and all additions, including vegetable cooking stock, are conditional on absorption. Removed an implied inherited white-cornmeal choice and made the unsweetened self-rising mix an explicit editorial assumption, preserving the original separate flour and three eggs. Asked the user for the family product and extra-flour confirmation; the working recipe discloses this unresolved provenance instead of inventing an answer. The 195-minute schedule is coherent and retained. Exact product, first-bake structure and stock uptake remain kitchen/family confirmation questions.
