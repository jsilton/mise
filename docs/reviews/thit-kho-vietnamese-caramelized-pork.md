# Thit Kho: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review; no physical kitchen test.

Read the complete source recipe, preserved pilot input and reference findings, the proposed rewrite and linked Vietnamese Thit Kho Dinner components. No named author or family attribution was present. Retain the Vietnamese caramel-pork identity without inventing a family history or ranking sugar types by authenticity.

Preserved 1.5 lb shoulder/belly, two shallots, four garlic cloves, two teaspoons initial fish sauce, three tablespoons brown/palm sugar, one cup coconut water/water, one tablespoon oil, two optional dried chilies, one teaspoon pepper, finishing fish sauce and the cilantro/scallion/lime garnish. Specify boneless shoulder or skinless belly so bone and skin do not silently change the stated cube geometry. Both cuts remain options; belly's additional fat is explained. The original three tablespoons additional fish sauce remains an optional maximum, not mandatory extra salt. All ingredients now have explicit method destinations. Removed inaccurate legacy nutrition.

Corrected a required two-minute crust claim and an amber-color test poorly suited to already-colored sugar. The method now separates caramel fragrance, tenderness and final reduction. Sugar cannot remove salt; dilute excessive concentration rather than adding sugar and continuing reduction. Start checking one-inch cubes at 45 minutes and allow roughly 60–90 minutes or longer as needed. The 2-hour-15-minute total is a planning allowance, including preparation, caramel, possible sauce reduction and rest, not a measured result. Heavy three-quart pot, liquid maintenance, a five-minute rest, temperature minimum and tenderness checks are explicit.

Ingredient scaling retains cut dimensions and uses leading quantities; fixed method quantities were removed where they would conflict with serving controls. Larger batches need a wider pot and liquid observation, not multiplied time. Exact evaporation, salt balance and finished yield remain kitchen-test questions. Palm sugar packing differs; no invented gram equivalence is supplied. Coconut milk is not presented as a direct replacement for coconut water.

Evidence read: [Andrea Nguyen's kho discussion and thin-sliced pork comparison](https://www.vietworldkitchen.com/blog/2019/03/red-boat-pork-belly-in-caramel-sauce-thit-kho-recipe.html) supports variation, sugar handling and water dilution; its thin slices cannot validate one-inch cube timing. [Nagi's cubed caramel pork](https://www.recipetineats.com/vietnamese-caramel-pork/) supports separating tenderness from evaporation and restoring water when necessary. Neither source validates this formula or proves its yield.
