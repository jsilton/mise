# Skillet Esquites (Mexican Corn Salad): individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Duplicate comparison and decision

Reviewed both mexican-street-corn-salad and esquites-mexican-street-corn-salad in full. Both used four cups of skillet-browned corn, cotija, lime, chile, and herbs. Differences in red onion versus scallion, optional crema versus mayonnaise/yogurt, and serving count did not establish distinct dishes or methods. Consolidated them here; the existing, widely referenced mexican-street-corn-salad URL is canonical. The longer alias redirects here.

## Findings and corrections

Both recipes understated the pan work for four cups of corn. Specified 600 g, a 12-inch skillet, two batches, and 30 minutes including preparation. Chose six side portions (roughly 100 g corn each) and a measured combined dressing of 2 tbsp mayonnaise plus 2 tbsp crema. Garlic, herbs, scallions, optional chile, and all finishing ingredients are explicitly accounted for. Added incremental lime and salt after salty cotija. Pure ancho is not interchangeable gram-for-gram with salty Tajín. Removed inaccurate grill/boil/no-cook tags and nutrition estimates inconsistent with ingredients.

Rick Bayless's account distinguishes brothy and fried esquites and sweet corn from Mexican field corn. Replaced the inaccurate universal-char narrative and the dismissive 'Mexico’s answer to coleslaw' comparison with a clearly named skillet variation. The source informs context; this is not presented as his tested recipe.

## Relationships and composition

Pairs with tinga, tacos, or carnitas as a corn side. It is not a green salad replacement merely because its name includes salad; meal composition should count its starch and rich dressing. A separate grilled corn-on-the-cob recipe would remain distinct.

## Kitchen-test questions

Verify two-batch browning and six-side yield with fresh versus frozen corn, dressing cling, cheese salinity, and yogurt substitution. The retained amount of fat and dairy is a considered starting point, not a tasting-certified optimum.
