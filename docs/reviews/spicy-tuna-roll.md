## Identity and source findings

Keep a spicy mayonnaise/chopped-tuna roll with nori outside, as the original method actually describes. It differs from the two strip-filled rolls and is not a duplicate simply because they share tuna/rice/nori. The initial source has no external attribution or corresponding pre-Astro HTML export. Remove its unsupported exact 1980s Los Angeles invention/offcut origin story; the primary Japanese-American recipe reference supports the broad style but not that exclusive claim. It uses an inside-out form with a different formula, so our nori-outside method is not presented as a copy of it.

The mayonnaise manufacturer supports egg-yolk-based Kewpie for the checked products; the proposal does not turn this into a claim that every Japanese mayonnaise, export product or plain-mayo substitution has identical ingredients. Raw tuna, egg and sesame need explicit recognition; unspecified sauces should not silently acquire allergen-free guarantees. No unverified nutrition remains.

## Proportions and geometry

Preserve the approximate eight ounces tuna as 230 g trimmed edible yellowfin, one tablespoon Japanese mayonnaise, one teaspoon sriracha and two scallions. Tuna is minced into small irregular pieces, not blended. Clean sauce can be tasted before fish contact; the original instruction to taste after mixing raw fish is unnecessary for judging heat. All sauce goes into the four evenly divided filling portions, about 58 g fish per roll plus dressing and scallion.

Preserve the original one drop sesame oil. Remove its absolute claim that precisely one drop is essential because the oil is so potent. A draft considered 1/8 tsp for easier spoon measurement, but independent review correctly identified this as a material untested increase; measurement convenience alone does not justify changing the recipe's aromatic intensity. A larger amount belongs in a documented taste comparison before changing the default.

Normalize two prepared rice cups to 320 g, 80 g per roll, using the primary hosomaki method's actual mass reference rather than treating every scoop as identical. The full master recipe makes considerably more; weigh only the allocation and refrigerate extra. No separate plain rice or second seasoning appears.

The original four half nori sheets, 80 g rice each, about 58 g fish plus sauce, and one-inch bare seam are a high-volume combination. The primary hosomaki reference uses about 80 g rice with a much smaller single tuna strip (its 7 oz fish allocation is for twelve rolls, approximately 17 g each). Its inside-out spicy reference can fit more fish because the rice is outside the nori; transferring those capacities directly to our nori-outside geometry would be a mistake.

Propose four three-quarter sheets, approximately 19 cm wide by 15 cm deep, cut from four full sushi sheets, with a one-centimeter bare seam. This preserves four rolls and the fish/rice amounts while expanding the wrapper. It is editorial geometry, not proven capacity. First-roll closure is explicitly checked before repeating. No rice dam is built: it adds bulk without establishing a reliable containment mechanism. Damp fingers, controlled tuck, pulling the mat out as rolling continues and gentle compression replace vague “tuck tightly.” Full sheets must be purchased; leftover dry strips are reserved, not lost in ingredient accounting.

Eight pieces per roll gives 32 pieces, replacing an indeterminate six-to-eight cut. Around 550–600 g total filling/rice plus nori yields two light main portions or four starter portions, not four complete main meals. The proposal keeps a main recipe role but states the practical portion context in the method. These weights are estimates pending finished yield measurement.

## Timing and handling

The total is about 115 minutes from dry rice: the master-shari 80-minute process plus approximately 35 minutes rolling/cutting, with clean setup, scallion cutting and clean mayonnaise mixing during the rice waits. About 40 minutes is plausible from freshly prepared barely warm rice; thawing and any refrigerated-rice reheating/cooling are extra. No body-temperature holding instruction remains. Fish is cut near rolling and unused filling portions stay refrigerated; this is not an extended room-temperature sushi buffet.

The supplier/species paragraph correctly distinguishes yellowfin's parasite exemption from universal raw safety, rejects label-only certainty and does not recommend using anonymous scraps or home freezing to repair uncertain handling. Earlier supplier limits and same-day service planning govern. FDA vulnerable-group advice is explicit without changing the raw roll into a new cooked recipe. Dipping water reduces sticking; it does not sanitize fingers, knives or raw equipment. Clean hands/utensils protect the rice allocation and shared station.
