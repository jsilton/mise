## Proportions and ingredient accounting

Preserve **500 g all-purpose flour, one standard cup/240 ml whole milk, four tablespoons whole-milk yogurt, two tablespoons dough oil plus one teaspoon coating oil, seven grams active dry yeast, two teaspoons sugar and one teaspoon baking powder**. Keep the optional three tablespoons butter and two garlic cloves from the site's original variation. This is a milk/yogurt/oil-enriched, yeast-and-powder dough; neither extra butter in the dough nor bread-flour substitution is required to improve the instructions.

Use ordinary unstrained plain yogurt. Milk and yogurt contribute roughly 270 g actual water against 500 g flour, around 54% effective hydration depending on dairy composition, rather than treating all dairy mass as water. This need not be the extremely sticky dough promised in the old prose. A separately measured **30 ml milk reserve** lets the cook correct a cracking/stiff dough gradually. It is optional, not a new automatic liquid dose. A separate **two-tablespoon dusting-flour reserve** replaces unbounded heavily floured kneading; adding flour until all tack disappears is a route to unnecessarily dry bread. Exact final hydration remains a kitchen observation.

Choose **7.5 g fine salt, about 1 1/4 teaspoons**, a deliberate 1.5% baker's percentage for a mildly seasoned bread with salted curries. The original two teaspoons unspecified kosher salt cannot be assigned a reliable mass. This is not a claimed density-equivalent conversion or a low-sodium reformulation. Seven grams yeast is 1.4% of flour, sugar about 1.6%, dough oil about 5.4%, baking powder around 0.8%. Keep both leaveners; baking powder does not authorize skipping the yeast rise.

Hydrate active yeast in the existing measured milk and sugar, rather than adding uncounted proofing water. The 110–115°F liquid range and equal-weight instant option are informed by the yeast producer; its exact water-based foam-volume test is not falsely copied onto a full cup of milk. Instant yeast can mix directly with flour using lukewarm milk. A lack of expansion needs troubleshooting, not a hotter oven.

All dough oil is incorporated at mixing; the separate teaspoon coats the rising ball. All optional garlic goes into the three tablespoons butter, warmed gently and shared across twelve breads, about three-quarters teaspoon butter per bread. No extra oil goes into the dry skillet. Dusting flour and corrective milk are bounded reserves, not unused mandatory ingredients. Raw dough must not be tasted to judge salt or doneness.

## Geometry, timing and actual serving

The baseline dough is approximately 860–865 g including coating oil, or about 72 g per piece; the rounded public 'about 70 g' is appropriate before variable bench flour/correction milk. Twelve roughly five-by-eight-inch ovals around 3 mm thick are plausible for that mass. Their real thickness and elasticity need physical observation. A twelve-inch cast-iron pan must have enough flat base for one oval. Do not enlarge individual breads when doubling: increase their number and cooking rounds.

The original ninety-minute total cannot include a ninety-minute rise plus mixing, kneading, rolling and twelve pan rounds. The revised header allows **three hours**, with an approximate **2 1/2–3-hour** window and **65–75 minutes active**. A representative upper schedule includes roughly 20–23 minutes milk warming/hydration/mixing/kneading, ninety minutes rising, about eight minutes dividing, ten minutes covered relaxation and 30–40 minutes rolling/cooking/handling. Pan preheating overlaps the final five minutes of ball rest; optional garlic butter is also prepared during that rest. This reaches roughly 170 minutes with room for ordinary transfers, not a claim that dough follows a fixed stopwatch. A cold room or repeated rolling rests can take longer.

Roll two breads at a time and take the skillet briefly off heat while preparing the next pair. This avoids either twelve drying raw ovals across the counter or an unrealistic demand that one cook roll dough while monitoring a thirty-second pan stage. Let the pan recover before each pair. The first thick center section should show fully cooked crumb, not merely a cooked thin edge. Brown bubbles plus a cooked center are the goal; broadly blackened surfaces are not a required tandoor simulation. Wrap finished breads promptly, accepting that early breads wait for later rounds.

Twelve breads are six two-bread portions or twelve small accompaniments, not twelve complete meals. Plain and garlic-butter versions remain distinct options. When a meal includes rice, one naan per diner is a useful starting allocation and avoids silently treating two full starch portions as mandatory.

## Storage, independent challenge and limits

Plain unfilled naan can be cooled and wrapped for the producer's stated two-day room-temperature window, or frozen. Garlic butter and brushed breads instead get prompt refrigeration, 40°F or below, and a 3–4-day leftover limit; keep curry-contact bread under its filling's handling rules. Reheating guidance distinguishes plain-bread flexibility from refrigerated garlic leftovers. No unverified nutrition fields are retained.
