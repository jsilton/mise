# Creamy Polenta: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review; no physical kitchen test.

Read the full recipe, its two-commit history, pilot reference/worker evidence and the Ratatouille Dinner relationship. No external author credit was present. Retain Italian polenta identity without unsupported claims about every instant product.

Preserved the full original formula: one cup coarse cornmeal, four cups water, one cup milk, one teaspoon kosher salt, two tablespoons butter, half a cup grated Parmesan and pepper. Extra loosening water/milk is explicitly an adjustable addition, not an unrecorded part of the measured starting liquid. Four portions are labelled generous sides; finished volume has not been measured. Salt density is unresolved for kitchen testing, not silently replaced with a brand-dependent weight.

Every ingredient reaches the method: water/milk/salt heat, cornmeal slowly whisked in, butter/cheese/pepper enrich off heat. Both tenderness and consistency now govern completion. Removed “pulls away from the sides” as a required endpoint because it conflicts with the intended soft, spreading texture. Removed unsupported universal no-shortcut and instant-polenta disparagement. Retained non-instant coarse meal as the specified product, with package differences acknowledged.

A heavy 3–4-quart saucepan gives working room for the initial five cups liquid and grain. Larger batches require more capacity/stirring; heat and time do not multiply. The new 60-minute planning estimate includes heating, two-minute dispersion, roughly 35–40 minutes cooking and finishing; actual coarse-grain timing may be longer. Added lump prevention, scorching recovery, separate stiff/gritty versus tender/wet recovery, prompt cooling and reheating a set mixture. Legacy nutrition removed.

Vegetarian suitability is now conditional on a vegetarian-labelled hard cheese rather than promised for every Parmesan. Milk-to-water substitution describes reduced richness while correctly retaining butter and cheese as dairy. No new dairy-free claim. There is no other standalone polenta in the collection; shrimp-and-grits is a composed seafood preparation and is not consolidated. Existing saucy chicken pairings use polenta as a base. Ratatouille Dinner remains pending and requires its own complete timing/component review.

Evidence: [Oldways](https://wholegrainscouncil.org/recipes/cooking-whole-grains) supports variable cornmeal cooking time and water needs, not this exact richness ratio; [FoodSafety.gov cooling](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) and [reheating](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures) support storage handling. The 3–4-day grain storage interval is from Oldways. All sources read directly; none certifies this recipe.

Kitchen test: grain size/product, actual liquid loss, tender-but-spreading endpoint, four-side yield, salt/cheese balance, reheated texture, half/double pan capacity and the 60-minute estimate.
