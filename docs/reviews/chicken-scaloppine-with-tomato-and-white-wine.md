# Chicken Scaloppine with Tomato & White Wine: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: editorial review; no physical kitchen test recorded.

## Findings and corrections

- Specified boneless skinless thighs, 900 g for six portions, and a 1/2-inch target. Original unspecified thighs could imply bones/skin incompatible with pounding.
- Added measured salt/pepper and divided the 3 tbsp oil across two or three batches. Retained 60 g dredging flour, 120 ml wine, 2 tbsp paste, and 14 g finishing butter; increased unsalted broth from 120 to 180 ml for six servings and evaporation.
- Moved spinach wilting onto heat before mounting butter. Original off-heat addition of an entire 255 g bag would cool and dilute the finished sauce.
- Added 165°F checks, clean plating, discard of raw-contact flour, and realistic 50-minute total instead of 35 minutes.
- Removed unsupported regional origin and metallic-taste absolutes, overconfident thigh claims, and unverified nutrition. Added an explicit alcohol-free alternative rather than implying wine boils away completely.

## Relationships and composition

A modest 150 g raw chicken per portion with spinach benefits from one starch. Removed redundant garlic bread beside pasta. Distinct from Marsala by tomato paste, spinach, and white wine, and from piccata by flour, thighs, and vegetable sauce. Retain as a separate variation in the thin-cutlet family.

## Kitchen-test questions

Test two versus three batches in a 12-inch skillet, flour uptake, six-portion sauce quantity, spinach water release, salt, and the 50-minute workflow.
