# Brined Roast Turkey, Inspired by Good Eats: individual editorial review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical kitchen test recorded.

Read the complete original, the dry-brined Simple Roast Turkey, the reviewed bone-in breast and Thanksgiving Feast. Wet-brined large whole turkey remains distinct from a smaller dry-brined/cider whole bird and a breast-only roast. The latter two are not merged by title. Simple Roast Turkey and the full holiday meal still need individual review. Attribution to Alton Brown's Good Eats remains, but the public title/text identify this as an editorial variation with a different roasting schedule rather than an exact reproduction.

The original unspecified cup of kosher salt and salted-stock variability made the two-gallon brine ambiguous. Defined 225 g salt for 7.6 L liquid, about 3% salt relative to liquid, with unsalted stock, 100 g sugar and measured aromatics. The salt amount is independent of crystal volume; flavor and internal distribution still require testing. Require a fully refrigerator-thawed 14–16 lb unenhanced bird. Kosher, injected and pre-brined birds are excluded from this extra brine. Make a one-liter concentrate, cool it before mixing with the remaining cold liquid, and verify 40°F / 4°C or below before adding poultry. Check refrigerator/container fit before starting. No plain-water top-up, counter brining, unmonitored cooler or raw-poultry sink rinse.

The twenty-four-hour plan includes brine cooling, twelve refrigerated brine hours, four uncovered refrigerated drying hours, hands-on preparation, 3 3/4–4 1/4 hours at 325°F and a thirty-minute rest. A fully frozen 14–16 lb bird needs approximately three to four additional refrigerator days, consistent with the directly accessed federal chart. Cold-water thawing is not offered as a route into overnight brining. Raw-storage time is not restarted by a marinade. The new steady oven schedule is explicitly different from the source's initial 500°F blast and unsupported 161°F pull/carryover guarantee.

Check both breasts and the innermost thigh/wing areas, clear of bone, to measured 165°F / 74°C before removal. Skin color and one breast probe are not a complete whole-bird check. Rest follows verified cooking. Oil, pan water/top-up water, apple/onion weights and herbs are all allocated; the cavity stays empty, with aromatics under the rack. Do not imply aromatic steam uniformly seasons the meat. Pan drippings need a salinity check before any separate gravy. Removed scientifically superior and simplistic osmosis rhetoric. Carving/shallow cooling and estimated twelve portions with sides replace the old generic rest and implausible nutrition.

Direct references: Alton Brown's Food Network recipe for attribution (https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/alton-brown/good-eats-roast-turkey-recipe4-1953681); his turkey selection/brining context (https://altonbrown.com/tag/good-eats-roast-turkey-2/); https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/meat-poultry-charts for the actual 325°F roast and thawing plan; FoodSafety.gov minimum endpoints and USDA poultry marinating/leftovers guidance. Original source content already existed in the repository; this review does not claim external testing of the redesigned brine or schedule.

The scaler is fixed for the specified whole bird, with an explanation visible on screen and paper. Halving servings must not imply half a turkey with unchanged geometry/time. The same equipment/geometry restriction was added to the already reviewed bone-in breast and pending smaller whole turkey; those changes do not create additional culinary reviews.

Kitchen questions: exact salt balance after twelve hours, brine capacity/contact, cold concentrate timing, four-hour skin drying, actual 325°F roast duration and breast/leg finish spread, drippings salinity, twelve-person yield and carve/refrigerate workflow. This is a substantial editorial redesign requiring physical testing, not a certified best-ever bird.
