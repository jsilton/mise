## Decision on the three-quarter-sheet geometry

A three-quarter nori sheet is a recognized format, not an invented size. The primary [Just One Cookbook maki guide](https://www.justonecookbook.com/sushi-rolls/) explicitly discusses half, three-quarter and full sheets. Its half-sheet example pairs 80 g rice with a small single filling; its tuna allocation works out to about 17 g per roll. Our originals require roughly 58 g tuna per roll before mayonnaise, cheese, avocado or cucumber. That comparison justifies questioning the inherited half-sheet and rice-ridge instructions. It does not physically validate the exact replacement.

Retaining approximately 19 × 15 cm wrappers is a defensible editorial choice for these particular nori-outside rolls, conditional on candid capacity limits and checking the first roll. I do not recommend switching automatically to full sheets while keeping every other instruction unchanged. Three-quarter versus full is a food/geometry tradeoff, not standard versus nonstandard.

A full 19 × 20 cm sheet with the same one-centimeter seam has about 361 cm² rice-covered area versus 266 cm² for the proposed sheet. The unchanged 80 g rice would be spread over 36% more area, making the nominal layer about 26% thinner. The extra wrapper may overlap more around the same filling. Full-sheet futomaki is a valid alternative style, but the [primary futomaki example](https://www.justonecookbook.com/futomaki/) uses much more rice per large roll. Its successful full-sheet form is not evidence that our 80 g allocation should be spread over a full sheet unchanged. Neither source proves which format these exact fillings will taste best in.

### Separate paper capacity checks

For a deliberately simplified circular cylinder with roll length 19 cm and one centimeter reserved for overlap, the maximum enclosed volume is `19 × C² / (4π)`, where C is the remaining wrapper depth. A half sheet with 9 cm available gives about 122 ml; the three-quarter sheet with 14 cm gives about 296 ml. Those are ideal geometric upper bounds, not practical usable capacities: a real seam, irregular filling, rice layer, nori folds and air spaces consume room.

To challenge the proposal conservatively, consider 80 g rice occupying approximately 118 ml before further compression (the recipe's approximate half-cup allocation), and use roughly 1 ml/g for the main filling ingredients only as an illustrative assumption:

| Roll            |                                      Illustrative volume per roll | Equivalent circular diameter | Implication                                                                                                          |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------: | ---------------------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spicy tuna      | Roughly 185–200 ml including a small seasoning/scallion allowance |             About 3.5–3.7 cm | Most room within the proposed wrapper; not equivalent to a tiny single-strip hosomaki.                               |
| Philly          |                      Roughly 230 ml before a small seed allowance |                 About 3.9 cm | Cheese/cucumber substantially increase the required space.                                                           |
| New York Crunch |         Roughly 260 ml, including 20 ml flakes before compression |                 About 4.2 cm | Closest to the three-quarter wrapper's ideal limit; avocado wedges, flakes and air spaces deserve the most scrutiny. |

These are scenarios, not measured food densities or predictions of finished diameter. Rice compaction can reduce volume, while irregular filling can increase it; therefore different assumed packing gives different results. The point is to screen the original half-sheet and identify the highest-risk draft, not to use attractive mathematics as proof of closure. The crunch roll needs a particularly careful first-roll check. Four rolls of different fillings do not acquire identical geometry merely from sharing 80 g rice.

## Concrete corrections raised

1. **Trim only the first wrapper before checking it.** All three first drafts cut all four sheets before their first closure check. Keep the other sheets full and dry until the first roll closes with its assigned portion. Exact approach: “Trim one sheet to 19 × 15 cm and make the first roll. Once its wrapper closes comfortably, trim the remaining sheets and repeat.” This gives the check practical value and avoids committing the entire batch before learning anything about its packing. The existing instruction to serve small removed excess separately is a reasonable failure recovery, but it must not be described as having successfully rolled the full original yield.
2. **Make the crunch service sequence agree with its cue.** The draft said each completed roll was cut for immediate service, but step 5 repeated all remaining rolls before step 6 cut anything. Specify cutting and serving each finished roll under step 6 before making the next for the best crunch. If serving a single finished platter instead, acknowledge that the first roll waits and loses some texture; do not promise lasting crispness.
3. **Do not normalize a drop of sesame oil into 1/8 tsp without recognizing the change.** The first spicy draft's approximately 0.6 ml is materially more than a literal drop, even though still small beside 230 g fish. Measurement convenience alone does not require an aromatic reformulation. The reviewer accepted retaining the original one-drop instruction; no additional oil increase is necessary to make the recipe reviewable.
4. **Name a rice cooker clearly.** Philly timing's “a slower cooker” is understandable in context but can read as a slow-cooker appliance. Use “a slower rice-cooker cycle.” This is wording, not a missing cooking method.

**Crunch:** The original tuna, avocado, cucumber and one-third cup tempura flakes remain recognizable. The new 150 g avocado and 100 g trimmed cucumber are explicit standardizations. The manufacturer page describes prepared flakes for sushi use; specifying ready-to-eat flakes removes hidden frying and uncooked-batter ambiguity. The potato-chip alternative changes flavor and salt and is labeled as such. Do not invent a gram equivalent for all tenkasu: different products pack differently. The actual Otafuku page lists a tablespoon as 10 g, illustrating why an assumed very light generic flake mass should not underlie a claimed finished weight.

The three rolls are meaningfully distinct: chopped spicy mayonnaise, firm tuna/cream cheese/cucumber, and tuna/avocado/cucumber/crunch. None is a duplicate merely because the wrapper, fish and rice repeat. Their original nori-outside behavior survives, while unsupported place/date invention stories and the unrelated roast-rest instruction are removed. No author or family provenance should be invented from a familiar restaurant name.

## Yield, time and cold service

The weighable rice requirement is 320 g for each whole recipe, split into four 80 g portions. The reviewed shari batch makes far more; its surplus is chilled promptly. The four-roll yield becomes 32 pieces by cutting each into eight, about 2.4 cm long if the roll spans 19 cm. Two main portions or four appetizer portions is more honest than four full dinners. Spicy is reasonably called a lighter main than the cheese/avocado versions. Those serving descriptions remain estimates of appetite, not nutrition analysis.

The 115-minute spicy and 120-minute cheese/crunch totals are plausible as eighty-minute rice preparation plus roughly 35–40 minutes final filling, portioning, rolling and cutting, with clean setup during rice waits. The ready-rice 40/45-minute versions are separate starting states. Fish thawing and reheating/cooling cold rice are additional. Four repeated refrigerator trips, first-roll assessment and cleaning take real time; a first attempt may exceed the allowance. No kitchen timing evidence exists.

Raw tuna remains at 40°F/4°C or below until needed and unused filling stays refrigerated. Cheese stays cold for both shape and handling. Vegetables and clean sauce are prepared before fish, rice is portioned with clean utensils, and vinegar hand-water is correctly not called a sanitizer. Do not let a knife or hand visibly contaminated with raw filling turn the dipping bowl into “clean” water for unused rice; replace it and clean hands/equipment as needed. The first completed rolls can wait briefly within the cumulative preparation/service limit, but the crunch version is best served progressively.

The species-specific supplier questions, yellowfin exemption limits, vacuum-pack removal before refrigerator thawing, susceptible-group advice and same-day raw service plan agree with the primary FDA guidance read during the preceding independent poke challenge. No vendor is certified; vinegar, domestic freezing and a freshness label do not create raw suitability. Extra rice has separate cooked-food storage; assembled raw rolls are not assigned a multi-day lunch shelf life.
