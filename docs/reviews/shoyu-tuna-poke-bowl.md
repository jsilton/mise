## Individual findings

The original calls itself child-friendly and requires “sushi-grade” tuna as if that label establishes raw suitability. It also claims exactly 10–15 minutes is required to avoid acid curing, despite using only a teaspoon of vinegar in a soy/oil dressing. Neither labeling nor the proposed timer is a validated safety process. Remove universal child-friendly and raw-for-child meal framing; do not silently substitute a different main. Explain the supplier conversation, specific yellowfin parasite exemption and its limits, refrigerator thawing/vacuum-package removal, and FDA vulnerable-group advice. Raw fish remains the recipe's deliberate identity.

The raw fish is now 450 g edible yellowfin, approximately the original pound. Selecting yellowfin resolves ambiguous “ahi”; bigeye should not be presented as a nutritionally interchangeable family default (FDA's mercury guidance distinguishes it). The original three bowls remain three generous bowls, not an unsupported fixed two-adults-and-one-child distribution. Each receives approximately 150 g raw fish, 1 1/3 cups cooked rice, 100 g cucumber, 50 g edible beans and a third of an avocado. This is a substantial bowl; no diet or low-calorie claim is justified.

The default dressing preserves 3 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tsp rice vinegar, 1/2 tsp ginger, 1/2 tsp sugar and one scallion. About 65 ml liquid plus aromatics coats 450 g fish; it is not a reduction in sauce disguised as normalization. Salt contribution depends on soy brand; fish is lifted out of pooled dressing instead of adding more table soy by default. No exact ten-minute texture guarantee remains.

The original duplicates a dry rice recipe and an unmeasured whole master-shari replacement. Its shari has very different seasoning, so the proposed default keeps its original light 2 tbsp vinegar / 1 tbsp sugar / 1/2 tsp fine salt, applied to four cooked cups plain rice. The already reviewed plain-rice batch uses 300 g dry rice, matching the approximate 1 1/2 US cups in the original via the primary Japanese-rice source's cup/weight discussion. Cooked yield is an estimate: measure four cups, reserve any excess and kitchen-test the actual brand. This resolves the original missing cooking water without silently converting to the more heavily seasoned master recipe. Required dependency changes to steamed-white-rice; shari remains an explicit same-cooked-volume replacement omitting all three inline rice seasoning ingredients. No second rice batch is required.

The whole English cucumber becomes 300 g (a chosen consistent portion, not a universal cucumber conversion). One cup shelled edamame becomes 150 g edible frozen beans, with required package cooking before chilling; mere thawing is insufficient for products not ready to eat. One avocado and 1 tbsp sesame remain. The original undefined sriracha mayo becomes a maximum offered 2 tbsp prepared mayonnaise, optional and label-aware. It adds egg in many products; the original unconditional egg-free tag is removed. Dairy-free assumes ordinary ingredients as listed; fish, soy, sesame and ordinary soy sauce's wheat remain. The recipe does not promise a nut-free product without checking toppings.

## Timing and technique

85 minutes allows the reviewed plain-rice 65-minute process, five minutes of seasoning, about ten minutes brief steam release and final assembly. Toppings/edamame preparation overlap the soak/cook; fish is cut near service. A made-ahead rice/bean option takes about 30 minutes, not the full-from-dry default. Refrigerator fish thawing is separate and often overnight. Barely warm rice is a service texture cue, not a safety endpoint or sanitized holding state. Refrigerate surplus rice promptly; adding fish happens only when a diner is ready to eat.

All listed ingredients are used; the two sets of vinegar and sugar are assigned to their specific components. Raw-contact fish dressing is kept out of the clean communal condiments. Storage recommends same-day raw preparation as a conservative service plan, not a validated raw-sauce shelf life; supplier history or earlier limit still governs.
