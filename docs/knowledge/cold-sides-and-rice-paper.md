## Deterministic accounting before culinary judgment

- Split combined herb and dressing lines into separately scalable quantities without changing totals.
- Use an existing measured shredded-cabbage volume instead of inventing a weight from an inconsistent head fraction. Record packing/yield as a test question.
- Assign measured salt once. If the recipe uses it twice, resolve the split explicitly rather than inventing a second dose.
- Count shrimp halves against wrappers only when shrimp are counted. Weight-based shrimp require distributing the actual cooked count. Preserve the whole allowance and check the real filling capacity.
- Keep wrapper diameters, vegetable cut dimensions and per-roll allocations fixed while ingredient totals scale.
- Separate preparation, chilling/resting and assembly time; larger cold batches still take longer to cut and wrap.

## Exceptions requiring individual review

Cooked versus merely thawed frozen vegetables; raw flour wrappers that need frying; nut/soy/wheat content of prepared sauces; honey versus a vegan alternative; actual vegetable size and leaf packing; the sweetness, saltiness and yield of a dressing; duplicate decisions across sauces and fillings. A sriracha ingredient is a sauce category, not a manufacturer endorsement.

## Identity and testing

Mango slaw, peanut green/red-cabbage slaw and napa-edamame-wonton slaw have different ingredients, textures and methods. Likewise avocado/chive rolls with sweet chile-lime sauce differ from lettuce/Thai-basil rolls with peanut-hoisin sauce. Similar titles nominate comparison, not consolidation.

For physical testing, record actual cabbage/leaf weights, shrimp size/count/weight, wrapper product and diameter, noodle cooking yield, complete filling fit, dressing yield, preparation time, portion weights and texture after a short refrigerated wait. Photograph the actual result separately from generated artwork.
