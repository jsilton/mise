## Prioritized implementation

1. **Complete ingredient accounting and scaling.** Separate split eggs and topping sugar into explicit amounts. Carry offered alternatives through every step. A list that doubles while the method retains its original split is misleading. The first pilot correction batch fixes the pilaf fat split and its dropped alternatives; carbonara eggs and muffin sugar remain queued for full review.
2. **Honest timing and equipment.** Include mandatory rest/cooling and state capacity assumptions. A single total is not credible if batches or vessel dimensions are unknown. Resolve these in the recipe-family edits rather than adding generic filler.
3. **Teaching at the decision.** Explain what the cook sees, what it means and what to do next. Retain mise's inline checkpoints and troubleshooting; do not restore separate skill/success panels. The scallop correction now makes interior doneness control removal instead of a categorical one-minute sear.
4. **Evidence and visuals.** Preserve actual author/source attribution, keep technical references on the review page/internal records, and commission intermediate-state images only where they explain a meaningful decision. Distinguish artwork from photographs and from physical-test evidence.

Mise already has ingredient/step checklists, scaling, cook mode, print presentation and footer review links. This comparison does not justify rebuilding those features. Their practical value depends on finishing the recipe content behind them. No recipe was copied or merged based on this benchmark.
