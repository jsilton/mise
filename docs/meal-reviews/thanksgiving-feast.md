# Brined Turkey Feast for Twelve: individual meal review

Date: 2026-09-06. Editorial review only; no physical meal test recorded.

Read the full original meal and all six complete linked components: the separately reviewed 14–16 lb wet-brined turkey, stuffing, cream-cheese mash, consolidated cranberry sauce, newly reviewed green beans and newly extracted gravy. Compared the reviewed eight-person dry-brined turkey dinner. These are meaningful different bird-size and brining plans, not duplicate meals. All six recipe components now have individual records.

One fixed-size bird serves twelve with sides. One stuffing batch is intentionally shared as twelve smaller roughly half-cup holiday portions alongside potatoes; its original eight-side yield is not silently assumed to feed twelve generously. Mash is one and a half batches: 2040 g peeled potatoes, roughly 4.5 L cooking water and eighteen grams cooking salt, 113 g butter and 173 g cream cheese rounded from half grams, 270 ml half-and-half plus ninety optional, named finishing salt and pepper. An eight-quart pot and seventy-minute allowance handle the greater load. No impossible whole-turkey or pan-size multiplication is offered.

One cranberry batch gives the twelve 2–3-tablespoon condiment portions, with its sixty-milliliter orange-juice replacement, one teaspoon zest and regular 150 g sugar. No separate duplicate cranberry sauce is made. Beans are three 450 g rounds with 180 g shallot, three tablespoons butter, measured lemon/pepper/finishing salt and six tablespoons reheating water. The cooking-water/salt quantities are per fresh batch, not arbitrarily added to already concentrated reused water. They are prepared, cooled and refrigerated a day ahead. At service, soften shallots together, split, then reheat each bean portion to 165°F with its share of fat/water. Earlier portions hold briefly hot and receive acid only at final service. The cost to texture/color is acknowledged rather than a claim that an ice bath permanently fixes either.

Gravy is one and a half batches of the new shared recipe, about three cups with exact 45 g butter/flour, 675 ml stock, 45 ml water, pepper, optional salt and loosening water. The stock-only option allows it to finish before the roast, reducing final stove conflicts. Do not add another full measure of drippings afterward. This reuses a single reviewed method and removes the original empty 'make gravy' instruction. A helper near carving is useful, but a sequential one-cook route is provided.

Twenty-six hours starts with an already refrigerator-thawed bird; 210 active minutes span both days. The brine cools before a twelve-hour refrigerated soak, followed by four hours refrigerated drying. Clock stages are set backward from roasting, with the raw-storage window retained. Refrigerated volume for brine and cold sides is a real constraint. The default oven fits turkey below one shallow stuffing dish with space, both at 325°F. The stuffing has a longer, independently measured bake. If they do not fit, a second oven or a linked, explicitly longer fully cooked/chilled/reheated-stuffing alternative is required. The eight-person alternative plan was read in full and its fallback limits are retained.

A larger-pot mash, ahead-of-roast gravy, three sequential bean rounds and short measured hot holds are coordinated around the turkey rest. None of the turkey/sausage/casserole center checks is substituted for another. Delays are handled by measured holding or cooling/reheating, not a warm-setting assumption. The old warm-carcass-to-stock shortcut is not included as an unplanned component. All leftovers use separate shallow containers and source-supported temperature/storage guidance.
