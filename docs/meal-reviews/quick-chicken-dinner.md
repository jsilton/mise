Half a mash batch uses 680 g peeled Yukon Gold potatoes and provides about three cups, divided into four roughly three-quarter-cup portions. All mash ingredients, including water, cooking salt, finishing salt and dairy, are halved together with the 1/2× ingredient setting. One bean batch is 450 g trimmed beans with its measured shallot and lemon dressing. ## Time and equipment reasoning

The new minimum 280-minute total is ten minutes assembling chicken marinade, 180 minutes refrigerated soak, then a ninety-minute cooking session. A longer soak adds directly to elapsed time. About 75 minutes hands-on across both sessions is an editorial allowance: the component prep fields total 60 minutes, with additional active temperature checks, draining/mashing, tossing and service. Cooking processes overlap rather than summing the three cook timers. This meal is intermediate to coordinate even though each component is individually easy. Removed automatic Monday-through-Thursday/quick-weeknight positioning and assigned unhurried Friday/weekend occasions.

The chicken needs coating/preheating before its up-to-55-minute bake and final five-minute rest. Starting potatoes once the bird is in the oven allows roughly 55 minutes for vegetable cutting, heating water, simmering, dairy warming, draining and gentle mashing while the chicken cooks. Beans need their own 35-minute preparation with shallot softening overlapped with water heating, and their skillet/lemon finish should be held until near service. The ninety-minute final session provides modest one-cook coordination allowance; it is not a tested stopwatch result, and oversize pieces, an unusually slow preheat or crowded oven extend it.

Explicit equipment includes one chicken sheet/rack set, thermometer, potato pot, bean pot, small dairy pan, 12-inch bean skillet and masher/ricer. Three burners can be occupied by potatoes, bean water and shallots; warm dairy before that peak or in a microwave-safe vessel. A short measured potato water-bath hold uses one of the same freed pots/burners, not an invisible oven at a conflicting temperature. Tight stove space gets the linked beans' day-ahead cooling/refrigeration route, with earlier labor counted, then measured reheating to 165°F before adding lemon. The recipe's cooked-bean route avoids automatically ice-bathing vegetables that are being served immediately.

The chicken's individually finished breasts/wings can leave the oven while legs continue. That solves overcooking but creates a service-temperature question: they wait on a clean rack during this short finish, not through an unspecified long side preparation. Finished mash may use its covered hold above 140°F for up to about thirty minutes; cooked chicken crust is less tolerant of covered holding. The cook should prioritize sides before the final chicken rest and finish lemon at plating. Larger chicken batches may require a second rack/pan, position swaps and a longer session; ingredient scaling cannot multiply or certify the oven schedule.
