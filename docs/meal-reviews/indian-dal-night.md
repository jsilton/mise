## One-cook timing and attention

**About 110 minutes elapsed and 80–90 active**, with totalActiveTime 85, replaces the generic weeknight claim. This is a four-component weekend/comfortable-evening dinner from raw/dry ingredients, not a 30-minute active meal simply because dal simmers. The raita relationship uses the schema’s `sauce` field, reflecting its actual cool condiment/side role rather than calling it a separate salad. The frontmatter's 45-minute aggregate preparation and 70-minute cooking allowance overlap; the published elapsed timeline is the governing dinner clock. A slower potato or older dal can extend it.

A representative plan:

- **0–10:** rinse/start mung; heat its water and settle to a simmer. Warm the rice milk/saffron and measure separate spice sets.
- **10–30:** cut the half-batch vegetables and each dish's onion/aromatics, rinse rice. Check dal; its 20–30-minute simmer begins after heating, so its tomato may need adding during the next stage. The public method explicitly allows that rather than forcing readiness at minute thirty.
- **30–48:** aloo whole spices, onion, short delicate aromatics, tomato reduction and potato coat/heat. This is a watched phase; do not begin another quick tempering simultaneously.
- **48–65:** potatoes cook covered while rice whole spices/onion and liquid heating happen on burner three. Pause rice stirring for the actual potato transition check. Add cauliflower and its water only when potatoes are partially tender, then let the covered pan return to a simmer.
- **65–85:** rice simmers and later rests; vegetables finish covered and then briefly uncovered. Toast raita cumin on the freed dal burner, cool it, grate/drain cucumber, mix and chill. The five-minute draining/cooling intervals permit grain and vegetable checks; they are not all additional serial waits.
- **85–105:** finished vegetables pause loosely covered; dal reheats gently if needed. Cook the dal's onion in two tablespoons ghee, then its remaining tablespoon/spices/garlic and off-heat powders. Turn off other demanding pans before this short tadka. Combine and balance dal.
- **105–110:** fluff rice/remove visible whole spices, verify hot dishes are still hot, briefly warm vegetables if needed and serve raita cold.

Three burners suffice. Dal occupies burner one at the beginning and is off heat during raita preparation; rice uses three and vegetables two. The small skillet reuses a free burner for raita cumin, then for the late dal onion/tadka. Cooking that onion last costs real time but prevents final spice fat from sitting while rice/vegetables demand attention. Rice rests off heat when necessary, freeing another burner. Brief covered pauses are within a continuous dinner preparation, not room-temperature storage for hours. The final method explicitly requires hot holding at 140°F / 60°C or above or prompt chilling if an unexpected delay becomes prolonged.

Active budget on paper: dal startup/early adjustment about 6–8 minutes; shared cutting/measuring about 25; aloo watched masala/turns/finish about 18–22; rice watched onion/coating/checks about 12–15 beyond shared prep; raita about 8–10; final onion/tadka about 10–12; plating/remaining checks about 4–5. Some checks overlap waiting rather than requiring all maxima, giving roughly 80–90. A cook at every upper bound can take longer, which is acknowledged. This is an editorial planning budget, not stopwatch evidence.
