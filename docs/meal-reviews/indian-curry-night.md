## One-cook schedule and practical critique

Use the original six-quart slow-cooker identity, not a new stove/pressure curry. The revised main uses High with checks and staged additions; the meal no longer claims six-to-eight Low hours or "set it and forget it." Start approximately 5 1/2 hours before serving, with the first 25 minutes for prep. That leaves roughly five cooking hours, an upper planning allowance which yields to tenderness.

Start half-batch naan three hours before dinner. Approximately 20–25 minutes yeast hydration/mixing/kneading, 60–90 minutes rise, division/rest, and 20–25 minutes rolling/pan work fits comfortably inside that window. A fast rise can be handled by cooking the breads early and warming them briefly, rather than prescribing an unreviewed long room-temperature dough hold. A slower rise or pan correction can extend dinner. Actual breads stay wrapped between rounds, as the reviewed component directs.

After three hours of curry cooking, check the slow vegetables; when nearly tender, the beans need approximately 45–60 minutes and the peas occupy the final 10–15. This determines whether to advance or delay the final rice/bread work. A finished curry may be held verified at 140°F or above before yogurt, ideally at most an hour for texture. The two-hour span between first check and planned dinner does not mean all of it must be a hot hold: the late vegetables still cook during the first part. If an unusually fast batch finishes much earlier, bring the meal finish forward when possible; a longer hold is acknowledged to soften the curry, not advertised as texture-free flexibility.

At roughly T minus 45–50 minutes, divide/rest naan and start rice. Rinsing plus fat/water heating takes around 8–10 minutes; simmer takes 15–18 and rest ten. Start skillet heating during the last five minutes of dough relaxation. Rolling and cooking the six breads takes 20–25 minutes, with the skillet taken off heat before rice checks, pea additions or rolling more dough. Rice rests while the last rounds finish; yogurt waits for five minutes off heat near service. Two burners suffice, and a preheating skillet/bread is not left cooking unattended while another task is done.

Hands-on budget is **90–110 minutes, represented as 100**: main roughly 35–45, half naan roughly 45–55, rice roughly ten. Prep field 55 comprises 25 main + roughly 25 bread handling before its rest/cook +5 rice; active additionally includes watched griddle rounds, cooker checks and serving. The five-hour cook field is the longest parallel cooking component, not the sum of all appliance times. Optional sauce reduction or repeated rice/dough recovery adds workload. It would be misleading to keep forty active minutes solely because the curry is mostly unattended.

The meal remains suitable for a weekend. Previously prepared plain naan cuts today's active work to about 45–60 minutes without changing the curry clock. With refrigerated curry and already-thawed naan, preparing both ahead changes service to about 45–60 elapsed /30–40 active, including a full reheating path rather than "warm through" in the slow cooker. This shortcut uses an additional four-quart saucepan for the roughly 1.9 kg curry. Rice and curry occupy the two burners first; the curry then holds covered and verified hot while the second burner warms the breads.
