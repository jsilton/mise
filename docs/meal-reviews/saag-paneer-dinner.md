## One-cook schedule and capacity

Default 90–95 elapsed/70–80 active is deliberately slower than the unsubstantiated original 25 active / 40 total. A cook with already washed leaves, efficient organization and quick endpoints can finish earlier; the visible clock is a practical allowance, not a guarantee.

- 0–20: start water, prepare greens/onion/aromatics/cheese and measure all main ingredients.
- 20–35: four spinach blanch batches, cool, squeeze and chop. This occupies attention and the large pot, so paneer frying is not scheduled simultaneously.
- 35–43: rinse rice and start its covered simmer on one burner.
- 43–53: toast and cool raita cumin in the small pan, grate/drain cucumber, chop herbs/mix/chill. Cumin cooling and cucumber draining overlap actual remaining prep; the refrigerator handles its ten-minute chill later.
- 53–67: two watched cheese rounds on burner two. Rice endpoint check falls during this period; explicit instruction takes the paneer pan off heat briefly if needed. Rice gets full ten-minute rest rather than an invented 20-minute complete rice time.
- 67–82: masala/greens, while paneer soaks and rice rests/holds. This window fits quick onion/green endpoints; slower stages can extend into the next broad finish window or move service later.
- 82–95: cream and cheese heating, finishing acid/spice, optional chile ghee and plating. Optional small-pan work begins after the main is off heat. No simultaneous scorching-sensitive blooms or fourth burner.

Main prep/blanch tasks 35 min plus rice handling 8,raita prep 10,paneer browning 10–14,masala/greens 13–18,finish 6–9 yields a conservative 88–104-minute envelope before preparation overlaps. 95 is a reasonable planning center. Active work removes water-heat, brief simmer and drain waits; 70–80 fits an efficient cook without assuming all waits are useful parallel work. Actual upper stage lengths may exceed it. This is a substantive schedule review, not summing component headline totals.

Rice finishes its rest around 70–75 and serves about 95, a short covered pause. Paneer remains in its warm soak while masala cooks, then is gently heated through. Raita stays cold. For unexpected long delays, public text states 140°F hot holding or prompt chilling/reheating rather than indefinite room-temperature waiting.

Prepared route assumes a **complete fully cooked chilled saag**, with its ghee/cream already included. 45–50 elapsed and 25–35 active includes fresh 40 min rice, raita during simmer/rest and thorough 165°F saag reheat.
