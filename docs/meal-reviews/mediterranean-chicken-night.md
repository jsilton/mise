### Equipment and one-cook sequence

A standard half-sheet pan is about 18 × 13 inches externally. One pan has 900 g thighs opened flat; another has 600 g prepared peppers/onion spread in a single layer. This loading is plausible but depends on the thighs' dimensions and actual interior pan area. Two racks with air space and a lower chicken tray are explicit. Rotate each pan front to back rather than exchanging their upper/lower positions. If either food is crowded, the cook needs a larger suitable oven/pan arrangement or a separately planned batch schedule; this proposal does not promise ninety minutes from a small one-rack toaster oven.

One burner holds the one-pita skillet. The rest of the main roasts hands-off; no third bread tray is placed into the 425°F oven and no false 350°F/425°F time equivalence is used. Rotate/turn trays after fifteen minutes, then begin bread warming. Start every-thigh temperature checks at twenty-five minutes, pausing between bread rounds with no bread left unattended on the skillet. Bread goes directly into its towel. Finished meat leaves the oven even if vegetables need more time. Five minutes of chicken rest and final slicing/assembly are included. This ordering avoids trying to turn hot oven trays, manipulate a thermometer and flip a bread simultaneously.

Retain **90 minutes elapsed**; the faster mint method is not credited as an automatic time saving. Proposed preparation allowance rises from 40 to 45 minutes and active time from 55 to **65 minutes**, a conservative editorial allowance for washing/drying the cold produce, main and sauce preparation, loading and raw-contact cleanup, four bread rounds, individual chicken checks, slicing and family-style assembly. A planning envelope is 45 minutes for preparation/marinating overlap and tray loading, up to 35 minutes roast, then 5 minutes rest and 5 minutes slicing/service; approximately twenty active minutes occur during/after the roast for bread and handling. The thirty-minute marinade starts after it is mixed, not at an imaginary ready-to-go zero. These estimates need a timed single-cook trial; they are not measured performance claims.

The optional dill batch needs its five-minute drain and fifteen-minute chill, so start it earlier during marination, before finishing the platter vegetables. Small sauce batches still require washing and cleanup; neither sauce's active time divides arithmetically by the scaling factor. Frozen meat and bread thawing, a four-hour chosen marinade, prolonged vegetable browning, or an oven too small for the two trays need additional time.

### Safety evidence and limitations

Independently accessed on 6 September 2026:

- https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety — produce washing/drying, separation of raw meat from ready-to-eat foods, refrigerated marination, refrigerator temperature and two-hour/one-hour-above-90°F handling limits. These facts support the work-area cleanup and separate cold-component storage.
- https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures — poultry and reheated leftovers at 165°F/74°C. Color and the roasting clock cannot establish these endpoints.
- https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts — raw poultry refrigerated 1–2 days and cooked poultry 3–4 days. A marinade does not restart those storage periods.
- https://www.foodsafety.gov/blog/food-safety-and-eating-out — general 3–4-day leftover guidance after prompt refrigeration. This is conservative household guidance for mixed cold yogurt sauce, not a microbial shelf-life test of this exact recipe.
