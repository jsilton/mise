## Ingredient arithmetic and finish

Four servings retain 170 g raw fish each, about half a cooked cup rice, 37.5 g frozen shelled-bean allocation before cooking, 75 g cucumber, one quarter large avocado, two tablespoons mayonnaise and about two tablespoons drained pickle. Approximate cooked yields are inherited estimates, not newly measured serving results. A large avocado is not assigned a fictional fixed peeled weight. The original rich sauce and mayonnaise amounts are preserved.

All half-salad amounts are stated because the recipe's method does not scale automatically: 300 g cucumber; 1/4 tsp fine salt (current full recipe has 1/2 tsp); 2 1/4 tsp black vinegar (half of 1.5 tbsp); 1 tsp soy; 1/2 tsp each sesame oil and sugar; one small garlic clove; optional 1/2 tsp chili crisp; 1/2 tsp sesame seeds; half a scallion. Salt-drain before dressing and finish near serving. The full fifteen-minute drain remains, independent of half quantity.

For a freshly made quarter pickle recipe, arithmetic is 37.5 g each root, 1/8 tsp fine salt, 1/4 tsp massage sugar, one tablespoon brine sugar and two tablespoons each vinegar/water. A narrow small container is needed; cutting less vegetable does not shorten the hour in brine. The optional coverage correction also scales if needed (1 1/2 tsp each vinegar/water plus 3/4 tsp sugar). Public meal prose points to quartering every ingredient and retains the ninety-minute dinner allowance rather than reproducing another long complete pickle recipe.

Raw-contact marinade is kept separately, boiled throughout before reuse, reduced without fish, and folded into the cooked cubes off heat. The three tablespoons marinade water and tablespoon correction reserve are distinct from rice and bean water. Two teaspoons oil are allocated to each of two roughly 340 g skillet loads. Fish is checked at its thickest centers for 145°F/63°C and transferred to a clean plate, not presumed cooked from ten minutes or appearance. This main method is retained; the meal does not add cold raw glaze at assembly.

## One-cook equipment and critical path

One lidded rice pot, one small bean saucepan, one 12-inch fish skillet, a thermometer, preparation tools and a jar suffice. Two burners are enough because edamame finishes before the skillet needs that burner; rice can finish/rest on the other. The pickle requires no stove. No oven is used. All cucumber, beans and mayonnaise are ready before the demanding fish batches; avocado is sliced near assembly. This avoids a fictitious need to sauté vegetables during continuous fish turning and sauce reduction.

Default **90 minutes elapsed / about 65 active** includes approximately twenty active minutes for the full pickle plus forty-five for the main. The independent challenge correctly identified that the earlier 35-minute main / 55-minute dinner active allowance undercounts the closely watched sear and sauce stages. A practical main allocation is about five minutes rice handling, eight fish/marinade preparation, five ready-to-eat toppings, three bean handling, three draining/pan setup, twelve-to-sixteen monitored fish cooking, four sauce boiling/reduction and four assembly: about 44–48 minutes, rounded to about 45 for planning. Active here includes a watched hot pan even while the hands briefly pause; it does not imply that the cook is free for another job. An illustrative one-cook envelope is:

| Elapsed | Work/dependency                                                                                      |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–20    | Prepare full jar, then refrigerate; its one-hour flavor wait begins at minute 20.                    |
| 20–25   | Clear work area and arrange the main's measured ingredients.                                         |
| 25–40   | Start rice, cut/mix fish and begin twenty-minute refrigerated marination.                            |
| 40–60   | Cook/drain beans, portion toppings and complete rice/rest; clean tools and organize fish batches.    |
| 60–80   | Drain fish, heat pan, cook two 6–8-minute rounds with recovery and individual temperature checks.    |
| 80–90   | Boil/reduce marinade, coat fish, slice avocado and assemble. Pickle has completed at least one hour. |

The exact minute forty marinade start is a planning envelope, not an enforced delay. Faster preparation can advance the main, but the pickle timer still governs. Rice's short hold while fish finishes is inside the full dinner time; it is not a new long countertop keep-warm instruction. If dinner is delayed, use proper hot holding or cool promptly rather than leave the covered pot out indefinitely.

With already-prepared pickles, retain **60 minutes elapsed / about 45 active**, retaining the accepted main’s elapsed schedule with a larger hands-on allowance. A half or quarter pickle does not warrant reducing the one-hour rest. The full fresh-jar version fits weekend preparation; weekday use is clearer with prepared pickles. Metadata `bestFor` changes to Wednesday/Saturday/Sunday rather than implying a twenty-active-minute Monday/Tuesday recovery meal; the weeknight occasion remains for the prepared-pickle branch.

The salad variation is **65–70 minutes / about 55 active**: start its salting before the fish marinade and mix dressing while draining; shake/blot and keep cold until final toss. It needs roughly five-to-ten additional minutes beyond the main's plain cucumber prep, even though much of the drain overlaps other work. The whole 25-minute salad cannot be assumed to fit inside a twenty-minute fish marinade while the same cook also handles beans and toppings.
