# Pilot kitchen validation plan

Written review cannot establish flavor, measured yield or reproducibility. This plan specifies the physical evidence still needed; it records no completed cooking.

## First six anchor tests

Choose one finalized recipe per pilot family after editorial changes are accepted:

| Family             | Anchor                          | Main questions                                                                                                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Quick seafood      | Garlic butter shrimp            | Exact peeled shrimp size/weight, pan loading, thick-center doneness, sauce retention and texture at service           |
| Braises            | Brisket with carrots and onions | Cut thickness, covered-pan fit, real tenderness time, vegetable staging, liquid loss and portions                     |
| Grain hydration    | Mexican red rice                | Actual rice type, rinse/drain state, tomato and water contribution, pot/lid dimensions, tenderness and finished yield |
| Pasta sauces       | Lemon ricotta pasta             | Ricotta moisture, all retained oil/cheese, pasta tenderness after tossing, water used, sauce consistency and portions |
| Quick batters      | Frozen blueberry muffins        | Standard cup dimensions, batter weight per cavity, two-pan oven behavior, doneness, cooling and actual count          |
| Roasted vegetables | Crispy smashed potatoes         | Potato size, boil tenderness, drying/smashing thickness, pan area, roast time and crispness after serving             |

These anchors do not certify other recipes in their families. Additional tests prioritize uncertain or substantially changed formulas, undercooked-egg methods, whole poultry, pressure cooking, preservation, unusual substitutes and extreme scaling.

## Before cooking

Use an exact recipe commit. Record cook/date, source of ingredients, edible weights, relevant product strength/salt, starting temperatures, equipment dimensions, oven/burner settings and thermometer checks. Photograph the actual ingredients and completed dish only with permission. Preserve original photos and provenance; they are inputs to the photo/illustration workflow, not automatically approved site assets.

## During cooking

Record each stage's actual start/end and attended time separately. Log all additions, discarded liquid/fat, heat changes, measured endpoints, reserve water/seasoning actually used and deviations. Do not follow an unsafe instruction for experimental completeness: stop, document it and correct the method before resuming. Record whether the written sensory cue was observable and whether following it achieved the intended result.

## At service

Weigh or measure final edible yield and plate the stated servings. Evaluate seasoning, tenderness, sauce consistency, contrast and portion satisfaction against the recipe's intended identity. Use specific observations rather than a single rating. Record holding time and resulting texture. Photograph failed results too when useful; do not select only flattering evidence.

## Repeat and acceptance

Revise the recipe with the observed reason for each change. Repeat the exact final version, ideally with another cook/kitchen, before making a strong reproducibility claim. Test a claimed substitution or batch size separately when it materially changes heat transfer or hydration. Document leftovers only after following the recipe's safe handling instructions; appearance and smell are not safety tests.

Use the substantive test-record requirements in docs/RECIPE_STANDARD.md. Editorial status stays distinct from kitchen-tested status. A recipe is not kitchen-tested merely because a related anchor, external author's version or generated picture exists.

## User participation required

A person with a kitchen must cook and report the observations, or provide authorized existing records of those exact versions. The agent can prepare test sheets, compare observations, revise recipes and process supplied photographs. It cannot fabricate cooking, taste, temperatures or measured yield. No ingredients are purchased or cooks contacted by this plan.
