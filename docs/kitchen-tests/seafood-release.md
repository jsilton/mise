## 1. Balsamic butter sauce and summer salmon

Recipe: [Seared Salmon with Summer Vegetables](../../src/content/recipes/seared-salmon-with-summer-vegetables.md). Priority: first in this release because the restored sauce allocations and coordinated finish materially change the cooking experience.

Cook one original batch without substitutions. Set out the four separate butter amounts (6, 2, 3 and 1 tbsp) and two oil amounts (2 and 1 tbsp), a scale, timer, fine-tip thermometer, fine strainer, nonreactive saucepan and skillet. Record the actual salmon thickness and mushroom/onion prepared weights without replacing the recipe's quantities.

Record when the aromatics soften, how long the vinegar/water reduction takes and the strained sauce mass. Note whether the base is still moist before adding the chilled butter, whether the sauce stays smooth, and its condition after the actual warm-water hold while vegetables and fish finish. Photograph the sauce both before and after the hold if documenting with photos. Record fish center temperatures as directed, each strip's cooking time, and the actual serving count. Do not cook to an earlier source's medium-rare endpoint for this test.

At service, assess sauce acidity, richness, amount per plate, fish texture and whether drained spinach dilutes the sauce. Plate either six small courses or three to four mains as chosen before cooking; record which. Report actual active and elapsed time against the recipe's one-hour allowance.

Repeat trigger: any separated sauce, dry fish, missing ingredient destination, substantial timing overrun or unsatisfactory portion allocation requires a specific revision and another cook before calling the revised version reproducible.

## 2. Cream sauce, pasta and shrimp capacity

Recipe: [Garlicky Shrimp Fettuccine](../../src/content/recipes/creamy-garlicky-shrimp-skillet.md). Priority: verify the source-restored jar interpretation and retained rich sauce.

Use one 12-ounce jar of peppers, drained; record actual drained mass. Use the full listed cream, Parmesan, butter, pasta and shrimp. Record the Parmesan's measured volume and actual mass, the shrimp's peeled mass/size, and the sauté pan's interior dimensions/capacity. Do not substitute a lighter sauce to make this test easier.

Log pasta-water heat-up, pasta entry/drain time, cream simmer, shrimp endpoint and cheese incorporation. Record precisely how much reserved pasta water is used, including zero. Note whether the specified deep pan permits safe tossing without spillage and whether pasta or sauce waits for the other.

Weigh the finished food and divide into the stated four generous portions. Describe coating consistency immediately and after five minutes on a plate, shrimp texture, pepper distribution and salt balance. A photograph of a glossy sauce alone does not demonstrate its eating quality or portion adequacy.

Repeat trigger: inadequate pan capacity, clumped cheese, shrimp overcooking, excess unabsorbed sauce or a changed pepper quantity. Test any resulting formula change separately rather than counting this initial cook as its validation.

## 3. Baked Cod Dinner: shared oven

Meal: [Baked Cod Dinner](../../src/content/meals/baked-cod-dinner.md). Cook one cod batch, half the garlic potatoes and one broccoli batch for four. Record purchased fillet weights/thickness; do not assume the unspecified fish weights are interchangeable.

Use the oven's actual two-rack configuration. Record potato piece size, roast entry, first tenderness check, garlic entry, fish entry and each component's exit. Record cod centers as directed, not by topping color. Observe whether steam from the shared oven prevents acceptable crumb browning before the fish is ready. Record broccoli water heating, steam time and garlic-oil attention separately.

Write down every moment when two components simultaneously require attention and what was delayed. Compare real elapsed/active time with the planned 85/45 minutes. Photograph the actual four plates if desired; note whether potatoes and broccoli provide adequate sides and whether any component waits long enough to lose texture.

Repeat trigger: a schedule that depends on leaving fish to overcook, unsafe pan handling, insufficient sides or repeated attention conflicts. A single-dish oven is a separate configuration test; do not claim it was covered by this run.

## 4. Honey Lime Shrimp Bowls: sequencing and balance

Meal: [Honey Lime Shrimp Bowls](../../src/content/meals/honey-lime-shrimp-bowls.md). Cook one full batch of each component for four, omitting only the rice's optional sugar. Retain all honey, coconut milk and fats. Record shrimp count/size and prepared bok choy weight.

Follow the serial attended-pan sequence: rice first, bok choy's two batches and sauce, then shrimp last. Record rice's cooking/rest completion, actual holding temperature and duration, bok choy's wait, shrimp endpoint and glaze reduction. Record any sauce reduction with the shrimp removed. If rice needs correction, log the additional water/time and delay shrimp accordingly.

Divide into four bowls. Record whether the rice allocation is too large, whether the shrimp topping is adequate, combined sweetness/salt, and the bok choy's texture after waiting. Compare actual elapsed/active time with the planned 60/55 minutes. Do not infer that two large seafood portions are interchangeable with four bowl portions.

Repeat trigger: portion mismatch, excessive sweetness or salt, rice holding problems, or a finish requiring simultaneous attention that one cook cannot provide. Revise only the component or allocation responsible; avoid changing every recipe at once.
