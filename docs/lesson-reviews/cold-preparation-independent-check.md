## Exact recommended corrections

1. **Experiment initial condition:** Change “from the same block of cream cheese” to “from the same refrigerated block of cream cheese.” Starting with an already warm block could leave little intended temperature difference, especially with only ten minutes in the refrigerator. The present instruction should not rely on a reader inferring the initial state from an earlier control.
2. **Experiment comparison:** After the ten-minute wait, say “With a clean blade for each sample, use the same knife motion to cut a thin strip from each.” Cheese residue from the first cut can change the second cut's drag. This is a small experimental-control improvement, not a new safety protocol. Retain “whether” the warmer sample smears, rather than predicting that every brand and sample must visibly change after exactly ten minutes.
3. **Cold-bar cue:** Replace “aim for 40°F / 4°C or below” with “confirm 40°F / 4°C or below; return portions to the refrigerator if the ice setup cannot keep them cold.” The actual Poke Bowl Bar already supplies this corrective action. A measured cue is more useful when it tells the learner what to do with an out-of-range result. Retain the cumulative time/discard guidance elsewhere; refrigeration must not read as rescue for an already exceeded limit.
4. **Citation already being handled by root:** Identify the Bad Bug Book source by chapter and link directly to `https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download?attachment=#page=90`. The Bacillus chapter begins at zero-based PDF page 89, displayed PDF page 90. A chapter title avoids mixing the book's pagination with the PDF viewer's page count. The JSON initially read for this review already used a chapter title without a page range.

These corrections do not require new recipe formulas, altered servings, new meal labels or another scientific claim.

## Scientific scope

**Cream cheese passes with the formulation qualification.** [Bemer et al.](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/4/14506/files/2019/07/Vegetable-organogels-incorporation-in-cream-cheese-products.pdf) is a full accessible primary paper about oleogel formulations, with commercial full-fat and fat-free spreads as controls. Section 3.5.1 reports a steep decrease in the full-fat control's storage modulus during heating from 5 to 30°C and discusses milk-fat melting within the protein network. The draft uses that qualitative temperature response and names formulation dependence. It does not represent the study as a block-cheese cutting trial, set a universal melting temperature, or generalize the experimental formulations' performance to the household's cheese. A ten-minute kitchen comparison is editorial guidance, not a replication of rheometer conditions. The public protein-network/fat wording is adequate; no universal fat-only explanation is needed.

**Nori passes at the modest level actually stated.** [Ogawa et al.'s publisher abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/suisan1932/57/2/57_2_301/_article) compares dry and wetted dried nori and reports different mechanical/sensory relationships. I read the English abstract, not a translated full Japanese paper. It supports the limited proposition that hydration changes the eating texture. It does not establish a number of crisp minutes in a sushi roll, or how every roasted nori/tenkasu brand behaves. The lesson makes neither claim. Keeping crisps separate until assembly is a reasonable application of the current recipes, and the template discloses the distinction between principles and editorial applications. The inaccessible 2022 article is unnecessary to the public evidence chain.

**Water treatment is application-specific.** The lesson distinguishes surface blotting from grating/salting/squeezing. It does not claim that squeezing removes all cucumber water or that refrigeration drains vegetables. “Extra oil or honey will not remove water” is appropriately limited: it does not need an overbroad claim that adding other ingredients can never change a sauce's apparent viscosity.

## Safety and sensory boundaries

The [FDA refrigerator guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/refrigerator-thermometers-cold-facts-about-food-safety) supports measured refrigeration and prompt chilling in smaller containers. The lesson does not imply refrigeration stops every organism or corrects earlier mishandling. The [FoodSafety.gov handling guidance](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) supports separation, washing after raw contact and the household time/temperature limits. Those limits remain outer bounds, not an invitation to deliberately hold food warm for two hours.

The rice control deliberately separates an assembly preference from safe holding. Barely-warm rice is explicitly a texture cue; extra rice goes promptly into shallow refrigerated containers without waiting for it to cool on the counter. The [Bad Bug Book Bacillus chapter](https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download?attachment=#page=90) supports the rice-related hazard and heat-resistant preformed emetic toxin. Reheating is therefore correctly rejected as a reset after mishandling. The lesson does not claim its vinegar seasoning establishes a protective measured pH or commercial acidification process.

The [FDA seafood page](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely) and [parasite chapter](https://www.fda.gov/media/80777/download) support the distinctions among appropriate sourcing, controlled parasite freezing, remaining microbial hazards and inadequate brining/pickling controls. No domestic freezer shortcut or blanket same-species treatment is taught. Detailed species exemptions and supplier records appropriately remain in the linked recipes. The cooked alternative for susceptible diners is consistent with those recipes and does not imply a raw family dinner has become universally suitable.

The cheese, cucumber, nori and rice observations concern shape, moisture and texture. The ice-bar observation uses thermometers. Neither smell, cool touch, lack of droplets, intact cheese edges nor lack of visible steam is offered as evidence of safe raw fish. The shared template's cue introduction does not override these explicit distinctions. The delayed-dinner troubleshooting discards food when handling history is uncertain instead of inviting a taste test.

## Recipe mappings and teaching structure

All seven arrays are individually justified and preserve the relevant existing lesson links:

- **Philly:** cold block cheese, dried cucumber, refrigerated fish/cheese portions and a clean dipping-water station directly apply. Keep starch.
- **New York Crunch:** sealed prepared flakes enter immediately before rolling, and each roll is cut for service. Late addition and separate dry storage are exact applications. Keep starch.
- **Spicy Tuna:** seasoned chopped fish is portioned and returned to refrigeration. Retain seasoning and starch alongside the new sequence lesson; it is not a cheese experiment recipe.
- **Shari:** the final cooking-to-assembly transition and extra-rice refrigeration fit. Starch and seasoning remain the primary lessons for hydration and flavor.
- **Poke Bowl Bar:** the actual method measures seafood over ice, uses separate spoons and reserves, and assembles each bowl at service. Replacing a primarily meat-doneness temperature lesson with this cold-service lesson improves the fit.
- **Dill Tzatziki:** grated cucumber receives measured salt, five minutes draining and squeezing. This directly practices water management.
- **Mint Tzatziki:** fine dice is patted dry and kept distinct; no grated-cucumber substitution, added lemon, oil reduction or changed honey amount is implied. Its seasoning lesson remains first.

The four public practice cards cover different applications rather than repeating three tuna rolls. The new title is broad, but its useful organizing idea is preparation order: keep components in their appropriate state and combine shortly before eating. Rice cooling belongs as a transition into cold assembly, not as a competing rice-cooking lesson. Five controls and seven source links are somewhat substantial; root should assess page length on the actual display, but there is no need to delete source limitations to shorten it.

`methods: []` is correct. The current lookup honors explicit recipe technique arrays before fallback matches. Adding practice cards alone would not attach this lesson to these reviewed recipe pages; the seven mapping changes must be applied deliberately. Conversely there should be no broad `no-cook` method assignment that maps pending recipes automatically. The existing detail template renders all fields and explicitly marks the experiment as suggested rather than completed; no special rendering dependency is required by this text.

## Remaining limits

A useful physical comparison should record starting refrigerator temperature, room conditions, cheese brand, equal sample dimensions and the observed difference or absence of one. Cutting resistance is a workability observation, not a shelf-life test. Real roll and bar trials would separately measure preparation time, reserve-food temperature, rice cooling conditions and the texture of later portions. None has been performed here, and none should be inferred from this editorial acceptance.
