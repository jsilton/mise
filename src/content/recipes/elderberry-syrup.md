## Chef's Note

This spiced elderberry-and-honey syrup is a culinary preparation. Use food-grade dried ripe elderberries and follow their cooking instructions; raw or unripe berries and other plant parts can cause illness. Do not treat it as medicine or a shelf-stable preserve. Freeze in small portions, and do not serve honey-containing syrup to children under 12 months.

## Directions

1. **Cook and reduce the berries:** Combine the water, elderberries, fresh ginger, cinnamon and whole cloves in a saucepan with room for bubbling. Use food-grade dried ripe berries and their cooking instructions; do not use unidentified plants, raw or unripe fruit, leaves or stems. Bring to a boil, then lower to a simmer and cover for the saved 45–60-minute route, checking that liquid remains. Follow any more specific cooking requirements on the berry package. The aim is about half the starting liquid remaining; a tight lid may slow reduction, so do not treat the clock as proof of concentration or add honey yet.
2. **Mash after cooking:** Remove from the heat and cool enough to handle. Carefully mash the cooked berries with a spoon or potato masher; avoid splashing the hot liquid.
3. **Strain and discard the solids:** Strain into a clean heatproof bowl or jar through a fine-mesh sieve or cheesecloth, pressing carefully. Discard all berry and spice solids.
4. **Add honey:** When the strained liquid is lukewarm, stir in all the honey until fully dissolved. Lukewarm is a mixing cue, not a preservation process or a medicinal claim.
5. **Freeze in small portions:** Cool promptly in small, clean freezer-safe containers, leaving the container’s recommended headspace, and freeze. Thaw only a portion needed in the refrigerator. Do not can this syrup or make it shelf-stable by adding alcohol or citric acid.
6. **Use as a food:** Use a little for flavor with food or in a drink. Do not serve honey-containing syrup to children under 12 months. There is no established standard medicinal dose for this homemade syrup.

## Cooking Options

For the current publisher’s uncovered reduction route, use the same complete formula but leave the pan uncovered after bringing it to a boil. Begin checking around 30 minutes and continue until about half the starting liquid remains, following the actual berry product’s cooking requirements. Mash, strain, cool to lukewarm, add the full honey and freeze as above. This replaces the covered 45–60-minute route.

The ginger can instead be the original same-volume dried ginger root, and ground cloves can replace the same listed volume of whole cloves. These are separate source options with different flavor strength; dried ginger root is not a same-volume powdered-ginger substitute.

The pressure-cooker shortcut is unresolved for the actual berry product and cooker. Use the stovetop route rather than assuming a sealed 9-minute cycle is equivalent. The quart-sized storage jar is a container choice, not a measured finished yield.
