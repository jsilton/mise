## Chef's Note

Thick challah or brioche needs time for the custard to reach the center and for that center to cook. Use the soaked bread’s condition to judge the dip, then cook gently enough that the inside sets before the surface burns. Keep all the milk, cream and syrup in the custard, and distribute the full pan butter across the cooking rounds.

## Directions

1. **Mix custard:** In a wide shallow dish, whisk all the eggs, milk, cream, measured maple syrup, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg and salt until no egg-white streaks remain. Keep the additional serving syrup separate.

2. **Coat bread:** Dip each slice on both sides, starting with 20–30 seconds per side for the original ¾–1 inch bread. Lift before it loses its shape; fresh or more porous bread may need a shorter dip. Let excess drip back into the dish and cook promptly. Do not pour leftover raw custard over cooked toast.

3. **Start cooking:** Heat a large nonstick skillet or griddle over medium-low heat. If all the slices fit in one uncrowded round, use all the listed pan butter in that round. Otherwise, use half for the first round and distribute the other half across later rounds. Add only as many slices as fit without crowding, usually 2–3 in the original large skillet.

4. **Cook through:** Start checking the first side at 3–4 minutes, then turn and begin checking the second at 2–3 minutes. Both sides should be golden and the center set, without liquid custard. Insert a food thermometer from the edge toward the center of a thick slice and check 160°F; lower the heat and continue gently if the crust is brown first.

5. **Repeat:** Use all the remaining pan butter across the remaining uncrowded rounds. Wipe away burnt residue between rounds if needed. Keep raw-custard utensils separate from cooked toast and refrigerate the custard if there is a pause; discard any unused bread-contact custard after the final round.

6. **Serve:** Divide the cooked slices among the listed portions. Dust with the separate unmeasured powdered sugar and serve with washed berries and warm additional maple syrup as wanted.
