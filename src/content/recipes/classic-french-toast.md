## Chef's Note

French toast was made to rescue stale bread, and rich, eggy challah or brioche makes the best of it. The bread holds a full soak of custard without falling apart. Cook it over medium-low heat so the center sets into a soft custard before the outside gets too dark.

## Directions

1. **Make the custard:** In a wide, shallow dish, whisk the eggs, milk, cream, maple syrup, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg and salt until no streaks of egg white remain.
2. **Heat the pan:** Heat the oven to 200°F and set a rack on a baking sheet inside. Melt a little of the butter in a large nonstick skillet or griddle over medium-low heat.
3. **Soak the bread:** Dip two or three slices in the custard for 20 to 30 seconds per side, until soaked through but still holding their shape. Let the extra drip back into the dish.
4. **Cook the toast:** Lay the slices in the pan without crowding. Cook until deep golden underneath, 3 to 4 minutes, then flip and cook until the second side is golden and the center springs back when pressed, 2 to 3 minutes more.
5. **Keep warm and repeat:** Move the toast to the rack in the oven. Soak and cook the rest of the bread in batches, adding more butter to the pan each time.
6. **Serve:** Dust with powdered sugar and serve right away with berries and warm maple syrup.
