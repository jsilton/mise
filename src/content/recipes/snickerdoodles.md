## Chef's Note

Mimi (Pat Hamilton)’s cinnamon-sugar cookies use cream of tartar alongside baking soda. The coating has its own sugar allocation: mix it separately, then roll the chilled dough in it before baking.

## Directions

1. **Cream:** Beat unsalted butter, granulated sugar, and large eggs until very light and fluffy, scraping the bowl to combine them evenly.
2. **Mix dry ingredients:** Whisk all-purpose flour, cream of tartar, baking soda, and kosher salt in a separate bowl. Stir into the butter mixture just until no dry flour remains.
3. **Chill:** Cover and refrigerate the dough at 40°F / 4°C or below for 30 minutes, until firm enough to roll. If still too soft to handle, allow more chilling rather than adding flour.
4. **Prepare the coating and oven:** Preheat the oven to 400°F while the dough finishes chilling. Mix granulated sugar and ground cinnamon in a shallow bowl; have baking sheets and a cooling rack ready.
5. **Roll and coat:** Shape the chilled dough into evenly sized walnut-sized balls and roll each in the cinnamon-sugar coating. Place them 2 inches apart on baking sheets. Use all the dough; the listed count is approximate and depends on ball size. Scale the coating with the dough and use more sheets or oven loads when needed, keeping ball size and spacing consistent.
6. **Bake:** Wait for the oven to reach 400°F. Bake for 8–10 minutes per load until the edges are set and the centers are soft but no longer wet. Keep remaining dough refrigerated between loads.
7. **Cool:** Leave cookies on the sheets until firm enough to lift, then transfer to a rack and cool completely before packing.

## Cooking Options

### Mimi’s fat and portion options

Mimi’s original formula allows margarine or butter. To use margarine, replace the full listed butter volume with the same volume of margarine; do not add it alongside the butter. The margarine product can change dough handling and spread. For Mimi’s original flour preparation, sift the flour before measuring the full listed volume (2¾ cups in the original batch), then add it with the cream of tartar, soda and salt. Keep the current main flour preparation above when making that version.

Mimi’s original batch was described as making “70 or more,” while the current original-batch planning count is about 36 cookies. The listed volumes are the same, but the flour preparations differ; count also depends on portion size. Smaller portions can finish sooner; start checking at the original 8-minute point, and use set edges and soft, cooked centers to judge them. Do not enlarge dough balls when increasing the batch.
