## Chef's Note

Kaitlin Gwock’s gratin alternates sweet potatoes and Yukon Golds beneath the full cream and cheese. Keep the slices evenly about 1/8 inch thick. Whisk the seasonings into the cream so every layer gets them; cook until a knife slips through the center layers, and tent the top if it browns first.

## Directions

1. **Mix:** Preheat the oven to 425°F. Whisk all the cream, garlic, thyme, sage, rosemary, nutmeg, cayenne, salt and pepper together.
2. **Slice:** Use a sharp knife or a mandoline with its hand guard to cut the peeled potatoes into even 1/8-inch rounds. Keep the colors separate for layering.
3. **Combine cheese:** Mix all the Gruyère and Parmesan together.
4. **Layer:** For the original batch, use a 9×13-inch oven-safe dish with enough depth for the full potatoes, cream and cheese. If necessary, divide the entire batch among additional dishes before layering, keeping similar depth and headroom. Divide all measured ingredients among the dishes. Pour one-sixth of each dish’s allocated cream over its bottom. Alternate overlapping sweet-potato and white-potato layers, dividing the rest of its cream and cheese through the layers and reserving cheese for its top. Use all the cream; do not leave a fixed-volume remainder behind.
5. **Top:** Arrange the last layer in alternating colors if desired and cover with the reserved cheese.
6. **Start covered:** Cover with foil and bake at 425°F for 15 minutes.
7. **Finish:** Remove the foil and reduce the oven to 400°F. Bake about another 45 minutes, until a knife passes easily through the potato layers in the center of each dish. If the top browns before the potatoes soften, cover loosely with foil and continue checking; thicker slices or deeper layers need longer.
8. **Rest:** Rest for 10 minutes before serving so the layers hold together. Add the optional additional thyme garnish if using it.

## Timing

The original 95-minute estimate includes 25 minutes of preparation, about 60 minutes of baking and the 10-minute rest. Extra loads, thicker slices or deeper dishes can take longer; the center’s tenderness governs.
