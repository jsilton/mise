## Chef's Note

Cook the minced fruit only until it begins to soften, then cool before blending with all the yogurt and honey. Reserve some fruit pieces for texture, keeping every drop of the remaining cooking liquid in the purée. Mold size and freezing conditions determine the final count and time; a short simmer does not guarantee a non-icy pop.

## Directions

1. **Cook the fruit:** Combine all the strawberries, rhubarb, orange juice and salt in a saucepan with room for stirring; a 4-quart pan is the original-batch reference. Cook over medium heat, stirring, until the fruit just begins to break down, about 3–4 minutes. Do not cook toward a fixed thick-syrup volume.
2. **Cool:** Cool the fruit promptly in shallow containers, using an ice-water bath if helpful, before adding dairy or using an unspecified blender. Keep total time outside refrigeration within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C. Refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below if waiting for the next stage.
3. **Reserve and blend:** For the original ten-pop planning batch, lift out about ½ cup of cooked fruit pieces with a slotted spoon; adjust that reserve proportionally with the listed batch. Blend all remaining fruit and all its liquid with the full yogurt and honey until smooth, following the actual blender’s cold-load capacity and instructions. Use separate loads and recombine if needed.
4. **Fill molds:** Stir all the reserved fruit back into the purée. Divide the whole mixture among suitable molds, leaving the manufacturer’s required expansion space. The original mold plan is 8–10 pops; the listed 10 is a planning count, not proof of volume. Use additional molds for a larger batch.
5. **Freeze:** Freeze for about 1 hour, then insert sticks when the mixture can hold them upright; use the mold’s own system if it differs. Freeze at least 3 more hours and longer if needed until solid. Keep the molds frozen at 0°F / −18°C or below.
6. **Unmold:** Briefly run warm water over the mold exterior just until a pop releases. Keep unused pops frozen and serve the released pops immediately.

## Cooking Notes

Whole-milk Greek yogurt may replace the full listed 2% Greek yogurt amount. This changes dairy richness while retaining all the honey, orange juice and fruit. A separate salt option uses ¼ tsp kosher salt for the original batch instead of the sea salt; scale that measure with the whole batch. Different salt crystal sizes are not assumed equivalent by weight.

Three rhubarb stalks is an original-batch count. Cut away and discard leaves, wash the stalks and fruit, then mince. Actual fruit volume, stalk size and mold capacity affect the pop count; use additional molds if the full mixture needs more space.
