## Chef's Note

Greek yogurt, cheddar, butter and Parmesan give this sauce its tangy, rich finish. Cook the cauliflower until soft and blend it fully before tossing with pasta. Keep the sauce warm rather than boiling it hard after the yogurt goes in.

## Directions

1. **Cook the cauliflower:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add fresh or frozen cauliflower and cook about 6–8 minutes, or until easily pierced and crushed with a fork. Frozen florets may take a different time after the water returns to a boil.

2. **Blend the sauce:** Check the actual model’s manual before blending hot food. Use only a vessel and lid expressly approved for hot ingredients, following that model’s fill, venting and speed instructions. Never blend hot food in a sealed personal cup. If the machine is not approved, cool the mixture as its manual requires before blending, then reheat gently; this adds time. Lift the cauliflower out with a slotted spoon or strainer, draining excess water while keeping the pot for optional pasta. Add yogurt, the measured warm milk, cheddar, butter, Parmesan, measured salt and optional garlic powder. Blend according to the machine’s instructions until smooth; recombine any batches.

3. **Cook optional pasta:** If serving with pasta, cook the measured pasta in the cauliflower water according to its package while making the sauce, once the cauliflower is removed. Drain and return the pasta to the pot. Pasta boiling follows cauliflower cooking even though blending can overlap it.

4. **Toss and serve:** Toss the sauce into hot pasta just before serving, or use the sauce on its own as a base. Taste after mixing; add the optional extra Parmesan, pepper and salt only if wanted. Loosen with a little additional warm milk if needed, adding a little at a time. Avoid a hard boil after adding yogurt.

## Cooking Notes

For a thicker, cheesier sauce, double the measured cheddar, from ½ cup to 1 cup in the original 4-cup cauliflower batch. Keep the other ingredients unchanged. Garlic powder remains optional.
