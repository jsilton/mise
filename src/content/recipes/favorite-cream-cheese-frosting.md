## Chef's Note

Use full-fat block cream cheese rather than tub spread. Softened butter and cream cheese should blend smoothly, but neither needs an exact matching temperature. Start the sugar on low speed, then beat until creamy; cream cheese frosting is softer than buttercream and does not promise stiff, intricate piping.

## Directions

1. **Blend:** In a large bowl, beat all the softened cream cheese and butter until smooth and creamy. Use a stand mixer with paddle or whisk, or a handheld mixer. Scrape the bowl and use smoothness rather than an exact two-minute promise.
2. **Add sugar:** On low speed, add all the main confectioners’ sugar, vanilla and salt. Beat about 30 seconds until the sugar is incorporated, then increase speed and beat about 2 minutes or until creamy, scraping as needed.
3. **Adjust:** If a thicker frosting is desired, beat in the separate listed optional extra sugar, not a second main sugar supply. If piping, the publisher suggests chilling the filled piping bag about 20–30 minutes; keep to simple shapes rather than expecting buttercream stiffness.
4. **Use or store:** Frost only completely cooled baked goods. Cover and refrigerate unused frosting promptly. After chilling or refrigerator thawing, allow only brief softening as needed and beat for a few seconds to restore a creamy consistency; keep this time within the cumulative cold-handling limits below.

## Cooking Notes

For taller carrot or red velvet cakes, use the complete frosting formula in that cake recipe. Sally McKenney’s chocolate cream cheese frosting is a separate recipe with its own ingredient amounts and method.
