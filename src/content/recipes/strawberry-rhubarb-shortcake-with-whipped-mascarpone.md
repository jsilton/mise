## Chef's Note

Cold butter pieces and brief handling help these peppercorn shortcakes keep a tender crumb. Roast the strawberries and rhubarb with lemon, thyme and elderflower liqueur, then spoon the cooled fruit and its syrup over the split shortcakes. The mascarpone topping has its own full cream, sugar, vanilla and liqueur allocation; keep it refrigerated until assembly.

## Directions

Allow about 90 minutes for the original batch, including the 30-minute dough chill. Preheat and roast the fruit while the dough chills, then make the topping while the shortcakes bake. Fruit maceration, roasting and cooling may outlast the dough chill, so use the endpoints rather than assuming every stage finishes together.

1. **Make the dough:** Preheat the oven to 350°F and line baking sheets with parchment. Whisk the flour, dough sugar, baking powder, salt and peppercorns. Cut in the cold butter until small visible pieces remain. Stir in all the buttermilk just until large shaggy clumps form.
2. **Shape and chill:** Turn onto the lightly floured work surface and knead gently about 3 times to bring the dough together. For the original six-shortcake batch, pat a 6-by-9-inch rectangle about 1 inch thick and cut six 3-inch squares. When scaling, divide into proportional similar-thickness slabs and pieces rather than forcing all dough into that original rectangle. Refrigerate on the lined sheet for 30 minutes to firm the butter and relax the handled dough.
3. **Prepare and roast fruit:** While the shortcakes chill, toss all the strawberries and rhubarb with the lemon wheels and juice, fruit sugar, fruit liqueur, vanilla seeds and reserved scraped pod, and roasting thyme. Macerate 10–15 minutes. Spread on a lined rimmed sheet and roast in the fully heated 350°F oven for about 20 minutes, until the rhubarb is tender and the juices are syrupy. Thickness and fruit moisture affect the time. Cool the fruit; discard the pod, roasting thyme stems and lemon wheels, keeping all the remaining fruit and syrup.
4. **Bake shortcakes:** Brush the chilled tops lightly with the separate additional cream and sprinkle with all the coarse sugar. Bake at 350°F for 25–30 minutes, rotating the sheet partway through, until golden on top and browned underneath. Use enough sheet area to preserve spacing and add time for extra loads.
5. **Whip the topping:** Have the mascarpone and topping cream at a similar room temperature during prompt preparation. Briefly chill the mixing bowl and whisk attachment. Whip the mascarpone until smooth, then gradually add all the topping cream, topping sugar and vanilla extract, whipping just until light and fluffy with peaks. Slowly mix in all the topping liqueur, then cover and refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below until assembly.
6. **Assemble:** Split the warm shortcakes and distribute the roasted fruit and all its remaining syrup among the servings. Add the mascarpone topping and finishing thyme leaves, then replace the lids. Keep the cream cold until this step and serve promptly.

## Cooking Notes

Heavy cream may replace the entire listed buttermilk amount in the dough; this is a separate richer dough option and does not replace or borrow from the full mascarpone topping cream. Melted butter may replace only the unmeasured additional cream used to brush the tops, keeping all the measured dough butter and coarse sugar.

For the original six-shortcake batch, the fruit option uses 3–5 rhubarb stalks depending on their size instead of the main 3 stalks; keep the complete strawberry, lemon, vanilla, thyme, fruit sugar and fruit liqueur amounts. Scale the whole option proportionally and check fruit tenderness and syrup rather than assuming a fixed stalk weight.

The shortcake is adapted from Grand Central Bakery’s shortcake, and the roasted fruit was inspired by Coley Cooks, as credited by Baking the Goods. Sparkling rosé with a splash of elderflower liqueur is an optional unmeasured accompaniment.
