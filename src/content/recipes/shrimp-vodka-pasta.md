## Chef's Note

Vodka sauce is an Italian-American creation from 1970s New York. A splash of vodka sharpens the tomato and keeps the cream sauce bright rather than heavy. The broccoli and shrimp cook first in the same skillet, so each stays tender, and go back in at the end.

## Directions

1. **Heat the water:** Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil.
2. **Cook the broccoli:** Heat a third of the oil in a 12-inch deep skillet over medium heat. Cook the broccoli, stirring, until bright green and the stems are just tender, 5 to 7 minutes. If the stems are still hard, add a splash of water and cover for a minute. Move it to a plate.
3. **Sear the shrimp:** Add another third of the oil and the shrimp in one layer. Cook until pink and just opaque through, 1 to 2 minutes per side. Move them to the plate.
4. **Boil the pasta:** Add the rigatoni to the boiling water and cook until 1 minute short of al dente. Scoop out a mug of the pasta water, then drain.
5. **Cook the vodka:** While the pasta cooks, lower the skillet to medium-low. Add the rest of the oil and the garlic and stir until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Take the pan off the burner, pour in the vodka, then return it to the heat. Simmer until it no longer smells sharp, 2 to 3 minutes.
6. **Simmer the sauce:** Stir in the marinara, cream, half the basil and the pepper. Simmer gently until the sauce turns a deep orange-pink, about 5 minutes. Taste and add salt as needed, since jarred marinara is often salty.
7. **Toss and serve:** Add the pasta and broccoli and toss over low heat until the sauce clings, about 1 minute, adding pasta water if it gets thick. Fold in the shrimp and their juices until warm. Scatter with the rest of the basil and serve right away.
