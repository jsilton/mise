## Chef's Note

Yu xiang qiezi, fish-fragrant eggplant, is a Sichuan classic with no fish in it. The name comes from the chili bean paste, garlic, ginger and black vinegar that season Sichuan fish. Browning the eggplant and then steaming it under a lid makes it silky without soaking up oil, and a little pork seasons the sauce.

## Directions

1. **Mix the sauce:** Stir the stock, Shaoxing wine, soy sauce and sugar together in a small bowl. In another small bowl, stir the cornstarch into the cold water.
2. **Brown the eggplant:** Heat a third of the oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the eggplant and brown it, turning now and then, 3 to 4 minutes.
3. **Steam until tender:** Turn the heat to medium, add half the water and cover. Cook until a fork slides easily into the center, 3 to 5 minutes, adding a splash more water if the pan goes dry. Uncover, let the water cook off and move the eggplant to a plate.
4. **Cook the second batch:** Repeat with another third of the oil, the rest of the eggplant and the rest of the water.
5. **Brown the pork:** Add the rest of the oil and the pork to the pan over medium-high heat. Cook, breaking it into small pieces, until no pink remains and the edges brown, 4 to 6 minutes.
6. **Fry the aromatics:** Turn the heat to medium-low. Stir in the doubanjiang and ginger and cook until the oil turns red and smells fragrant, about 30 seconds. Add the garlic and scallion whites and stir until fragrant but still pale, 20 to 30 seconds.
7. **Thicken the sauce:** Pour in the sauce mixture and simmer 1 minute. Stir the cornstarch mixture, add half and simmer until glossy, about 30 seconds.
8. **Coat the eggplant:** Return the eggplant to the pan and fold it gently into the sauce. Add the rest of the cornstarch mixture only if the sauce is thin. Cook until the sauce clings, about 1 minute, adding a splash of water if it gets tight.
9. **Finish and serve:** Off the heat, fold in the black vinegar, sesame oil and scallion greens. Serve hot with rice.
