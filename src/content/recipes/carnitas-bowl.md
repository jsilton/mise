## Chef's Note

Carnitas have a strong association with Michoacán. This oven version keeps the pork close together under a tight cover, then returns its rendered fat to the meat before broiling for crisp edges. Stir once between the two broiler passes so new surfaces face the heat. The rice, beans, pickled onions, charred corn and creamy cotija dressing make this a full bowl; the faster ground-pork [Chipotle Pork Bowls](/mise/recipes/chipotle-pork-and-sweet-corn-tamale-bowls) use a different method.

## Directions

1. **Season and refrigerate:** Use pork shoulder and kosher salt: coat the pork chunks with all the measured initial kosher salt. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes, or overnight. Do not leave the raw pork seasoning on the counter.
2. **Arrange and braise:** Preheat the oven to 275°F. Put the seasoned pork close together in an oven-safe dish that holds it snugly without a broad empty area; the original 9×13-inch dish suggestion applies only if the actual meat fits that arrangement. Use appropriately fitted dishes for smaller batches or additional snug vessels for larger batches rather than spreading the same meat over an oversized pan. Add orange, yellow onion, garlic cloves, bay leaves, cinnamon stick, and vegetable oil: squeeze the orange over the meat before nestling its quarters, the onion, garlic, bay and cinnamon among the chunks, then drizzle all the oil over the top. Cover tightly with foil or the dish's oven-safe lid. Bake for 3–3½ hours as a starting range, until the meat pulls apart readily with a fork. Check thick pork chunks in more than one area of each vessel reach at least 145°F / 63°C before removing from heat; allow at least 3 minutes rest before cutting or shredding. That minimum temperature alone does not promise pull-apart shoulder.
3. **Strain and return the fat:** Remove and discard the orange quarters, onion, garlic, bay leaves and cinnamon. Strain the pork, reserving all the cooking liquid. Separate the rendered fat from that liquid and return ALL the separated fat to the meat. Keep the remaining cooking juices separately for another preparation. Cool and refrigerate reserved juices promptly in a shallow container if not using them immediately.
4. **Shred and season:** Pull the pork into large pieces with two forks. Taste the cooked meat, then add salt only as needed; the full measured initial salt has already been used.
5. **Crisp in two passes:** Spread the meat and all its returned fat in a single layer on a broiler-safe rimmed metal sheet pan, using additional pans or loads if needed. Broil, starting to check at 6 minutes, until exposed edges crisp; stir so other surfaces face the heat, then broil again, starting to check within another 6 minutes. Watch the meat throughout and stop when the edges are crisp without drying every piece. Broiler strength and load change the timing.
6. **Dress the prepared corn:** Use ears of corn, mayonnaise, sour cream, 1/2 of the cotija cheese, and ancho chile powder: mix all the mayonnaise, sour cream and chile powder with half the measured cotija, reserving the other half for the bowls. Toss this dressing with all the already-charred corn kernels. Keep this corn/dressing refrigerated until assembly if making it ahead.
7. **Build the bowls:** Divide all the carnitas and dressed corn among the planned portions. Add 1/2 of the cotija cheese, [Cilantro Lime Rice](/mise/recipes/cilantro-lime-rice), [Seasoned Black Beans](/mise/recipes/quick-seasoned-black-beans), [Pickled Red Onions](/mise/recipes/pickled-red-onions), green cabbage, avocados, pepitas, fresh cilantro, and limes: use the prepared rice as the base, add the prepared beans and pickled onions in amounts wanted, then distribute all the measured cabbage, avocado, pepitas, remaining cotija and lime wedges across the bowls with the cilantro. The linked recipes have their own batch yields; this bowl does not specify that a full batch of each is needed.
