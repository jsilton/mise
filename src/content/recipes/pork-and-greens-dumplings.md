## Chef's Note

Sarah of The Woks of Life describes a family dumpling recipe learned while folding with her grandmother. Blanch, squeeze and finely chop the greens, then stir the full rich filling until cohesive. A cooked test dumpling lets you adjust seasoning before wrapping the rest without tasting raw meat.

## Directions

1. **Prepare greens:** Wash the greens and blanch in a large pot of boiling water, about 1 minute for delicate leaves or 2 minutes for cabbage or bok choy as first checks. Cool in an ice bath or under cold running water, drain, squeeze out excess water and chop very finely. Use a food processor in small pulses if desired, scraping and stopping before a puree forms.
2. **Mix filling:** Combine all the prepared greens, chosen ground meat, wine, filling neutral oil, sesame oil, salt, soy sauce, white pepper and measured filling water. Stir vigorously for 6–8 minutes, or up to about 10 minutes if needed, until very well combined and paste-like. Keep raw filling refrigerated at 40°F / 4°C or below except for a small working amount.
3. **Test seasoning:** Wrap a small test dumpling and boil it until its filling reaches 160°F / 71°C for pork or beef, or 165°F / 74°C for chicken. Taste only after it is fully cooked, then adjust the remaining filling if necessary.
4. **Shape:** Dampen each wrapper edge with the separate sealing-water allowance. Put slightly less than 1 tablespoon of filling in the center, fold in half and pinch the top together, then make two folds on each side for a fan shape and pinch to seal. Keep completed dumplings on parchment-lined trays without touching, and keep the main filling and completed trays refrigerated during pauses. Use all filling across enough wrappers; three packages do not establish a wrapper count. Follow package instructions for wrappers. If unused wrapper edges dry, cover them with a damp paper towel in a sealed bag for a couple of hours in the refrigerator before retrying; this adds preparation time.
5. **Freeze if desired:** Cover the separated shaped dumplings on their trays and freeze overnight until solid, then promptly transfer to sealed freezer bags and return to the freezer. Freeze unused wrappers in an airtight bag. This freezing time is additional to the fresh-dumpling total.
6. **Choose one cooking method:** Cook manageable batches. For boiling, bring water to a boil, add dumplings, return to the boil and simmer, beginning checks after 6–8 minutes. For pan-frying, use 2 tbsp of the separate neutral oil for each actual pan load in a non-stick pan over medium-high heat, fry for about 2 minutes, add a thin layer of the separate cooking water, cover, lower to medium-low and steam until the water evaporates. Uncover and raise the heat to crisp the bottoms, adding cooking water again if needed to finish the filling before crisping. This oil allowance follows actual pan loads, not a multiplied recipe-wide dose. For steaming, use the selected lining, keep dumplings separated above simmering water and begin checks after 8–10 minutes. Frozen dumplings need longer; the intervals are first checks.
7. **Check and serve:** For every cooking method and batch, check representative dumpling centers for 160°F / 71°C with pork or beef, or 165°F / 74°C with chicken, and continue cooking until that endpoint is reached. Floating or browned wrappers alone do not establish doneness. Use clean serving utensils and serve with the separately prepared dipping sauce, chili oil or black vinegar as desired.

## Cooking Notes

The original full batch plans 16 portions, about 7–8 dozen dumplings. These are planning estimates; wrapper size, drained greens and filling portions determine the actual count. Buy by actual package contents and keep enough wrappers for the full filling. Scale the full filling supplies together; extra dumplings mean extra cooking loads with their own pan oil and water. The full listed neutral oil and sesame oil for the filling remain distinct from the pan-frying oil.

Keep frozen dumplings at 0°F / −18°C or below; use within 3 months for best taste. Cook from frozen or thaw under refrigeration; a frozen dumpling takes longer than the fresh first-check interval.
