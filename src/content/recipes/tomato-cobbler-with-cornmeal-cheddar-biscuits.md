## Chef's Note

This savory cobbler pairs a tomato-onion filling with cornmeal-Cheddar biscuits, keeping its Southern-style contrast of soft filling and buttery topping. Soften the onions before folding in the tomatoes, and leave gaps between the biscuit mounds for the bubbling filling. The full butter, Cheddar and whole buttermilk make a rich biscuit cap.

## Directions

1. **Prepare oven and pans:** Preheat to 375°F with a rack in the middle. For the original batch, use a 12-inch high-sided oven-safe stainless skillet or well-seasoned cast iron, or prepare the filling in a skillet and transfer it to a 9-by-13-inch/3-quart baking dish. Check room for all filling, dough and bubbling headroom before starting. Larger batches may need several complete, proportionate dishes; smaller batches need similar filling depth and biscuit size, rather than a deep oversized pot.
2. **Soften and brown onions:** Have olive oil, medium red onions, and 1/3 of the salt ready. Warm the oil over medium-high heat, then add the onions and the assigned one third of the filling salt. Stir and cook until the onions are very soft and browned, beginning to check around 20 minutes for this main version. Lower the heat if they scorch; the clock does not guarantee mahogany color.
3. **Add garlic and wine:** Have garlic cloves and red wine ready. Stir the garlic into the onions for 30–60 seconds until fragrant, then add the wine and cook until it has mostly evaporated.
4. **Thicken filling:** Have cherry tomatoes, 2/3 of the salt, and all-purpose flour ready. Stir the filling flour into the onions until paste-like, then remove from the heat. Gently fold in all the tomatoes and the remaining two thirds of the filling salt. Transfer the full mixture to the chosen baking dish if the skillet is not the baking vessel.
5. **Cut in butter:** Have all-purpose flour, cornmeal, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and unsalted butter ready. Pulse the flour, cornmeal, baking powder, baking soda and biscuit salt together in a food processor. Add the cold butter and pulse until it remains in pea-sized pieces. If the processor cannot hold the batch comfortably, mix in a bowl and use fingers or a pastry cutter to cut in the full butter instead.
6. **Mix biscuits:** Transfer to a bowl and stir in 2/3 of the sharp Cheddar and whole buttermilk, gently mixing until no dry flour remains and a shaggy, sticky dough forms. Reserve the other third of the cheese for the top.
7. **Top filling:** Drop all the dough in walnut-sized mounds over the tomatoes, leaving gaps rather than sealing the surface. For multiple dishes, distribute all filling and dough proportionally first. Brush the tops lightly with additional buttermilk. Set each baking vessel on an oven-safe baking sheet to catch drips.
8. **Bake:** Begin baking. The original batch takes about 55–60 minutes in total, with the following cheese finish included in that window. Changed dough size or dish depth can alter the time; look for bubbling tomatoes and cooked biscuit centers rather than following the clock alone.
9. **Finish cheese:** About 10 minutes before the expected end of baking, scatter 1/3 of the sharp Cheddar over the biscuits and return to the oven. Treat this as part of the preceding baking window, not an additional 10 minutes after the cobbler is done. Finish until the tomatoes bubble strongly and biscuits are browned and cooked through, with no gummy raw dough in the center.
10. **Rest:** Let rest for at least 15 minutes before serving so the hot tomato filling settles.

## Larger Biscuit and Buttermilk Options

For the larger-biscuit version, the original batch uses 3½–4 pounds of tomatoes and ¾–1 cup buttermilk: the smaller milk amount makes firmer biscuits, while the full cup makes a looser dough. Keep every other measured filling and biscuit supply, including all the butter, both salt components, leaveners and Cheddar. Drop all the dough as 7–8 larger biscuits for that original batch, leaving gaps and brushing with the additional buttermilk; at another scale, keep the biscuit size similar and adjust the number of pieces to use the full dough. Use the same 375°F oven and 55–60-minute original-batch first-check window, finishing by bubbling tomatoes and cooked biscuit centers.

For softer onions with less browning, cook until very tender for at least 10 minutes. For deeper caramelization, lower the heat and allow another 20–30 minutes as needed before the garlic and wine stage. Both routes keep the full oil and filling salt.

Two tablespoons balsamic vinegar can replace the ¼ cup red wine for the original batch; scale that replacement proportionally and use it in the same garlic-and-liquid stage, without adding both. Biscuits can be mixed entirely in a bowl using fingers or a pastry cutter for the butter. A well-seasoned cast-iron pan is the cast-iron choice for acidic tomatoes; use high-sided stainless steel or the baking-dish route if preferred.
