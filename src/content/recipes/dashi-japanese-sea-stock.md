## Chef's Note

Kombu and dried bonito flakes make a combined, seafood-based dashi for Japanese soups and simmered dishes. Remove the kombu before the stock boils strongly, then give the bonito a brief hot extraction and strain gently. The measured water is the starting amount; absorption and evaporation change the finished volume.

## Directions

1. **Soak:** Put all the measured cold water and kombu in a saucepan with room for the flakes. Let stand about 10 minutes. Set a strainer over a clean vessel large enough for the finished liquid.

2. **Heat and remove kombu:** Heat over medium-high until bubbles begin forming and the liquid is close to boiling. Remove the kombu before a sustained boil. This is a short extraction, not a prolonged kombu simmer.

3. **Add bonito:** Open the measured packages of plain dried bonito flakes and sprinkle all the flakes over the liquid. Let the liquid just reach a boil, then immediately take the pan off the heat.

4. **Steep:** Let stand about 3 minutes. Flakes may settle, but complete sinking is not a required endpoint or a reason to keep boiling.

5. **Strain:** Pour carefully through a fine-mesh sieve, or a coffee-filter-lined sieve, into the waiting vessel. Let it drain without squeezing the flakes. Discard spent kombu/flakes, or cool and refrigerate them promptly and separately if saving them for another recipe. Possible separate uses are kombu for pickled Napa cabbage and bonito flakes for rice with soy-glazed bonito flakes and sesame seeds; this stock method does not supply their pickling or seasoning instructions.

6. **Use or cool:** Use the strained dashi promptly in the dish being prepared. If saving it, divide into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly; do not leave a large bowl of warm stock out until completely cold. Measure the strained amount before using it in a dish that calls for a fixed stock volume.

## Quick Off-Heat Variation

For a version without the initial soak, put the measured cold water and kombu in a saucepan and heat just until the water begins to boil. Take the pan off the heat and remove the kombu. Add all the measured bonito flakes and steep off heat for about 3 minutes; if necessary, stir gently to wet floating flakes. Strain as in step 5. This route omits the later bonito boil as well as the soak, and uses the same ingredient amounts. Actual heating time still depends on the pot and starting water.
