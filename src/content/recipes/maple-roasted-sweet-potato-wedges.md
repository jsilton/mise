## Chef's Note

Brown the sweet potato wedges on their cut faces before brushing on the maple syrup. The late glaze gives a sticky, sweet finish with less time to scorch; it still needs close attention during the final 8–10 minutes. Check a thick center before glazing and at the end so browned sugar is not mistaken for a cooked potato.

## Directions

1. **Heat and cut:** Heat the oven to 425°F. Use a large rimmed baking sheet or enough sheets for the measured wedges; if lining, use parchment rated for this heat or suitable foil. Scrub the sweet potatoes under running water, leave the skins on and cut lengthwise into wedges about ½ inch thick, with a skin edge and flat cut faces. Dry the cut surfaces. Make wedges rather than parallel planks.
2. **Coat and arrange:** Toss sweet potatoes, olive oil, kosher salt, and black pepper in a large bowl until the wedges are evenly coated. Arrange in one layer on a flat cut face, with space between wedges. The measured oil and seasonings coat the full batch, not each sheet.
3. **Brown the first face:** Roast for 18–20 minutes. Lift a wedge to check the underside for golden browning; test a thick center for softening. If still hard, continue the unglazed roast before adding maple. The final stage should finish nearly tender wedges, rather than leave syrup to scorch while a raw center cooks.
4. **Turn and brush:** Turn the wedges onto another cut face and set out pure maple syrup and smoked paprika (if using). Brush the syrup across all the wedges, using the whole measured maple amount for the full batch. Sprinkle the optional paprika over them if using. Keep the thin glaze on the wedges rather than pouring a pool onto the empty pan.
5. **Finish and watch the maple:** Return to the oven for 8–10 minutes, checking the maple closely. Centers should be easily pierced and the glaze bubbling and browned, without blackened sugar. Remove ready wedges sooner if they darken quickly. The two stages normally total 26–30 minutes, but thicker pieces or extra sheets may need longer before glazing.
6. **Taste and serve:** Transfer to a serving dish and use flaky sea salt if needed after tasting. Serve warm; expect tender centers and browned, sticky edges rather than guaranteed fry-like crispness.
