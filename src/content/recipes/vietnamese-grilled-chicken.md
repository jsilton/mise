## Chef's Note

Finely prepared lemongrass gives aroma without tough stalk pieces. Fish sauce supplies the main salt seasoning, while a little sugar encourages browning. This home version includes ginger and lime; it does not promise to reproduce every Vietnamese street-stall preparation. Deep blackening is not the goal, and thigh meat can still dry if overcooked.

## Directions

1. **Prepare and marinate:** Trim and mince the tender lemongrass core as described. Open the chicken thighs flat; gently even unusually thick folded areas to roughly 3/4 inch. Mix fish sauce, lime juice, brown sugar, marinade oil, lemongrass, garlic, ginger and optional chili. Coat chicken, cover and refrigerate thirty minutes, or up to two hours. Keep its original raw-meat storage limit.
2. **Heat and drain:** Heat a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Lift half the chicken from excess marinade, letting it drip off; keep the second half refrigerated. Add 1/2 tsp of the cooking oil to the skillet. Discard marinade after removing the chicken; do not serve it raw.
3. **Cook the first round:** Lay thighs flat with space and cook about four minutes on the first side. Turn, reduce heat to medium as color develops, and cook another four minutes. Continue turning as needed, checking each thick center from the side until it reaches at least 165°F / 74°C, usually about 10–12 minutes total for this thickness. Do not remove at 160°F on the assumption that resting will finish it.
4. **Repeat:** Transfer cooked pieces to a clean plate and cover loosely. Carefully wipe away any burned residue, add the remaining 1/2 tsp cooking oil and cook the second half with the same measured endpoint. The first round has a short hold and will lose some surface crispness.
5. **Rest and finish:** Let the final cooked pieces rest five minutes. Slice across the meat, divide into four portions and add the listed cilantro and scallions. Rice and a vegetable side are additional components.

## Outdoor grill option

Preheat a clean grill to medium-high with a cooler zone available. Use the measured teaspoon cooking oil to brush the drained thighs. Grill with space, turning periodically and moving to the cooler zone if the coating darkens quickly. Measure each center to at least 165°F / 74°C before removing it. A single full batch may take about 12–15 minutes, depending on thickness and heat; use the thermometer rather than that estimate.
