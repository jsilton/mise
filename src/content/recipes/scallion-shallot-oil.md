## Chef's Note

One small pan yields a cooking oil and a finishing garnish. They need different treatment at the table: use the strained oil where you want an allium aroma, and add solids near serving so they retain their texture. The yield varies with slicing, frying and draining; measure what you actually recover rather than treating the starting oil as the finished amount.

## Directions

1. **Set up before heating:** Slice shallots evenly and dry the washed scallions thoroughly. Place a fine metal sieve over a stable dry heatproof bowl and line a tray with paper towels. Choose a small heavy saucepan with enough headroom for bubbling; ingredients and oil should occupy well under half its depth.
2. **Begin with shallots:** Put oil, shallots and optional ginger in the cold pan. Heat over medium until steady bubbles develop, stirring to separate rings. Reduce to medium-low as needed to maintain a gentle fry without smoking. Cook about 8–12 minutes, until shallots soften and begin to lose their raw white appearance.
3. **Add scallions and watch closely:** Add the dry sliced scallion whites and pale greens carefully. Continue frying about 5–10 minutes, stirring frequently, until shallots are pale golden and scallion pieces begin to color. Lift out any golden pieces early with a dry slotted spoon; do not wait for every piece to turn the same shade. If pieces are still pale and wet, allow more time at controlled heat rather than raising it sharply.
4. **Separate promptly:** Turn off the heat and carefully pour through the metal sieve. Spread the drained solids on the lined tray, discard ginger slices and sprinkle the measured salt over the alliums. Let them cool in one thin layer. Keep the hot oil on a stable surface out of the working area until safe to handle.
5. **Measure and store:** Expect roughly 180–210 ml strained oil and about 1/2 cup loose fried solids, with variation. Once cool enough to package, place oil and solids in separate clean, dry containers, date them and refrigerate promptly. Use within four days or freeze as directed; do not leave the garnish on the counter for several days.

## Using the batch

[Chicken Lo Mein](/mise/recipes/lo-mein) uses 3 tbsp pan oil, with its marinade oil separate. About 180 ml recovered oil supplies four such 45 ml allocations, not four dinners from one-third of a cup. The lo mein works fully with neutral oil, so this condiment is optional and its fifty-minute preparation should not be hidden in the dinner time. Start with a teaspoon over a bowl of rice or vegetables and increase to taste. Keep fried solids out of prolonged high-heat searing.
