## Chef's Note

Aromatic oils are the quiet backbone of Cantonese and Vietnamese cooking, spooned over poached chicken or stirred into noodles. One pan gives you two things: a fragrant cooking oil and a crunchy garnish. Starting the shallots in cold oil lets their moisture cook off slowly, so they crisp all the way through.

## Directions

1. **Set up the strainer:** Set a fine metal sieve over a heatproof bowl and line a tray with paper towels.
2. **Fry the shallots:** Combine the oil, shallots and ginger in a small heavy saucepan. Heat over medium until the oil bubbles steadily, then lower the heat to medium-low. Cook, stirring often, until the shallots soften and turn translucent, 8 to 10 minutes.
3. **Add the scallions:** Add the scallions and keep frying, stirring often, until the bubbling slows and the shallots turn pale gold, 5 to 8 minutes. Lift out any pieces that color early.
4. **Strain:** Take the pan off the heat the moment the shallots are pale gold and pour everything through the sieve. They darken a shade or two as they cool.
5. **Drain and salt:** Pick out the ginger. Spread the crisp alliums in a thin layer on the paper towels and sprinkle with the salt while they are still glossy. Let the oil and alliums cool completely before storing.
