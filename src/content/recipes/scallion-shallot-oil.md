## Chef's Note

Aromatic oils are the quiet backbone of Cantonese and Vietnamese cooking — scallion oil spooned over poached chicken, shallot oil stirred into noodles, the crisp fried bits saved as garnish. One pan gives you two ingredients: a fragrant cooking fat and a crunchy finish. Start the aromatics in cold oil rather than hot; the moisture cooks off gradually and they crisp all the way through instead of browning outside and staying limp inside.

## Directions

1.  **Slice thin and even:** Shallots should be paper-thin rings, scallion whites cut into thin coins. Uneven pieces burn at different rates, and burnt shallot turns the whole batch bitter.
2.  **Cold start:** Combine the oil, shallots, scallion whites, and ginger in a small saucepan while the pan is still cold. The aromatics should be just submerged.
3.  **Slow fry (12-15 min):** Set the heat to medium. When the oil starts to bubble steadily, drop to medium-low. Stir every few minutes so nothing clumps on the bottom. The bubbling will be vigorous at first (that's water leaving), then quiet down — that's your cue the end is near.
4.  **Pull early:** The moment the shallots turn pale gold — not brown — strain everything through a fine-mesh sieve into a heatproof bowl. Carryover heat darkens them another shade or two in the sieve. Waiting for brown in the pan means burnt in the bowl.
5.  **Drain and salt:** Spread the crisp bits on a paper towel in a single layer and salt them immediately while they're still glossy with oil.
6.  **Store:** Cool completely. The oil keeps 2 weeks in a sealed jar in the fridge (it will cloud when cold — that's normal, it clears at room temperature). Keep the crisp bits at room temperature in an airtight container for up to 3 days; refrigeration makes them soggy.

## How Much to Make

**This is a base, not a step.** A half-cup batch yields about 1/3 cup of strained oil, which is roughly four dinners' worth — one batch of [lo mein](lo-mein) needs 3 tablespoons. Making a third of a cup of oil for a single dish leaves you with most of it unused and a sink full of extra dishes.

**If you'd rather make it à la minute:** use 3 tablespoons oil, 1 shallot, and the whites of 4 scallions in the smallest pan you own. It takes about 8 minutes and yields close to 2 tablespoons of strained oil plus a garnish's worth of crisp bits — one dish, no leftovers.

## Where Else to Use It

- Tossed through plain rice or steamed noodles with a splash of soy
- Fried eggs — swap it for the butter
- Drizzled over steamed fish or [steamed bok choy](steamed-bok-choy-with-oyster-sauce)
- As the searing fat for any protein headed into a stir-fry
