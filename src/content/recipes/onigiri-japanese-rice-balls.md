## Chef's Note

Onigiri are Japanese rice balls, shaped with lightly salted wet hands and filled with tuna mayonnaise, pickled plum, salmon or sesame salt. Use warm rice that is comfortable to handle, enclosing a modest filling rather than overpacking the pocket. Keep the nori separate for a crisp wrapper, and keep perishable rice balls cold when preparing them ahead or packing them for later.

## Directions

1. **Cook the Rice:** Use the listed volume of cooked Japanese short-grain rice. If starting from dry rice, follow the actual rice or rice-cooker instructions and measure the cooked output; do not assume a cup of dry rice produces the required cooked volume or shares a universal water ratio. Allow the package-directed covered rest after cooking, then use warm rice that is comfortable to handle. Cooking and resting from dry rice are extra time.

2. **Prepare the Work Surface:** Have a small bowl of water nearby. Wash your hands, then wet them slightly using clean water. Use small pinches from the listed kosher-salt supply across the whole batch, keeping it separate from the sesame filling’s sea salt. Rewet only as needed.

3. **Form the Triangle (Basic Shape):** Divide the full rice among the listed rice-ball portions; the original four-ball batch uses about half a cup per ball. Make a small indent for the chosen filling. For tuna, mix its full listed tuna, mayonnaise and soy sauce supplies, spoon about one tablespoon into each original-size rice pocket, and refrigerate any unused prepared mixture promptly rather than overfilling. For umeboshi, use one of the listed pitted plums per original-size ball. For salmon or sesame salt, mix the full listed supplies for that one choice and divide the mixture across the listed rice-ball portions; the sesame mixture is smaller than a tablespoon per ball. Choose one filling batch, or plan proportional smaller filling batches for a variety; do not combine all four ingredient options into every ball.

4. **Encase the Filling:** Cup your hand to gently bring rice up and around the filling, enclosing it completely. You should have a ball of rice with filling inside. Squeeze gently but firmly - you want it compact enough to hold together but not so tight that it becomes dense.

5. **Shape into Triangle:** Place the rice ball on your work surface. Using both hands (re-wet and use the remaining listed hand-seasoning salt only as needed), gently shape it into a triangle by applying even pressure on three sides. The shape comes from your hands, not from pressing into a mold. A triangular onigiri is iconic and practical - it sits flat and is easy to wrap.

6. **Wrap with Nori:** Cut nori into strips about 1 inch wide and 3-4 inches long. Wrap one strip around the middle of the onigiri, like a belt. The nori will stick to the rice from its slight moisture. If wrapping right before serving, the nori stays crispy; if you wrap earlier, it softens slightly from the rice moisture.

7. **Garnish and Store:** Sprinkle with sesame seeds if desired. Serve warm or at room temperature. For make-ahead service, refrigerate promptly at 40°F or below and transport with an ice pack. Count preparation and service together: discard perishable onigiri left out over 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. If frozen, thaw in the refrigerator rather than on the counter. Keep nori separate until serving for crispness.

**Filling Ideas:** Keep fillings minimal - a single flavor is more traditional. Tuna mayo, umeboshi (pickled plum), grilled salmon, kombu (kelp), or just sesame salt all work beautifully.

**Handling:** Use rice warm enough to shape but comfortable against your hands. Keep the hand-seasoning salt separate from the chosen filling salt, and avoid handling another food or raw ingredients with unwashed hands.

**Nori Texture:** For maximum crispness, wrap nori just before serving. For softer nori, wrap 30 minutes ahead and let the moisture from the rice soften it slightly.
