## Chef's Note

Onigiri are the rice balls of Japanese lunchboxes and corner stores. The salt goes on your wet hands, not into the rice, so the outside is seasoned and the inside stays soft. Tuna with Japanese mayonnaise is one of the most popular fillings, and the nori goes on last so it stays crisp.

## Directions

1. **Mix the filling:** Stir the tuna, mayonnaise and soy sauce together in a small bowl.
2. **Salt your hands:** Set a small bowl of water beside the rice. Wet your hands and rub a pinch of the salt over your palms.
3. **Fill the rice:** Scoop a portion of warm rice about the size of a tennis ball into one palm. Press a deep dent in the center with your thumb and spoon in a little of the filling.
4. **Shape the triangle:** Bring the rice up around the filling to close it in. Press it into a triangle with cupped hands, turning it a few times, until it holds together but isn't dense. Wet and salt your hands again before each ball.
5. **Wrap and serve:** Just before serving, wrap a nori strip around the base of each triangle and up over both faces. Sprinkle with sesame seeds, if using.
