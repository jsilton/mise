## Chef's Note

This paprika-coated chicken is adapted from Jimmy Bannos Jr.'s recipe published by Food & Wine. Its yogurt and dried chiles make a separate serving sauce; the chicken itself receives paprika, oil and seasoning. This adaptation uses two tablespoons coating oil instead of the original half cup, while keeping the sauce's tablespoon; the lighter coating aims to reduce excess dripping, but skin still renders fat. Expose the skin to steady heat and keep a cooler part of the grill available, because rendering fat can flare up before the centers finish.

## Directions

1. **Start the grill:** For gas, preheat with the lid closed, then set a cooking zone to medium and leave another burner low or off. For charcoal, light the coals and let them establish before arranging them on one side for a direct-heat zone and a cooler side. Follow the grill's lighting instructions. Begin the chicken and sauce while it heats.
2. **Cut and season the chicken:** Pat the boneless thighs dry without rinsing. Trim loose excess fat, keeping the attached skin. Cut about twenty-four pieces roughly 1 1/2 inches across, with similar thickness and no thick rolled bundles. Toss with the chicken's two tablespoons olive oil, paprika, six grams fine salt and half the pepper. Cover and refrigerate for fifteen minutes while making the sauce.
3. **Mix a separate sauce:** With clean hands and utensils, whisk yogurt, its tablespoon olive oil, lemon juice, the first quarter-teaspoon cayenne, red pepper flakes, sauce salt and remaining pepper in a clean bowl. Taste and add the extra cayenne only if wanted. Cover and refrigerate. Do not dip raw-chicken tools into this bowl or use its serving spoon for the skewers.
4. **Thread and oil the grate:** Divide chicken among eight skewers, about three pieces each, keeping pieces unfolded and skin facing outward rather than tucked between meat. Do not compress pieces tightly; leave exposed surfaces available for browning. Let excess oily coating drip back into its bowl and discard the residue. Brush clean grill grates lightly with the measured teaspoon cooking oil, using a folded paper towel held in long tongs. Put out a clean serving platter and clean cooked-food utensils.
5. **Grill and measure:** Lay the skewers over moderate direct heat with space between them, starting with the exposed skin toward the grate where possible. Grill about ten to fifteen minutes, turning every two to three minutes after the first surface releases. Move to the cooler zone if skin fat flares or paprika darkens sharply. Close the lid between turns. Begin checking around ten minutes: probe the thickest pieces on every skewer from the side, away from the metal, and continue until they reach at least 165°F / 74°C. Check any unusually large pieces separately. Remove finished skewers rather than waiting for all to reach the endpoint together.
6. **Rest and serve:** Transfer to the clean platter and rest five minutes. Serve two skewers per person with the sauce and lemon wedges. The sauce yields roughly one cup plus the oil and lemon additions; offer about a quarter-cup per diner, with any extra on the side. For four plates, add one full four-side batch of either couscous recipe and a vegetable or salad. Those are additional components and preparation time; the two couscous recipes are alternative starches.
