## Chef's Note

This silky soup comes from Bon Appétit. The carrots and fennel cook slowly in butter, covered, for nearly an hour before any broth goes in. That long, gentle sweat draws out their sugar and gives the soup real depth. Brown-butter chestnuts and a swirl of maple crème fraîche finish each bowl.

## Directions

1. **Sweat the vegetables:** Melt two-thirds of the butter in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add the fennel, onion, carrots, potato, thyme and bay leaf, season with salt and pepper and stir to coat. Lower the heat to medium-low, cover and cook, stirring now and then, until the vegetables are very soft but not browned, 45 to 60 minutes. Turn the heat down if anything starts to color.
2. **Make the maple cream:** While the vegetables cook, whisk the crème fraîche and maple syrup in a small bowl and chill.
3. **Simmer the soup:** Add the broth and bring to a boil. Lower the heat and simmer until the potato falls apart under a spoon, about 10 minutes. Remove the thyme stems and bay leaf.
4. **Purée the soup:** Off the heat, blend with an immersion blender until very smooth. Or blend in batches in a countertop blender filled no more than halfway, with the lid vented and covered with a towel. For the silkiest texture, press it through a fine-mesh sieve back into the pot. Taste and season with salt and pepper.
5. **Brown the chestnuts:** Melt the rest of the butter in a small saucepan over medium heat. Add the chestnuts and cook, stirring often, until the butter smells nutty and turns golden brown, about 4 minutes.
6. **Serve the soup:** Ladle the soup into bowls. Top each with a swirl of maple cream and a spoonful of the chestnuts and their brown butter.
