## Chef's Note

Aloo gobi pairs potatoes and cauliflower in an Indian vegetable dish that can be dry or saucy. Here, a small amount of tomato forms a thick spice coating. Start the potatoes first, cover to retain enough steam for tenderness, and add the cauliflower later so its florets keep their shape.

## Directions

1. **Prepare everything:** Wash, trim, weigh and cut the vegetables as listed. One large or two smaller cauliflower heads may be needed; weigh the florets after trimming. Drain the cauliflower well. Lightly crush the coriander seeds so there are no large, hard whole seeds. Measure the spices and divide the cooking water into two equal portions; keep the separate hot-water reserve aside. Finish all cutting and measuring before heating the oil.
2. **Start the whole spices:** Heat all the measured oil in the deep pan over medium heat. Add cumin and crushed coriander seeds and stir for about **20–30 seconds**, until they sizzle and smell fragrant. Lower the heat if they darken quickly; do not let them burn.
3. **Soften the onion and aromatics:** Add the onion and cook for **6–8 minutes**, stirring, until soft and beginning to turn pale gold. Add garlic, ginger and the slit serranos and stir for **30–60 seconds**, until fragrant. For the milder powdered-chile option, leave the serranos out and add its cayenne in the next step.
4. **Build the tomato coating:** Lower the heat to medium-low. Stir in turmeric, ground coriander and the cayenne if using the milder powdered-chile option for about **20 seconds**, then promptly add the tomato and its juice. Cook for about **5–7 minutes**, stirring, until the tomato softens and the mixture looks thick rather than watery. Do not insist on dry-frying ground spices when they are beginning to catch.
5. **Give the potatoes a head start:** Add the potatoes and the initial fine salt, turn to coat for **1–2 minutes**, then pour in the **first half of the cooking water**. Bring the liquid to a gentle simmer, cover tightly and cook over medium-low for about **12–18 minutes**, gently turning once or twice. Test a cube: it should be partly cooked, with a tender exterior and some resistance at the center. If still hard all the way through, continue covered, adding 1 tbsp corrective hot water if the pan is dry, before adding cauliflower.
6. **Add cauliflower and finish cooking:** Fold in the cauliflower and the **remaining half of the cooking water**. Let the pan return to a gentle simmer, then cover and cook about **15–20 minutes**, gently turning every five minutes. A knife should enter both a potato center and a cauliflower stem easily. If the pan dries before that point, use the corrective water a tablespoon at a time, within its separate listed allowance. Continue beyond the estimate when needed; avoid vigorous stirring that breaks up the florets.
7. **Dry the coating and finish:** Uncover and cook over medium heat for about **3–5 minutes**, folding gently, until no loose liquid collects underneath. Stir in garam masala and the optional finishing cayenne and cook for **30 seconds**. Turn off the heat, add the lemon juice gradually and fold in cilantro. Taste and use the reserved fine salt only as needed. Remove the slit chiles before serving if you do not want a diner to bite into one.
8. **Serve:** Divide into four main portions with bread, or six generous vegetable sides alongside dal. If serving with rice, remember that the potatoes already provide a substantial starch component; offer rice in smaller portions.
