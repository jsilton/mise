## Chef's Note

Baingan bharta often begins with fire-roasted eggplant. This pressure-cooked version builds its flavor from golden onion, cumin, garlic and warm spices. Keep the eggplant and tomato juices in the pot, then simmer them down until the mash is glossy and thick enough to scoop with bread. Cilantro and a little lemon brighten the finish.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Peel and weigh the eggplant, then cut into even 1-inch cubes. Chop the onion and tomatoes, keeping all tomato juice. Measure the spices and the separate quantities of water. Keep the tomatoes aside for the top layer.
2. **Sauté the onion:** Heat all 4 tbsp oil using the cooker's moderate sauté setting. Add cumin and stir about 30 seconds until fragrant and a shade darker. Add onion and the initial 1 tsp salt. Cook about 8–12 minutes, stirring often, until softened with golden edges. Lower the heat if edges brown before the onion softens; do not add more oil.
3. **Bloom spices and deglaze:** Lower the sauté heat. Add garlic, ginger, coriander, garam masala, black pepper, turmeric, paprika and cayenne. Stir about 30 seconds, then cancel sauté and pour in all 375 ml pressure-cooking water. Scrape the entire base with a wooden spatula for 1–2 minutes, until it feels smooth with no stuck patches. The onion mixture should be loose and watery, not a paste.
4. **Load the vegetables:** Spread the eggplant over the deglazed base. Spoon all the tomatoes and their juices on top. Do not stir them down to the bottom. Check that everything is below the cooker's pressure maximum and clear of its lid fittings.
5. **Pressure-cook and release naturally:** Close and seal according to the cooker instructions. Cook on high pressure for 7 minutes; the countdown starts after the cooker reaches pressure. Turn Keep Warm off when cooking ends and leave the valve sealed until pressure has fallen completely on its own, usually 20–30 minutes or longer. Do not quick-release or force the lid open. Open only when the pressure indicator has dropped and the model's instructions confirm it is fully depressurized.
6. **Check and mash:** Test eggplant cubes from the middle and edge of the pot; they should collapse readily under a fork. If firm, simmer gently in the retained liquid until soft. Mash everything together with a potato masher, leaving a little texture. Keep all the liquid in the pot.
7. **Reduce patiently:** Select a sauté setting that maintains a steady uncovered simmer. Cook about 20–35 minutes, stirring and scraping the base often, until the mash is glossy and cohesive and a spoonful has no watery rim. It may need longer with especially juicy vegetables or a gentle cooker. Lower the heat and stir more frequently as it thickens; do not drain off the juices to hurry it. Taste only after reduction, then add some or all of the reserved 1/2 tsp salt if wanted.
8. **Finish and serve:** Cancel the heat and fold in cilantro and 2 tsp lemon juice. Taste before adding the remaining teaspoon lemon juice. Divide into four vegetable-main portions and serve with separately prepared basmati rice or naan and a little cucumber raita. Final volume varies with the vegetables and reduction; expect approximately 4–6 cups.
