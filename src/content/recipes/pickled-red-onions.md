## Chef's Note

Thin slicing makes a quick pickle practical: the brine reaches a greater share of each slice in a short time. The result is a tart refrigerated condiment, not a fermented onion or a shelf-stable preserve. Drain the portion you use so its vinegar accents a taco without soaking the bread.

## Directions

1. **Slice and pack:** Peel and rinse the onion, trim its ends and slice about 1/16–1/8 inch thick. Pack into a clean heatproof pint jar, leaving roughly 1/2 inch at the top.
2. **Heat the brine:** Put the measured vinegar, water, sugar and salt in a small saucepan. Bring to a simmer, stirring until dissolved, then remove from heat.
3. **Cover the onion:** Pour hot brine over the onion and gently press it down with a clean spoon. Leave about 1/2 inch headspace. Use a narrower container or additional same-ratio brine if coverage is inadequate; do not add plain water.
4. **Refrigerate and develop:** Cover with a clean lid and refrigerate promptly on a heatproof pad, with space around the jar. After about thirty minutes, taste a drained slice once cool enough. It will still have some raw bite; longer refrigerated time, ideally until the next day, makes the flavor more even.
5. **Use:** Lift out only the needed portion with a clean fork and drain. The full batch yields approximately 1 1/2 cups drained onions; use a tablespoon or two as a condiment, leaving the rest submerged and refrigerated.
