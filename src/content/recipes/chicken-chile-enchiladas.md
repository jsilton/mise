## Chef's Note

These red-sauce enchiladas use corn tortillas warmed briefly in oil before dipping and rolling. Look for a tortilla that bends easily, then divide the filling among all the rolls and keep the full cheese amount for their centers and topping. The soft-tortilla option below uses the same chicken, pepper, chiles and cheese with a different assembly method.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the filling and dish:** Preheat the oven to 375°F. Choose an oven-safe baking dish that fits all the rolled tortillas in a single layer without stacking, or additional dishes for a larger batch. Keep cooked chicken refrigerated until working with it. Toss cooked shredded chicken, red bell pepper, and diced green chiles together in a bowl. Cooking raw chicken or making homemade sauce is a separate preparation, outside this assembly clock.
2. **Soften the corn tortillas:** Use corn tortillas and cooking oil: heat the splash of oil in a small skillet and briefly warm each tortilla in it, starting at about 5 seconds per side and continuing only until it bends readily. Work one at a time; do not crisp the tortilla so it breaks when rolled. Transfer each warm tortilla for filling.
3. **Dip, fill, roll and cover:** Use red enchilada sauce and Mexican blend cheese for dipping, filling and topping. Dip each warm tortilla into the sauce, divide all the prepared chicken filling among the measured tortillas, add a little of the cheese inside each and roll seam-side down in the dish. Pour all the remaining sauce over the rolls and distribute all the remaining cheese on top. Use up the full measured cheese and sauce between the centers and topping.
4. **Bake and check the center:** Bake at 375°F, starting to check after 15–20 minutes. The cheese should be melted and the sauce bubbling, but also check the filling in the center of the dish with a thermometer: it must reach 165°F / 74°C. If it is still cool inside, continue baking; cover if the surface is browning too much. Cold chicken, dish depth and extra dishes can extend the time.
5. **Serve with the garnishes:** Serve with sour cream, fresh cilantro, and sliced jalapeños. Keep the garnishes cold until serving.

## Soft-tortilla option

Use the same number of taco-size soft tortillas instead of corn tortillas and omit the flash-frying oil. Spread a thin layer from the measured sauce over the bottom of the dish, then divide all the measured chicken, pepper and green chiles among the tortillas. Put about 2 tbsp cheese and 2 tbsp sauce inside each taco-size tortilla, using only the measured totals, then roll seam-side down and put all remaining cheese and sauce on top. For the original eight taco-size soft tortillas, use one 9×13-inch or two 8×8-inch dishes only if the rolls actually fit. Use suitably fitted dishes for a different batch size, with the same single-layer arrangement. Bake at 375°F, starting at 15–20 minutes, and verify the filling reaches 165°F / 74°C. Mexican blend, Monterey Jack, cheddar or a mixture can supply all the measured cheese; the original batch uses 12 oz.

## Cooking Notes

For freezing and reheating, use a baking dish whose manufacturer permits the intended temperature changes. Follow its instructions for moving between freezer, refrigerator and oven.

You can freeze assembled, unbaked enchiladas or freeze them after baking and promptly chilling. Wrap well and freeze for up to 3 months for quality. For frozen unbaked enchiladas, the soft-tortilla recipe starts with 375°F, covered for 30 minutes then uncovered for 15 minutes; for previously baked frozen enchiladas, it starts with 350°F, covered for 10 minutes then uncovered for 10–20 minutes. Treat these as first checks, not guarantees for a different dish, batch size or tortilla: continue until the center is 165°F / 74°C. Cover again if the top is getting too dark. Individual cooked portions may be reheated in a microwave-safe dish following the appliance instructions; cover and rotate for even heating, allow the appliance's specified standing time, then check more than one location for 165°F / 74°C rather than relying on an unspecified microwave's fixed time.
