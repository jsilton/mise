## Chef's Note

Adapted from Mary Later's recipe. Cinnamon warms the rich custard beneath a crisp corn-flake and brown-sugar topping. Coarsely crush the flakes rather than grinding them into powder to keep some crunch. Glass retains heat, so a gentle bake and a 15-minute rest help the custard set without drying out the edges. For a half-raisin, half-plain pudding, ¼ cup golden raisins gives the raisin half a light scattering of sweetness.

## Directions

1.  **Prepare the dish:** Preheat the oven to **325°F** with a rack in the center. Grease a room-temperature **9×13-inch glass baking dish**, preferably a deep 4-quart model. This is a generous batch; if using a shallower 3-quart dish, have a small greased oven-safe dish ready for any excess.
2.  **Cook the noodles:** Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the egg noodles just until **al dente**, using the package timing as a guide. Drain well and let cool briefly until warm, not steaming hot.
3.  **Mix the custard:** In a very large bowl, beat the softened cream cheese with a little of the sour cream until smooth. Stir in the remaining sour cream and cottage cheese; the cottage cheese curds can remain. Whisk in the beaten eggs, granulated sugar, **1½ teaspoons cinnamon**, and **6 oz (12 tablespoons) melted butter**, cooled until lukewarm.
4.  **Assemble:** Fold the warm noodles into the custard. For a half-raisin, half-plain pudding, transfer half the mixture to a second bowl and fold **¼ cup golden raisins** into that half only. Spoon the two mixtures into opposite halves of the prepared glass dish, keeping them side by side, and gently level the top. For an entirely plain pudding, omit the raisins. Leave at least **½ inch of space below the rim** for the topping and slight puffing. Bake any excess separately in the smaller dish, checking it earlier.
5.  **Add the topping:** Mix **2 cups coarsely crushed corn flakes** with **½ cup packed light brown sugar**. Drizzle in the remaining **2 oz (4 tablespoons) melted butter** and toss until evenly coated. Scatter evenly over the noodles without pressing down. If using an overflow dish, divide the topping between the dishes in proportion to their surface area.
6.  **Bake:** Bake at **325°F for approximately 65–80 minutes**, checking at 60 minutes. The edges should be set, the center should have only a slight wobble without liquid custard, and the topping should be golden and crisp. Loosely tent with foil if the topping browns before the center sets. Use the doneness cues rather than the clock; dish depth affects timing.
7.  **Rest and serve:** Set the hot glass dish on a dry trivet and let rest for **15 minutes** before slicing into 12 portions. The total time includes this rest and an estimated 75-minute bake.
