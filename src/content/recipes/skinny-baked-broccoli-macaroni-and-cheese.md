## Chef's Note

Thicken the full milk-and-broth sauce before taking it off heat to melt the cheddar. Add broccoli late in the pasta cooking for firmer florets, then drain well so cooking water does not dilute the sauce. Watch the breadcrumb-Parmesan topping closely under the broiler.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 375°F. Wash and trim the broccoli into evenly sized florets. Coat enough baking dishes with cooking spray to hold the full pasta-and-sauce batch with headroom. Any dish placed under the broiler must be broiler-rated; otherwise finish with baking until the topping colors.
2. **Cook pasta and broccoli:** Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add all the pasta. For the late-broccoli route, add all the broccoli about 2 minutes before the pasta is al dente according to the actual package, checking that the florets reach the tenderness you want. Drain both well; they continue cooking in the oven.
3. **Roux:** In a large heavy skillet, melt all the butter over low heat. Add all the onion and sauté until softened, beginning checks around 2 minutes. Whisk in all the flour and cook about 1 minute until evenly combined and lightly golden.
4. **Thicken:** Gradually whisk in all the skim milk and all the chicken broth. Bring to a boil while stirring and scraping, then simmer until smooth and thick, beginning checks around 5 minutes. Season with the salt and pepper to taste.
5. **Combine:** Take off heat. Stir in all the cheddar until melted, then fold in all the drained macaroni and broccoli. Divide the full mixture among the prepared baking dishes.
6. **Top:** Scatter all the breadcrumbs and all the Parmesan over the dishes, divided in proportion to their filling. For a lightly crisped finish, coat the topping with a little of the cooking-spray allowance.
7. **Bake:** Bake at 375°F, beginning checks at 15–20 minutes, until bubbling and 165°F / 74°C in each dish's center. If using broiler-rated dishes, broil briefly to color the topping, watching continuously and beginning checks before 2 minutes. Remove when golden rather than following a fixed broil clock.
