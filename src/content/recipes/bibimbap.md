## Chef's Note

Bibimbap means mixed rice. Sue Pressey of My Korean Kitchen describes her mother's bowls made from prepared vegetable side dishes, and this version keeps that component approach: hot rice, separately seasoned banchan, cooked meat, egg and gochujang sauce are mixed just before eating.

## Directions

1. **Mix sauce:** Mix gochujang, toasted sesame oil, sugar, water, and rice vinegar in a small bowl until the sugar is dispersed. Have the cooked meat and prepared banchan ready before assembling; their cooking and chilling time is separate from this assembly plan.
2. **Base:** Divide all cooked rice among the bowls for the portions being served. The original four-portion batch uses one cup per bowl. Do not warm stored rice by leaving it on the counter; reheat properly refrigerated rice to 165°F / 74°C throughout.
3. **Arrange components:** Arrange prepared [Korean Marinated Ribeye](/mise/recipes/marinated-korean-ribeye), prepared [Korean Pickled Cucumbers](/mise/recipes/korean-pickled-cucumbers), prepared [Seasoned Spinach](/mise/recipes/simply-seasoned-korean-spinach-salad), and prepared [Seasoned Bean Sprouts](/mise/recipes/seasoned-bean-sprouts) in separate piles around the rice, dividing all the prepared serving supplies you chose among the bowls. Reheat stored cooked meat or vegetables intended to be served hot to 165°F / 74°C; keep cold banchan refrigerated until assembly. Simply placing raw sprouts in hot rice does not establish thorough cooking.
4. **Add eggs:** Place fried eggs on the bowls, dividing them among the portions. For the main route, cook fried eggs until both white and yolk are firm and serve immediately. The separate soft-yolk option below requires pasteurized eggs.
5. **Sauce and mix:** Divide all the mixed gochujang sauce among the bowls, then mix the rice, toppings and egg before eating.

## Critical Components

Use these prepared recipes or suitable store-bought alternatives in the serving amounts you want. Their complete batches are not automatically the amounts needed here.

- **The Meat:** [Korean Marinated Ribeye](/mise/recipes/marinated-korean-ribeye) (Bulgogi)
- **The Crunch:** [Korean Pickled Cucumbers](/mise/recipes/korean-pickled-cucumbers) (Oi Muchim)
- **The Green:** [Seasoned Spinach](/mise/recipes/simply-seasoned-korean-spinach-salad) (Sigeumchi Namul)
- **The Sprout:** [Seasoned Bean Sprouts](/mise/recipes/seasoned-bean-sprouts) (Kongnamul)
- **The Rice:** [Short Grain Rice](/mise/recipes/steamed-white-rice), cooked before assembly

## Soft-yolk and crisp-rice options

For a soft fried yolk that mixes into the rice, use shell eggs labeled as pasteurized, or a suitable pasteurized egg product, and follow its directions. Ordinary shell eggs use the firm-white-and-yolk main route. Cook thoroughly for diners especially vulnerable to foodborne illness.

For a crisp rice bottom, the original four-cup cooked-rice batch uses a separate additional 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil for the rice pan; scale that supply proportionally. Heat a cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat, add that rice oil, then press the hot cooked rice into an even layer. Begin checking after about 5 minutes for a golden, crackly bottom and assemble on top. Use enough pans to maintain an even layer rather than packing a scaled batch deeper; timing does not multiply. This additional pan oil is separate from the full listed sesame oil in the main sauce.

## Ground-beef and sautéed-vegetable variation

This separate My Korean Kitchen version plans three to four portions. Its original batch uses 100 g ground beef with 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sesame oil, 1 tsp brown sugar and ¼ tsp minced garlic; 250 g mildly seasoned spinach and 350 g mildly seasoned bean sprouts; 100 g sliced shiitake mushrooms; 120 g peeled, julienned carrots; ½ tsp fine sea salt divided equally between mushrooms and carrots; 3 cups cooked rice; and 3 fried eggs, or 4 if dividing into four bowls. It also needs cooking oil as required separately for the meat, mushrooms, carrots and eggs, and seasoned seaweed strips as desired. Its sauce uses 2 tbsp gochujang, 1 tbsp sesame oil, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp water, 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds, 1 tsp apple vinegar and 1 tsp minced garlic. This replaces the component main and its sauce; scale every supply of this variation proportionally, including both separately owned sesame-oil and garlic amounts.

Mix the meat with all its soy sauce, sesame oil, brown sugar and garlic, and refrigerate for about 30 minutes while preparing the vegetables. Mix all seven sauce ingredients in a clean bowl. Prepare the spinach and thoroughly cooked bean sprouts using the linked recipes, taking the listed prepared amounts for these bowls; any leftover component batch is separate. Sauté the carrots with their cooking-oil allowance and half the measured fine salt over medium-high heat, checking after 2–3 minutes for tenderness. Cook the mushrooms separately with their own oil allowance and the other half of the fine salt, checking after 2–3 minutes until cooked. Cook the marinated ground beef in its own oil allowance, breaking it up and checking after 3–5 minutes; continue until representative meat portions reach 160°F / 71°C, using clean serving tools. Make the eggs with their separate oil allowance, using the main firm endpoint or the pasteurized soft-yolk option. Divide all the hot rice, cooked meat, prepared vegetables, eggs, mixed sauce and seaweed among the bowls and mix before eating. This preparation includes the refrigerated marinade and several pan loads; allow substantially more time than the twenty-minute component assembly plan.
