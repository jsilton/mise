## Chef's Note

The collection records this as a family cornbread dressing, with mushrooms, shallots and fresh herbs. It keeps a different character from Grandmommy's finer cornbread-and-white-bread dressing. Drying the bread and cooking off mushroom liquid make the moisture easier to control, but cornbread will still crumble somewhat when folded. The contrast comes from tender pieces underneath and crisp exposed edges on top.

## Bread preparation

For a defined starting bread, make [Buttermilk Cornbread](/recipes/cornbread/) in its stated 8-inch square pan. Its small amount of sugar and honey makes this dressing lightly sweet; an unsweetened cornbread of the same finished weight is also suitable. The linked batch is expected to provide about 700 g or more after baking, but weigh it before drying and reserve any excess. If your batch yields less, make up the weight with additional cornbread. The same-day schedule assumes the first batch supplies 700 g. If extra baked bread is not already available, another batch adds baking and cooling time; making and weighing the bread a day ahead avoids discovering a shortage during assembly. The dressing quantities here are for 700 g, not any pan sold as a tray. Cool on a rack until the center is no longer warm before cutting; bake the bread the day before if possible.

## Directions

1. **Dry the cornbread:** Heat the oven to 300°F / 150°C. Spread the 3/4-inch pieces in one layer on a large rimmed sheet without crowding; use two sheets if necessary and switch their positions halfway through. Dry about 30–40 minutes, turning carefully once, until a cooled sample is firm and dry through the middle. Avoid deeply browning the bread at this stage. Cool on the sheet for 10 minutes. Prepare the vegetables while the bread dries.
2. **Brown the mushrooms:** Melt two tablespoons of the butter in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add mushrooms and cook about 6–8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until their liquid has evaporated and the pieces have brown patches. Lower the heat if the butter begins to scorch.
3. **Soften the shallots and celery:** Reduce heat to medium and add shallots, celery and a quarter-teaspoon of salt. Cook 6–8 minutes, stirring, until the shallots are translucent and the celery is tender. If the pan is dry, take one tablespoon from the remaining measured butter. Add garlic and stir for 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. **Add herbs and remaining butter:** Stir in parsley, thyme, sage, pepper and all the remaining measured butter. As soon as the butter melts, remove from heat. There should be no watery mushroom puddle, though melted butter is expected.
5. **Fold and moisten:** Heat the oven to 350°F / 177°C. Grease a roughly 3-quart 9-by-13-inch baking dish with the separate teaspoon of butter. Transfer cooled dried bread to a large bowl and fold in the mushroom mixture with a broad spatula. Add one and a half cups of stock in two additions, folding gently between them. Wait five minutes, then break open a piece. If its middle is firm or dry, add more stock a quarter-cup at a time until moist through, stopping before liquid pools in the bowl. Taste a cooled spoonful and add the remaining salt only if needed.
6. **Bake immediately:** Spread loosely in the dish, leaving an uneven surface and some exposed pieces. Bake uncovered about 30–40 minutes, until the center reaches at least 165°F / 74°C and the top is golden. If the surface browns first, cover loosely with foil and continue until the center is ready.
7. **Rest and serve:** Rest 10 minutes, then spoon into eight side portions. Serve beside roast turkey or chicken, with cranberry sauce or a tart green salad to balance the butter.
