## Chef's Note

Buttermilk's acidity reacts with baking soda for extra lift and tang. Rest the batter for 10 minutes before cooking so flour fully absorbs the liquid - this produces waffles that are crispy outside and airy inside. The batter should become slightly bubbly during the rest.

## Directions

1.  **Dry Mix:** In a large bowl, whisk together flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
2.  **Wet Mix:** In a separate bowl, whisk the buttermilk and melted butter. Whisk in the eggs until just combined.
3.  **Combine:** Pour the wet ingredients into the dry. Whisk until **just combined** - a few small lumps are essential for a light texture. Fold in the vanilla.
4.  **The Rest:** Let the batter rest for 10 minutes while the waffle iron preheats according to its instructions. It should become slightly bubbly.
5.  **Cook:** Lightly grease the hot plates if recommended for your iron. Use the manufacturer's batter amount; 1/3 to 1/2 cup suits some small irons, but a large Belgian iron may need more. Leave room for the batter to expand rather than filling to the edge.
6.  **Finish:** Cook until the steam has mostly subsided and the waffle releases readily and is golden, following the iron's readiness guidance. Check that the middle is cooked through rather than wet with batter. Repeat with the remaining batter; do not treat the first waffle's cooking time as the time for the whole batch.
7.  **Serve:** Serve immediately with salted butter, maple syrup, and fresh berries.

## Cooking Notes

The quantities and ten-minute rest match the saved Lil' Luna recipe by Kristyn Merkley. Six means portions, not six waffles of a fixed size. Allow roughly 25-40 minutes to cook the batch on a single iron; its capacity and cycle time control the schedule. A smaller iron may take longer. Keep cooked waffles in a single layer on a rack over a sheet pan in a 225°F oven while finishing, rather than stacking and trapping steam.
