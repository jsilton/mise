## Chef's Note

Artusi’s first passatelli formula uses a small amount of beef marrow to soften the breadcrumb and Parmigiano dough. Nutmeg, lemon zest or both give the broth a fragrant lift. Keep the strands short, simmer them gently and serve as soon as they are cooked through.

## Directions

1. **Make a firm dough:** Gather fine plain breadcrumbs, beef marrow, Parmigiano Reggiano, eggs, and freshly grated nutmeg, finely grated lemon zest, or both. Mash the marrow with the flat of a knife until smooth; do not melt it. Hold back a little of the listed breadcrumbs, then mix the rest with the marrow, Parmigiano, eggs and your chosen aroma. Knead into a firm, cohesive dough. Add the reserved crumbs only as needed for a dough that holds together and can be pressed into strands; you may not need them all. Do not taste the raw dough.
2. **Rest the dough:** Cover the dough and refrigerate for 30 minutes to let the crumbs hydrate. This is also a good time to prepare the broth pot and warm the serving bowls.
3. **Heat the broth:** Bring prepared meat broth to a boil in a wide pot, then lower it to a steady gentle simmer. Use enough broth and pot space that the strands can move freely. If making a larger batch, cook the strands in batches rather than crowding the pot.
4. **Press the passatelli:** Put the dough through a passatelli press or a sturdy potato ricer with large holes. Press short strands directly into the gently simmering broth, cutting them from the tool with a knife; avoid piling them together. A fine-holed ricer is too restrictive for this dough.
5. **Cook and serve:** Maintain a gentle simmer with minimal stirring so the strands keep their shape. Begin checking as they rise to the surface; allow roughly 3–5 minutes for a small batch, continuing if needed. Cut a thick strand to check that it is set and tender all the way through, without a raw, sticky center. Floating alone does not establish doneness. For a temperature check, lift an intact strand onto a clean spoon and insert a needle thermocouple suitable for thin foods lengthwise into its center, only if the full sensing region fits inside as the probe’s instructions require; check for at least 160°F. A broth reading cannot verify that center temperature. Ladle the cooked passatelli and hot broth into bowls and serve immediately.
