## Chef's Note

Use thick bread with sturdy crusts so the sticks survive dipping and turning. Keep the full milk, honey and cinnamon formula. Coat all sides, let excess drip away, then cook in uncrowded rounds; the color of one face does not establish that the center is cooked.

## Directions

1. **Cut:** Cut each listed Texas-toast slice into four similar sticks. Keep the original bread thickness and stick size when scaling; increase or decrease the number of slices and cooking rounds rather than making larger sticks.

2. **Mix:** Whisk all the eggs, whole milk, cinnamon, honey and vanilla together until the honey is dispersed and no egg-white streaks remain.

3. **Coat:** Dip a cooking-sized batch, turning each stick to coat all sides and lifting while it still holds its shape. Let excess custard drip back into the bowl. Coat the surfaces without leaving the sticks so saturated that they collapse. Keep raw-coated sticks and their utensils separate from cooked sticks.

4. **Heat pan:** Heat a large skillet over medium heat. Melt some of the unmeasured pan butter, starting with 2–3 tablespoons for an original large-skillet round, enough to coat it. Use more of the separate pan supply as needed for later rounds; add enough for a thin coating in each round.

5. **Cook and repeat:** Add only as many sticks as fit in a single uncrowded layer. Begin checking a broad side after about 2 minutes; turn as each face becomes golden and cook the remaining faces as needed. Check that the center is set, without liquid custard, and reaches 160°F using a thermometer inserted along a thick stick. Lower heat if the butter or coating browns too quickly. Repeat coating and cooking with all the remaining sticks, adding pan butter as needed. Refrigerate custard during pauses and discard unused bread-contact custard after the last round.

6. **Serve:** Divide the cooked sticks among the listed portions and serve promptly with warm maple syrup and washed berries as wanted. Let a child’s serving cool enough to handle comfortably.
