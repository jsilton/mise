## Chef's Note

Good gravy starts in the roasting pan. The defatted juices and the browned bits scraped from the bottom carry the roast flavor, and unsalted stock makes up the rest so you control the salt. Season only at the end, once you have tasted it.

## Directions

1. **Defat the juices:** Once the turkey is out of the roasting pan, pour the pan liquid through a fine-mesh strainer into a fat separator. Let the fat rise, then pour off the juices. You can also skim the fat with a spoon.
2. **Deglaze the pan:** Set the roasting pan over medium heat and add a splash of the stock. Scrape up the browned bits until they dissolve, about 2 minutes. Strain this into the juices, leaving any blackened bits behind.
3. **Make up the liquid:** Top up the juices with stock to make the full amount of liquid. If you have more than you need, simmer it down first. If the juices taste very salty, as they can from a brined bird, use less of them and more stock.
4. **Cook the roux:** Melt the butter in a 2-quart saucepan over medium-low heat. Whisk in the flour and cook, whisking, until bubbling and pale, about 2 minutes.
5. **Whisk in the liquid:** Whisk in a splash of the liquid until smooth. Keep adding it a little at a time, whisking smooth each time, until it is all in. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
6. **Simmer:** Lower the heat and simmer, stirring often, until the gravy coats a spoon and no longer tastes of raw flour, 10 to 15 minutes. If it gets too thick, whisk in a splash of hot water.
7. **Season:** Stir in the pepper. Taste, then add salt only as needed.
