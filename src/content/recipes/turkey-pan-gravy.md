## Chef's Note

A turkey's pan liquid varies with its brine, aromatics and evaporation. Build the gravy with unsalted stock first, then use a small measured amount of good drippings for roast flavor. Flour gives body, while the butter disperses it; neither ingredient can remove excess salt or burned flavors. This recipe was developed for mise's holiday dinners and is separate from the turkey roasting method.

## Directions

1. **Assess the pan liquid:** After removing the fully cooked turkey, strain its pan liquid into a heatproof container. Separate the fat with a fat separator or skim carefully with a spoon. Taste a cooled drop of the watery liquid. Measure thirty milliliters only if it is palatable; otherwise use thirty milliliters water. Keep this distinct from the measured roux butter.
2. **Cook the roux:** Melt butter in a 2-quart saucepan over medium-low heat. Whisk in flour and cook for about two minutes, stirring across the base, until evenly bubbling and pale. Reduce heat if it begins to darken or catch.
3. **Add liquid gradually:** Whisk in a small splash of the measured stock to make a smooth thick mixture. Keep adding stock in several portions, whisking smooth each time. Add the measured acceptable pan liquid or water. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, stirring the base.
4. **Simmer to texture:** Reduce heat and simmer gently for ten to fifteen minutes, stirring often, until the gravy coats a spoon and no longer tastes of raw flour. It should pour, not sit in a stiff mound. If too thick, whisk in some of the listed hot water a tablespoon at a time. Do not cook away all its water just to make it look thicker.
5. **Season and serve:** Stir in pepper, taste, then add only as much of the optional salt as needed. Serve about a quarter cup per person. For a brief hold, cover and keep hot food at 140°F / 60°C or above, stirring occasionally and checking the temperature. Loosen before serving if it thickens.

## Without fresh drippings

Use the water substitution and a flavorful unsalted chicken or turkey stock. This makes the gravy possible before the roast is finished. If preparing a day ahead, cool and refrigerate it promptly, then reheat to 165°F / 74°C throughout before serving or hot holding. Do not add a second full measure of drippings on top of the defined liquid amount later.
