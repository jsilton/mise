## Chef's Note

A turkey's pan liquid varies with its brine, aromatics and evaporation. Use all the good defatted juices and the browned bits from the roasting pan for roast flavor, then make up the volume with unsalted stock so you control the salt. Flour gives body, while the butter disperses it; neither ingredient can remove excess salt or burned flavors. Serve alongside roast turkey.

## Directions

1. **Collect the juices and fond:** After removing the fully cooked turkey, strain its pan liquid into a heatproof container. Separate the fat with a fat separator or skim carefully with a spoon. If the roasting pan is stovetop-safe, set it over medium heat, add about 120 ml of the stock and scrape up the browned bits until they dissolve. Otherwise, pour in the hot stock and scrape well. Strain this into the defatted juices, leaving any blackened bits behind. If the juices and deglazing liquid come to more than 480 ml, simmer them down to 480 ml or set the excess aside before adding them to the roux. Taste a cooled drop. If it is palatable, top it up with unsalted stock to 480 ml total. If it tastes very salty, use only part of it and more stock. Keep this distinct from the measured roux butter.
2. **Cook the roux:** Melt butter in a 2-quart saucepan over medium-low heat. Whisk in flour and cook for about two minutes, stirring across the base, until evenly bubbling and pale. Reduce heat if it begins to darken or catch.
3. **Add liquid gradually:** Whisk in a small splash of the measured juice-and-stock mixture to make a smooth thick mixture. Keep adding it in several portions, whisking smooth each time, until all 480 ml is in. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, stirring the base.
4. **Simmer to texture:** Reduce heat and simmer gently for ten to fifteen minutes, stirring often, until the gravy coats a spoon and no longer tastes of raw flour. It should pour, not sit in a stiff mound. If too thick, whisk in some of the listed hot water a tablespoon at a time. Do not cook away all its water just to make it look thicker.
5. **Season and serve:** Stir in pepper, taste, then add only as much of the optional salt as needed. Serve about a quarter cup per person. For a brief hold, cover and keep hot food at 140°F / 60°C or above, stirring occasionally and checking the temperature. Loosen before serving if it thickens.

## Without fresh drippings

Use the full 480 ml of a flavorful unsalted chicken or turkey stock. This makes the gravy possible before the roast is finished. If preparing a day ahead, cool and refrigerate it promptly, then reheat to 165°F / 74°C throughout before serving or hot holding. If drippings become available later, use them in place of part of the stock rather than adding them on top of the defined liquid amount.
