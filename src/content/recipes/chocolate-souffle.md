## Chef's Note

Fold the chocolate and egg foam gently but thoroughly so no streaks remain. The thumb-run clears a shallow channel at the rim; it helps an even rise but does not replace a correctly prepared dish and properly beaten whites. Have bowls and accompaniments ready before baking so the soufflé can be served immediately.

## Directions

1. **Prepare dish:** Preheat the oven to 375°F. Butter an original-batch 6-cup soufflé dish generously. Reserve 3/19 of the full listed granulated sugar for coating and 16/19 for the egg whites; for the original batch these are 1 tablespoon and ⅓ cup respectively. Coat the buttered dish with its allocated sugar and knock out the excess.
2. **Melt chocolate:** Melt all the chocolate in a heatproof bowl over barely simmering water, stirring until smooth. Remove the bowl from the heat and let it cool enough that it will not cook yolks on contact, while staying fluid. Stir in all the yolks; the mixture stiffens.
3. **Beat whites:** In a clean bowl, beat all the whites with the pinch of salt until they just hold soft peaks. Gradually add all the reserved batter sugar, then beat until just-stiff glossy peaks hold. Avoid dry, clumpy whites. Keep egg preparation brief rather than leaving eggs out for an indefinite warming period.
4. **Lighten:** Stir some meringue into the chocolate to make it easier to fold; the source uses about 1 cup for its original batch. At other scales, start with a proportionately smaller or larger spoonful and stop once the base is loosened, reserving most foam for folding.
5. **Fold:** Fold in all the remaining meringue gently but thoroughly, lifting from the bottom until no white streaks remain.
6. **Fill:** Spoon all the batter into the prepared dish without overflowing it, then run the end of your thumb around the inside rim to make a shallow groove. For scaled batches, divide the full batter and coating among appropriately sized dishes before baking.
7. **Bake:** Bake in the middle of the oven, starting checks around 24–26 minutes for the original dish. Look for a puffed, crusted top and a center that moves slightly; check the egg-containing center reaches 160°F /71°C. Continue if it has not, checking each dish rather than using jiggle alone as the endpoint.
8. **Serve:** Dust with optional powdered sugar and serve immediately with the optional lightly sweetened whipped cream.
