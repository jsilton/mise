## Chef's Note

Adapted from Mei Lin’s Food & Wine ribs, this version bakes foil-wrapped pork until tender, then glazes it over medium-low heat to limit scorching. Gochujang, honey, soy sauce and the full white pepper make the sauce sweet, spicy and savory. Reserve clean glaze before handling the raw ribs so it can finish the cooked meat.

## Directions

1. **Mix and reserve clean sauce:** Whisk gochujang, hoisin sauce, honey, soy sauce, sake, unseasoned rice vinegar, fresh ginger, fresh garlic, and ground white pepper together. Before touching raw ribs, divide the sauce into a baking portion and a separate clean glazing portion. Keep the glazing portion covered and refrigerated until needed; use separate clean utensils for it. For the original roughly three-rack batch, the baking portion was 3/4 cup per rack, with the remaining sauce reserved for the grill. Rack sizes and finished sauce volume vary: scale the whole recipe together and distribute its baking portion across the actual ribs, retaining sauce for glazing.
2. **Wrap the ribs:** Preheat the oven to 325°F. Prepare pork spareribs. Lay double layers of heavy-duty foil for each actual rack or section and set the meat meaty-side up. Brush with the baking sauce portion without returning a raw-contact brush to the clean glaze. Wrap tightly and arrange on enough rimmed baking sheets to support the packets and catch leaks; the original used two large sheets. More ribs need more packets and oven loads, not a fixed three-packet instruction.
3. **Bake until tender:** Bake about 2 hours as a first check, continuing until the meat is tender but still holds to the bones. Check thick meat away from bone with a thermometer: whole pork must reach at least 145°F before heat removal, and ultimately rest at least 3 minutes before eating; tenderness may need much more cooking than that minimum. Open foil carefully away from your face. Keep foil juices separate from the clean glazing sauce.
4. **Prepare for the grill:** Preheat the grill to medium-low. Unwrap the ribs, wipe off excess marinade and cut into 4-rib sections as the current version does. Use enough grill area or successive loads so the pieces are easy to turn.
5. **Glaze without scorching:** Brush lightly with the clean reserved sauce and grill about 3–4 minutes per side as a first check, turning and watching until the glaze caramelizes without burning. Keep utensils that touch the cooked ribs out of the clean sauce bowl, using a separate small working portion for brushing. The char and clock do not replace the meat endpoint. Discard any remaining contaminated working sauce.
6. **Rest and serve:** Transfer to a platter and rest at least 3 minutes after the final heat before eating. Serve with lime wedges. Keep any unused clean sauce separate; do not serve raw-contact marinade or unheated foil juices as extra sauce.

## Ketchup and Finishing-Salt Version

For the complete original sauce, add 1/2 cup ketchup for the original batch, scaled proportionally, to the same full gochujang, hoisin, honey, soy sauce, sake, vinegar, ginger, garlic and white pepper before dividing the sauce. This is an additional supply for this exclusive version; the current main is ketchup-free. Bake in foil at 325°F as above. After unwrapping, cut into 3- or 4-rib sections and season with kosher salt as needed. The source used high grill heat and about 4 minutes total, turning and brushing with clean reserved sauce until glazed and lightly charred; watch closely, because this sweeter sauce burns quickly. The current medium-low grilling route can instead be used, with its longer first checks. Keep the same meat endpoint, clean-sauce handling and rest. Lime is the current optional serving addition, not part of the original sauce.

## Make Ahead

The source allows baking through the foil stage, then refrigerating overnight. Complete cooking and check the meat endpoint before cooling; do not refrigerate partially cooked ribs. Divide and cool promptly in shallow containers, retaining the cooked juices with the ribs if desired, and refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below. For next-day service, reheat the ribs and any retained cooked juices to 165°F throughout in the oven or another suitable reheating method before the brief grill finish. A room-temperature wait or quick glaze is not a reheat endpoint. Keep the full clean reserved sauce cold separately, then glaze and rest as above.
