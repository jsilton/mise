## Chef's Note

Lisa Leake’s whole-wheat muffin batter was written to let her children choose their own fillings. Juice supplies liquid and sweetness; it does not guarantee a light crumb. Either fold the full fruit amount into the batch or gently mix it into the individual wells, stopping once the batter is combined.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 400°F and line a muffin tin. The original batch plans twelve muffins. Use enough similar wells for a scaled batch, leaving room for the complete fruit amount.
2. **Mix dry ingredients:** Whisk white whole-wheat flour, baking powder, ground cinnamon, ground nutmeg, and salt together, including the full salt amount.
3. **Mix wet ingredients:** Whisk apple or orange juice, coconut oil, honey, pure vanilla extract, and large eggs in a separate bowl. Keep the melted coconut oil warm rather than hot when it touches the eggs; other same-volume oils may replace it.
4. **Combine:** Stir the wet mixture into the dry mixture with a spatula until no dry flour pockets remain, then stop mixing.
5. **Portion and add fruit:** Divide the batter among the lined wells, starting around ⅔ full and leaving room for blueberries, diced strawberries, or peeled diced pears. Divide the complete listed fruit among the wells and gently mix it into each with a small spoon or fork. If the chosen fruit and batter will overflow, use another well or load instead of discarding part of the fruit. Alternatively, fold the full fruit amount into the batter before portioning; use one route, not both.
6. **Bake:** Wait until the oven reaches 400°F. Bake 10–15 minutes per load, until a center toothpick comes out clean. Fruit pieces and pan fill may change the time; check another central spot if the first tester passes through wet fruit.
7. **Cool and serve:** Let the muffins firm enough to remove from the tin. Serve warm or finish cooling on a rack, with salted butter (if using) only if desired. Cool fully before wrapping or freezing.

## Cooking Notes

### Filling choices

The main route uses the full listed fruit amount, either folded into the bowl or divided across the filled wells. For the original batch, the source also permits one cup total of a different filling: applesauce; diced or mashed banana with chopped walnuts; raisins with chopped pecans and an extra pinch of cinnamon; grated carrot with chopped walnuts; mixed dried fruit; or jelly. Scale the chosen total volume with the batch and use it instead of the entire main fruit entry, not in addition. The split within mixed fruit-and-nut choices is unspecified; nuts and drier fillings can change the crumb and bake time.

For a separate individually flavored source route, omit the main fruit entry and add 1–2 tsp of a chosen filling to each original-size muffin, stirring gently in its well. Increase the number of wells with the batch, not the dose per muffin. Citrus zest uses the smaller source amount ¼–½ tsp per original-size muffin instead of the general filling amount. These individual-dose choices are separate from the full one-cup batch route.

### Oil, juice and keeping

Another oil may replace the full listed coconut-oil volume. Frozen berries may replace fresh berries at the same listed volume; keep them frozen until mixing so their thawing juice does not spread through the batter. Plain milk may replace all the juice at the same listed volume, but it is less sweet; the full honey remains. Do not automatically add an unmeasured extra sweetener or liquid to the main.

Cool the plain muffins before airtight room-temperature storage, or freeze cooled muffins for later and thaw a portion when needed. Keep any optional serving butter separately according to its label. For a nut-free preparation, choose a filling without nuts and check the other ingredients for cross-contact. Nutmeg is a spice, not a tree nut.
