## Chef's Note

This Hyderabadi-style biryani uses the kacchi approach: raw marinated chicken is layered beneath partially cooked rice, then both finish in a covered pot. The central problem is coordination. The rice must retain enough firmness to finish with the chicken, and the burner must sustain steam without scorching the bottom. Aromas and elapsed time help you follow the process; a thermometer and a tasted grain establish the final endpoints.

## Directions

1. **Marinate 4–24 hours:** Mix yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, Kashmiri chile powder, turmeric, garam masala, ground cumin, ground coriander, the chicken salt, lemon juice, half the cilantro, and half the mint. Coat the chicken, cover, and refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below. Do not leave raw chicken out while preparing the other components.
2. **Fry onions:** Heat the neutral oil in a wide skillet over medium heat. Fry onions in two batches, stirring frequently, until evenly golden brown, about 12–18 minutes per batch. Lower heat as they color; lift them out before dark brown. Drain in a metal strainer over a heatproof bowl, then spread on a tray lined with paper towels. Reserve 2 tbsp drained onion oil for the biryani; the remaining frying oil is not poured into the dish.
3. **Prepare rice and saffron:** While the onions cook, rinse basmati in several changes of cool water, soak for 20–30 minutes, and drain. Steep saffron in the warm milk for at least 15 minutes. Bring the measured rice water to a boil in a large pot with its salt, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, bay leaves, and cumin seeds.
4. **Partially cook rice:** Add the drained soaked rice to the boiling water. Stir once gently and return to a boil. Begin tasting at 3 minutes; drain when the outside is soft but the center is distinctly firm, often about 4–6 minutes depending on the rice. Do not wait until fully tender. Retain the whole spices with the rice, or remove large whole spices now if preferred.
5. **Layer promptly:** Spread the reserved 2 tbsp onion oil over the bottom of the heavy cooking pot. Take chicken from the refrigerator and spread it with all its marinade in an even layer. Scatter one-third of the fried onions over it. Spread the hot, drained rice on top without packing it down. Drizzle with saffron milk, melted ghee, and optional rose or kewra water. Scatter the remaining onions, cilantro, and mint over the top. Cover tightly; use a sheet of foil under the lid if needed for a snug fit.
6. **Cook under steam:** Heat over medium until you hear a gentle simmer and see light steam at the lid edge, about 5–8 minutes. Reduce heat enough to maintain that gentle simmer, rather than automatically using the lowest burner setting. Cook covered for 30 minutes, lowering heat if you smell scorching. Keep the lid closed between checks to retain steam.
7. **Verify before resting:** Open carefully away from your face. Probe several of the largest chicken pieces at the center and near the pot edge, avoiding the pot itself; each must reach 165°F / 74°C. Check the rice temperature in several places also reaches 165°F / 74°C, and taste a grain for tenderness without a chalky core. If either temperature or texture is short, re-cover and cook another 5–10 minutes before checking again. If rice is drying, sprinkle 2 tbsp hot water over it, with up to the remaining 2 tbsp as needed. Do not use resting time as a substitute for these endpoints.
8. **Rest and serve:** Once chicken and rice meet the checks, turn off heat and rest covered for 15 minutes. Lift portions from the bottom with a wide spoon so each serving includes chicken and rice, disturbing the grains as little as possible. Remove any visible whole spices and tell diners that some may remain. Serve with raita or kachumber salad; additional rice is unnecessary.
