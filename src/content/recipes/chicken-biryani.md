## Chef's Note

Hyderabadi biryani is cooked kacchi style. Raw marinated chicken goes under half-cooked basmati, and the two finish together in a sealed pot, on dum. The yogurt and chicken juices steam the rice from below while saffron, ghee and fried onions perfume it from above. Drain the rice while its center is still firm so the grains finish long and separate.

## Directions

1. **Marinate the chicken:** Mix the yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, chili powder, turmeric, garam masala, cumin, coriander, salt, lemon juice and half the cilantro and mint. Coat the chicken, cover and refrigerate for 4 to 24 hours.
2. **Fry the onions:** Heat the oil in a wide skillet over medium heat. Fry the onions in two batches, stirring often, until evenly golden brown, 12 to 18 minutes per batch. Lift them out before they turn dark brown, since they keep darkening off the heat. Drain on paper towels and save the oil.
3. **Soak the rice:** While the onions fry, rinse the rice in several changes of cool water until it runs clear. Soak it for 20 to 30 minutes, then drain. Steep the saffron in the warm milk.
4. **Parboil the rice:** Bring the water to a boil in a large pot with the salt, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, bay leaves and cumin seeds. Add the rice, stir once and return to a boil. Cook until the outside is soft but the center is still distinctly firm, 4 to 6 minutes, then drain.
5. **Layer the pot:** Spoon a little of the onion oil over the bottom of a wide, heavy 5- to 6-quart pot. Spread the chicken and all its marinade in an even layer and scatter a third of the onions over it. Spread the hot rice on top without packing it down. Drizzle with the saffron milk, ghee and rose water, then scatter the rest of the onions, cilantro and mint over the top.
6. **Cook on dum:** Cover tightly, with a sheet of foil under the lid if it is loose. Heat over medium until you hear a gentle simmer and see steam at the lid edge, 5 to 8 minutes. Lower the heat just enough to keep that gentle simmer and cook for 30 minutes without lifting the lid.
7. **Check for doneness:** The biryani is done when the largest pieces of chicken read 165°F and the rice is tender all the way through. If not, re-cover and cook 5 to 10 minutes more, sprinkling a little hot water over the rice first if it looks dry.
8. **Rest and serve:** Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, covered, for 15 minutes. Scoop from the bottom with a wide spoon so every serving gets chicken and rice.
