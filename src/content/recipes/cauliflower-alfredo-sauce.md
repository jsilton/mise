## Chef's Note

Cook the cauliflower until it crushes easily before blending it with its milk or broth. The fine vegetable purée gives the sauce body; it is not a starch-thickened cream sauce. Garlic cooked briefly in the chosen fat and Parmesan or nutritional yeast provide the savory finish. For the complete dairy-free version, choose dairy-free milk or broth, olive oil and nutritional yeast together.

## Directions

1. **Simmer:** Put cauliflower and the chosen milk or broth in a pot. Bring just to a boil, cover and reduce to a gentle simmer for about 12–15 minutes, until the florets crush easily with a fork. Stir occasionally and watch milk so it does not boil over or scorch.

2. **Cook the garlic:** While the cauliflower simmers, gently sauté the grated garlic in the chosen butter or olive oil for about 1 minute, until fragrant without darkening. Keep all this garlic and fat for the sauce.

3. **Prepare to blend:** Check the actual model’s manual before blending hot food. Use only a vessel and lid expressly approved for hot ingredients, following that model’s fill, venting and speed instructions. Never blend hot food in a sealed personal cup. If the machine is not approved, cool the mixture as its manual requires before blending, then reheat gently; this adds time. Transfer the softened cauliflower and all its cooking liquid to the approved vessel. Add all the garlic and its cooking fat, chosen Parmesan or nutritional yeast, measured kosher salt and nutmeg.

4. **Blend to texture:** Blend according to the vessel’s instructions for the mixture’s actual temperature until the sauce is smooth, pausing safely to check for grainy pieces. About 1 minute is a starting estimate, not a universal speed or time. Blend in batches if required and recombine them.

5. **Serve:** Use the sauce promptly with separately cooked pasta or roasted vegetables. It thickens as it stands; rewarm gently and stir. Those accompaniments are separate preparations, outside the sauce’s time and yield.
