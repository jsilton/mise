## Chef's Note

This chilled cocktail takes its cue from the Earl Grey, milk and vanilla flavors of a London Fog. Oat milk gives it a lighter body than the original cream version, while a small amount of lavender supports the bergamot. Keep the infusions brief and chill both preparations before shaking so the drink arrives cold, gently frothy and tea-forward.

## Directions

1. **Brew the concentrate:** Steep the 4 g Earl Grey tea in 120 mL water at **95°C / 203°F for 4 minutes**. Strain through a fine-mesh strainer, then add enough water to bring the strained tea back to **120 mL**. This makes two 60 mL cocktail portions.
2. **Make the syrup:** Stir the 60 g sugar into 60 g hot water until dissolved. Off the heat, add the culinary lavender and steep for **3 minutes**. Strain finely, then stir in the vanilla extract. Expect approximately **90–100 mL syrup**, enough for about six cocktails after straining losses.
3. **Chill:** Transfer the tea and syrup to separate clean containers and refrigerate until thoroughly cold, allowing about **1 hour**, or longer as needed. Chill a **7 oz coupe glass** before mixing.
4. **Measure one cocktail:** Shake the oat milk carton. Add **1 1/2 oz (45 mL) gin, 2 oz (60 mL) cold tea concentrate, 1/2 oz (15 mL) oat milk and 1/2 oz (15 mL) cold syrup** to the shaker. The remaining tea and syrup are for additional cocktails; do not add the whole batches.
5. **Shake and strain:** Fill the shaker with solid ice cubes and shake firmly for **10–12 seconds**, until the shaker is very cold and often frosted. Strain through both the cocktail strainer and a fine-mesh strainer into the chilled coupe. Look for a lightly cloudy tea-beige drink with fine bubbles on the surface; the amount of foam depends on the oat milk.
6. **Serve:** Serve immediately, **without ice or garnish**. Taste before increasing lavender in a future syrup batch; the bergamot should remain clear.
