## Chef's Note

This generous Martini uses London dry gin, dry French vermouth and lemon oil. Crack some of the stirring ice into large pieces, then stir until the drink tastes chilled and rounded while the gin remains clear in the finish. A small Nick and Nora glass may hold only part of the drink; keep the rest in a chilled sidecar rather than pouring to the brim.

## Directions

1. **Chill the glass:** Place a freezer-safe Martini glass or Nick and Nora glass in the freezer for about 10 minutes before mixing. This recipe has 4¾ fluid ounces of gin and vermouth in the original batch before dilution, so set out a small sidecar glass for any excess. Scaled batches need corresponding serving glasses and room for ice and stirring in the mixing vessel.
2. **Combine over ice:** Use London dry gin, dry French vermouth, and large ice cubes. If starting with large cubes, crack some into large pieces with the back of a bar spoon, catching them in a clean cloth; put them in the mixing glass with additional whole cubes. Add the gin and vermouth. Keep enough free space to stir without spilling. All ounce measures are fluid ounces.
3. **Stir and check:** Stir smoothly around the inside of the glass until the drink is cold. Taste a small sample with a clean spoon; the gin and vermouth should be recognizable with a rounded finish. Continue briefly if it is still warm or harsh, then strain before it becomes thin. Ice and starting temperature change the stirring time.
4. **Strain with room to spare:** Strain through a Hawthorne strainer into the chilled serving glass, leaving headroom. Use ice (if using) if needed: strain excess into a small sidecar glass set in a bowl of ice, keeping the ice outside the cocktail. Do not force the full drink into a small glass or discard the excess; serve it alongside.
5. **Express lemon:** Use large lemon: remove a roughly 1-inch strip of peel for each serving, twist it over the drink to release the oils, rub it around the rim and float it yellow side up. Serve promptly; keep the sidecar chilled until poured.
