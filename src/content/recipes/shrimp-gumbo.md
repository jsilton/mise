## Chef's Note

This is Craig Plummer's mom's shrimp gumbo, built on a dark oil roux with onions, tomatoes and okra, then finished with filé. Stir the roux the whole time it browns, since a few black specks mean it has scorched. Add only hot liquid to it, as the card warns, so it stays smooth.

## Directions

1. **Brown the roux:** Heat the cooking oil and all-purpose flour together in a large heavy skillet over medium-low heat. Stir constantly until the roux is a rich brown, the color of milk chocolate, 30 to 45 minutes.
2. **Heat the liquid:** While the roux browns, bring the water, tomatoes, and bay leaves (if using) to a boil in an 8-quart pot over high heat.
3. **Cook the onions:** Stir the medium onions, green onions (if using), and celery ribs (if using) into the roux. Cook over low heat, stirring, until the onions are clear and soft, about 10 minutes.
4. **Add the shrimp:** Add the shrimp to the boiling pot and bring it back to a boil.
5. **Loosen the roux:** Stir a few ladles of the hot liquid from the pot into the roux until smooth. Never add cold or room-temperature liquid to the roux.
6. **Simmer the gumbo:** Scrape the roux into the pot and stir until it dissolves. Simmer uncovered over low heat, stirring now and then, for 45 to 60 minutes.
7. **Add the okra:** Stir in the frozen cut okra, crab meat (if using), and salt. Simmer until the okra is tender, about 15 minutes.
8. **Serve over rice:** Take the pot off the heat and discard the bay leaves. Ladle the gumbo over hot cooked white rice in bowls.
9. **Finish with filé:** Sprinkle filé powder over each bowl just before eating. Never let the gumbo boil once the filé is in.

## Suggested Tweak

This is a family recipe, so it stays as written. If you want to try it:

- Shrimp that simmer for an hour turn firm. Simmer the shells in the water for 20 minutes to make a quick stock, strain it, and add the shrimp with the okra for the last 5 minutes instead.
- Season the pot with 1/2 tsp cayenne along with the salt for a little heat.
- A roux this heavy in flour is stiff and scorches easily. Use 1 1/4 cups oil, equal to the flour, so it stays loose and browns evenly.
