## Chef's Note

Nana’s biscuits are mixed by hand, rolled into small balls and flattened with the palm. Keep the butter version’s fat cold and handle the dough gently. The self-rising flour supplies the main leavening; retain the listed pinch of baking soda without adding a second measured baking-powder dose.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 425°F. Lightly grease a metal baking sheet with butter. If you plan to broil the tops, use a broiler-rated pan without parchment and follow your oven’s rack-clearance instructions.

2. **Cut the fat:** Put the sifted self-rising flour in a large bowl. Rub in all the cold cubed butter with your fingertips until coarse crumbs and some pea-sized butter pieces remain.

3. **Mix gently:** Add the listed buttermilk and pinch of baking soda. Mix gently by hand just until the dough comes together. If dry flour remains, add extra buttermilk a little at a time; if too sticky to handle, use a light dusting of self-rising flour.

4. **Shape:** Roll into similarly sized small balls and flatten slightly with your palm on the greased sheet. The original-size butter batch plans about 12; follow the listed scaled count without enlarging the biscuits or crowding the sheet.

5. **Bake and serve:** Bake for about 10–12 minutes until the centers have set. If baked through but still pale on top, broil briefly, watching continuously and removing the pan as soon as the color develops, about 1 minute. Broiling colors the top; it does not replace baking the middle. Let the biscuits stand briefly before serving.

## Shortening Variation

For the original batch, replace the ¼ cup dough butter with about ¼ cup solid vegetable shortening; scale that replacement with the listed dough butter amount. Keep the full self-rising flour, buttermilk and pinch of baking soda. Work the shortening into the sifted flour by hand, adding the listed buttermilk and soda a little at a time until the dough comes together. Use extra flour only as needed, then roll and flatten as in step 4 and bake at 425°F, with the same optional brief broil for color after the centers are baked. This is a separate fat and mixing route; keep the butter version’s cold-cutting method for the main recipe.
