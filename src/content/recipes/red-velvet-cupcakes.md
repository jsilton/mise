## Chef's Note

Use natural cocoa, the full buttermilk and the listed vinegar; this is a lightly chocolate-flavored cupcake with both butter and oil. Soft-peaked egg whites are folded in last. Small butter pieces in the mixture are acceptable, and the cupcake fill level and center tester matter more than forcing fourteen cakes into one twelve-cup tin.

## Directions

1. **Prepare and separate the eggs:** Preheat the oven to 350°F. Line a 12-cup muffin tin. Separate large eggs, keeping whites in a clean, grease-free bowl and yolks separate. The original batch plans 14 cupcakes: use a second tin or bake the remaining two in another load. Scale the number of lined wells and oven loads with the batter, not the well size.
2. **Whip the whites:** Beat the separated whites on high until soft peaks form, about 2–3 minutes: the tip bends over when the whisk is lifted. Set aside. Use all the whites from the listed eggs; do not keep a fixed two-white count when scaling.
3. **Mix the dry ingredients:** Have all-purpose flour, cornstarch, natural unsweetened cocoa powder, baking soda, and salt ready. Sift the flour and cornstarch together, then whisk in the cocoa, baking soda and salt until evenly combined.
4. **Cream butter and sugar, then add oil:** Use granulated sugar, canola or avocado oil, and unsalted butter in this order: with a mixer, beat the butter until creamy, about 1 minute. Add the sugar and beat about 2 minutes, scraping the bowl. Add the oil and beat about 2 minutes. Small butter pieces may remain; do not keep beating solely to make the butter-oil mixture perfectly smooth.
5. **Add yolks and flavorings:** Beat in all the reserved yolks, then distilled white vinegar, pure vanilla extract, and red food coloring (if using), using the food coloring only if desired. Scrape the sides and bottom of the bowl.
6. **Combine with buttermilk:** With the mixer on low, add the dry mixture in three additions, alternating with whole buttermilk and beginning and ending with the dry mixture. Mix each addition just until incorporated.
7. **Fold in the whites:** Gently fold in all the soft-peaked whites with a spatula until incorporated. Avoid beating them into the batter.
8. **Fill and bake:** Fill each lined well ½–⅔ full; do not force all the batter into twelve wells or overfill to hit the planning count. Wait for the oven to reach 350°F. Bake 20–21 minutes per load, until the tops spring back gently and a center toothpick comes out clean. Refrigerate unbaked batter between sequential loads and use it promptly.
9. **Cool:** Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then transfer to a rack to cool completely. Complete rack cooling has no fixed duration here.
10. **Frost:** Prepare [Cream Cheese Frosting](/mise/recipes/favorite-cream-cheese-frosting) following that separate recipe. Frost completely cooled cupcakes close to serving; store any extra frosting according to its own instructions.

## Cooking Notes

### Flour and milk options

For the original 14-cupcake batch, 1⅔ cups (195 g) cake flour may replace BOTH the 1⅓ cups all-purpose flour and ¼ cup cornstarch. Scale that replacement with the batch; keep all other ingredients unchanged.

For a buttermilk substitute in the original batch, put 1 tsp white vinegar OR lemon juice into a measuring cup, then add whole milk to a TOTAL of ½ cup. Stir and stand 5 minutes, then use this in place of the full buttermilk. Scale both the acid and final volume proportionally; the separate listed ½ tsp vinegar in the batter remains. Lower-fat or nondairy milk is a separate source option with less richness, not the whole-milk main.

### Color and size options

Coloring is optional. For the original batch, the liquid-color example is 2 tbsp; scale that liquid example proportionally, or tint to preference. Gel has no measured interchangeable dose. The source has no tested natural-color alternative. Apple cider vinegar may replace the listed white vinegar in the same amount.

For mini cupcakes, use the full same batter in lined mini wells, or wells greased with a suitable baking spray. The original batch plans around 36 minis; maintain ½–⅔ fill and plan additional tins or loads when scaling. Bake at 350°F for 12–13 minutes per load, until a center toothpick comes out clean, then cool before frosting. The source’s layer cake is a different recipe, not a pan conversion for this batter. Its thin-frosting piping-tip advice describes its frosting; it does not establish how this linked frosting pipes.
