## Chef's Note

Maple bacon, Dijon, rosemary and garlic flavor these oven-roasted tenderloins. The hour in the refrigerator gives the coating time on the surface; mustard does not provide a guaranteed enzyme-tenderizing treatment. Check each tenderloin’s thickest center before removing it from the oven, then give it the full 10-minute rest. Bacon browning and a pink center are not substitutes for a thermometer.

## Directions

1. **Mix the coating or marinade:** Mix Dijon mustard, Fresh rosemary, Garlic cloves, and Freshly ground black pepper in a clean bowl or raw-meat container before adding the pork.
2. **Coat and refrigerate:** Spread the prepared Dijon–rosemary coating over Pork tenderloins. Wrap or cover in a raw-meat container and refrigerate for 1 hour. Keep raw-contact dishes and utensils separate from the serving board.
3. **Heat the oven and add bacon:** Heat the oven to 375°F. Remove the pork’s plastic or cover. Distribute Maple bacon slices over the tenderloins, using two slices per original-size tenderloin in the original batch; tie with kitchen twine. For scaled or partial batches, divide the full listed bacon amount across the meat rather than automatically using four slices per pan.
4. **Arrange the roast:** Set Fresh rosemary sprigs and Garlic cloves in a roasting pan large enough to leave space between tenderloins. Lay the bacon-topped meat on these aromatics; use more pan area when increasing the batch.
5. **Check each center:** Roast, beginning checks after about 25 minutes. Insert an instant-read thermometer into the thickest center of every tenderloin and continue until each reaches at least 145°F /63°C before removal;25–30 minutes is an original first-check range, not a guarantee. Remove a ready piece to a clean board rather than overcook it while a thicker one finishes. Check that the maple bacon is cooked according to its package guidance as well; the tenderloin-center reading does not certify the bacon. If a tenderloin is ready first, remove its twine and bacon with clean utensils, let the pork rest, and finish the bacon separately before serving it with that pork.
6. **Rest and remove twine:** Rest each cooked tenderloin for 10 minutes before slicing, meeting the minimum 3-minute rest after its measured 145°F endpoint. Remove all twine. Do not rely on assumed carryover heat or meat color.
7. **Slice and serve:** Slice across the grain into roughly 1-inch rounds and serve. The roasted rosemary sprigs and smashed garlic may garnish the platter; avoid serving woody stems as edible pieces.
