## Chef's Note

Tonkotsu is the pork-bone ramen of Fukuoka, on Japan's southern island of Kyushu. A hard, rolling boil for most of a day melts the collagen from the bones and whips the fat into the broth, so it turns creamy and opaque. Season each bowl with the soy-miso tare, and taste before you reach for salt.

## Directions

1. **Blanch the bones:** Put the pork bones and shoulder in a large, heavy stockpot and cover with cold water. Bring to a boil over high heat and boil for 2 minutes. Drain, rinse the bones and meat of gray scum, and scrub out the pot.
2. **Start the broth:** Return the bones and shoulder to the pot with the water, adding more if needed to cover. Bring to a rolling boil over high heat. Skim off the gray foam for the first 20 minutes, but leave the fat.
3. **Add the aromatics:** Add the ginger, onion, garlic and shiitake. Keep the kombu for later.
4. **Boil the broth:** Keep the broth at a rolling boil, partly covered, for 8 to 12 hours. Stir along the bottom now and then and top up with boiling water to keep the bones covered. It is ready when the broth is creamy and opaque and a cooled spoonful feels sticky on your lips.
5. **Make the tare:** While the broth cooks, whisk the soy sauce, miso, mirin and sesame oil until smooth.
6. **Cook the eggs:** During the last hour of the broth, lower the cold eggs into a pot of boiling water. Cook 7 minutes for jammy yolks. Move them to ice water for 15 minutes, then peel and halve.
7. **Strain the broth:** Lift out the bones and strain the broth through a fine-mesh strainer into a clean pot. Discard the solids. If it tastes thin, boil it to concentrate.
8. **Steep the kombu:** Take the pot off the heat. Add the kombu, cover and steep for 10 minutes, then lift it out.
9. **Blanch the bok choy:** While the kombu steeps, bring a large pot of water to a boil for the noodles. Blanch the bok choy until the stems are tender-crisp, about 2 minutes. Lift it out with a slotted spoon.
10. **Warm the pork:** Warm the chashu with a splash of water in a small covered saucepan over low heat until hot, about 5 minutes.
11. **Season the bowls:** Bring the broth back to a boil. Warm four bowls with hot water and dry them. Divide most of the tare among the bowls, holding a little back. Divide the hot broth among the bowls and stir. Taste and add more tare or a pinch of salt.
12. **Cook the noodles:** Boil the noodles in the pot of water until springy, by the package time. Drain well and divide among the bowls.
13. **Top and serve:** Add the pork, bok choy and egg halves. Scatter with scallions, nori and sesame seeds, and add the menma, garlic chips and chili oil if using. Serve right away.
