## Chef's Note

Inspired by Fukuoka's pork-bone ramen, this bowl pairs a rich, opaque broth with springy noodles, tender pork, greens and fresh scallions. Gelatin from connective tissue gives the broth body; an active boil disperses fat through it, rather than leaving all the richness floating on top. Give the pot a full day with regular attention, then settle the broth's concentration before seasoning each bowl with the soy-miso tare.

## Directions

### The Broth (allow 8–12 hours, with regular checks)

1. **Prepare the Bones:** Choose a large, heavy stockpot with room for the bones, shoulder, water and several inches of bubbling headroom. Pick off loose bone fragments. The blanching step removes debris; the finished broth is meant to be opaque.

2. **Blanch and Clean:** Cover the bones and shoulder with cold water, bring to a boil and boil for 2 minutes. Drain carefully. Rinse off clotted blood and gray scum without splashing, clean the pot and return the bones and meat to it. Clean the sink and work surfaces afterward.

3. **Cover with Fresh Water:** Add the 10 cups fresh water, plus enough extra to submerge the bones if needed. Bring to a rolling boil, with the liquid moving actively throughout the pot. Adjust the heat to maintain that movement without boiling over.

4. **Add the Aromatics:** Add all the ginger, onion, garlic and shiitake. Reserve the kombu. The onion skin and mushrooms may give the broth a cream-to-golden color; pure white is not the test of success.

5. **Remove Scum:** Skim gray or brown foam as it appears, especially during the first 20–30 minutes. Leave the fat needed for richness. Stop chasing clarity; this broth should become cloudy.

6. **Boil and Monitor:** Partially cover if useful, keeping a rolling boil. Stir periodically along the bottom to prevent sticking, and replenish with boiling water to keep the bones submerged. Stay nearby and check regularly; do not leave the boiling pot unattended overnight. Begin checking at 6 hours and allow 8–12 hours overall: connective tissue should be well softened and the opaque broth should have body when you taste a cooled spoonful. Bone color alone does not establish readiness. Make the tare during cooking and begin the eggs near the end.

7. **Strain:** Lift out the large bones carefully, then strain the broth through a fine-mesh strainer into a clean, heatproof pot. Discard the spent bones, shoulder and aromatics. The cooked pork topping is a separate ingredient. Keep the broth unsalted for now.

8. **Measure and Infuse:** Measure the strained broth, aiming for 6 US cups. Reduce excess liquid at a boil; if the broth is overly concentrated, add boiling water a little at a time. Stop adding water if it thins the body. Off the heat, add the kombu to the steaming broth, cover and steep for 10 minutes, then remove it. Measure again before portioning. If fewer than 6 cups has good body, make fewer 1 1/2-cup bowls rather than diluting it solely to reach four servings.

9. **Check the Fat Dispersion:** Keep the broth's dispersed fat for richness. A thick, separate oil cap is not proof of a creamy emulsion: bring the strained broth back to a boil and whisk as needed. Skim a little excess free surface oil only if the broth still feels greasy; do not skim away all the fat.

### The Tare (Flavor Base)

10. **Make the Tare:** Whisk all the soy sauce, white miso and sesame oil together until smooth. This makes approximately 4 1/3 tbsp: enough for 1 tbsp in each of four bowls, with 1 tsp left for adjustments across the whole batch. Cover and refrigerate if making ahead, then stir thoroughly before portioning. Judge the saltiness after mixing with broth.

### Finish the Bowls

11. **Cook the Eggs:** Lower the cold eggs into a saucepan of boiling water. For firm eggs, boil gently for about 11–12 minutes, until both yolks and whites are firm. For the optional jammy version, use pasteurized shell eggs and cook about 7 minutes for set whites and soft, thick yolks; timing varies with egg size and starting temperature. Transfer to ice water for 15 minutes, then peel and halve. Choose firm eggs for children and anyone at higher risk of foodborne illness. Cook jammy eggs close to serving; refrigerate cooked eggs promptly if assembly is delayed.

12. **Prepare Greens and Warm Pork:** While the kombu steeps, bring the noodle pot's separate cooking water to a boil. Add the bok choy for about 2–3 minutes, until the stems are tender-crisp and the leaves wilted; lift it out and drain. In the small saucepan, heat the cooked chashu or pulled pork with the 2 tbsp water, covered, turning or stirring as needed until it reaches 165°F / 74°C throughout. Keep it covered and hot for immediate assembly. Have the scallions, nori, sesame and any optional toppings ready before cooking the noodles.

13. **Prepare the Bowls:** For a full six-cup batch, warm four bowls with hot water, then empty and dry them. Stir the tare and put 1 tbsp into each bowl; reserve the remaining 1 tsp total. If making fewer full bowls, portion only the noodles and toppings you need and refrigerate unused perishable components promptly.

14. **Season and Cook Noodles:** Bring the strained broth back to a boil; chilled broth must reach 165°F / 74°C throughout. Return the separate noodle water to a boil and cook the ramen according to its package timing. As the noodles finish, add 1 1/2 cups hot broth to each prepared bowl and mix with its tare. Taste the diluted broth and adjust with the reserved tare or small pinches of salt, considering the saltiness of the pork and optional menma. Drain noodles thoroughly as soon as they are springy and cooked, then divide among the bowls. Do not use poured-over broth to cook ordinary dried noodles.

15. **Add the Toppings:** Divide the warm pork and bok choy among the bowls and add two egg halves to each. Scatter all the scallions, sesame seeds and nori evenly over the four bowls. If using, divide the drained bamboo shoots and garlic chips among them, then drizzle with the chili oil to taste, up to the listed tablespoon across the batch.

16. **Serve Immediately:** Bring the bowls to the table while the broth is steaming and the noodles still have bite. Keep extra noodles out of the broth until needed; they soften as they sit.

## Make-Ahead and Serving Notes

- **Make-ahead broth:** Divide broth into shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Use within 3 days and reheat to 165°F / 74°C throughout before serving. A solid fat layer does not replace rapid cooling or refrigeration. It also freezes beautifully for up to 2 months.
- **Cook in stages:** The broth and cooked pork can be prepared ahead and chilled separately. Reheat each fully before assembly; make the eggs and noodles close to serving. Making chashu from scratch is a separate cooking project.
- **Season bowl by bowl:** This tare supplies 1 tbsp per 1 1/2 cups broth, with only 1 tsp spare across four bowls. Taste that mixture before adding salt; the concentrated tare alone cannot tell you how the soup will taste.
- **Fresh contrast:** Scallions and tender-crisp greens break up the richness. A small cucumber salad alongside gives a cool, sharp contrast without changing the broth.
