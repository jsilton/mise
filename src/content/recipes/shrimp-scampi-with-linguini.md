## Chef's Note

Scampi is the Italian word for langoustine, but Italian-American restaurants gave the name to shrimp cooked in garlic, butter and white wine. Swirling cold butter into the pan off the heat makes a glossy sauce that clings to every strand instead of pooling on the plate.

## Directions

1. **Heat the water:** Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil.
2. **Sear the shrimp:** Pat the shrimp dry and season with salt and pepper. Heat the shrimp oil in a 12-inch deep skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the shrimp in one layer until pink and just opaque through, 1 to 2 minutes per side. Move them to a plate.
3. **Boil the pasta:** Add the linguine to the boiling water and cook until 1 minute short of al dente. Scoop out a mug of the pasta water, then drain.
4. **Soften the shallots:** While the pasta cooks, lower the skillet to medium. Add the shallot oil and the shallots and cook until soft, 4 to 5 minutes. Add the garlic and pepper flakes and stir until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
5. **Reduce the wine:** Pour in the wine and scrape up the browned bits. Simmer until reduced by half, 2 to 3 minutes.
6. **Toss the pasta:** Add the drained pasta and a splash of pasta water. Toss over low heat until the pasta is tender with a little bite, about 1 minute.
7. **Finish with butter:** Take the pan off the heat. Toss in the cold butter a few pieces at a time, then drizzle in the finishing oil. Add splashes of pasta water until the sauce clings. Fold in half the lemon juice, the parsley and the shrimp with their juices.
8. **Season and serve:** Taste and add more lemon juice, salt and pepper. Serve right away with Parmigiano at the table.
