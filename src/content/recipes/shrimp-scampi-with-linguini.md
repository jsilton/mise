## Chef's Note

The sauce is deliberately rich, with butter and olive oil coating a full pound of pasta. Cook the shrimp separately, give the shallots time to soften and use a little pasta water to bring the finish together. Add lemon gradually so its sharpness balances the butter. Adapted from [Bon Appétit’s Shrimp Scampi](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/shrimp-scampi) into a pasta dish.

## Directions

1. **Prepare and start pasta:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it. Prepare garlic, shallots, parsley and lemon; pat the shrimp dry and season lightly with salt and pepper. Cook linguine about 1 minute short of its package al dente time, coordinating it with the sauce below. Reserve the listed cooking water before draining.

2. **Cook shrimp:** Heat the oil labeled for shrimp over medium to medium-high. Add shrimp in a single layer; use batches if crowded, sharing that oil. Cook about 1–2 minutes per side, continuing as needed until firm, pearly and opaque through the center; a fine-tip thermometer inserted into a thick shrimp should read 145°F / 63°C. Transfer to a clean plate.

3. **Soften aromatics:** Lower heat to medium and add the oil labeled for shallots and garlic. Cook shallots about 4–5 minutes until softened. Add garlic and pepper flakes and stir about 30–45 seconds until fragrant without darkening.

4. **Reduce wine:** Add wine and scrape the pan. Simmer about 2–4 minutes until reduced by roughly half. Add the drained pasta and a small splash of reserved water; toss over low heat until the pasta is tender with a little bite.

5. **Finish off heat:** Remove the pan from heat. Add cold butter a few pieces at a time while tossing, then drizzle in the finishing oil. Add more reserved water in small splashes if needed for a coating that clings. Fold in half the lemon juice, parsley, shrimp and plate juices. Warm very briefly over low heat only if necessary.

6. **Taste and serve:** Taste a strand with sauce before adding remaining lemon juice, salt or pepper. Divide shrimp and pasta evenly among plates and offer Parmesan at the table. Serve promptly rather than holding the pasta over heat.
