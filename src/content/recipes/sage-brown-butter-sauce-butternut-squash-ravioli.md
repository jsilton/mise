## Chef's Note

Watch the milk solids, rather than a fixed browning clock. They should be golden and smell nutty; black flecks taste bitter. This broth-and-cream sauce is fairly thin while warm and thickens as it cools. The minced sage flavors the sauce; whole leaves are a separate optional crisp garnish.

## Directions

1. **Boil:** Cook the ravioli in salted boiling water according to its fresh or frozen package directions. Drain gently; time it to finish near the sauce.

2. **Brown butter and sage:** Melt all the measured butter in a wide skillet over medium heat, stirring and swirling to watch the milk solids. A light-colored pan helps you see them. When golden flecks begin to appear, add all the minced sage and cook briefly until fragrant, watching that the butter stays golden rather than black. Take the pan off heat and cool for 2 minutes.

3. **Add liquids:** Slowly whisk in all the broth, taking care with splatter, then slowly whisk in all the cream. Stir in the measured Parmesan and lemon juice, and season with the listed coarse salt and pepper. The sauce need not be thick at this stage.

4. **Finish:** Add all the cooked ravioli and turn gently until coated. If it needs warming, use gentle heat without a hard boil. Serve promptly with optional extra Parmesan and the crisp whole sage leaves, if using.

## Cooking Notes

For the optional crisp garnish, fry the whole sage leaves in the same measured butter before continuing with the sauce, as described in the whole-sage garnish option. Lift onto a paper towel and season lightly; do not discard the sauce butter.
