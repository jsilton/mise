## Chef's Note

Keep the pieces around 1 inch and give the roots enough sheet area to brown. Size helps, but a beet can still take longer than a sweet potato: group the kinds on the sheets and test each center before the final maple-or-honey and balsamic glaze. Add the glaze late and watch it closely so the sugars brown rather than burn.

## Directions

1. **Cut the roots:** Heat the oven to 425°F. Wash the roots under running water before peeling and cutting into similar 1-inch pieces. Similar size helps, but beets, carrots, parsnips and sweet potatoes can still finish at different times. Set out enough large rimmed baking sheets for an uncrowded layer; the original four-pound batch uses about two large sheets.
2. **Coat the full mixture:** Toss mixed root vegetables (carrots, parsnips, sweet potatoes and beets), extra-virgin olive oil, fresh rosemary or thyme sprigs, garlic cloves, kosher salt, and black pepper thoroughly in a large bowl, coating all the roots with the measured oil and seasoning. Keep the herb sprigs whole. Spread over the sheets with space between pieces, grouping each kind in its own area so ready pieces can be lifted out without guessing. Tuck smashed garlic against the vegetables and check it while turning.
3. **Check each kind separately:** Roast, turning with a spatula around halfway through the first 30–35 minutes and rotating sheets if needed. Start checking by 30 minutes: pierce a thick piece of each kind. Lift tender, browned pieces into a bowl if others remain firm; continue the firm roots, checking frequently. The original 35–40 minute window may suit a particular mix, but a beet-heavy or thicker batch can take longer. Remove blackened garlic, and discard woody herb stems while keeping any tender leaves.
4. **Glaze near the end:** When the roots are tender or nearly tender, return any ready pieces to the sheets. Whisk maple syrup or honey and balsamic vinegar together and drizzle over all the vegetables, sharing the whole measured glaze across however many sheets are used. Toss to coat, then roast for about 5 minutes, checking for bubbling glaze and browned edges before the sugars blacken. Centers should be easily pierced when served. Expect roughly 35–60 minutes total roasting depending on the mix; do not leave tender sweet potatoes in solely to meet the longer clock.
5. **Serve:** Transfer all the glazed roots and cooked garlic to a serving dish and serve warm. There is no required rest; do not leave the pans at room temperature while an unrelated main dish cooks for a long interval.
