## Chef's Note

Jordan Silton's miso salmon pairs a honey-miso marinade with lemon, rice vinegar, garlic and scallions. Oyster-seasoned bok choy and jasmine rice complete the plate. Drain the fish well before roasting and watch closely during the brief broiler finish.

## Directions

1. **Start rice and heat the oven:** Heat the oven to 400°F / 205°C with a rack in the middle and another positioned for the later broil. Rinse the jasmine rice and drain well. Put it in a heavy 2-quart saucepan with 540 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and reduce to low. Simmer about 15 minutes until water is absorbed, then move off the heat and rest covered for 10 minutes. Fluff before serving. This is a starting method for regular jasmine white rice; follow the package if its ratio differs.
2. **Mix and divide the marinade:** While the rice cooks, whisk miso, honey, soy sauce, mirin, lemon juice, rice vinegar, half the minced garlic, half the scallions and half the pepper. Reserve 2 tbsp in a clean bowl for finishing. Check fish for pin bones and pat dry; place it in a shallow dish with the remaining marinade, turn to coat, cover and refrigerate for 15 minutes. Do not leave this acidic marinade on overnight.
3. **Prepare the vegetables and pans:** During the marinade, split small bok choy heads lengthwise; quarter larger heads so thick stem sections are about 1 inch across. Wash between the stalks, drain and dry thoroughly, then divide into two equal batches. Divide the remaining garlic, scallions and pepper between those batches. Line a metal half-sheet pan with foil and spread its teaspoon of oil where the fish will sit.
4. **Roast the salmon:** Lift fish from the marinade, letting excess drip off. Gently scrape off thick clumps, leaving a thin coating. Arrange on the prepared pan with space between portions and discard the raw-contact marinade. Roast about 12–16 minutes, until the fish is nearly cooked; check its center toward the end. If it reaches 145°F / 63°C before the final broil, it is already cooked and needs at most a very brief coloring step.
5. **Cook bok choy in two batches:** While fish roasts, heat a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat with 2 tsp neutral oil. Add half the bok choy cut-side down and cook 2–3 minutes until lightly browned. Turn, add half the reserved vegetable garlic and scallions, and stir 30 seconds until fragrant. Add 1 tbsp water, cover and steam 1–2 minutes until stems are crisp-tender. Uncover, evaporate any pooled water, then toss with 1 tbsp oyster sauce, 1/2 tsp sesame oil and half the remaining pepper. Transfer to a serving dish and loosely cover. Repeat with the second batch and the remaining vegetable ingredients. Turn off the skillet before broiling; if the fish is waiting for its finish, set its pan briefly on a heatproof surface.
6. **Glaze and briefly broil:** Spread the clean reserved 2 tbsp marinade over the four fillets, using a clean utensil. Switch to high broil and place the fish surface 7–9 inches below the element. Watch continuously for 1–3 minutes, until the coating bubbles and browns. Check each fillet sideways in its thickest center and cook to 145°F / 63°C. If color arrives first, finish gently in the oven as described above. Let the fish stand briefly while fluffing and serving the rice.
7. **Serve the complete dinner:** Divide all the rice and bok choy among four bowls or plates and add one fillet to each. The original batch supplies about 225 g raw salmon and 150 g trimmed bok choy per diner, with roughly a cup of cooked rice. A small cucumber salad is an optional cool contrast, rather than a second required vegetable batch.
