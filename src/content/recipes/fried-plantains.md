## Chef's Note

Maduros use very ripe sweet plantains rather than the firm green fruit used for tostones. Mostly black skins and yielding flesh are useful cues; ripening time varies with the starting fruit and room. Fry the half-inch slices gently until golden and soft through the center, lowering heat if the sugar darkens too quickly.

## Directions

1. Choose the listed very ripe plantains: mostly black skins with some yellow and flesh that yields to gentle pressure. If firm and yellow, allow more room-temperature ripening and check daily; there is no guaranteed one- or two-day finish. Reject fruit with visible mold or spoiled flesh rather than treating every black mark as proof of readiness.
2. Wash the skins under running water and dry before cutting. Score lengthwise, peel and slice diagonally into roughly 1/2-inch pieces.
3. Heat all the listed oil in a large skillet over medium heat until shimmering without smoking. Add only enough slices for one uncrowded layer, keeping space to turn; the oil is the total pan-frying supply, not a fresh 3 tbsp dose for each round.
4. Start checking around 3–4 minutes per side, and flip gently with a thin spatula once the underside is golden. Continue until the centers are soft; lower heat if the surface darkens before the center softens. Remove finished pieces while thicker ones continue.
5. Transfer to a paper-towel-lined plate and divide the listed salt pinch and optional cinnamon pinch over all the cooked slices as each batch finishes. Repeat with the remaining slices using the oil left in the pan; adjust heat between rounds so sugary residue does not scorch.
6. Serve promptly for the clearest contrast between browned edges and soft centers. More plantains require more rounds or separate pans, dividing the same full oil supply among them.
