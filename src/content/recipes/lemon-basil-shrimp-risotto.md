## Chef's Note

Add warm broth gradually and stir frequently, keeping the rice at a gentle simmer. Judge the finish by a tender center with a slight bite and a loose, creamy consistency. Return the cooked shrimp only at the end; lemon and basil brighten the rich rice.

## Directions

1.  **The Crunch:** In a large wide skillet, melt butter over medium-high. Add shrimp in a single layer. Cook on both sides until the flesh is pearly and opaque through the center, checking the thickest shrimp rather than relying on a fixed time. Work in batches if needed, sharing the butter between them. Remove to a clean plate; chop the large shrimp into bite-sized pieces if desired.
2.  **Foundation:** In the same pan, add onions and sauté for 2-3 minutes until translucent. Add garlic and sauté for 30-45 seconds.
3.  **Toast:** Add the Arborio rice. Stir for 2 minutes until the edges of the grains look translucent (The Toast Method).
4.  **Deglaze:** Pour in the wine. Stir until completely absorbed.
5.  **The Bind:** Reduce the heat to maintain a gentle simmer. Add hot broth a ladle at a time, stirring frequently and adding the next ladle when the previous one is mostly absorbed. Begin tasting after about 18–22 minutes; keep cooking and adding liquid until the center is tender with a slight bite. The full broth amount may not be needed; if it runs out before the rice is ready, continue with a little hot water.
6.  **The Texture:** Once the rice is al dente, stir in the heavy cream, Parmesan, lemon zest, and lemon juice to taste. Season with the listed salt and pepper. Loosen with a little hot broth or water if the rice sits in a stiff mound.
7.  **Combine:** Fold the seared shrimp and fresh basil into the risotto. Toss for 1 minute until the shrimp are warmed through.
8.  **Serve:** Garnish with optional extra basil and black pepper, and serve immediately. Allow about 50 minutes including preparation; extra shrimp batches or rice that needs longer will extend the time.
