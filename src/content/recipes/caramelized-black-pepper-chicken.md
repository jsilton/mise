## Chef's Note

This brown-sugar shortcut takes its flavor direction from Charles Phan's caramelized black pepper chicken. The sugar, fish sauce, vinegar, and pepper are intentionally assertive, so plain rice is part of the meal's balance. The method reduces a sauce; you do not need to take dry sugar to a dark caramel or burn the chicken to obtain its color.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Pat chicken dry. Mix brown sugar, fish sauce, water, vinegar, ginger, garlic, pepper, and optional chiles in a bowl. Set a separate clean plate aside for the finished chicken.
2. **Brown in batches:** Heat half the oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the chicken in a single layer and cook about 2–3 minutes per side, until golden in patches. Transfer to a temporary plate. Repeat with the remaining oil and chicken. The chicken is not fully cooked yet; keep its plate and juices away from ready-to-eat food.
3. **Soften the shallot:** Reduce heat to medium. Add shallot to the skillet and cook about 3 minutes, stirring, until softened. Add a tablespoon of water if the pan is dry or the browned residue is darkening too quickly.
4. **Start the glaze:** Stir in the prepared sauce, scraping up golden residue. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 2–4 minutes, stirring, until the sugar dissolves and the sauce begins to coat a spoon. Lower the heat if it thickens rapidly.
5. **Finish the chicken:** Return chicken and any accumulated juices to the sauce. Simmer gently, turning the pieces, for about 3–6 minutes, until several larger pieces measure 165°F / 74°C at the center. Add water as needed to keep the sauce fluid while the chicken finishes.
6. **Serve:** Taste the sauce with a bite of plain rice if serving it. Loosen with water if too concentrated. Fold in cilantro and serve the chicken and a restrained spoonful of glaze with rice and vegetables.
