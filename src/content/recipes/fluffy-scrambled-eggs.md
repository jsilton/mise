## Chef's Note

French-style soft scrambled eggs use gentle heat and frequent stirring to make small curds. Heat unfolds proteins and helps them join into a network; too much heat tightens that network and makes the eggs dry. Butter and crème fraîche add richness, but neither instantly stops the cooking, so transfer the finished eggs out of the hot pan.

## Directions

1. **Whisk:** Beat the eggs and salt in a bowl until the whites and yolks are fully combined. Melt butter in a nonstick skillet over low to medium-low heat; it should foam gently without browning.
2. **Stir gently:** Add eggs and continuously sweep a flexible spatula across the bottom and sides. As small curds form, keep folding them through the liquid. If the eggs set rapidly or stick in a dry layer, lift the pan off the heat briefly and lower the burner. Expect about 5–7 minutes, depending on the pan.
3. **Finish cooking:** Stir in the crème fraîche when most of the eggs have formed curds. Continue gently until the eggs are set with no liquid egg pooling and reach 160°F / 71°C. Measure in the eggs without touching the pan. The curds can remain moist without being runny.
4. **Serve promptly:** Transfer to plates immediately. Taste and adjust salt and pepper, then add chives. The hot pan would continue to firm the eggs if you left them in it.
