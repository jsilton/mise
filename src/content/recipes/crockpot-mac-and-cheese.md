## Chef's Note

Regular uncooked elbow pasta cooks in the full milk-and-cheese mixture on LOW. Check and stir after the first hour rather than leaving it unattended for a fixed two hours. Freshly shredded cheese and stopping at tender pasta help the texture, but neither ingredient choice guarantees a smooth sauce in every cooker.

## Directions

1. **Set up:** For the original batch, use the source’s 6-quart slow cooker only if its actual instructions permit the load. Aim for a suitable fill between half and two-thirds full, with a lid that closes; scaling may require smaller, larger or separate cookers, not the same vessel at every amount. Keep milk and cheese refrigerated until preparation. Spray the insert with the listed cooking-spray allowance. Rinse regular uncooked elbow pasta in cold water and drain well.

2. **Build:** Add all the drained uncooked pasta, whole milk, evaporated milk, both cheeses, salt, pepper, dry mustard, garlic powder and cayenne. Stir thoroughly so the pasta is submerged as much as possible. Dot with all the cubed butter. Start cooking immediately; do not use a delayed-start timer.

3. **Cook on LOW:** Cover and cook on LOW for 1 hour, then quickly uncover and stir, reaching the bottom and edges. Check a cooled pasta sample: it should be tender without a hard center. Return the lid promptly if it needs longer. Keep LOW for this method; HIGH is not an equivalent shortcut.

4. **Check and finish:** Continue on LOW as needed, checking and stirring about every 30 minutes as in the saved method, or sooner as it approaches tenderness. Total cooking may take about 1–3 hours depending on the actual cooker and pasta. Stop when the pasta is tender and sauce creamy; check several central locations reach 165°F before serving. That casserole-temperature check and pasta tenderness are separate endpoints, not a claim that a timer alone makes it safe.

5. **Rest and serve:** Let stand 5–10 minutes before serving; it thickens as it cools. If holding for service, use a suitable hot setting and verify at least 140°F rather than assuming WARM is sufficient. Refrigerate leftovers promptly rather than leaving the switched-off cooker to cool.
