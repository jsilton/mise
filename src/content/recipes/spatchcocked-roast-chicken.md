## Chef's Note

Spatchcocking means cutting out the backbone and pressing the chicken flat. The bird then roasts evenly at high heat, so the thighs finish while the breast stays juicy and all the skin turns crisp. Garlic and herbs go under the skin, where they flavor the meat without burning.

## Directions

1. **Remove the backbone:** Set the chicken breast-side down on a board. Cut along both sides of the backbone with kitchen shears and pull it out. Save it for stock.
2. **Flatten:** Turn the chicken skin-side up and press hard on the breastbone until it cracks and the bird lies flat. Pat the skin dry with paper towels.
3. **Season under the skin:** Mix the garlic and herbs with half the rub oil and a third of the salt and pepper into a paste. Loosen the skin over the breasts and thighs with your fingers and spread the paste on the meat underneath.
4. **Season the skin:** Mix the rest of the rub oil, salt and pepper with the paprika and rub it over the skin and underside. If you have time, refrigerate the bird uncovered for up to 8 hours so the skin dries.
5. **Prepare the pan:** Heat the oven to 475°F with a rack in the lower-middle position. Grease a rimmed baking sheet with the pan oil and pour in the water or stock.
6. **Roast:** Set the chicken skin-side up in the pan. Roast until the skin is deep golden and the thickest part of the thigh reads 165°F, 35 to 45 minutes.
7. **Rest:** Move the chicken to a board with a lip and let it rest for 10 minutes.
8. **Make the pan sauce:** While it rests, scrape the pan drippings and browned bits into a small saucepan. Simmer over medium-high heat until slightly reduced, 2 to 3 minutes. Taste before adding salt, since the drippings are already seasoned.
9. **Carve and serve:** Cut the chicken into two leg quarters and two breast halves. Serve with the pan sauce, lemon wedges and a pinch of flaky salt.
