## Chef's Note

This shrimp stir-fry combines Korean sweet potato glass noodles with soy, lime and ginger. The noodles stay chewy when cooked to the package’s endpoint and drained well. Keep their sesame oil separate from the sauce oil, and prepare the sauce and vegetables before the shrimp enter the pan. The cornstarch coating joins the sauce during the final toss; cook the shrimp centers through without waiting for char or a particular curl.

## Directions

1. **Coat and stage:** Pat the prepared raw shrimp dry with paper towels. Toss large shrimp, soy sauce, and cornstarch until evenly coated. Keep chilled during the 10-minute stand while preparing the other ingredients; cook promptly after preparation. Keep coating and sauce soy sauce separate.
2. **Cook noodles:** Have sweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon), toasted sesame oil, and water ready for the noodles. Boil the noodles following the package for water, timing and any rinse instruction, checking for pliable, springy noodles without a hard center. Drain thoroughly and toss with their measured sesame oil. Start checking around 6–7 minutes only if consistent with the actual package.
3. **Whisk sauce:** Whisk soy sauce, fresh lime juice, honey, toasted sesame oil, and fish sauce until the honey disperses; keep beside the stove.
4. **Start shrimp:** Heat a wok or heavy skillet over medium-high to high heat appropriate to the pan, following its preheating instructions; keep the oil below its smoke point. Add 1/2 of the high-smoke-point oil (avocado or grapeseed), reserving the other half of this oil for aromatics. Spread the coated shrimp into a single layer, using batches and dividing this oil allowance across them if needed. Cook about 1 minute per side as an initial check, until the surface changes color; transfer immediately to a temporary dish for further cooking in step 7. Do not taste partly cooked shrimp.
5. **Cook aromatics:** Add 1/2 of the high-smoke-point oil (avocado or grapeseed), garlic cloves, fresh ginger, the green-onion whites, and gochugaru or red pepper flakes (if using) to the pan. Keep all green-onion greens for finishing. Stir about 30 seconds until fragrant, reducing heat if garlic begins to brown too quickly.
6. **Cook vegetables:** Add large carrot and snow peas, carrots first for about 2 minutes, then snow peas for another 1–2 minutes, stirring until crisp-tender. These are first checks, not fixed endpoints for every cut.
7. **Toss and finish cooking:** Return all noodles, shrimp and collected shrimp juices to the pan. Add all the sauce and lift/toss to distribute it, starting to check after 1–2 minutes. Cook until the thickest shrimp are firm, pearly and opaque throughout; FDA guidance is 145°F for seafood when checking with a suitable thermometer. Pink color or a fixed minute count alone does not establish doneness. The sauce should coat the noodles without dry starch patches. A larger batch needs separate complete pan loads; do not crowd the final toss after batching shrimp.
8. **Finish:** Finish with the green-onion greens, lime, and toasted sesame seeds: fold in all green-onion greens, scatter sesame seeds as wanted, and offer the lime wedges for squeezing to taste. This lime is separate from the measured sauce juice. Transfer to a clean serving dish and serve promptly.
