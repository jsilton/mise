## Chef's Note

Drain and press the tofu so free liquid does not dilute the binder. Softened vegetables, finely ground walnuts and breadcrumbs help the mixture hold together; the parsley belongs in the mixture too. Shape small, evenly sized balls so the centers can cook before the outside becomes too dark.

## Directions

1. **Prepare and sauté:** Preheat the oven to 350°F. Press and drain the tofu according to its product instructions; drain off free liquid rather than trying to remove all its moisture. Cook large carrot, large onion, green pepper, garlic cloves, and olive oil in a roomy skillet: heat the oil over medium, add the vegetables and sauté until softened, about 10 minutes, then spread the mixture in a bowl to cool slightly.
2. **Mix:** Combine extra-firm tofu, ground walnuts, whole-wheat breadcrumbs, large eggs, Dijon mustard, toasted sesame oil, soy sauce, dried basil, dried oregano, fresh parsley, and salt and black pepper, to taste in a large bowl: beat the eggs first, then add the other binders and seasonings and crumble in the pressed tofu. Add all the cooled vegetables and their oil. Mix evenly; do not taste the raw egg mixture.
3. **Shape:** Use oil for greasing baking sheets to grease enough baking sheets lightly. Form the mixture into approximately 1½-inch balls and leave space between them; keep this size when scaling, using additional trays or batches.
4. **Bake:** Bake at 350°F, checking from about 20 minutes; the source range is 20–30 minutes. If turning them, wait until the outside is set enough to lift without breaking. Cook until browned and set outside and several thick centers reach 160°F / 71°C with a thin probe. Color alone does not establish a cooked egg binder.
5. **Serve:** Serve the cooked balls with prepared tomato sauce and separately cooked pasta. [Fresh Egg Pasta](/mise/recipes/fresh-egg-pasta) is an optional separately prepared pasta choice: follow its full recipe and plan its dough rest separately.
