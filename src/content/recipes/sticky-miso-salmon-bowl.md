## Chef's Note

Adapted from Andy Baraghani’s NYT Cooking recipe, these bowls combine Japanese miso and short-grain rice with grapefruit, honey and butter. The grapefruit zest supplies a fragrant, slightly bitter edge to the sweet coating, while cool toppings contrast with the warm rice. Watch the glaze closely under the broiler and check the fish's center separately.

## Directions

1. **Start the rice:** Rinse the rice in several changes of cool water until less cloudy, then drain well. Put it in a heavy 3-quart saucepan with the measured 535 ml water and soak for 30 minutes. Keep this water for cooking.
2. **Prepare the bowl ingredients:** During the soak, slice the scallions and cucumber, snip the nori, and measure the optional toppings. Whisk the miso, honey, tablespoon of oil, ginger, grapefruit zest and juice until smooth. Taste before it touches fish; no additional salt is specified because the miso and optional toppings already supply seasoning. Check the salmon for pin bones, pat dry, and refrigerate it until cooking time.
3. **Cook and rest the rice:** Bring the rice and soaking water to a boil over medium heat with the lid slightly ajar. Cover tightly, reduce to low and simmer gently for 12–15 minutes. Briefly check for unabsorbed water; if there is a pool, cover and cook a little longer. Move off the heat and leave covered for 10 minutes. A finished grain should be tender all the way through. Fold in the butter and scallions without mashing.
4. **Heat the broiler and coat the fish:** As the rice begins its low simmer, heat the broiler on high and set the rack so the fish will sit 7–9 inches from the element. Line a rimmed metal half-sheet pan with foil and spread the teaspoon of oil where the fillets will sit. Arrange fish with at least an inch between portions. Divide all the glaze over their tops and sides in an even layer, leaving no thick mounds. Wash tools and hands that touched raw fish; none of this coating is a cold serving sauce.
5. **Broil with attention:** Broil for approximately 10–16 minutes, checking the surface after 4 minutes and frequently thereafter. Rotate the pan if browning is uneven. Brown patches are welcome; a black, smoking crust is not. Check each fillet with a thermometer inserted sideways into its thickest center and remove it when it reaches 145°F / 63°C and flakes under gentle pressure. If the top browns too soon, use the gentler oven finish described above. Let the fish stand briefly while portioning the rice.
6. **Build the bowls:** Slice the avocado. Divide all the buttered scallion rice among four bowls, then add one salmon fillet, a quarter of the cucumber and avocado, and the nori. Offer the measured kimchi and chile crisp at the table so each person can choose the heat and fermented flavor. This is already a complete rice bowl; any edamame side is additional.
