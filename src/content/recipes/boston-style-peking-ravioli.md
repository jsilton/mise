## Chef's Note

Boston’s Peking ravioli are pork-and-cabbage dumplings with a satisfying wrapper and a browned base. Make a resilient cold-water dough for the classic route, keep the filling juicy without free cabbage liquid, and steam each pan load through before finishing its underside.

## Directions

1. **Make and rest the dough:** Combine the all-purpose flour, water, and additional water (if using), starting with the measured main water and reserving the optional additional water. Stir, then knead about 3–4 minutes until smooth and pliable; add the optional water gradually only if dry flour remains. Cover with a damp towel and rest at least 30 minutes while preparing the filling and sauce.
2. **Salt and squeeze the cabbage:** Have the Napa cabbage and fine salt for the cabbage ready. Finely chop the cabbage, sprinkling its curing salt over it as you chop. Enclose in a clean cloth and squeeze firmly to remove released liquid. Discard that liquid and keep all the squeezed cabbage for the filling. The drained volume varies; do not add more raw cabbage or discard solids merely to reach a cup mark.
3. **Mix the filling:** Mix the ground pork, soy sauce, dry sherry, fine salt for the filling, MSG, sugar, neutral cooking oil, melted lard, or bacon drippings, additional neutral oil, melted lard, or bacon drippings (if using), and toasted sesame oil with all the squeezed cabbage until evenly combined and slightly tacky. Include the additional fat only if the pork is lean. Cover and refrigerate while rolling; keep waiting raw filling cold, bringing out only what you will wrap soon.
4. **Mix the dipping sauce:** Stir the soy sauce, Chinkiang black vinegar, brown sugar or molasses, fresh ginger, and chili crisp (if using) until the sweetener disperses, using the chili crisp only if desired. Keep the sauce separate from raw-filling utensils and set aside for serving.
5. **Roll and fill:** Use the additional all-purpose flour sparingly to prevent sticking. For the original two-cup flour batch, divide the rested dough into about 32 equal pieces and distribute all the filling among them. Scale that planned piece count with the formula rather than making larger dumplings. Roll a few pieces at a time to roughly 3-inch rounds, leaving the center a little thicker and the rim thinner. Keep unused dough covered. Place a small mound of filling in each center, leaving a clean margin; fold into a half moon and pinch the edge firmly shut, adding a few pleats if desired. If a wrapper will not close comfortably, roll it a little wider rather than forcing in more filling. Keep finished dumplings covered on a lightly floured tray and refrigerated between wrapping rounds.
6. **Steam and brown one load:** Have the neutral cooking oil and cold water measured for this skillet load. Heat an 8–9-inch skillet over medium-high heat and coat it with the oil. Arrange one flat layer of dumplings; Joyce Chen’s example holds about 16 lightly touching pieces, but use fewer if the shapes crowd. Carefully add the cold water, cover immediately, and cook about 6–7 minutes. When most water has evaporated, lower the heat and continue about 2 minutes, checking the largest fillings with a thin-tip thermometer: at least 165°F / 74°C throughout, with tender cooked dough at the seams. If filling or wrapper is not ready, add a little more water, cover, and keep cooking. Once cooked and the water is gone, uncover briefly if needed to finish golden bottoms. Loosen with a spatula; do not rely on the clock or color alone.
7. **Serve and repeat:** Transfer the cooked load to a clean plate and serve promptly with the dipping sauce. Repeat with the same per-skillet oil and water amounts for each new load, lowering the heat if the emptied pan overheats. Four main portions are a planning serving choice; the finished piece count depends on how you divide and roll the dough.

## Variations and serving notes

**Aromatic hot-water variation — the original four-portion batch:** Replace the classic wrapper flour and all its water with **250 g all-purpose flour and 130 ml water total**. Use two-thirds of that measured water boiling hot and one-third at room temperature: stir the hot portion into the flour with chopsticks or a spoon, then add the cool portion. When safe to handle, bring into a dough, rest covered 10–15 minutes, knead smooth, then rest covered another 30–60 minutes. Use only a little extra flour for dusting.

For this variation, use **1 lb ground pork** in place of ¾ lb, add **3 minced scallions, 1 tbsp grated ginger, 2 minced garlic cloves, and ½ tsp white pepper** to the filling, and use **1 tbsp Shaoxing wine** in place of the dry sherry. Keep the **1 lb raw cabbage and its separate 1 tsp curing salt**, all the other classic filling seasonings/fats, and the entire ginger dipping sauce. Divide this dough and filling into about 32 dumplings at the original size, keeping a thin sealed rim; roll wider as needed to close rather than overfilling. Use the same **per-pan** oil/water and 165°F filling/tender-wrapper endpoints. Scale the wrapper, filling, and sauce quantities proportionally; the per-pan water/oil remains fixed for the stated pan. Use this softer hot-water wrapper for the covered-pan method; use the classic cold-water wrapper for boiling.

**Boiled classic variation:** Use the full classic cold-water wrapper and filling formula. Bring a generous pot of water to a boil, add only enough dumplings to move freely, then maintain a controlled simmer, stirring gently to prevent sticking. Cook until several larger fillings reach 165°F / 74°C and their wrapper seams are tender. Lift with a slotted spoon, drain, and serve with the same dipping sauce. Floating is only a progress cue. The covered-pan water/oil is omitted for this method.

Serve with [smashed cucumber salad](/mise/recipes/smashed-cucumber-salad/), or accompany with separately prepared [Cantonese wonton broth](/mise/recipes/cantonese-wonton-broth/). These pairings do not replace the dumpling cooking liquid.
