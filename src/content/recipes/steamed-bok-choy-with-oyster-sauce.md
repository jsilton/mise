## Chef's Note

Bok choy has two different textures to manage: tender leaves and firmer, juicy stems. A little covered steam cooks the stems before the final garlic sauce goes in. Oyster sauce already supplies salt and sweetness, so the finish needs only a small measured amount of soy sauce.

## Directions

1. **Wash and prepare:** Trim damaged ends while keeping enough base to hold small heads together. Halve lengthwise; quarter broad heads. Swish in water and rinse carefully between stalks, then lift out and drain well. Divide into two batches.
2. **Mix the sauce:** Stir oyster sauce, soy sauce and the separate tablespoon sauce water together. Set aside with sliced garlic and sesame oil.
3. **Cook the bok choy in two batches:** Heat 1 tsp neutral oil in a lidded skillet over medium-high. Arrange half the bok choy cut-side down and cook about 1 minute. Carefully add 2 tbsp steaming water, cover and cook 2–3 minutes, until a knife enters the thick stem with slight resistance. Add a tablespoon more water and cook longer if the pan dries before the stems soften. Transfer to a plate, draining free water. Repeat with the second teaspoon oil, remaining bok choy and 2 tbsp water.
4. **Make the garlic finish:** Pour away excess pan liquid. Lower heat to medium, add the final teaspoon neutral oil and sliced garlic. Stir 20–30 seconds until fragrant and still pale. Add the mixed sauce and let it bubble briefly.
5. **Coat and serve:** Return drained bok choy and turn gently in the sauce for about 30 seconds. Take off heat and drizzle with sesame oil. Divide into four sides, spooning over only enough sauce to coat lightly. Add optional fried garnish immediately before serving.
