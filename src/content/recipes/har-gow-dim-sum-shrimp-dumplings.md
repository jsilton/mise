## Chef's Note

Har gow are the Cantonese dim sum shrimp dumplings with clear, pleated wrappers. The dough is wheat starch scalded with boiling water, which makes it pliable and lets it steam up glassy. Stirring the shrimp hard in one direction until sticky gives the filling its springy bite.

## Directions

1. **Make the filling:** Coarsely chop the shrimp. In a bowl, combine the shrimp, oyster sauce, vegetable oil, sesame oil, white pepper, salt, sugar and ginger. Stir hard in one direction until very sticky, about 3 minutes.
2. **Chill the filling:** Fold in the bamboo shoots. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes while you make the dough.
3. **Make the dough:** Whisk the wheat starch and cornstarch in a heatproof bowl. Pour in the boiling water while stirring fast with chopsticks, then stir in the lard.
4. **Knead and divide:** When the dough is cool enough to handle, knead until smooth, about 2 minutes. If it cracks, knead in a little more boiling water. Roll it into a log, cut it into 18 pieces and cover them with a damp towel.
5. **Shape the wrappers:** Press each piece into a thin 3-inch circle with a lightly oiled cleaver blade or a rolling pin. Keep the rest covered as you work.
6. **Fill and pleat:** Put a spoonful of filling in the center of each wrapper. Fold it over and pleat one edge, 7 to 10 pleats, then pinch the seam closed.
7. **Steam:** Line a bamboo steamer with perforated parchment and set the dumplings an inch apart. Steam over boiling water on high heat until the wrappers turn translucent and the shrimp shows pink through them, about 6 minutes. Work in batches.
8. **Serve:** Serve hot with chili oil and soy sauce.
