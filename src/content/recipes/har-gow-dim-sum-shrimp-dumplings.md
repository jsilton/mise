## Chef's Note

Har gow are Cantonese dim sum shrimp dumplings with translucent wrappers. They use a boiling-water starch dough, distinct from egg-and-flour wonton wrappers. Keep the dough covered as you work. Stir the shrimp filling in one direction until sticky, then fold in the bamboo shoots; fill lightly enough that the wrappers close without tearing.

## Directions

1. **Mix filling:** Thaw frozen shrimp in the refrigerator if needed and pat dry. Mash and coarsely chop large shrimp; for small shrimp, the source also permits leaving them whole or halving them. Combine with all the oyster sauce, measured vegetable oil, white pepper, sesame oil, sea salt, sugar and minced ginger. Stir vigorously in one direction for about 3 minutes until sticky, then fold in all the minced bamboo shoots. Cover and refrigerate for a 30-minute chill while preparing the dough; keep the filling cold between assembly batches.
2. **Mix dough:** Whisk all the wheat starch and your chosen cornstarch or tapioca starch in a heatproof bowl. Slowly pour in the full measured boiling water while stirring rapidly, then add all the measured lard or neutral oil. Wheat starch is not interchangeable with wheat flour.
3. **Knead and divide:** When cool enough to handle without burning, knead about 2 minutes until smooth. If a test piece cracks when gently rolled, the source permits the listed optional extra boiling-water allowance in dry conditions; work it in cautiously, let it cool enough to handle and check again rather than automatically adding it. Divide into roughly the listed dumpling count, keeping each piece the original size, and cover with a damp towel. More or fewer same-sized pieces are needed at other scales.
4. **Roll:** Roll each piece into a thin circle about 3 inches across, keeping the remaining pieces covered. If using the flat-blade shaping option, lightly oil the blade with the separate tooling allowance; the measured dough and filling fats remain in their own mixtures.
5. **Fill:** Divide all the filling among the wrappers, starting with a modest spoonful rather than a fixed tablespoon in every piece. Fold and pleat to close, keeping the rim clean. Do not taste raw filling, and wash raw-contact hands and utensils before handling cooked dumplings.
6. **Steam:** Line a steamer with perforated parchment and bring its water to a boil. Leave about 1½ inches around dumplings for expansion and work in batches, replenishing the steamer according to its instructions. Cover and steam on high heat; begin checking around the source’s 6-minute mark rather than stopping at an exact clock. Check representative fillings, including a larger dumpling in each batch, for 165°F /74°C, continuing when needed. Use a thin probe without touching the steamer and check the center. Serve on a clean plate.
7. **Serve:** Serve hot with the optional chili oil and soy sauce. These are separate serving allowances, not required additions to the filling.
