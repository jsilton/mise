## Chef's Note

Keep the butter cold and leave pea-sized pieces in the flour. Two gentle folds create layers; press the cutter straight down and keep the biscuits close together in the pan. Check the middle biscuit as well as the tops before the ten-minute rest.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 450°F. Grease a cast-iron skillet large enough to hold the biscuits close together without crushing them. Whisk all the flour, baking powder and salt in a large bowl.

2. **Cut in and mix:** Work the cold butter into the dry mixture until coarse crumbs and pea-sized butter pieces remain. Stir in the measured buttermilk just until the dough comes together.

3. **Fold and cut:** Turn onto a lightly floured surface. Pat into a rectangle about 1 inch thick, fold over twice, then pat back to about 1 inch thick. Cut with a floured biscuit cutter, pressing straight down without twisting. Gently gather scraps for the remaining biscuits. A 2-inch cutter is a useful size for this batch; the count depends on thickness and scraps.

4. **Keep cold and brush:** Arrange close together in the skillet. If the oven is still heating or the butter has softened, refrigerate the biscuits; a brief freezer chill is another option. Load only after the oven reaches 450°F. Brush with extra buttermilk if desired, or use the optional beaten egg mixed with a splash of water instead; discard leftover raw egg wash.

5. **Bake and rest:** Bake on a lower rack for about 15 minutes, then move higher for about 3 minutes if the tops need color. They should be golden and the middle biscuit should have a set crumb rather than wet dough; continue baking if needed. Brush with the optional melted finishing butter, or rub the finishing butter stick over the warm tops, then rest in the skillet for 10 minutes before serving.

## Cooking Notes

For the original batch, a 2-inch cutter commonly gives about 8–10 biscuits; nine is a planning count. For more dough, use additional suitable skillets or bake in loads while keeping unbaked biscuits cold. Keep thickness and cutter size unchanged; oven temperature and bake time do not multiply.
