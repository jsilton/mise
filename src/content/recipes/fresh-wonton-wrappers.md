## Chef's Note

A firm dough and a covered rest make thin sheets easier to handle. Cornstarch helps keep the sheets and cut wrappers separate. Roll by the feel and evenness of the sheet rather than a machine number, and cover each small batch so the edges remain pliable for folding.

## Directions

1. **Mix:** Combine all-purpose flour, kosher salt, egg, and water in a bowl: first whisk the flour and salt, then make a well and mix in the beaten egg and water. Stir and press until a firm, shaggy dough forms. Do not taste the raw dough.
2. **Knead:** Knead for about 10 minutes until the dough holds together and becomes more elastic. If persistent dry crumbs will not join the dough, work in additional water, stopping as soon as they adhere. The dough should be firm rather than sticky or soft.
3. **Rest covered:** Wrap tightly and rest for 30–60 minutes so the dough becomes easier to roll. In a kitchen above 90°F, rest in the refrigerator instead. Keep track of total counter time from mixing onward, including rolling; refrigerate the dough if work is interrupted.
4. **Roll thin sheets:** Divide the dough into manageable pieces, about four for the original batch, and keep the pieces you are not rolling covered; refrigerate them if rolling is slow. Larger batches may need more pieces rather than larger sheets. Flatten one piece into a rectangle and dust both sides with cornstarch. With a pasta roller, start at its widest opening and pass the dough through progressively narrower openings, keeping the sheet even. Cut a long sheet crosswise if it becomes awkward to handle. With a rolling pin, roll evenly from the center outward, giving dough that springs back another brief covered rest. Stop just short of paper thin: the sheet should be evenly thin and flexible, but still lift and fold without tearing. Machine numbers vary, so use the sheet itself as the guide. Re-dust sheets and finished wrappers with the same cornstarch as needed to keep them separate.
5. **Cut and cover:** Cut into approximately 3½-inch squares with a knife or pizza cutter and a straightedge. Keep cut wrappers covered as you work so the edges stay pliable. Gather clean offcuts, press them together, cover and rest briefly before rolling them again. For the original batch, expect about 60 wrappers, but thickness, trimming and re-rolling change the count.
6. **Fill or chill promptly:** Fill the wrappers as they are made, using a wonton filling recipe and cooking the filled wontons according to that recipe. Keep the uncooked filling chilled while working. For a pause, stack the dusted wrappers in small portions, cover tightly and refrigerate promptly at 40°F or below. Plan to use cut wrappers the same day. Count mixing, resting, rolling and filling together: keep total time out of refrigeration under 2 hours, or under 1 hour above 90°F.
7. **Freeze unused wrappers:** For longer storage, promptly pack dusted wrappers in flat portions separated with parchment, wrap airtight and freeze at 0°F. Thaw a wrapped portion in the refrigerator and use the same day. The dough and wrappers still need thorough cooking; freezing does not make raw flour or egg ready to eat.
