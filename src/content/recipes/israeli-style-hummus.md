## Chef's Note

This tahini-rich version is adapted from Michael Solomonov's Israeli-style hummus. The key checkpoint is the cooked chickpea: its center should mash into cream before it reaches the processor.

## Directions

1. **Soak overnight:** Put the sorted, rinsed chickpeas in a large bowl with half the baking soda and half the soaking/cooking water. Stir, cover, and refrigerate for 12 hours. Leave plenty of room for expansion. Drain, discard the soaking water, and rinse well.
2. **Cook to a creamy center:** Put the chickpeas in a 4-quart pot with the remaining baking soda and fresh cooking water. Bring to a boil, allowing about 15 minutes, then reduce to a steady simmer. Skim foam as needed and leave the lid ajar. Keep the beans submerged, adding water if needed. Begin checking at 60 minutes; allow up to 2 hours of simmering or longer for older beans. Press several chickpeas with a fork: they must smear without a firm center. Some splitting is welcome. Keep them in the liquid until the tahini base is ready.
3. **Infuse and strain garlic:** While the chickpeas simmer, put the garlic, initial measured lemon juice and salt in a mini chopper or the processor's small insert. Pulse until the cloves are finely broken up in the juice, stopping to scrape. Leave for 10 minutes, then press through a fine-mesh strainer into a bowl. Discard solids and skins. Transfer the strained juice to the clean main processor bowl, checking that no skin fragments remain.

4. **Make the sesame base:** Add the stirred tahini to the garlic-lemon juice and process. Add half the measured blending water gradually, stopping to scrape, until the paste is uniform. It may become very thick before it smooths out.
5. **Drain and puree:** Drain the chickpeas thoroughly through a fine-mesh strainer, retaining any soft chickpea pieces but discarding the cooking water. Transfer to the processor while warm, following its temperature and fill limits, and add the cumin. Process for 4–5 minutes, pausing to scrape and following the machine's duty-cycle guidance. Add the remaining measured blending water a tablespoon at a time only as needed, then additional fresh water in small increments if the puree still holds a stiff mound. Aim for a smooth spread that holds a shallow spoon swirl; the hummus will firm as it cools.
6. **Balance and serve:** Taste a small spoonful at a comfortable eating temperature. Add some of the optional lemon juice if the hummus needs more acidity, and adjust salt in small pinches. Spread the amount being served in a shallow dish, make a well, and finish with the serving oil and optional paprika; use a proportional share of each garnish if serving only part of the batch. Offer warm pita and a crisp, tangy salad alongside. As a side to chicken, allow about 1/4 cup hummus per person; for five people that is 1 1/4 cups, leaving the rest to refrigerate. Pita, salad, and chicken are additional components.
