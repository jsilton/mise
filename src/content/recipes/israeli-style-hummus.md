## Chef's Note

This is Michael Solomonov's hummus, the very smooth, tahini-heavy style served in Israeli hummus shops. Baking soda in the soak and the pot cooks the chickpeas until they fall apart. Ice water whipped into the tahini turns it pale and fluffy before the hot chickpeas go in.

## Directions

1. **Soak the chickpeas:** Put the chickpeas, half the baking soda and half the water in a large bowl. Cover and refrigerate for 12 hours or overnight. Drain and rinse.
2. **Simmer until very soft:** Put the chickpeas, the rest of the baking soda and the rest of the water in a 4-quart pot. Bring to a boil, then simmer with the lid ajar, skimming the foam, until the chickpeas fall apart and mash into a smooth cream with no firm center, 1 to 2 hours. Add water if they peek above the surface.
3. **Make the garlic lemon:** While the chickpeas simmer, pulse the garlic, lemon juice and salt in a mini chopper until the garlic is finely chopped. Let it sit for 10 minutes, then press it through a fine-mesh strainer into the bowl of a food processor. Discard the solids.
4. **Whip the tahini:** Add the tahini to the processor and blend until thick. With the motor running, pour in half the ice water a little at a time until the paste turns pale, smooth and creamy.
5. **Blend the chickpeas:** Drain the hot chickpeas well and add them with the cumin. Process until very smooth, 4 to 5 minutes, scraping down the bowl now and then. Add the rest of the ice water a spoonful at a time until the hummus holds a soft swirl.
6. **Season and serve:** Taste and add more lemon juice and salt if it needs them. Spread in a shallow bowl, make a well in the center, then drizzle with the olive oil and sprinkle with paprika, if using.
