## Chef's Note

This rich drop-biscuit dough is scooped rather than rolled. Keep the butter cold, then stir the milk only until the dough holds together. Similar mound sizes and a fully heated oven are more useful than an exact stirring clock.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 400°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment, or grease it with the handling butter. Keep the measured dough butter cold.

2. **Dry mix:** Whisk all the flour, baking powder, sea salt and optional garlic powder in a bowl.

3. **Cut in:** Toss in the cold butter cubes, then quickly rub or cut them into the flour with your fingers or a pastry blender until coarse meal and a few pea-sized pieces remain. Alternatively, pulse the dry mixture and butter in a food processor 2–3 times to reach the same coarse state, then transfer to a bowl.

4. **Hydrate:** Pour in all the milk and stir with a fork just until a slightly sticky, shaggy dough comes together. Stop when no dry pockets remain; the dough does not need kneading or an exact stirring duration.

5. **Drop:** Divide into similar mounds, leaving room between them. The original-size main batch plans 12 biscuits; follow the listed scaled count. For the separate smaller option, use a teaspoon or small scoop for about 20 walnut-sized mounds from the original batch. For the larger option, use a ¼ cup scoop for about 8 from the original batch. Scale those approximate counts with the amount of dough while keeping the chosen scoop size.

6. **Bake:** Load only once the oven has reached 400°F. Bake until golden with baked centers, beginning checks around 15 minutes for small biscuits and around 20 minutes for the ¼ cup size. The 12-piece batch may fall between them; use the crumb and color rather than assuming a fixed duration. Bake further trays as needed while keeping waiting dough cold.

7. **Cool and serve:** Let the biscuits cool slightly until they lift intact, then transfer to a rack. Serve warm or at room temperature, with the optional salted butter and honey if desired.

## Salt Variation

Replace the listed sea salt with the same measured volume of kosher salt for the separate kosher-salt version; all other ingredients and steps remain unchanged. Different salt crystal sizes make equal spoon volumes differently salty, so this is a distinct flavor choice, not a universal weight equivalent.
