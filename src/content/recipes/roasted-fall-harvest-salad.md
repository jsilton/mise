## Chef's Note

Massaging kale with olive oil and salt softens the leaves and reduces their bulk. Roasting well-dried chickpeas and squash at 450°F gives the chickpeas browned, crisp edges that contrast fluffy quinoa and soft roasted squash. Apple cider in the tahini dressing adds tangy brightness that balances the earthy quinoa and sweet squash.

## Directions

1.  **Roast:** Preheat oven to 450°F. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, then dry them well. Toss chickpeas and squash with the cumin, coriander, cinnamon, cayenne, enough oil to coat lightly, and salt. Spread in one uncrowded layer on a metal sheet pan. Roast for 25-30 minutes, until the squash is tender and the chickpeas are browned with crisp edges; give them more time if needed.
2.  **Quinoa:** Bring all the quinoa and listed cooking water to a boil with a pinch of salt, then reduce the heat and simmer, partly covered, beginning to check at 10–12 minutes, until the water is absorbed and the grains are tender with no hard center; follow longer actual package instructions if needed. Let stand, covered, for 10 minutes before fluffing.
3.  **Massage:** Place kale in a large bowl with a little oil and salt. **Massage for 2 minutes** until soft and dark green.
4.  **Dress:** Whisk all the tahini, lemon juice, measured cider and syrup until creamy. If needed, add only the separate additional cider allowance a little at a time to loosen it. The original batch’s optional thinning guide is an additional 1–2 tbsp; scale that guide proportionally and add only as much as needed.
5.  **Assemble:** Toss kale with quinoa and half the dressing.
6.  **Finish:** Top with the roasted chickpeas, squash, pepitas, and optional raisins. Serve the remaining dressing alongside, adding only as much as wanted.

## Cooking Notes

The squash amount is a small portion of a larger butternut squash, not a whole large squash. Prepare the kale and dressing while the quinoa cooks and the vegetables roast. The original batch plans four side portions; follow the listed portion count when scaling. Chickpeas soften after dressing, so add them just before serving.
