## Chef's Note

Macerate peaches with their share of sugar, then mash them finely so large pieces do not freeze into hard chunks. Keep both the drained syrup and fruit: syrup flavors the custard, while fruit goes into the final moments of churning. Cook the yolk custard gently and chill it fully before using the machine.

## Directions

1.  **Macerate:** Toss peeled peaches with two-fifths of the sugar and the lemon juice (1/2 cup sugar at the original batch size). Let stand for 30-60 minutes, until juicy. Mash until only very small pieces remain, then strain, reserving both the liquid and fruit. Refrigerate both until needed.
2.  **Custard:** In a saucepan, heat cream, milk, salt, and another two-fifths of the sugar (1/2 cup at the original batch size) until steaming but not boiling. This can overlap the peach maceration.
3.  **Emulsify:** Whisk yolks with the remaining one-fifth of the sugar (1/4 cup at the original batch size). Slowly whisk in a little of the hot dairy at a time until the yolks are warmed; at the original batch size, about 1 cup in total is used to temper. Return everything to the pan.
4.  **Cook:** Stir over medium-low heat until the custard reaches 170-175°F and coats a spoon; do not boil. Remove from the heat and stir in vanilla.
5.  **Chill:** Strain into a clean, shallow bowl. Whisk in the peach syrup. Refrigerate promptly for **at least 4 hours** until thoroughly cold, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Prepare the ice cream maker, freezing its bowl in advance if required by the manufacturer.
6.  **Churn:** Pour into the prepared ice cream maker, respecting its fill limit; churn in batches if necessary. Churn according to its instructions until soft-serve consistency. Additional batches add time. Divide the mashed peaches in proportion to the custard portions, keep waiting custard and fruit refrigerated, and add each fruit portion during that batch’s last 30 seconds.
7.  **Set:** Freeze for 4-6 hours to ripen the texture.
