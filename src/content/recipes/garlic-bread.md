## Chef's Note

This Italian-American home version combines garlic butter, parsley and Parmesan. Weigh the bread so the topping has a useful ratio: a vague large loaf could be twice the size of a baguette. Baking warms the center; an optional brief broil adds top color. The finished bread should taste of garlic and butter without scorched garlic or an oil-soaked crumb.

## Directions

1. **Prepare oven and bread:** Heat the oven to 400°F / 205°C. Halve the loaf lengthwise and put both pieces cut-side up on a rimmed baking sheet. Use already softened butter; allow extra time if starting with a cold block.
2. **Mix spread:** Stir butter, garlic, parsley, Parmesan and the measured salt until uniform. Use less salt if the bread and cheese are already strongly seasoned.
3. **Spread evenly:** Divide all the butter mixture across the two cut surfaces, reaching the edges in a thin, even layer. Avoid large isolated clumps of garlic.
4. **Bake:** Bake about 12–15 minutes until the edges are golden and the center is hot with melted butter. Thick loaves may need a few more minutes; cover loosely with foil if the edges brown too soon.
5. **Optional top color:** If wanted, broil briefly for about 20–60 seconds, watching continuously and stopping as soon as the top colors. Keep the pan at least 6 inches from the element and follow the oven's broiler guidance. Do not put parchment close to a broiler.
6. **Portion:** Cut each half into six pieces, making twelve pieces total, or two pieces per side portion. Serve hot. For a small accompaniment to a pasta-containing soup, make half a batch and share its six pieces among six diners.
