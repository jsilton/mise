## Chef's Note

Pearl couscous, also called Israeli couscous or ptitim, is a small toasted pasta rather than true couscous. Toast it in butter before the broth goes in, the way you start a pilaf, and it turns nutty and chewy. It makes a buttery, garlicky bed for grilled chicken and anything with a sauce.

## Directions

1. **Toast the couscous:** Melt half the butter in a 2-quart saucepan over medium heat. Add the couscous and stir until a few pearls turn golden and smell toasty, 2 to 3 minutes.
2. **Add the garlic:** Stir in the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Don't let it brown.
3. **Simmer:** Pour in the broth and add the salt and pepper. Bring to a boil, then cover, turn the heat to low and simmer until the pearls are tender with a little chew and the broth is absorbed, 10 to 12 minutes.
4. **Rest and finish:** Take the pan off the heat and leave it covered for 5 minutes. Fluff with a fork and fold in the rest of the butter, the parsley and the lemon zest. Taste and add salt if needed.
