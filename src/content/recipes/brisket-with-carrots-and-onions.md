## Chef's Note

This tomato-brisket braise uses flat cut, carrots, celery, onions and six bay
leaves. A tight cover gives the brisket time to become
tender without adding a sear. The final reduction is a separate stage: remove the
meat, discard the bay leaves, and concentrate the vegetables and tomato juice
only until the sauce still generously coats the sliced brisket.

## Directions

1. **Heat the oven and prepare the pan:** Heat the oven to 350°F / 175°C. Choose a heavy roasting pan that holds the brisket in one layer with the vegetables and has room for the full can of tomato juice below its rim.
2. **Season the brisket:** Mix the kosher salt, pepper, garlic and oregano. Rub the mixture over all sides of the brisket.
3. **Layer the braise:** Put the brisket in the pan. Arrange all the carrots, celery, onions and bay leaves on and around it.
4. **Add the tomato juice and seal:** Pour in the entire 46-ounce can of tomato juice. The pan geometry and vegetables determine how high it comes around the meat; it does not need to reach a fixed mark. Lay two sheets of parchment over the pan, then seal tightly with heavy-duty foil.
5. **Braise until tender:** Bake, covered, for about 3 1/2 hours. Check the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer: it must reach at least 145°F / 63°C, followed by a 3-minute rest, and it must also be fork-tender. If the temperature is safe but the fork meets resistance, re-cover and continue in 15-minute intervals until tender.
6. **Rest and reduce:** Transfer the brisket to a cutting board and tent loosely with foil. Remove and discard all bay leaves. If the roasting pan is approved for stovetop use across two burners, use it; otherwise transfer all vegetables and sauce to a wide saucepan. Boil over medium-high heat for 20–30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until glossy and thickened but still saucy.
7. **Slice and serve:** Find the direction of the meat fibers and slice across the grain. Serve the brisket with the reduced sauce, carrots, celery and onions.
