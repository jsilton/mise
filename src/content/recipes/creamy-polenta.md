## Chef's Note

The cornmeal needs enough water and time to become tender; thickness alone is not a doneness signal. This version keeps its milk, butter and Parmesan for a rich, soft base under braises or ratatouille. Keep the heat gentle, scrape the pan as you stir, and finish with a cooled taste for any remaining grit.

## Directions

1. **Heat the liquid:** Combine the measured water, milk and salt in a heavy 3–4-quart saucepan. Bring to a gentle boil over medium-high heat, stirring occasionally and watching that the milk does not boil over.
2. **Whisk in the cornmeal:** Reduce to medium-low. Pour the cornmeal in a slow, thin stream while whisking continuously. Continue whisking for about 2 minutes so no dry pockets remain.
3. **Cook gently:** Reduce to low so the mixture barely bubbles. Cook about 35–40 minutes, stirring every few minutes and scraping the bottom and corners. Add small splashes of warm water or milk if it becomes too stiff to stir before the grain is tender. Stir more often as it thickens.
4. **Check both texture and thickness:** Cool a small spoonful before tasting. It should taste tender and creamy, without grit, and spread softly from the spoon. If gritty, loosen and continue cooking. If tender but watery, stir over low heat a little longer.
5. **Enrich:** Remove from heat. Stir in butter and Parmesan until incorporated. Add black pepper, taste, and adjust seasoning only after the cheese is mixed in.
6. **Serve:** Divide into four generous side portions and serve promptly. If the polenta tightens while plating, stir in a little warm water or milk until it is spoonable again.
