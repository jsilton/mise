## Chef's Note

This Silton family Thanksgiving side keeps its full butter, sugar, maple syrup, nutmeg and pepper. Roasting gives the current version golden edges; the original boiled-and-coarsely-mashed version remains an option. For either route, squash that yields easily to a fork makes the mash smoother than squash that is only brown outside.

## Directions

1. **Roast until tender:** Preheat the oven to 400°F. Toss large butternut squash, olive oil, and salt on enough rimmed baking sheets to spread the cubes in a single layer. Roast about 30–40 minutes until a fork easily pierces the centers and the edges are golden. Browning alone is not proof that the centers are soft; larger loads need more trays or batches, rather than a deeper pile.
2. **Mash with all the butter:** Transfer the hot squash to a large bowl and add unsalted butter. Mash until smooth or leave it slightly coarse. For a processor finish, use only a model that permits the actual hot-food temperature and load; otherwise let the squash cool to its permitted temperature or use the hand masher.
3. **Add sweeteners and spices:** Stir in sugar, pure maple syrup, freshly grated nutmeg, and freshly ground black pepper. The measured sugar and syrup are separate full supplies; the original gradually-to-taste sugar option is below.
4. **Warm and finish:** If needed, warm over low heat in a saucepan, stirring to prevent sticking. Taste and add salt. Serve hot.

## Original Boiled and Mashed Version

Use the same whole squash, butter, sugar, maple syrup, nutmeg and pepper. Omit the roasting oil and roasting salt; peel, seed and quarter the squash instead of cubing it. Bring enough water to cover it in a large pot to a boil, add the squash and begin checking around 25 minutes, continuing until easily pierced with a knife or fork. Drain well, transfer to a heavy saucepan and coarsely mash. Mix in all the butter, sugar, maple syrup, nutmeg and pepper, then stir over low heat until heated through. The original has no separate measured salt. The original batch’s six portions are an inherited plan; doubling or tripling needs enough pots and draining space and does not guarantee the same elapsed time.

For the original gradually-to-taste sugar option, add the listed sugar gradually and stop at the desired sweetness; the listed amount is the full supply, not a new smaller measured dose. All the butter and maple syrup remain. Bottled ground nutmeg can replace the listed freshly grated nutmeg, with a less fresh aroma.
