## Chef's Note

Breanna Kinkead created this brown-butter crumble at Parish in Atlanta. Brown and strain the butter, then chill it until solid before cutting it into the flour and both crumble sugars. That sequence makes a crumbly topping rather than a paste of hot butter and flour. The fruit is thickened separately, and the finished crisp should bubble beneath a golden topping.

Prepare the crumble ahead if convenient; the butter must become solid before mixing. A roughly 70-minute reference covers preparation, butter browning, fruit cooking, baking and the 10-minute serving rest; butter chilling, extra oven heating and additional pan loads add time.

## Directions

1. **Brown the butter:** Melt Unsalted Butter in a small pan over medium heat. Stir and watch the milk solids until golden brown and nutty, rather than black or acrid; about 5 minutes is a reference. Remove from the heat and strain into a heat-safe container, discarding the browned solids. Chill over an ice bath or in the refrigerator until the butter is solid.
2. **Make the crumble:** Whisk All-Purpose Flour, Light Brown Sugar, Granulated Sugar, and Sea Salt. Cut in the solid browned Unsalted Butter with a pastry blender, knives or fingers until small crumbs form. Spread on a lined sheet and refrigerate while preparing the fruit. Do not taste the raw flour mixture.
3. **Prepare the fruit:** With Fresh Blueberries, Water, Fresh Lemon Juice, Granulated Sugar, Cornstarch, and Butter ready, preheat the oven to 350°F and grease a 9-inch square baking dish with the greasing butter for the original nine-portion batch. In a saucepan, combine the blueberries, listed water and lemon juice. Whisk the fruit granulated sugar with the cornstarch, then stir into the fruit. Cook over medium heat, stirring, until the juices have thickened, about 4 minutes. Transfer the whole fruit mixture to the dish.
4. **Bake:** Sprinkle as much chilled crumble as desired over the fruit; keep any unused crumble refrigerated for another bake. Bake in the fully heated oven for about 30 minutes, until the fruit is bubbling and the topping is golden. Do not aim for a dark mahogany topping. When scaling, use enough dish area to keep a similar fruit depth instead of piling the entire mixture into the original dish.
5. **Serve:** Rest about 10 minutes before serving warm with [Vanilla Bean Ice Cream](/mise/recipes/old-fashioned-vanilla-ice-cream), or the separate vanilla-bean option below. The fruit thickens further as it cools.

## Cooking Notes

Peaches, strawberries or cherries can replace blueberries, with baking time adjusted for their moisture and tenderness. For cherries, almond flour and sliced almonds can be used in the crumble; toasted chopped pecans suit peaches. Those additions have no specified amounts. Sprinkle only as much crumble as desired and use surplus on another bake. The unbaked crumble may be kept covered in the refrigerator for up to one week before baking.

### Separate vanilla-bean ice cream option

For one original batch, use 2 cups half-and-half, ½ small vanilla bean split lengthwise, 1⅓ cups granulated sugar, ½ tsp salt, 2 eggs, 2½ cups heavy cream and 1 tbsp vanilla extract. Use pasteurized dairy. These amounts make a separate ice cream batch and are not additions to the crisp; scale all of them together if making a different batch size. Finished volume is not specified.

Heat the half-and-half and vanilla bean gently to 175°F, then remove the bean. Stir in the sugar and salt. Whisk the eggs in a separate bowl, gradually whisk in some hot half-and-half, then return the tempered mixture to the pan. Cook gently while stirring until the egg base reaches 160°F and coats a spoon; do not boil. Stir in all the cream and vanilla extract off the heat. Cool promptly in an ice-water bath, then refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below until thoroughly cold. Churn according to the actual machine instructions and maximum fill, using separate cold loads if needed. Transfer to freezer containers and freeze for at least 4 hours, or longer until firm. This option needs its own chilling, churning and freezing time; it is not included in the crisp clock.
