## Chef's Note

Spicy tuna rolls were born in Los Angeles sushi bars in the 1980s as a way to use the trimmings left from cutting sashimi. Chop the tuna by hand so it keeps some texture, and keep the spicy mayo light so the fish still tastes like tuna.

## Directions

1. **Make the rice:** Make the [sushi rice](/mise/recipes/master-sushi-rice/) and let it cool until barely warm.
2. **Mix the filling:** Stir the mayonnaise, sriracha and sesame oil together in a bowl. Chop the tuna into small, rough pieces, not a paste, and fold it into the sauce with the scallions. Taste for heat and keep it cold.
3. **Set up to roll:** Wrap the bamboo mat in plastic wrap and set the bowl of vinegar water nearby. Divide the rice and the filling into four equal portions.
4. **Spread the rice:** Lay a nori sheet shiny side down on the mat with a long edge toward you. Wet your fingers and spread one portion of rice evenly over the nori, leaving a 1/2-inch strip bare along the far edge.
5. **Fill and roll:** Spoon one portion of filling in a low line across the rice about 1 1/2 inches from the near edge. Lift the mat and roll the nori over the filling, tucking it in, then keep rolling and lift the mat away as you go. Press the bare edge to seal and squeeze the roll gently through the mat. Repeat with the rest.
6. **Cut and serve:** Wet a sharp knife. Cut each roll in half, then cut each half into four pieces, wiping the blade between cuts. Serve right away.
