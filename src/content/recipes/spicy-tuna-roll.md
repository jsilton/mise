## Chef's Note

Spicy tuna is a Japanese-American sushi style with many versions. This one keeps the nori outside and uses a small amount of mayonnaise so the chopped fish remains distinct. A larger wrapper accommodates the filling without adding a ridge of rice or squeezing the roll into a dense tube.

## Before buying raw tuna

Ask the supplier about species, handling and intended raw service; “sushi-grade” wording alone is not enough. Yellowfin has a specific FDA parasite-freezing exemption, which does not eliminate other raw-fish hazards. Do not treat trimmings of unknown history, domestic freezing or vinegar as a way to make tuna suitable for eating raw. Thaw frozen fish in the refrigerator as directed, removing vacuum packaging before refrigerator thawing.

Raw seafood remains a risk. FDA advises children, pregnant people, older adults and people with weakened immune systems to avoid it. Choose a fully cooked preparation for those diners.

## Directions

1. **Prepare rice and equipment:** Make [Sushi Rice (Shari)](/mise/recipes/master-sushi-rice). Weigh 320 g for these rolls and refrigerate surplus rice promptly. Let this portion become barely warm during final preparation. Wrap a clean bamboo mat tightly in food-safe plastic film. Mix the water and vinegar in a small bowl; this is to reduce sticking, not to sanitize hands or equipment. Do not dip a raw-filling-coated hand or blade into this clean water. Wash hands and clean the blade before returning to it, use a separate damp cloth for wiping the cutting blade, and replace the water if contaminated.
2. **Prepare the filling:** While the rice cooks, slice scallions and mix mayonnaise, sriracha and sesame oil; taste this clean mixture before it touches fish. Near rolling time, remove tough visible connective tissue, weigh the listed usable amount of cold tuna, then mince into small irregular pieces rather than a paste. Fold in the mayonnaise mixture and scallions. Refrigerate promptly. Wash hands and clean the raw-fish work area.
3. **Portion:** Divide the rice into four 80 g portions with a clean utensil. Divide the tuna mixture into four equal portions, each containing about 58 g tuna plus its seasoning. Keep the filling portions refrigerated and take out one at a time. Trim the first full nori sheet to about 19 x 15 cm; keep its offcut dry and airtight for another use. Leave the other full sheets untrimmed until the first roll closes successfully.
4. **Spread and fill:** Put one rectangle shiny side down on the mat, with its 19 cm edge nearest you. Lightly dampen fingers and shake off excess water. Spread one rice portion evenly, leaving only a 1 cm strip bare along the far edge. Lay one filling portion in a low horizontal line across the rice, about 4 cm from the near edge, using a spoon rather than raw-fish-covered fingers.
5. **Roll:** Lift the near edge of the mat, bring nori and rice over the filling and tuck the leading edge just beyond it. Hold the filling in place gently. Lift the mat away as you continue rolling so it does not become trapped inside. Seal against the bare edge, barely moistening that edge if needed, then give a gentle even squeeze through the mat. Do not build a rice ridge. Check that the first roll closes, then trim the remaining sheets to match and make the remaining three.
6. **Cut and serve:** Set seam-side down while preparing the knife. Wipe and lightly dampen a sharp blade, cut each roll in half, then halve each section twice to make eight pieces per roll. Wipe and dampen between cuts as needed. Serve promptly as 32 pieces: roughly two light main portions or four appetizer portions. Keep unneeded filling cold while working; do not leave all four raw portions on the counter throughout preparation.
