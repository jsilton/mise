## Chef's Note

This Spanish-style dish reduces wine and tomato juices before steaming the mussels. The mussels then release their own liquid into the full chorizo-and-olive-oil base, so the finished broth need not resemble the concentrated base. Tarragon or parsley and oiled toast complete the dish.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Scrub and debeard the live mussels just before cooking. Discard shells that are cracked or broken, and open mussels that do not close when tapped. Keep them refrigerated until ready. Use a heavy pot with a tight lid and enough room to turn the mussels; divide the full measured base and mussels proportionally among additional pots or batches if needed.

2. **Render:** Heat the full measured pot oil with the dried Spanish chorizo over medium heat. Stir for about 4 minutes, until it begins to brown and crisp. Add garlic and fennel seeds; cook for 30–45 seconds until fragrant. Keep all the oil and rendered chorizo fat in the pot.

3. **Reduce:** Add all the tomatoes and wine, and season with pepper. Simmer over medium-high for about 6–8 minutes, until the liquid has reduced by about three-quarters and the tomatoes soften. This is the base before mussel juices enter; do not cook it dry or require the finished broth to be a syrup.

4. **Steam:** Add all the mussels and cover tightly. Cook about 6–8 minutes, stirring or shaking the pot occasionally, until shells open during cooking. Check them rather than waiting for every shell to open; discard any that remain closed. Keep finished mussels with their broth.

5. **Serve:** Ladle mussels and all the chorizo broth among deep bowls. Add all the measured tarragon or parsley. Toast or char the bread separately and drizzle it generously with its separate oil allowance; serve alongside. The original four-slice bread option uses one thick slice per listed portion; use the scaled portion count as a planning guide rather than a fixed four slices.
