## Chef's Note

Butterflied pork shoulder is pounded to an even thickness so its centers cook more evenly. Cook it and the skin-on peach halves on a solid cast-iron pan or griddle over the grill, where the full butter and pan oil can stay with the food. Pounding does not turn this into a slow-braised cut, and buttered peaches need not finish at the same time as the pork. Keep a clean resting board and check the pork before its 5-minute rest.

## Directions

1. **Pound and season:** Prepare Boneless pork butt or shoulder, Salt, and Black pepper on a raw-meat board. Pound the butterflied pork with a meat mallet to an even approximately ¾ inch thickness and season with the salt and pepper allowances. Keep it refrigerated if waiting while preparing the grill.
2. **Coat both sides:** Mix Garlic cloves, Fresh rosemary, and 3/4 of the Extra-virgin olive oil into a paste in a small bowl and spread over both sides of the prepared pork, dividing evenly. This is three-quarters of the total olive oil; the remaining quarter is reserved for the cooking surface.
3. **Heat solid cooking surface:** Heat a large cast-iron pan or griddle over the grill. Once hot, reduce a gas grill to medium or move away from an intense fire as needed. Brush the solid surface with 1/4 of the Extra-virgin olive oil, shared across the chosen pan area. For two pans, divide this allocation across both. Use shimmering oil as the cue and manage heat rather than insisting on prolonged smoking.
4. **Brown the pork:** Place the coated pork on the hot solid surface. Leave it long enough to form a golden crust, beginning checks before the original approximately 8–10 minute first-side interval if the heat is strong. Lower or move away from intense heat if garlic is blackening before the pork center cooks. Turn with raw-pork tongs, then keep those tools separate from serving tools.
5. **Cook buttered peaches:** While the pork cooks, arrange Fresh peaches and Unsalted butter in a separate solid pan brushed with part of the reserved pan oil. The separate pan keeps ready fruit apart from raw pork juices. Put peaches cut-side down and dot with all the diced butter. Begin checking after about 5 minutes for softened fruit and browned faces; remove ready peaches with clean utensils to a clean platter. If the pan cannot hold a single layer, cook fruit in additional loads, sharing the full butter across them. Keep the fruit pan and its utensils separate from the raw-pork paste and cooking tools.
6. **Finish and rest:** Continue cooking pork on its second side, beginning checks during the original 5–7 minute interval and allowing longer if needed. Check several thick centers for at least 145°F /63°C before removal. Transfer with clean utensils to a clean board and rest 5 minutes, meeting the minimum 3-minute rest. Do not infer doneness from color or assume it finishes with the peaches.
7. **Slice and serve:** Slice the rested pork across the grain and serve with the buttered peaches. If serving pooled aqueous juices from the pork-cooking pan, bring those juices to a rapid boil before serving and use clean utensils. This means the watery juices, not the oil; do not add liquid to create a boil. Juices from the clean resting board may be served with the rested pork. Never add liquid from the raw-pork board or paste bowl.
