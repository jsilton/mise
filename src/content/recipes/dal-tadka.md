## Chef's Note

Dal tadka brings together tender pulses and spices cooked in fat; the exact pulses and tempering vary across Indian homes and regions. This version specifies split hulled mung dal to make the timing useful. Cook the onion before the short garlic-and-spice stage, and add ground chile off heat so the aromatics stay fragrant rather than bitter.

## Directions

1. **Soften the dal:** Combine rinsed mung dal, water, turmeric, and salt in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Cook 20–30 minutes, stirring occasionally and adding hot water if needed, until a lentil crushes easily without a firm center. Skim excessive foam; there is no need to remove all starch.
2. **Build the base:** Stir in tomato and optional green chile. Simmer 5–8 minutes until tomato softens. Mash a little dal with the back of a spoon to thicken, and adjust with hot water until spoonable rather than stiff.
3. **Cook the onion:** While dal simmers, heat 2 tbsp ghee or oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add onion and cook 7–10 minutes, stirring, until soft and light gold. Move onion to one side and lower the heat if the pan is very hot.
4. **Temper the spices:** Add the remaining 1 tbsp fat to the clear space. Add mustard and cumin seeds; when they begin to sizzle and pop, add dried red chiles and dried-off curry leaves carefully, as they can splatter. Add garlic and cook 30–60 seconds, stirring until pale gold. Mix with the onion. Turn off heat and stir in asafoetida and ground chile for a few seconds.
5. **Combine and balance:** Pour the fragrant onion and spice mixture over the hot dal. Stir, then taste. Add lemon juice gradually and adjust salt and consistency. Scatter with cilantro and serve with rice or bread.
