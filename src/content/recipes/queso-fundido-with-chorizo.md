## Chef's Note

This Mexican-style melted-cheese dip uses Oaxaca for stretchy strands; mozzarella is the listed alternative, with the full pepper Jack alongside it. Roast and peel the poblanos before assembling, and cook the fresh chorizo before it goes back over the cheese. Serve promptly with warm tortillas, because the cheese firms as it cools.

## Directions

1. **Roast peppers:** Wash the poblanos. Roast directly over a suitable gas flame or under the broiler, turning until the skins blacken, starting checks around 8 minutes. Put in a heatproof bowl, cover with a heat-safe cover and rest about 5 minutes, until safe to handle. Peel, remove stems and seeds, and dice.
2. **Cook chorizo:** Preheat the oven to 400°F. Use an oven-safe skillet with room for the entire cheese and vegetable batch; the original batch uses a 10-inch cast-iron skillet, but its depth matters. If necessary use additional oven-safe vessels and divide everything proportionally. Cook all the fresh raw chorizo over medium-high heat, breaking it into small pieces. Begin checks around 5–6 minutes, then continue until cooked through: 160°F / 71°C for pork or beef chorizo, 165°F / 74°C if the product contains poultry. Transfer to a clean plate and leave the rendered fat in the pan. A cured ready-to-eat sausage is a different product from the fresh chorizo called for here.
3. **Soften vegetables:** Add all the onion and garlic to the rendered fat. Sauté until softened, beginning checks around 3 minutes. Add all the prepared poblanos and stir to combine.
4. **Assemble:** Take the skillet off heat. Scatter all the Oaxaca or mozzarella and all the pepper Jack over the vegetables, then return all the cooked chorizo on top. Leave headroom for bubbling.
5. **Bake:** Bake at 400°F, beginning checks around 8–10 minutes, until the cheese is melted and bubbling and the center reaches 165°F / 74°C. Check every vessel if the batch is divided.
6. **Finish and serve:** Top with all the cilantro and the optional pickled jalapeños. Warm the tortillas according to their package instructions and serve immediately, taking care with the hot skillet handle.

## Vegetarian Variation

Omit all the chorizo and its rendered-fat stage. For the original batch, use 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp smoked paprika and 1/2 tsp cumin instead; scale these amounts with the batch. Roast the full pepper supply as above. Heat the oil, sauté all the onion and garlic until softened, then stir in the paprika, cumin and all the diced poblanos. Take off heat and assemble with the full two-cheese supplies; bake and finish as in the main. Choose cheeses suitable for a vegetarian diet if needed.
