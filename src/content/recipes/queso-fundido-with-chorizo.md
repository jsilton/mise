## Chef's Note

Queso fundido is northern Mexico's skillet of molten cheese, scooped up with warm flour tortillas. Oaxaca cheese melts into long, stretchy strings, and crumbled chorizo and charred poblanos bring smoke and heat. Take it to the table in the hot skillet, because it sets as it cools.

## Directions

1. **Roast the poblanos:** Char the poblanos directly over a gas flame or under the broiler, turning, until blackened all over, about 8 minutes. Cover them in a bowl for 5 minutes, then peel, seed and dice.
2. **Cook the chorizo:** Heat the oven to 400°F. In a 10-inch cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat, cook the chorizo, breaking it into small pieces, until cooked through and crisp at the edges, 6 to 8 minutes. Move it to a plate and leave the fat in the pan.
3. **Soften the vegetables:** Turn the heat to medium. Cook the onion and garlic in the chorizo fat until soft, about 3 minutes. Stir in the poblanos and take the skillet off the heat.
4. **Layer the cheese:** Scatter the Oaxaca and pepper Jack evenly over the vegetables and top with the chorizo.
5. **Bake:** Bake until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling at the edges, 8 to 10 minutes.
6. **Serve:** Top with the cilantro and the pickled jalapeños, if using. Serve right away in the skillet with warm tortillas for scooping.
