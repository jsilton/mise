## Chef's Note

The egg batter expands as it heats and settles after leaving the oven. Keep all the butter and choose the pan route before mixing. The baking-dish and skillet versions have different shapes and batter depths; use the center, not the height of the rim, to judge readiness.

## Directions

1. **Choose and heat:** For the original-batch baking-dish version, use a 9×13-inch vessel suitable for 425°F and for the hot-pan/batter temperature change. Follow the vessel’s manufacturer instructions; do not assume a glass or ceramic dish permits preheating empty or adding cooler batter. Use a metal pan rated for this oven temperature if the glass or ceramic vessel’s instructions do not allow the required temperature change. Heat the oven to 425°F. For one pan, put all the listed butter in that pan; when using several pans, divide all the listed butter proportionally among them, keeping the same butter-to-batter ratio. Let the butter melt during preheating; remove each pan promptly once it has melted if the oven is not ready, so it does not burn.

2. **Mix:** Blend all the eggs, milk, flour, vanilla and salt until smooth, starting with 30–45 seconds, or stop once no flour lumps remain. This is unheated batter; follow the blender’s ordinary capacity limit and work in batches if needed, recombining them before filling the pan. Keep raw batter away from serving utensils.

3. **Fill:** Once the oven is ready and the butter has melted, carefully place each hot vessel on a heat-safe surface. Pour all the batter into the single appropriately sized pan, or divide it proportionally among the prepared pans, keeping similar batter depth and headroom. Keep oven gloves on for the pans. Do not pour a scaled larger batch into the same original pan.

4. **Bake:** Bake until puffed and golden with a set center, beginning checks around 20 minutes for the original baking-dish batch. Check 160°F in the egg-batter center; continue if it remains wet or below the endpoint, even if the rim is already tall and brown. Avoid unnecessary early opening, but open the oven when a needed center check is due.

5. **Serve:** Remove with oven gloves. Some settling is normal as the pancake cools; immediate service keeps the edges crispest. Divide into the listed portions and add the separate unmeasured lemon juice and powdered sugar, or warm maple syrup and powdered sugar, as wanted. Wash the lemon before cutting.
