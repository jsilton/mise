## Chef's Note

This herb-heavy chickpea falafel is served with bread, sesame sauce, and fresh vegetables in many Middle Eastern meals. Soaking supplies water to the chickpeas; frying supplies heat to cook their moist centers while the surface dries into a crust. Grind finely enough for the mixture to hold a small portion, then fry one test piece to check both crust and center.

## Directions

1. **Soak ahead:** Put the sorted and rinsed dried chickpeas in a large bowl with the soaking water. Cover and refrigerate for 12–24 hours, allowing plenty of room for expansion and adding water if needed to keep them covered. Drain and rinse. The beans should be plump and hydrated but still firm; do not boil them. Cut open a few: the inside should be evenly hydrated rather than showing a hard, dry core. If needed, continue the refrigerated soak within the stated twenty-four-hour window. Spread on a clean towel and blot away surface water.
2. **Prepare herbs and spices:** Wash and thoroughly dry the herbs, then roughly chop them. Put cumin and coriander seeds in a small dry skillet over medium-low heat. Stir for about 1–2 minutes, until fragrant but not smoking or blackened. Tip out promptly, cool, and grind finely. Peel and roughly chop onion and garlic.
3. **Grind in batches:** Put chickpeas, herbs, onion, and garlic in the processor. Divide the chickpeas, herbs, onion and garlic evenly between processor batches if needed, so the bottom does not become paste while the top stays coarse. Pulse, stopping to scrape, until finely and evenly chopped with no large chickpea fragments. A spoonful pressed between your palms should hold a rough mound. Combine all processor batches in a large bowl.
4. **Season and chill:** Mix in the ground toasted spices, baking powder, measured salt, and pepper evenly. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes. The rest makes the mixture easier to handle; it does not cook the chickpeas. Season by tasting a cooked test piece in step 6, rather than the raw mixture.
5. **Prepare one test portion:** After chilling, weigh one 25 g portion and press gently into a small ball, closing large cracks without compressing it into a hard lump. About 1 1/2 inches across is a guide; weight governs if the two differ. Keep the remaining mixture covered and refrigerated while the oil heats.
6. **Heat, test and adjust:** Set a wire rack over a rimmed sheet. Add oil to the deep pot to a two-inch depth with at least three inches of clear space above it. Heat to 350°F / 175°C under supervision, with the thermometer sensing in the oil rather than against the pot bottom. Lower the test with a dry metal slotted spoon. Fry about four to five minutes, turning as needed, until evenly brown. Drain on the rack, turn off the burner and leave the stable pot in place. Stay nearby while the test cools enough to split and taste: its center should be tender and finely crumbed, without hard fragments or dense wet paste. Add some of the reserved salt to the remaining mixture only if the test needs it. Correct any breakup or undercooked center and fry another test before shaping the rest; do not carry the hot oil pot to another work area.
7. **Shape the remaining batch:** With the burner off and the pot undisturbed, shape 25 g portions at an adjacent clear work surface away from the oil. Stay in the kitchen with the pot in view and keep splashing water away. Expect about forty to forty-five pieces in total, depending on hydration; close cracks gently. Refrigerate the shaped portions while bringing the oil back to 350°F / 175°C, allowing another five to ten minutes.
8. **Fry in small batches:** Cook five or six pieces at a time for about four to five minutes, turning for even color. Monitor the oil and reduce the next load if its temperature drops sharply or recovers slowly. Let it return to 350°F between rounds and skim loose crumbs. Check another center when batch size or browning changes. Keep waiting portions refrigerated, drain cooked pieces in one layer, and serve successive batches promptly. After the final round, turn off the burner and let the oil cool in place before handling or disposal.
9. **Serve with accompaniments:** Divide the actual batch among six diners, usually six or seven small falafel each, sharing extras; an eaten test piece reduces the number for the table. Offer about two tablespoons tahini sauce each, or three-quarters of a cup total, plus warm pita and vegetables. Six pocket breads require more warming rounds than the linked four-bread recipe. For six generous fattoush sides, make one and a half batches of that recipe, including its separately counted crisp pita; account for that bread alongside the pocket breads. Hummus can be a small optional dip. These accompaniments are additional recipes and time.

## Serving successive batches

This home-pot method produces hot falafel in successive rounds. Set out the cold sauce and prepared vegetables first; a second cook can warm pita as rounds arrive, or serve the bread at room temperature. Pita warmed before the first fry will not stay freshly warm through the whole batch. A simultaneous plated dinner needs a separate holding plan and the early falafel will lose some fresh-fried texture; all forty-plus pieces do not finish together.
