## Chef's Note

Finely grated apple and carrot give these banana muffins small pieces of fruit through the crumb. Fold the flour only until moistened and check the center, since fruit size changes both moisture and batter volume. For a smoother, soft crumb, use the cooked-fruit variation below.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the oven and tin:** Preheat the oven to 350°F and set out butter or neutral oil and water (if using) for preparation. Grease a standard 12-cup muffin tin with the pan fat; use the water only for the cooked-fruit variation below. Melt the batter butter and let it cool to lukewarm. For the cooked-fruit variation below, prepare the apple and carrot before making the wet mixture.
2. **Prepare the grated fruit:** Use the apple and large carrot, grating with the fine holes of a box grater. Squeeze off excess free juice; keep the shreds damp rather than trying to dry them completely.
3. **Combine the wet ingredients:** Whisk the large banana, large eggs, unsalted butter, and vanilla extract in a large bowl until evenly combined, then stir in the prepared apple and carrot.
4. **Mix the dry ingredients:** Whisk the all-purpose flour, baking powder, ground cinnamon, and salt in a separate bowl so the baking powder and seasonings are distributed.
5. **Fold and portion:** Gently fold the dry mixture into the wet just until no dry flour pockets remain. The original batch makes about 12 standard muffins; for different quantities, use more or fewer standard cups at approximately the same portion size, rather than spreading every batch across twelve cups. Fruit size affects the batter volume; use extra prepared cups if necessary rather than filling to the rim.
6. **Bake and cool:** Bake for about 20–25 minutes, starting the center check at 20 minutes. The tops should be set and a tester through the central crumb should have no wet batter; continue baking if needed. Let the muffins stand briefly until firm enough to lift out, then transfer carefully to a rack. Cool before serving and cool completely before packing.

## Cooking Notes

These muffins are intended for children; keep toppings simple and skip delicate herb garnishes.

For mini muffins, use prepared mini cups and begin checking around 15 minutes; the original batch makes about 30 minis, depending on cup size and fruit volume. Keep the same no-wet-batter center cue and cool before serving. Replace the butter with the same listed quantity of mild vegetable oil for the dairy-free version.

## Cooked Apple and Carrot Variation

For a smoother fruit mixture, peel, core and dice the apple instead of grating it. Peel and grate the carrot. Put both in a small covered saucepan with a little water, just enough to keep them from catching while they soften. Simmer for about 5–6 minutes, until the apple and carrot are soft. Let the softened fruit cool to room temperature before puréeing it with a blender used according to its instructions. Add the purée to the banana, eggs, melted butter and vanilla. Beat this wet mixture until evenly combined and lightly frothy. Add the flour, baking powder, cinnamon and salt, then beat briefly for about 30–60 seconds to make an evenly mixed batter. Portion into the prepared cups. The original batch makes about 12 standard or 30 mini muffins; different quantities need more or fewer cups at the same portion size, with extra oven batches if needed. Start checking standard muffins around 25–30 minutes, or mini muffins around 15 minutes, and continue until the centers have no wet batter. Cool as directed above. Allow extra preparation and cooling time for the cooked fruit; do not apply the raw-fruit total time to this route.
