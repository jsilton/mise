## Chef's Note

This is a nonfood craft dough. Do not eat or taste it, and do not give dough made with raw flour to children for play. A hot-water or short stovetop texture change is not a validated treatment that makes this formula edible; adults handle the hot equipment and dough. Keep this activity and its tools separate from food preparation.

## Directions

1. **Dry supplies:** In a large bowl, mix all the flour, fine salt and cream of tartar. Avoid spreading flour dust onto ready-to-eat food or food utensils.
2. **Hot supplies:** An adult boils the listed water and combines it with all the vegetable oil in a heatproof jug. For one color throughout, add the selected coloring according to its label now.
3. **Mix:** An adult slowly pours the hot liquid into the dry supplies and stirs with a long-handled spoon until the mixture comes together. Keep hands away from the hot liquid and bowl.
4. **Cool and knead:** Let the entire dough cool enough for comfortable handling, including its center, before kneading. Begin checking after 2–3 minutes of kneading for a smoother, cohesive texture; timing alone does not establish coolness or a flour safety endpoint.
5. **Color portions:** For several colors, divide the cooled uncolored dough into the portions wanted and knead the separate selected colors into them. Do not add a second full coloring allowance to an already colored batch.
6. **Store and clean:** Store the cooled craft dough in a closed, clearly labeled nonfood container, separate from food. Wash hands, tools and work surfaces after handling it. Discard it if it develops mold, off odors or unsuitable texture.

## Cooking Notes

### Less-water variation

A separate less-water version keeps the full original 2½ cups flour, 1¼ cups salt, 1½ tbsp cream of tartar and 5 tbsp oil, but begins with 1½ cups boiling water and adds more water only as needed to bring the dough together. These are original-batch supplies; scale them proportionally for a different batch. Use the same adult hot-liquid handling and complete cooling before kneading.

If sticky, knead the cooled dough longer before adding a little extra flour as needed. If dry, add small amounts of water as needed. A stovetop option cooks the mixture while stirring until it comes together, followed by cooling before handling. These adjustments change texture; none certifies decontamination or edibility.
