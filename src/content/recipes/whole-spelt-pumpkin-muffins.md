## Chef's Note

Lisa Leake’s spelt muffins keep the full pumpkin, honey and melted butter in a simple batter. Whisk the leaveners through the flour before combining wet and dry, and stop folding once no dry pockets remain. A golden top and clean center tester are more useful than an assumed rise or a rigid mixing clock.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 350°F and line a muffin tin. The original batch plans twelve regular muffins; use enough similar wells for the scaled batter and keep comparable fill rather than overflowing the original tin.
2. **Mix dry ingredients:** Whisk whole spelt flour, pumpkin pie spice, baking soda, baking powder, and sea salt together until the two leaveners and salt are evenly distributed.
3. **Mix the wet ingredients:** In a separate bowl, whisk plain pumpkin puree, unsalted butter, honey, and large eggs until combined. Cool melted butter until warm rather than hot before it touches the eggs.
4. **Combine:** Make a well in the dry mixture and fold in the pumpkin mixture until no dry flour pockets remain. Do not keep beating to make a perfectly smooth batter.
5. **Fill and bake:** Divide among the lined cups, about ¾ full, using another well or load if needed. Wait until the oven reaches 350°F, then bake for 18–20 minutes per load, until golden and a center toothpick comes out clean.
6. **Cool:** Let the muffins firm enough to lift from the pan, then finish cooling on a rack before covering. They may be served warm; cooling completely for storage takes additional time.

## Cooking Notes

### Flour and make-ahead choices

Whole-wheat flour may replace the whole spelt flour at its full listed volume. Keep the full pumpkin, honey, butter, eggs, spices and both leaveners; the crumb and bake time can differ with flour choice. Spelt is wheat and is not a gluten-free substitute.

Cool before covering for room-temperature storage, or freeze the cooled muffins airtight for later. Thaw the portion you need before serving. The plain muffins have no cream-cheese filling or glaze.
