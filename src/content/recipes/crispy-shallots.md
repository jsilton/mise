## Chef's Note

Crispy shallots, hành phi in Vietnamese, add crunch, sweetness and aroma to soups, salads and stir-fries all over Southeast Asia. Starting the shallots in cold oil lets their water cook off slowly, so they crisp all the way through. Pull them at pale gold, since they keep browning as they cool.

## Directions

1. **Start in cold oil:** Separate the rings. Put large shallots and canola or vegetable oil in a cold small saucepan, so the oil just covers the shallots.
2. **Fry slowly:** Set the pan over medium heat. When the oil bubbles steadily around the rings, lower the heat to medium-low. Cook, stirring often to break up clumps, until the bubbling slows and the rings turn pale golden, 15 to 20 minutes. Stir constantly near the end, because they color fast.
3. **Strain at pale gold:** Pour everything through a fine-mesh strainer set over a heatproof bowl the moment the rings are pale golden. They keep darkening after they leave the oil.
4. **Salt and cool:** Spread the shallots on a paper towel-lined plate and sprinkle with kosher salt while they still glisten. Let them cool completely, and they will turn shatteringly crisp.
