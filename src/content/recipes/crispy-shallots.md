## Chef's Note

Crispy shallots add sweetness and crunch to Vietnamese, Thai and Chinese soups, salads and stir-fries. A cold-oil start and evenly thin rings help this version cook steadily. Drain at pale gold: carryover heat deepens their color before the rings cool and crisp.

## Directions

1. **Prepare rings and draining station:** Prepare large shallots. Separate the rings and dry any surface water before frying. Set a fine-mesh strainer over a stable heatproof bowl and line a plate or tray with several layers of paper towels before starting the heat. Use a mandoline guard if slicing with a mandoline.
2. **Start in cold oil:** Combine the sliced shallots with neutral cooking oil in a cold, heavy saucepan. Choose a pan that lets this oil surround the rings, with room for bubbling and stirring. If the rings cannot stay mostly submerged, divide the full shallot and oil supplies proportionally among separate cold-start batches in a suitably smaller pan; do not crowd the whole batch into shallow oil.
3. **Fry gently:** Start over medium heat. Once oil bubbles around the rings, lower to medium-low and cook about 15–20 minutes, stirring frequently and separating clumps. Stay at the pan. As bubbling slows, watch the color closely and stir continually so small rings do not burn.
4. **Drain at pale gold:** When the rings are pale golden, turn off the heat and promptly strain the oil into the prepared heatproof bowl. They continue darkening briefly after removal; do not wait for dark brown in the oil.
5. **Blot and season:** Spread the rings in a single layer on the prepared towels and sprinkle with the listed pinch of kosher salt while oil still clings to them. Gently blot and move them onto a dry towel layer as needed, then let the separate shallots and oil cool. The shallots should become dry and crisp, not remain soft or greasy.
6. **Keep solids and oil separate:** Once completely cool and thoroughly drained, keep the crispy shallots in an airtight container; the original 3-day room-temperature plan is for fully dried solids. Refrigerate the separate shallot-flavored oil promptly at 40°F / 4°C or below and use within 4 days, or freeze in small portions. Do not treat strained oil as shelf-stable.

## Cooking Notes

Use the shallots as a topping and save the separate oil for cooking. A pinch is an unstandardized hand measure, not a salt-weight equivalence. The listed oil is a frying supply; the finished shallots do not absorb the entire cup.
