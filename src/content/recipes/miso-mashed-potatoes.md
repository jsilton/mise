## Chef's Note

White miso gives a familiar butter-and-milk mash a savory fermented seasoning. This Japanese-inspired variation uses miso as both flavor and seasoning, so taste before adding finishing salt. Whisk the miso into warm dairy first, then fold gently into fully tender potatoes; warmth helps keep dinner hot, while limited mixing protects the texture.

## Directions

1. **Cut and cover:** Peel and trim the potatoes to the listed net weight, then cut into even one-inch pieces. Rinse briefly, put in a 5-quart pot and cover with about two liters cold water, adding enough to sit an inch above the potatoes. Add the eight grams of cooking-water salt.
2. **Simmer to tenderness:** Bring to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer. Cook about 18–22 minutes after the water begins simmering, until several tested pieces yield easily to a knife and a cooled bite is completely soft in the center. Keep cooking if needed; larger pieces take longer.
3. **Warm the miso dairy during cooking:** In a small saucepan over low heat, whisk the initial half-cup milk with the miso and garlic powder until the miso has no large clumps. Add the butter and warm gently until melted. Do not boil or reduce the mixture. Wash and slice the optional scallions while the potatoes finish.
4. **Drain and briefly dry:** Drain well in a colander. Return potatoes to the pot over low heat for one to two minutes, shaking gently, until surface water evaporates and the pieces look matte. Remove from heat before they stick or brown.
5. **Mash and fold:** Pass the hot potatoes through a ricer or mash gently by hand. Fold in the warm miso dairy in two additions with a spatula, stopping as soon as it is incorporated. If stiff, warm and add the reserved milk one tablespoon at a time until the mash forms a soft mound. Do not beat it smooth in a blender.
6. **Taste and serve:** Taste before adding the optional one-eighth teaspoon of salt; the miso may supply all the finishing seasoning needed. Scatter with the optional scallions and divide into four generous side portions, or six smaller portions on a plate with another starch. Pair with a simply seasoned roast and a crisp or lemony vegetable.
