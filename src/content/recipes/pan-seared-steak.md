## Chef's Note

Butter basting is the French bistro move called arroser, where foaming butter, garlic and thyme are spooned over a steak for its last minutes in the pan. Salting 45 minutes ahead gives the salt time to soak in and leaves the surface dry, so the crust forms fast. Flipping every minute cooks the steak evenly from edge to edge.

## Directions

1. **Salt the steaks:** Pat the steaks dry with paper towels. Season them all over, edges included, with kosher salt and black pepper. Leave them on a wire rack at room temperature for 45 minutes.
2. **Heat the pan:** Pat the steaks dry again. Heat neutral oil in a 12-inch cast-iron skillet over high heat until the oil just starts to smoke, about 3 minutes.
3. **Sear the steaks:** Lay boneless ribeye or New York strip steaks in the pan, away from you. Cook, flipping every minute, until both sides have a deep brown crust, about 6 minutes.
4. **Baste with butter:** Lower the heat to medium. Add unsalted butter, garlic cloves, and fresh thyme sprigs. When the butter foams, tilt the pan toward you and spoon the butter over the steaks again and again, flipping them once, until the thickest part reads 125°F for medium-rare, 4 to 6 minutes.
5. **Rest the steaks:** Move the steaks to a cutting board and pour the pan butter over them. Rest for 8 minutes. The temperature climbs to about 130°F as they sit.
6. **Slice and serve:** Slice the steaks across the grain, 1/2 inch thick. Spoon the juices from the board over the slices and finish with flaky sea salt.
