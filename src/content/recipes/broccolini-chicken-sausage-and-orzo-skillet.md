## Chef's Note

Use fully cooked chicken sausage, then keep it and the sautéed broccolini aside while the orzo absorbs the broth. Return both only when the pasta is tender, and stir in the full Parmesan off heat. The peas option replaces the broccolini and goes into the simmer near the end.

## Directions

1. **Brown the cooked sausage:** Heat all the olive oil over medium-high heat in a roomy skillet with a lid. Add the fully cooked sausage rounds and brown for about 3–4 minutes, then transfer to a clean plate. Raw chicken sausage needs its own complete cooking method and is not a direct substitute.

2. **Cook the vegetable:** Wash and dry broccolini, trim the woody ends and coarsely chop it. Sauté it in the same oil-coated pan with some of the listed salt and pepper for about 5 minutes, until the stems are tender-crisp; continue if still tough. Remove to the sausage plate. If choosing frozen peas instead, omit all broccolini and skip this sauté; keep the peas for the final simmer.

3. **Toast briefly:** Add all the dry orzo and minced garlic to the pan. Stir for 30–45 seconds until fragrant; stop before the garlic darkens.

4. **Simmer the pasta:** Pour in all the chicken broth. Bring to a boil, cover and reduce to a gentle simmer. Begin checking around 10 minutes, guided by the orzo package, until the center is tender with a little bite. If tender pasta has pooled broth, uncover and simmer briefly. If the pan is dry while the center is firm, add a little of the hot-water allowance and continue. When using the frozen-peas option, add the full chosen amount near the end and allow enough time to heat it through.

5. **Return and enrich:** Return all the reserved fully cooked sausage and broccolini, if used. Stir over low heat until hot throughout, then remove from heat and incorporate all the measured Parmesan.

6. **Finish:** Use a little hot water only if needed to loosen the pasta. Taste and adjust with the listed salt and black pepper. Serve promptly, with the separate optional extra Parmesan or chile flakes.
