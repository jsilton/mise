## Chef's Note

Grace Parisi adapted the sweet soy glaze of unagi kabayaki to rainbow trout when eel was difficult to find. Broil and glaze both sides while watching the surface; the thin julienned vegetables bring a crisp-tender contrast. Keep some glaze clean for the rice and table before a fish brush touches it.

## Directions

Have steamed rice ready. The 45-minute plan uses instant dashi or already-prepared homemade stock; making the linked Dashi and rice from scratch adds their own preparation time. Use a broiler-rated sheet with the fish in one layer and the oven's permitted rack position; position the rack 6–8 inches from the heat only if the oven instructions permit. Cook in more batches if needed, allowing longer elapsed time.

1. **Make the glaze:** Put all the soy sauce, mirin, sugar and vinegar in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then simmer over moderate heat until thick enough to coat a spoon, starting checks around 10 minutes. For the original four-fillet batch, the source target is about 1½ cups reduced glaze; scale that volume in proportion to the full listed glaze ingredients for a different batch, keeping the same spoon-coating cue. Pan size and reduction time do not multiply. Keep this supply clean. Pour a working portion into a separate dish for brushing fish, replenishing it with a clean spoon as needed. Never dip the fish brush into the clean supply; keep clean glaze for the rice and table.

2. **Prepare the vegetable broth:** In a separate medium saucepan with room for the vegetables, combine all the instant dashi powder with its measured water and bring to a boil. Add the carrots and leeks and simmer just until crisp-tender, starting checks around 1 minute. Remove the pan from the heat and let the vegetables stand in the broth while the fish broils. The water amount is the starting liquid; do not reduce the broth to an assumed finished yield.

3. **Set up the fish:** Preheat the broiler. Arrange the fillets skin-side up on the sturdy sheet, leaving them in one layer. Check for remaining bones and keep fish-handling utensils separate from serving utensils.

4. **Remove the skin and glaze:** Broil until the skin begins to sizzle, starting checks around 2 minutes. Carefully peel off and discard the skin. The fish is still partly cooked: brush the exposed surface with glaze from the working dish, keeping the brush out of the clean serving supply.

5. **Broil both sides:** Broil the glazed surface for about 3 minutes, brushing once or twice more as it lightly caramelizes. Carefully turn each fillet over so the other side faces the broiler. Brush that side with more working glaze and broil about 3 minutes longer, brushing once or twice and watching for scorching. Check the thickest center of each fillet for 145°F/63°C with a suitable thermometer; continue as needed. The broiler clocks are first checks. Discard any unused raw-contact brushing glaze.

6. **Assemble:** Mound the prepared sushi rice in shallow bowls and drizzle lightly with clean glaze. Lift the crisp-tender carrots and leeks from their saucepan with a slotted spoon, then add the trout and nori.

7. **Serve:** Spoon the vegetable dashi into the bowls and serve immediately, with all remaining clean glaze alongside. If the broth needs reheating while the fish cooks, bring it back to a boil before serving.

## Homemade-Stock Variation

For a homemade-stock variation, use strained [Dashi](/mise/recipes/dashi-japanese-sea-stock) in the same volume as the water listed for the chosen batch (1½ cups for the original four fillets), in place of BOTH the instant powder and its water. Then follow step 2 with that prepared stock. This uses the same starting liquid volume for the vegetables; the stock’s flavor and seasoning differ from instant dashi. Prepare the linked stock first and measure the portion actually needed here. Its original batch starts with six cups of water; do not assume that equals six cups of finished stock or add unlisted top-up water.
