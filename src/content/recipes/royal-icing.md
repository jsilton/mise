## Chef's Note

Royal icing forms a firm coating for decorated cookies. Make a thick base first, then thin only the portion needed for flooding so the outlines can contain it. Use pasteurized egg whites for this uncooked icing and keep the tools free of grease.

## Directions

1. **Froth:** Use large pasteurized egg whites in a clean, grease-free mixing bowl. With a stand mixer, beat on medium-low until frothy but not peaking, about 1 minute as a guide. Hand whisking is possible but takes more effort and time. Check actual mixer bowl capacity for the batch.
2. **Add sugar:** With the mixer on low, gradually add all confectioners’ sugar and pure vanilla extract. Scrape as needed and let the sugar incorporate without dumping it in at once.
3. **Beat to peaks:** Once fully incorporated, increase to medium-high and beat until stiff, shiny peaks form, beginning checks after 2–3 minutes. Avoid continuing to beat after the cue merely to complete a fixed time.
4. **Color:** Divide the icing into the colors needed and stir in gel or liquid food coloring (if using). Gel is concentrated; liquid coloring also thins the icing, so add it gradually. Keep every unused portion covered while working.
5. **Outline and flood:** Use thick icing for outlines, then move the amount needed for flooding into a separate bowl. Add water a few drops or a small spoonful at a time; the original batch uses approximately ½ tsp additions as a guide, not a required full water dose. Stir with a spatula and check whether a ribbon levels back into the bowl in about 15 seconds. Adjust the cue for the design instead of requiring exactly 15 seconds at every scale.
6. **Cover and decorate:** Keep bowls or piping bags tightly covered when not immediately in use; the surface dries quickly. Apply outlines to completely cool cookies before filling their centers with the thinner portion. Let the decorated icing dry thoroughly before stacking; allow about 6–8 hours at room temperature, with actual humidity and design affecting the time.

## Cooking Notes

Separate pasteurized shell eggs while cold, then allow only the measured whites to lose their chill briefly before mixing. Keep the remaining product refrigerated and follow its label. You can whisk by hand, with more time and effort. Add sugar gradually, and use pure vanilla for its flavor. A drop or two of water can loosen a portion whose surface has begun to dry; already hardened flakes may spoil a smooth piping line. Too much water can create a runny icing that escapes its outline. Liquid color can thin the mixture more than gel.

The original batch lists 36 planning portions; cookie size, outline/flood depth and design change how many it decorates. Follow the listed supplies when scaling; decorating and drying do not multiply arithmetically.
