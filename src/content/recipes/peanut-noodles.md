## Chef's Note

Cold sesame peanut noodles are a Chinese-American takeout classic from New York, and they are just as good warm or straight from the fridge the next day. The trick is balance. Soy sauce and rice vinegar keep the peanut butter bright instead of heavy, and the noodles go in unrinsed so the sauce clings.

## Directions

1. **Make the sauce:** Whisk the peanut butter, soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil, honey, garlic, ginger and red pepper flakes in a bowl. Whisk in the warm water a little at a time until the sauce is smooth and pourable, like thick cream.
2. **Cook the noodles:** Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the noodles until just tender, following the package time, then drain well. Don't rinse them, so the starch helps the sauce cling.
3. **Toss:** Toss the warm noodles with the sauce in a large bowl until every strand is coated. Add a splash of warm water if it looks thick. Taste and add a little more vinegar or soy sauce if it needs it.
4. **Finish and serve:** Top with the green onions, cilantro, sesame seeds, and the peanuts and vegetables if using. Serve warm with lime wedges, or chill until cold, about 1 hour, and loosen with a splash of warm water to serve cold.
