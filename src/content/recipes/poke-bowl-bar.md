## Chef's Note

Poke is seasoned cut seafood from Hawaii; this multi-topping bar is a contemporary bowl format. Choosing two seafoods or rice types changes the variety, not the total portion needed. Cucumber salad, soy and furikake each add seasoning, so offer them separately and start lightly.

## Raw seafood sourcing

Tell the supplier the seafood will be served raw and ask for its species, cold-chain handling and applicable parasite-control information. “Sushi-grade” and “sashimi-grade” labels alone do not establish suitability. For wild salmon, require documented supplier parasite-destruction freezing; fresh wild-caught fish is not automatically preferable for raw service. For farmed salmon, obtain supplier documentation of either parasite-destruction freezing or qualifying raising and feeding conditions; a farmed label alone is insufficient. Yellowfin tuna and scallop products consisting only of the shucked adductor muscle have specific parasite-freezing exemptions, not a general guarantee of raw safety. Do not use a household freezer as an assumed substitute for a documented commercial process.

Freezing does not remove every seafood hazard, and this dressing is not a safety treatment. FDA advises children, pregnant people, older adults and people with weakened immune systems to avoid raw seafood; choose a fully cooked meal for those diners. Follow supplier refrigerator-thawing instructions and remove vacuum packaging before refrigerator thawing.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the rice and cucumber salad:** Make [Sushi Rice (Shari)](/mise/recipes/master-sushi-rice) and measure four cooked cups for the bar, refrigerating any extra promptly. For two rice types, use two cups shari and two cups safely prepared [Plain Short-Grain White Rice](/mise/recipes/steamed-white-rice). During the rice process, make a half batch of [Oi Muchim](/mise/recipes/korean-pickled-cucumbers), starting with 300 g cucumber, then lift the finished half batch from excess dressing for the four bowls.
2. **Prepare edamame and vegetables during rice soaking/cooking:** Cook shelled edamame early in the rice process according to its package, drain and chill promptly in a shallow container. Wash and cut the fresh cucumber, carrot, cabbage, scallions and optional jalapeno and herbs with clean equipment. Slice avocado last and coat with 1 tsp lime juice, adding the measured optional salt only after tasting. Refrigerate vegetables until needed and keep nori dry.
3. **Mix the dressing:** Whisk soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, rice vinegar, ginger and the remaining 1 tsp lime juice. Taste before any seafood touches it. Leave out sriracha for a mild dressing or add it now; for one mild and one spicy seafood bowl, divide the clean dressing in proportion to the seafood weights and add sriracha only to the spicy portion.
4. **Cut and dress seafood while rice cools:** During the final rice cooling period, with separate clean equipment, cut cold tuna or salmon into 1/2-inch cubes, or slice scallop muscles about 1/4 inch thick. Divide the dressing among the chosen seafoods in proportion to their weights and refrigerate for about 5–10 minutes. Keep each type in its own bowl with its own spoon. This short wait seasons the seafood; it does not cook it or establish safety.
5. **Set out and assemble:** Nest small seafood bowls in ice, with meltwater unable to enter them. Check the seafood with a clean food thermometer to confirm 40°F / 4°C or below; clean the probe between seafoods and return portions to the refrigerator if the ice setup cannot keep them cold. Set out the rice barely warm and the prepared vegetables cold, with separate spoons and garnishes alongside. For four bowls, allow about one cup total rice and one quarter of the total seafood per person; a diner choosing two seafoods splits that portion. Lift seafood and cucumber salad from pooled dressing. Add avocado, edamame and fresh vegetables, then sesame, scallion and dry nori. Offer the measured optional garnishes and sauces sparingly. Assemble only when ready to eat and refrigerate unused rice promptly.

## Scaling the bar

Scale the total fish and rice allocation across the choices, then divide dressing in proportion to the fish. Larger rice batches need a suitable pot or separate batches, and more fish needs additional refrigerated space and ice capacity. Allow extra preparation time; keep reserve seafood refrigerated.
