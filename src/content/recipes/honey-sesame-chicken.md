## Chef's Note

A lighter take on the Chinese-American takeout favorite, with no deep frying. A thin coat of soy and cornstarch browns in the pan and helps the honey glaze cling. Sear the chicken hard first, then turn the heat down before the honey goes in so it never scorches.

## Directions

1. **Coat the chicken:** Toss the chicken with the soy sauce for the chicken and the cornstarch until evenly coated.
2. **Mix the sauce:** Stir the honey, the soy sauce for the sauce, rice vinegar and water together in a small bowl.
3. **Sear the first batch:** Heat a third of the neutral oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the chicken in a single layer and leave it until it browns and releases, 2 to 3 minutes. Turn and cook until browned all over and 165°F in the thickest pieces, 6 to 8 minutes total. Move it to a bowl.
4. **Sear the second batch:** Add another third of the oil and cook the rest of the chicken the same way. Move it to the bowl.
5. **Build the glaze:** Lower the heat to medium-low and add the last of the oil. Stir in the garlic and ginger until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Pour in the sauce and scrape up the browned bits as it comes to a simmer.
6. **Glaze the chicken:** Return the chicken and its juices to the pan. Fold until hot and glossy and the glaze coats each piece, 1 to 2 minutes. Add a splash of water if it turns sticky.
7. **Finish:** Turn off the heat and fold in the sesame oil, sesame seeds and scallions. Serve with rice.
