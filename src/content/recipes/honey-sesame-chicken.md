## Chef's Note

This Chinese-American home version uses a thin starch coating and a small amount of oil. It gives browned chicken and a clinging glaze, with a softer texture after saucing. Keep honey out of the searing stage: first cook the chicken, then lower the heat for garlic and sauce. Sesame oil is a finishing flavor, distinct from the neutral cooking oil.

## Directions

1. **Season chicken:** Toss chicken with its 1 tbsp light soy sauce and cornstarch until lightly coated. Refrigerate while preparing sauce and garnishes. Do not wipe off the coating afterward.
2. **Mix sauce:** Stir honey, the sauce's 2 tbsp soy, rice vinegar, and measured water until combined. Prepare garlic, ginger, sesame seeds and sliced scallions.
3. **Sear in two batches:** Heat 2 tsp neutral oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the chicken without overlap. Leave 2–3 minutes until it releases more easily, turn, and continue cooking until several thick pieces reach 165°F / 74°C, usually 6–8 minutes total. Transfer to a clean bowl. Repeat with another 2 tsp oil and the rest of the chicken.
4. **Build the glaze:** Reduce heat to medium-low. Add the final 2 tsp neutral oil and stir garlic and ginger for about 30 seconds until fragrant. Pour in the mixed sauce, scraping up brown residue but leaving any blackened bits out. Bring to a gentle simmer. Return chicken and its juices and fold for about 1–2 minutes until hot and lightly coated. If the glaze becomes sticky or pasty, add a small splash of water.
5. **Finish and serve:** Turn off heat and fold in toasted sesame oil, sesame seeds and scallions. Taste before serving and divide into four main portions. Serve with separately prepared [plain rice](/mise/recipes/steamed-white-rice) and a vegetable side.
