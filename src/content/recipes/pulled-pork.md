## Chef's Note

A bone-in Boston butt rubbed with brown sugar, smoked paprika and hickory smoked salt, then cooked low all day over onion, garlic and cider vinegar. The pork makes its own sauce as it cooks. Skim the fat from those juices before you toss them back in, so the meat tastes rich and tangy, not greasy.

## Directions

1. **Rub the pork:** In a small bowl, mix the brown sugar, hickory smoked salt, smoked paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, cumin and pepper until no lumps of sugar remain. Rub it into every surface of the pork. For deeper flavor, do this the night before and refrigerate the pork uncovered.
2. **Build the pot base:** Scatter the onion and garlic over the bottom of a 6-quart slow cooker. Pour in the vinegar and Worcestershire sauce.
3. **Load the shoulder:** Set the pork on the onions, fat cap up. Don't add any more liquid. The pork releases plenty of juice as it cooks.
4. **Slow cook:** Cover and cook on LOW until a fork twists the meat apart easily and the bone slides out clean, 10 to 11 hours.
5. **Pull the pork:** Lift the pork onto a cutting board. Pull out the bone and discard any large pieces of fat or gristle. Pull or chop the meat into rough pieces.
6. **Skim the juices:** Pour the cooking liquid into a heatproof jug and let it settle for 5 minutes. Spoon the fat off the top.
7. **Moisten and serve:** Return the pork to the cooker. Add the skimmed juices a little at a time, tossing as you go, until the meat is moist but not swimming. Taste, add salt if it needs it, and serve hot.
