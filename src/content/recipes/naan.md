## Chef's Note

Meera Sodha learned this naan from her aunt Harsha. Milk, yogurt and a little oil give the breads a tender crumb. Keep the dough soft, let it rise fully, and adjust the pan after the first bread: brown bubbles should develop while the center cooks through. A towel keeps the early breads supple as the rest finish.

## Directions

1. **Hydrate the yeast:** Warm the measured cup of milk to 110–115°F / 43–46°C. Stir in the sugar and active dry yeast and leave for 5–10 minutes, until the granules have hydrated and activity appears at the surface. Do not use hot milk. Meanwhile, whisk the 500 g flour, fine salt and baking powder together in a large bowl.
2. **Mix and knead:** Add the yogurt, 2 tbsp oil and all the yeast-milk mixture to the flour. Mix until no loose pockets of flour remain, then knead on the work surface for about 5–8 minutes until smoother and elastic. Use the dusting flour sparingly. If the dough is stiff or cracks, knead in the correction milk a teaspoon at a time, only as needed for a soft, slightly tacky dough.
3. **Let rise:** Coat the dough with the separate teaspoon of oil and put it in a bowl with room to double. Cover and leave in a comfortably warm place for 60–90 minutes, until roughly doubled. Allow longer in a cool room; do not speed it up in a hot oven.
4. **Divide and relax:** Gently deflate and divide into 12 equal pieces, about 70 g each before any correction milk or dusting flour. Round into balls, cover and rest for 10 minutes. During this rest, melt the optional butter with the minced garlic in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir for about 1 minute after melting, until fragrant but not browned, then take off heat. Set out the towel-lined platter. During the final 5 minutes of the dough rest, begin heating the dry skillet over medium-high.
5. **Heat the pan and roll:** After the dry 12-inch cast-iron skillet has heated for about 5 minutes, roll two balls on a lightly dusted surface into ovals about 5 by 8 inches and roughly 1/8 inch / 3 mm thick. Keep the remaining balls covered. If a piece springs back, cover it for another 5–10 minutes and work on another. Do not stack uncooked ovals directly together.
6. **Cook one bread:** Brush loose flour off the oval and lay it flat in the hot pan. Cook about 30–45 seconds, until bubbles form and the underside has brown patches. Flip and cook about 30–45 seconds more. Flip again for about 10–20 seconds, pressing an undercooked edge lightly against the pan if needed. Check the first bread by tearing through a thick section near its center: there should be no sticky, raw dough inside. Adjust heat and time for the next round; a few dark freckles are enough, and blackening the whole surface is not the aim. Do not taste raw dough.
7. **Wrap and repeat:** Brush the hot bread with a little of the optional garlic butter, sharing the full amount across twelve, then enclose in the towel. Cook the second rolled bread. Between rounds, take the pan briefly off the burner while rolling the next pair; return it to the heat and let it recover before cooking. Wipe away scorched flour with a dry folded paper towel held in tongs. Allow about 30–40 minutes for rolling, pan adjustments and all twelve cooking rounds.
8. **Serve:** Bring the wrapped stack to the table promptly. Twelve breads make six portions of two, or twelve smaller one-bread accompaniments. If serving rice as well, start with one naan per person and offer the rest as wanted.
