## Chef's Note

Whip the separated egg whites to stiff peaks, then fold them into the flour batter just before cooking. The foam adds air, so fold gently and use a fully heated iron. Let each waffle release readily with a golden exterior and a cooked center before lifting it out.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the waffle iron according to its manual. Lightly grease the plates with the separate vegetable-oil allowance if recommended; the measured oil all goes into the batter. Separate the listed eggs, keeping yolks apart from whites. Use a clean, dry bowl and beaters without grease for the whites.
2. **Dry mix:** Sift together the flour, baking powder, sugar, and salt into a large bowl.
3. **Wet mix:** In another bowl, whisk the egg yolks, milk, measured vegetable oil, and vanilla until evenly blended.
4. **Whip the whites:** Beat the whites from the listed eggs until stiff peaks stand upright when the beaters are lifted. Stop before the foam becomes dry and grainy.
5. **Combine:** Add the yolk mixture to the dry ingredients. Stir gently with a whisk just until all flour is moistened, scraping the bowl bottom and sides. Small hydrated lumps may remain.
6. **Fold:** Use a rubber spatula to fold in the whipped whites gently until evenly incorporated, without large pockets of unmixed white. Avoid beating or stirring away the foam, and begin cooking promptly.
7. **Cook the batch:** Fill the hot iron with the amount specified in its manual, leaving expansion room. Cook according to the iron’s settings and readiness guidance; the cycle may be around 5–10 minutes, depending on the iron. Wait until steam has mostly subsided, the waffle releases readily and it is golden with no wet batter in the middle. Repeat with the remaining batter. Keep finished waffles separately on a rack while the next ones cook rather than stacking them.
8. **Serve:** Serve immediately with warm maple syrup and [Perfect Cranberry Sauce](/mise/recipes/perfect-cranberry-sauce) if desired.

## Cooking Notes

The original batch plans six portions, not six waffles of a fixed size. About 40 minutes is a starting allowance for preparation and cooking; a smaller iron or longer cycle can extend the batch. Use the iron’s actual fill amount and cycle count to plan service.
