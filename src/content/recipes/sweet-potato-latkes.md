## Chef's Note

Keep the pancakes small and flatten them so the centers can cook before the outsides darken. The measured frying oil is a shallow-frying supply; its depth depends on the actual skillet, not a universal half-inch claim.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Wash, peel and coarsely grate the potatoes. For the current squeezed preparation, gather in a clean towel and squeeze out excess liquid; do not expect the shreds to become completely dry.

2. **Mix:** Combine all the potatoes, scallions, flour, listed sea salt and black pepper, then all the beaten eggs. Keep raw-egg utensils separate from applesauce and finished pancakes.

3. **Heat:** For the original batch, heat all the listed neutral oil in a deep 12-inch skillet over medium-high, until hot but not smoking. Scaled batches need an appropriately sized suitable skillet or separate batches, keeping pancake size fixed; the original ¾ cup does not guarantee a ½-inch depth.

4. **Portion:** Use about ⅛ cup mixture per latke, flattening to roughly 3 inches across. Fry about four at a time only if they fit uncrowded; keep pancake size fixed when scaling.

5. **Fry:** Reduce heat toward medium as needed. Start checking around 1½–2 minutes per side; cook until golden outside and the egg-bound center is set and reaches 160°F / 71°C. Sample more than one pancake with a thin probe. Adjust heat if outsides darken first.

6. **Drain:** Transfer cooked latkes to a clean towel-lined plate or rack. Use optional additional salt to taste while hot.

7. **Serve:** Serve promptly with separately prepared Nana Ruth’s Applesauce, if desired. Keep remaining raw mixture refrigerated between extended batches.

Serve with [Nana Ruth’s Applesauce](/mise/recipes/nana-ruths-applesauce), prepared separately.
