## Chef's Note

Smashing gives cucumber rough surfaces that catch the dressing. The aim is a cracked cucumber with a lively bite, not a flattened pulp. A short salt-and-drain step helps control released water, but the salad will still soften if dressed far in advance. This garlic, vinegar, and sesame version is one interpretation of pai huang gua.

## Directions

1. **Crack and cut:** Set cucumbers on a stable board. If an English cucumber has a loose, watery seed core, halve it and scrape that part away. Cover with a clean towel to contain splashes and tap gently with a rolling pin until cracked. Cut into rough 1-inch pieces.
2. **Salt and drain:** Toss the pieces with the measured salt in a colander over a bowl. Let drain for 15 minutes, preferably chilled. Shake off the released liquid and gently blot the pieces; do not squeeze them into mush.
3. **Mix dressing:** While the cucumber drains, stir vinegar, soy sauce, sesame oil, sugar, garlic, and optional chili crisp together until the sugar dissolves.
4. **Taste and toss:** Taste a drained cucumber piece. If overly salty, rinse briefly and blot well, then begin with only half the dressing. Otherwise add the cucumber to the dressing and toss gently until coated. Taste and adjust the amount of dressing to suit the cucumber.
5. **Finish:** Fold in scallion and sprinkle with sesame seeds. Divide into four side portions and serve soon after dressing. Keep chilled if there will be a short delay.
