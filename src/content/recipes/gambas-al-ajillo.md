## Chef's Note

Gambas al ajillo puts garlic and chile into the olive oil that becomes the sauce. Have everything ready before heating the pan: pale golden garlic can turn bitter quickly, and shrimp need to come off the heat as soon as their centers are cooked. Serve with crusty bread for the full garlicky oil and sherry sauce.

## Directions

1. **Prep Everything First:** Thaw frozen shrimp in the refrigerator, then pat dry with paper towels. Season with the measured salt and smoked paprika. Have sliced garlic, your chosen dried peppers or pepper flakes, sherry, parsley and measured lemon juice beside the stove. Keep the shrimp refrigerated until the pan is ready.

2. **Heat the Oil:** Pour the full measured olive oil into a cast-iron skillet or a cazuela suitable for your hob over medium heat. When it shimmers, add the garlic and dried guindilla peppers, or the alternative pepper flakes. Cook for 30–45 seconds, swirling, until the garlic is pale golden; lower the heat if it colors too quickly.

3. **Add the Shrimp:** Increase to medium-high. Add the shrimp in a single layer, cook for about 1 minute, then turn and begin checking after another 30 seconds. These are first checks, not a required stopping point. For a larger batch, use additional suitable pans or cook separate proportionate batches, dividing all measured oil, seasonings and liquids among them.

4. **Add the Sherry:** Pour in the measured sherry carefully and let it bubble, about 30 seconds as a first check. Continue only as needed for the thickest shrimp to reach 145°F, with firm, pearly, opaque flesh through the center. Lower the heat if the garlic darkens before the shrimp finish. A brief simmer does not remove all the alcohol.

5. **Finish:** Take the pan off the heat as soon as the shrimp are cooked. Add all the measured lemon juice and parsley. Curl and outside color alone do not establish that the centers are done.

6. **Serve:** Serve promptly with crusty bread and all the garlicky oil and sherry sauce. Set a hot skillet on a heatproof trivet and keep its handle out of reach.
