## Chef's Note

This Indian-style rice combines saffron and green cardamom with ghee and golden onion. Steep the saffron while preparing the pot, then add every drop of the infusion so its color and aroma reach the grains. A gentle simmer and a covered rest keep the rice tender without breaking it up.

## Directions

1. **Steep the saffron:** Warm the milk until steaming but not boiling, in a small saucepan or in brief microwave bursts in a heatproof bowl. Lightly crumble the measured saffron threads into it and steep for at least 15 minutes while continuing with the rice and onion. Keep all the threads and liquid.
2. **Rinse and prepare:** Rinse basmati gently in several changes of cool water until much of the loose starch washes away. Drain thoroughly without soaking. Slice the onion thinly and lightly crack the cardamom pods. Measure the remaining spices, water and salt before heating the ghee.
3. **Cook spices and onion:** Heat ghee in the heavy saucepan over medium heat. Add green cardamom, optional black cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and bay leaf; stir 20–30 seconds until fragrant. Add onion and cook for about 10–15 minutes, stirring often, until soft and lightly golden with a few browned edges. Lower the heat if the spices darken rapidly. This is a golden onion base, not a dark caramelized one.
4. **Coat rice and add all liquid:** Fold in drained rice and stir gently for about 1 minute to coat it with ghee. Add the measured cooking water, all the saffron milk and threads, and the salt. Stir once and bring to a gentle boil, allowing about 5 minutes to heat. Cover snugly and lower the heat to maintain a very gentle simmer.
5. **Simmer and check:** Cook for about 15–20 minutes. Near the end, briefly part the rice at the edge and taste a grain: the liquid should be absorbed and the center tender. If firm and dry, use the measured hot-water reserve as described in troubleshooting. If firm with liquid remaining, re-cover and cook a little longer. Do not force the rice dry over high heat.
6. **Rest, fluff and finish:** Once the grains are tender and free liquid is absorbed, turn off the heat and rest covered for 10 minutes. Remove visible cardamom pods, cloves, cinnamon and bay leaf; let diners know that small pieces may remain. Lift and fluff gently with a fork or rice paddle, folding in cilantro. Add the optional toasted nuts at serving. Expect roughly 4 1/2–5 cups, enough for four generous rice sides or smaller portions beside dal and potatoes.
