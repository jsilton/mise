## Chef's Note

This Chinese-American restaurant-style dish brings ginger, scallions and a sweet-savory glaze to thinly sliced beef. A light starch coating browns in the pan, then softens somewhat as it catches the sauce. Cooking the glaze separately lets you control its thickness without repeatedly boiling the beef.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the beef:** Pat the steak dry and slice across the grain into 1/4-inch-thick, roughly 2-inch-long strips. Toss with the 3 tbsp coating cornstarch, separating slices and shaking off loose excess. Divide into three roughly equal batches.
2. **Mix the sauce and slurry:** Stir soy sauce, 120 ml water and brown sugar together until the sugar mostly dissolves. In a separate cup, mix the 1 tsp slurry cornstarch with 1 tbsp cold water. Keep ginger, garlic, chilies and the two scallion portions beside the stove.
3. **Sear three batches:** Heat a heavy skillet over medium-high. Add 1 tbsp frying oil; it should shimmer without smoking heavily. Add one batch in a single layer and sear about 1–2 minutes per side, until browned and cooked through. Probe thicker strips from the edge to check at least 145°F / 63°C. Transfer to a clean plate. Repeat twice with 1 tbsp oil for each batch, letting the pan regain heat. Reduce heat if the coating burns before the meat cooks.
4. **Cook aromatics:** Reduce heat to medium. Carefully discard excess oil and any blackened starch, leaving browned but unburned bits. Add the separate teaspoon oil, ginger and optional chilies; stir about 20 seconds. Add garlic and scallion whites and stir another 20–30 seconds without browning the garlic deeply.
5. **Thicken gently:** Pour in the mixed sauce and simmer about 1 minute. Stir the slurry again, add half, and simmer 20–30 seconds. Add the rest only if needed for a light spoon-coating consistency; discard unused slurry. Loosen with a tablespoon of water if it becomes sticky or pasty.
6. **Coat and check:** Return beef and its juices with the scallion greens. Toss over medium heat about 30–60 seconds, just until evenly coated and greens wilt. Verify sliced beef reaches at least 145°F / 63°C; give it at least 3 minutes before serving. Transfer off the hot pan for this wait. For the chicken variation, verify 165°F / 74°C in several thick strips instead.
7. **Serve:** Remove whole chilies or tell diners they are present. Divide among four portions with plain rice and vegetables prepared separately. Serve promptly; the coating will continue softening in the glaze.
