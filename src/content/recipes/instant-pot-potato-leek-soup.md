## Chef's Note

Sandy Clifton’s potato-leek soup softens leeks in the full butter and olive oil, then disperses a small flour addition into plenty of broth. Add the dairy after pressure cooking, and stop blending when the potatoes are smooth to keep the soup creamy rather than gluey. White pepper and optional nutmeg complement the potatoes and cream.

## Directions

1. **Soften the leeks:** Use a Cuisinart CPC-600 with its clean cooking pot, fitted sealing ring and unobstructed pressure valves. Keep the combined food and liquid at or below 60% of the pot’s capacity and never above its marked limit. At larger scales, divide every ingredient proportionally into complete cooker loads before starting; each soup load must contain at least ½ cup of the listed stock or broth. Repeat the whole cooking process for each load. Do not multiply the pressure timer. Select Sauté with MENU and press START. Add unsalted butter, extra-virgin olive oil, and leeks. Cook, stirring, for about 5–7 minutes or until the leeks soften; a little color is fine, but stop and lower to Simmer if they begin to catch.
2. **Add garlic and flour:** Use garlic cloves and all-purpose flour: stir in the garlic and cook about 30 seconds until fragrant. Sprinkle in the flour and stir for 1 minute, coating the leeks without leaving dry clumps.
3. **Dissolve the flour in broth:** Pour in chicken broth or vegetable broth gradually while stirring. Scrape the entire pot bottom with a wooden or silicone spoon so no flour or browned leek residue remains stuck. The mixture should be a loose broth with vegetables, not a thick paste.
4. **Add potatoes and seasoning:** Stir in Yukon Gold potatoes, fresh bay leaf, nutmeg (if using), and salt and white pepper. Check total food-and-liquid fill before closing the lid. Keep the dairy and garnish out of the pressure stage.
5. **Pressure-cook:** Press START/CANCEL to stop Sauté, and wait 2–3 minutes for the inner pot to cool slightly. Fit and lock the lid, set the pressure-limit valve to its closed • position, select High Pressure with MENU, set TIME to 7 minutes, then press START/CANCEL. The countdown starts only after pressure has built.
6. **Release fully naturally:** When the timer finishes, leave the CPC-600 on its automatic Keep Warm setting while pressure falls fully naturally. Wait until the red float is completely down and the lid unlocks; this commonly takes 12–30 minutes and can take longer. Then press START/CANCEL to turn off and open the lid away from your face. Do not force the lid or release steam manually for this recipe. Remove and discard the bay leaf. A fork should pass easily through the potato cubes; if they remain firm, select Simmer with the lid off until soft.
7. **Blend just until smooth:** Turn off and unplug the cooker. Use an immersion blender suitable for hot liquids, keep its head submerged, protect the nonstick pot and stop its motor before lifting. Stop once the soup is smooth; prolonged blending can make potato soup gluey. Alternatively, use a countertop blender that permits hot liquids, following its venting and hot-fill instructions and blending carefully in small batches.
8. **Add the dairy:** Stir in half-and-half after blending. Taste and adjust seasoning. If reheating for service, warm gently while stirring rather than reducing the finished cream soup hard.
9. **Garnish and serve:** Add sour cream and chopped fresh chives or parsley to each serving. Serve hot, or divide into shallow containers and chill promptly under refrigeration for chilled service.

## Cooking Notes

Sandy Clifton adapted the soup from her grandfather’s recipe. Serve it hot or chilled.
