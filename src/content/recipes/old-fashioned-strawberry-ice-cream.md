## Chef's Note

Macerate the fruit in the refrigerator while making and chilling the custard. Use one-third of the listed granulated sugar for the strawberries and the remaining two-thirds with all the brown sugar for the yolks. Tempering slows the eggs’ contact with heat, but the cooked base still needs a measured endpoint and prompt cooling. Machine capacity and overrun affect the finished ice cream volume.

## Directions

1. **Macerate:** Wash, hull and slice the strawberries. Toss all the fruit with the lemon juice and one-third of the listed granulated sugar; that is ¼ cup in the original batch. Cover and refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below for at least 3 hours, keeping all the fruit juice.
2. **Heat the dairy:** Use pasteurized milk and cream. In a saucepan, combine all the milk, cream, vanilla seeds and reserved pod. Heat gently until steaming and just beginning to simmer.
3. **Temper:** In a bowl, whisk the yolks, salt, remaining two-thirds of the granulated sugar and all the brown sugar. Gradually whisk some hot dairy into the yolk mixture, then return the entire tempered mixture to the pan.
4. **Cook the custard:** Stir constantly over medium-low heat, scraping the bottom, until the base reaches 160°F / 71°C and coats a spoon. Measure without touching the pan. Do not boil; stop heating at the endpoint rather than relying on a finger or visual coat alone.
5. **Cool and chill:** Transfer the base to shallow heat-safe containers and cool promptly over an ice-water bath, stirring. Refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below for at least 4 hours and until thoroughly cold. Keep cumulative time outside refrigeration within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C. An alternative is to continue the ice-water bath until the base is thoroughly cold before churning; cooling time varies, and the base must reach refrigerator-cold conditions rather than merely lukewarm. Remove and discard the vanilla pod once the base is cold.
6. **Churn:** Stir in all the macerated strawberries and all their juice. Churn according to the actual machine instructions and maximum fill. The 25–35-minute churning time is a reference; use separate cold loads if the whole mixture exceeds capacity, keeping the remainder refrigerated. Finished volume depends on the machine and air incorporation.
7. **Harden:** Transfer all the ice cream to covered freezer containers and freeze at 0°F / −18°C or below for at least 2 hours, or longer until firm enough to scoop. Keep frozen until serving.

## Cooking Notes

A separate option uses ½ cup granulated sugar for the original batch rather than the main ¾ cup. In that option, macerate the strawberries with ¼ cup granulated sugar and use the remaining ¼ cup in the custard, along with all the ¼ cup brown sugar. Every other fruit, lemon, yolk, dairy, vanilla and salt amount stays the same. Scale the entire selected option together; use its sugar allocation instead of the main allocation, keeping the full brown sugar.

The listed eight-cup yield is approximate. Check the maker’s actual bowl preparation, maximum fill and loading instructions in advance. Use separate cold loads if needed. Churning time depends on the machine; the two-hour freezer hardening is a minimum check, with longer freezing if the container is still soft.
