## Chef's Note

Jerk comes from the Maroons of Jamaica's Blue Mountains, who rubbed meat with allspice and Scotch bonnet and cooked it over pimento wood. Here a pork shoulder soaks in the marinade overnight and braises until it shreds. A spoonful of fresh marinade tossed in at the end keeps the jerk flavor bright and sharp.

## Directions

1. **Blend the marinade:** Blend all the marinade ingredients until mostly smooth, with a little texture left. It should taste aggressively spiced, salty and sharp. Spoon a few tablespoons into a small covered jar and refrigerate it for the finish.
2. **Marinate the pork:** Score the pork in a shallow crosshatch, 1/4 inch deep. Rub the rest of the marinade all over, working it into the cuts. Cover and refrigerate for at least 8 hours and up to 24.
3. **Sear the pork:** Heat the oven to 325°F. Scrape the excess marinade off the pork into a bowl and save it. Heat a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat and sear the pork until deeply browned on all sides, 3 to 4 minutes per side. Lower the heat if the sugary coating starts to blacken.
4. **Braise:** Pour the scraped marinade, stock and orange juice around the pork, not over it. Cover tightly and braise in the oven until a fork pulls the meat apart easily, 3 to 3 1/2 hours. If the pot looks dry, add a splash of hot water.
5. **Reduce the sauce:** Move the pork to a board to rest for 20 minutes. Skim the fat from the braising liquid and boil it on the stove, uncovered, until glossy but still pourable, about 10 minutes.
6. **Shred and finish:** Shred the pork with two forks, discarding any large pieces of fat. Toss it with the reduced sauce and the jar of fresh marinade, and serve.
