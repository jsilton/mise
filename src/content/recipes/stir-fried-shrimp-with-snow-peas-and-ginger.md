## Chef's Note

A short saltwater preparation is followed by thorough drying, then quick pan cooking. The ginger and garlic enter after the snow peas have softened, and the slurry is stirred again just before pouring. That keeps the aromatics from scorching while the sauce thickens evenly. Adapted from [The New York Times’ Stir-Fried Shrimp with Snow Peas and Ginger](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/7036-stir-fried-shrimp-with-snow-peas-and-ginger).

## Directions

1. **Prepare shrimp:** Dissolve the soak salt in the measured cold water, add shrimp and refrigerate for 5 minutes. Drain and discard the liquid. Rinse briefly with a gentle flow of cold water to remove surface salt, avoiding splashing, and pat dry. Clean the sink and raw-contact tools.

2. **Prepare sauce and vegetables:** Whisk broth, wine, soy sauce, cornstarch, sugar and white pepper together. Trim peas, mince garlic and ginger and chop scallion. Keep the separate seasoning salt for the vegetables.

3. **Cook shrimp:** Heat half the oil over medium-high in the skillet or wok. Add shrimp in a single layer, using batches if necessary and sharing this oil allocation. Cook about 1–2 minutes per side or as needed until firm, pearly and opaque through the center; a fine-tip thermometer inserted into a thick shrimp should read 145°F / 63°C. Transfer to a clean plate.

4. **Cook peas and aromatics:** Add remaining oil and snow peas. Stir-fry about 2 minutes until tender with a little bite, adding only as much of the seasoning salt as needed. Add garlic and ginger and stir about 20–30 seconds until fragrant without darkening.

5. **Thicken and finish:** Stir the sauce again to lift settled starch, pour into the pan and bring to a gentle bubbling simmer for about 30–60 seconds, stirring, until lightly thickened. Return shrimp and their juices only long enough to warm through. Turn off heat and stir in scallion and optional sesame oil. Loosen an overthick sauce with a small splash of water and serve promptly with separately prepared rice.
