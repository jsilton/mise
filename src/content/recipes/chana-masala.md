## Chef's Note

Chana masala brings chickpeas together with an onion-and-tomato gravy and aromatic spices. Here, whole cumin gives small bursts of flavor, while amchur adds fruity acidity. Brown the onion gently, reduce the tomatoes, then mash a few chickpeas into the sauce so it coats the rest without becoming soupy.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Drain and rinse the chickpeas and check that one crushes easily. Finely dice the onion, mince the garlic, grate the ginger and slit the serranos. Measure the spices and separate the garam masala into two equal portions. Keep the initial cooking water and the correction reserve separate.
2. **Start with cumin:** Heat the oil or ghee in the wide pot over medium heat. Add cumin seeds and stir for about **20–30 seconds**, until sizzling and fragrant. As soon as they darken slightly, add the onion; do not wait for black seeds or smoke.
3. **Soften and brown the onion:** Cook for about **12–15 minutes**, stirring regularly, until soft and evenly golden. Lower the heat if edges brown before the centers soften. If it begins sticking, use a small splash from the hot-water reserve and scrape up the golden residue. Stir in garlic, ginger and serranos for about **1 minute**.
4. **Concentrate the masala:** Lower the heat. Stir in coriander, turmeric, amchur, ground red chile and **half the garam masala** for about **15–20 seconds**. Immediately add all the crushed tomatoes and their juice. Return to medium heat and cook uncovered for **8–10 minutes**, stirring frequently, until thick and aromatic. A spoon should leave a brief trail through the moist base; do not keep frying just to force out a pool of oil.
5. **Simmer and thicken:** Add chickpeas, the initial fine salt and all the measured cooking water. Bring to a gentle simmer, allowing a few minutes to heat through, then cover and cook over medium-low for **20 minutes**, stirring once or twice. Uncover and mash about **one-quarter of the chickpeas** against the pan with the spoon. Stir them through and simmer uncovered for **3–5 minutes**, until the sauce clings but still moves around the beans. Use the remaining hot-water reserve only if needed to keep it spoonable.
6. **Balance and serve:** Stir in the remaining garam masala and simmer for **1 minute**, then turn off the heat. Taste before adding lemon. Add the measured lemon juice gradually; if replacing amchur, combine both listed lemon portions and use only as much as needed. Add the reserved salt after tasting a chickpea with its sauce. Remove the serranos or tell diners they are present, then fold in cilantro. Divide into portions of roughly **1 1/4 cups each**, with plain basmati or warm naan.
