## Chef's Note

Parmigiano-Reggiano brings salty, aged-cheese flavor to a sweet cream-cheese filling. Add the crust zest, cinnamon and sugar before pulsing in its cheese and butter. Mix the filling slowly once the eggs go in; excess air and overbaking can contribute to cracks. The water bath moderates heat, but it does not guarantee a crack-free cake.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 350°F. A 10-inch springform is the original twelve-portion pan reference; check its actual depth and capacity for the full crust and filling. Choose sufficient suitable pans and water-bath space for the selected batch before pressing or baking the crust. Line each base with fitted parchment and distribute the full listed greasing butter among the pan sides. Plan corresponding shares of the full crust and filling for each chosen pan; do not plan to divide a baked crust after filling it.
2. **Make the crust:** Pulse the crackers and already-toasted sunflower seeds to fine crumbs. Add the zest, cinnamon and crust sugar. Pulse in the crust Parmigiano in small batches. Mix in the listed melted crust butter; a spoonful squeezed between the fingers should hold its shape. Add the separate extra-butter allowance only if needed for that consistency.
3. **Bake the crust:** Divide the complete crumb mixture proportionally among the chosen pans, then press each share across its pan base and up the sides, about 1 cm thick, leaving about 1 cm below the rim. Bake until lightly golden; 15–18 minutes is the original-pan reference, then cool. Lower the oven to 325°F for the filling.
4. **Mix the filling:** Beat the room-temperature cream cheese until smooth. On low speed, gradually mix in the filling sugar, then the sour cream, vanilla and filling Parmigiano. Add the listed eggs one at a time, mixing on low only until incorporated and scraping the bowl as needed. Do not taste the raw-egg batter.
5. **Prepare the bath:** Once the crusts and pans are cool enough to handle, wrap each springform base and sides securely in two layers of heavy-duty foil. Distribute all the filling among the prepared crusts, leaving room below each pan rim. Set each pan in a roasting pan with sufficient bath space, then carefully add hot water to about halfway up the springform sides, keeping water below the foil edge and out of the cake. Do not overfill the cake pans.
6. **Bake:** Bake at 325°F, beginning to check around 85 minutes; 85–90 minutes is the original-batch reference. Look for set edges and a center that moves gently rather than sloshing. Measure the center without touching the pan and cook the egg-containing filling to 160°F / 71°C. That measured endpoint may produce a firmer center than a very soft cheesecake; pan depth and oven conditions can require more time.
7. **Cool and chill:** Turn off the oven and crack the door for a one-hour cooling rest, then remove the cake from the bath and discard the foil. Refrigerate promptly rather than waiting indefinitely for room temperature; limit total time outside refrigeration after baking to 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C. Shorten the oven rest if needed to meet that limit. Refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below for at least 6 hours, until thoroughly chilled and sliceable.

## Cooking Notes

The full listed sunflower-seed amount is the nut-free crust choice. A separate pecan crust uses the same listed volume of already-toasted pecans in place of all the seeds; every other crust and filling amount stays the same, and that option contains nuts. Graham-cracker sheet sizes vary; use the actual cracker type specified by the chosen recipe or compare the crumb consistency before adding the conditional extra butter. No universal cracker weight is implied.

A separate water-bath and cooling route prepares room-temperature bath water 1–1½ inches deep in the roasting pan while the oven lowers to 325°F, then places the wrapped cake into that heated bath. After baking to the measured egg-filling endpoint, remove the pan from the bath and cool on a rack, refrigerating within the same cold-handling limits, then chill for 4–6 hours until fully cold and set. This replaces the main hot-water/one-hour oven-rest/at-least-six-hour-chill route; do not add both cooling schedules together.

True Parmigiano-Reggiano is made with calf rennet, so this version is not classified as vegetarian. Choosing another cheese changes the defining ingredient and has not been evaluated here.
