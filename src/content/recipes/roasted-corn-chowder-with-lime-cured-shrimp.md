## Chef's Note

Roasted corn, rendered bacon fat and smoked paprika give this creamy chowder layered sweetness and smoke. Cook the potatoes until tender before adding shrimp, then finish the shrimp gently and stir in lime off the heat. For a richer roux-and-cream chowder with peppers and celery, use [Robert Irvine’s roux-and-cream version](/mise/recipes/roasted-corn-chowder-robert-irvine).

## Directions

1. **Roast the corn:** Preheat the oven to 425°F / 220°C. Toss corn kernels, neutral vegetable oil, and salt and black pepper on a rimmed sheet pan and spread in an uncrowded layer. Roast about 15 minutes, stirring as needed, until fragrant with browned edges, without blackening. Use more pans or roasting loads at larger scales. Begin the pot stages while the corn roasts.
2. **Render the bacon:** Use slices of bacon in a roomy heavy-bottomed soup pot over medium heat. Cook until crisp, stirring, then transfer the bacon bits to a plate and leave all rendered fat in the pot.
3. **Soften the onion:** Add red onion to the bacon fat and cook about 5 minutes until softened without burning.
4. **Add garlic and paprika:** Stir in garlic cloves and smoked paprika and cook 30–45 seconds until fragrant; lower the heat if they catch.
5. **Cook the potatoes:** Add Red Bliss potatoes and chicken stock, scraping up browned bits. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer for about 15–20 minutes, until a fork passes easily through the potato pieces. Stir occasionally so the bottom stays clear; the pot needs headroom above the full liquids and vegetables.
6. **Thicken while keeping texture:** Stir in heavy cream and all the roasted corn. Take the pot safely off the heat. With a hot-liquid-rated immersion blender, keep the blade guard submerged, pulse briefly and stop the motor before lifting it; begin with 3–4 short pulses and leave recognizable corn and potato pieces. Return to low heat and bring to a gentle simmer.
7. **Cook the shrimp completely:** Stir in all raw shrimp. Start checking after 2–3 minutes and continue until the shrimp are firm, pearly and opaque throughout, with thick centers reaching 145°F / 63°C. Size and starting temperature change the cooking time; pink color alone is not the endpoint.
8. **Finish with lime and bacon:** Turn off the heat and stir in fresh lime juice and all reserved bacon bits. Taste and adjust seasoning. Serve promptly while the shrimp remain tender.

## Cooking Notes

For a fresh count-based version of the six-portion chowder, use all kernels from 4 ears instead of the measured 3 cups and scale that ear count with the planned portions; ear size makes its corn density variable. Keep all other main ingredients and method stages the same. For the richer chowder, use [Robert Irvine’s roux-and-cream version](/mise/recipes/roasted-corn-chowder-robert-irvine) and its complete ingredient list and method.
