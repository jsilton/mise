## Chef's Note

Chorizo seasons the vegetables before the rice bakes in broth with chickpeas and raisins. This covered method uses Arborio rice. Bomba and Calasparra absorb differently and need their own liquid and timing adjustments; do not substitute them directly in this version.

## Directions

1. **Render chorizo:** Use Spanish-style chorizo for this stage. Preheat the oven to 400°F. Use a roomy oven-safe skillet or Dutch oven with a tight-fitting lid that holds the rice, full broth, chickpeas and chorizo with room to stir and without a brim-full load. Read the Spanish chorizo label: cured does not always mean ready-to-eat. Prepare any cook-before-eating pork sausage according to its package, checking 160°F / 71°C, before using this rendering route. A raw poultry product needs 165°F / 74°C and is not an assumed equivalent. Render the prepared rounds over medium heat until crisped and their red fat is released; remove to a clean plate. Keep the rendered fat for the current main route.
2. **Soften vegetables:** Add red bell pepper, yellow onion, and garlic cloves to the chorizo fat. Cook about 8–10 minutes until softened, stirring and lowering heat if garlic browns too quickly. Use chorizo that releases enough fat to cook the vegetables without catching. For the olive-oil version, choose the complete uncovered variation below.
3. **Add paste and spices:** Stir in tomato paste, smoked paprika (pimentón), and saffron threads (if using) and cook about 1 minute until fragrant without scorching.
4. **Coat rice:** Add Arborio rice and stir about 2 minutes to coat the grains in the spiced fat.
5. **Add wine:** Add dry sherry or white wine, scrape the bottom and let bubble about 1 minute.
6. **Bring to a boil:** Add chickpeas, chicken broth, and raisins and all the reserved chorizo. Bring to a boil before moving to the oven.
7. **Bake covered:** Use hot water for this stage. Cover tightly and bake at 400°F about 20–25 minutes, then check absorbed liquid and a tender rice center. If the center is firm and the pan is dry, add a little hot water, cover and continue; if wet and firm, keep cooking before adding more liquid. Do not replace Arborio directly with Bomba or Calasparra or use the source variant’s uncovered method here.
8. **Rest and serve:** Rest off heat, covered, for 10 minutes, then fluff gently and divide among the listed portions. Allow about 80 minutes overall including 20 minutes preparation, roughly 50 minutes rendering/sautéing/heating/baking and the rest; extra batches or firm rice extend the estimate. Larger quantities need sufficient pan space or separate original-size pans, not proportional oven times.

## Source-Rich Uncovered Variation

This Saveur variation uses 2½ lb chorizo and 5 tablespoons olive oil in its original four-portion batch. Cook it instead of the covered recipe. Scale every ingredient together for other batch sizes, use suitably roomy pans and judge the rice center rather than multiplying oven time.

Use ¼ cup plus 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil (5 tablespoons total), 2½ lb Spanish-style chorizo sliced ¾ inch thick, 6 thinly sliced garlic cloves, 2 medium yellow onions finely chopped, 1 large red bell pepper seeded and coarsely chopped (the source describes about 1 cup), 1 tablespoon red pepper paste such as choricero, ñora or ají panca OR 1 teaspoon sweet unsmoked paprika, 1 tablespoon tomato paste, ¼ cup dry sherry, 3¼ cups chicken or vegetable stock OR water, a pinch of cinnamon, a large pinch of saffron threads, one 15-ounce can chickpeas drained (the source describes 1½ cups), ½ cup raisins, kosher salt to taste, 1½ cups short- or medium-grain rice such as carnaroli, Arborio or Goya medium-grain, and finely chopped chives for garnish. Use the listed whole pepper and 15-ounce can; their cup descriptions are approximate. This variation uses its own rice, chorizo, oil and spice choices.

Read the chorizo label and complete any required raw-pork cooking to 160°F / 71°C before this browning sequence; package directions apply. Use a large oven-safe Dutch oven, leaving room for stirring the full rice/liquid/meat load. Over medium-high heat, use 1 tablespoon of the total olive oil across uncrowded chorizo batches. Turn until crisped on both sides, beginning checks around 5–7 minutes; move to a clean lined plate. Discard accumulated rendered fat; keep the remaining olive oil for the vegetables.

Set a center oven rack and preheat to 400°F. Add the remaining ¼ cup olive oil, garlic, onions and pepper to the empty pot. Cook over medium heat, stirring, until jammy and browned, beginning checks around 20 minutes. Stir in the chosen red pepper paste OR sweet paprika and tomato paste; cook about 5 minutes, lowering heat if it catches. Add sherry and bring to a boil. Add all the chosen stock OR water, cinnamon, saffron, chickpeas, raisins, reserved chorizo and salt to taste; return to a boil. Add the rice and return to a boil again. Bake uncovered about 20–25 minutes, checking that liquid is absorbed and the rice is al dente. Remove, cover and rest about 10 minutes until just tender; finish with all the chives and serve. Rice and pan differences can require longer cooking.

For the source’s distinct Bomba OR Calasparra choice, use 4½ cups stock instead of 3¼ cups and allow roughly 10 minutes longer cooking, judging the center rather than treating that addition as a guarantee. This applies only to this source-rich uncovered branch; it does not authorize direct substitution in the repaired covered main.
