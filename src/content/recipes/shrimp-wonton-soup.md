## Chef's Note

Cantonese wontons rely on a filling that's sticky and cohesive, achieved by stirring ground pork and shrimp vigorously in one direction until proteins bind into a tacky paste. This traditional technique prevents the filling from crumbling during boiling. The clear broth is finished with sesame oil and shredded nori for an aromatic base.

## Directions

1.  **The Filling:** In a large bowl, combine pork, shrimp, ginger, scallions, egg white, the filling soy sauce, and the filling sesame oil. Stir vigorously in one direction for 3 minutes until sticky.
2.  **Assemble:** Place 1 tsp filling in the center of a prepared wonton wrapper. If making [Fresh Wonton Wrappers](/mise/recipes/fresh-wonton-wrappers), allow their preparation and resting time separately. Dab edges with water, fold into a triangle, and pinch the corners together.
3.  **The Base:** Heat the prepared broth in a saucepan. Divide the nori among four serving bowls and add a few drops of sesame oil to each. Add up to 1 tsp of the bowl soy sauce per bowl, tasting with a little broth first because the broth is already seasoned.
4.  **Boil:** Bring a separate pot of water to a gentle boil. Add the wontons in batches without crowding. Start checking after 3-4 minutes; floating alone does not establish doneness. Check several wontons with a thin-tip thermometer inserted into the center of the filling and continue until they reach 160°F / 71°C. Cook the greens in the hot broth until tender.
5.  **Bind:** Ladle 1 cup of the hot prepared broth into each serving bowl to "wake up" the seasonings.
6.  **Serve:** Transfer the cooked wontons and greens to the bowls. Garnish with fresh coriander and offer the black vinegar at the table. Refrigerate unused wrappers according to their package instructions.
