## Chef's Note

This elote-style version uses crema, queso fresco, cilantro and chile seasoning. Coat the cooked ears while warm so the cheese adheres. Check for tender kernels as well as charred spots; the heat source and ear size determine the actual time.

## Directions

1. **Cook the corn:** Pat ears of fresh corn dry after husking. Preheat the actual grill or broiler according to its instructions. For the broiler route, arrange ears in one layer on a foil-lined broiler-safe sheet, about 4–6 inches from the heat if the appliance permits, then broil, turning every 2–3 minutes. For the grill route, put ears directly on clean hot grates and turn every 2–3 minutes. Start checking around 8–10 minutes: kernels should be tender with browned spots. Continue or move away from intense heat if color develops before tenderness; more ears need uncrowded batches.
2. **Mix the coating:** Mix Mexican crema or sour cream, milk (if using), kosher salt, and black pepper in a clean bowl: use either the listed crema alone, or the same listed sour-cream amount with all the optional milk. Add all the measured salt and the pepper allowance. Refrigerate this coating while the corn cooks.
3. **Coat and top:** Let the cooked ears cool just enough to handle with tongs or corn holders. Brush all the coating across the ears over a shallow dish to catch drips. Distribute the full listed amounts of queso fresco, fresh cilantro, and chili powder or chile-lime seasoning over the cooked ears, turning to coat the sides. The cheese and coating remain separate from raw-food-contact utensils.
4. **Serve:** Serve warm with lime wedges for diners to squeeze as desired. Keep any unused coating and topped leftovers refrigerated promptly.
