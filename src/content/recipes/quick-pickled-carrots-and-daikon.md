## Chef's Note

Do chua brings sweet-tart crunch to Vietnamese bánh mì, rice plates and noodle bowls. Equal amounts of carrot and daikon keep the bite balanced. A little salt and sugar soften the matchsticks before rinsing; a separate sugar measure sweetens the rice-vinegar brine. Drain the serving portion so it brightens a sandwich or bowl without soaking it.

## Directions

1. **Cut even matchsticks:** Wash and peel the carrots and daikon, trim the ends, then weigh the required amounts. Cut into roughly 2-inch matchsticks, about 2 mm thick for carrot and 2–3 mm for daikon. Put them in a bowl; have a clean jar and colander ready.
2. **Massage briefly:** Toss vegetables with the 1/2 tsp fine salt and the separate 1 tsp sugar. Knead gently for about 3 minutes, until liquid collects and a daikon stick bends without breaking. If the sticks are still rigid, let them stand for a few more minutes rather than crushing them.
3. **Rinse and drain:** Rinse briefly under cold running water in the colander. Drain and press gently to remove excess liquid; do not wring the sticks bone-dry. Pack into the jar without crushing them.
4. **Mix the sweet brine:** Stir the 1/4 cup brine sugar into the measured 1/2 cup lukewarm water until dissolved, then stir in the 1/2 cup rice vinegar. The brine should be cool or lukewarm, not hot. Pour over the vegetables and gently press them below the liquid with a clean spoon. Leave about 1/2 inch space at the top. If coverage is inadequate, use the container or extra same-ratio brine correction above.
5. **Refrigerate and develop:** Cover and refrigerate immediately for at least 1 hour. Taste a drained stick: it should retain a crisp bite with both sweetness and vinegar tang. More refrigerated time, ideally overnight, gives fuller flavor; do not leave the jar on the counter to speed it up.
6. **Drain only what you need:** Lift portions with a clean fork or tongs and let brine drain back into the jar. At the stated quantities, expect approximately 2 cups drained pickles. For four salmon bowls, use about 1/4 of the finished batch—roughly 1/2 cup total, or 2 tbsp per bowl—as a small topping. Keep the rest covered by brine and refrigerated; a larger sandwich or rice-plate portion is a separate allocation.
