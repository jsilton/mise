## Chef's Note

This is a slow-cooker variation of [wine-braised chicken](/mise/recipes/chicken-coq-au-vin), with boneless thighs, pearl onions, and a generous quantity of vegetables. It has a different workflow and finish from the bacon-and-butter stovetop version. A measured broth and tomato-paste base replaces the soup-mix packet so the salt and sauce can be controlled. Finish the sauce in a saucepan because a closed slow cooker retains water instead of reducing it.

## Directions

1. **Load vegetables:** Place carrots and pearl onions in the bottom of a 5–6 quart slow cooker, then add mushrooms. Keep the chicken refrigerated until ready to sear.
2. **Brown chicken:** Pat thighs dry and season with measured salt and pepper. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sear half the thighs about 3 minutes per side, then transfer straight to the vegetables. Repeat with remaining oil and chicken. Do not pause or refrigerate the partly cooked meat between searing and slow cooking.
3. **Make the braising liquid:** Lower skillet heat to medium. Cook tomato paste and garlic for about 1 minute, stirring. Take the pan briefly off the burner to add wine. Return to heat, scrape up golden residue, and simmer until wine reduces by roughly half, about 4–6 minutes. Stir in stock, Worcestershire, and thyme, then pour over chicken. Cover and start cooking immediately.
4. **Slow-cook and verify:** Cook on HIGH about 3–4 hours or LOW about 5–6 hours, keeping the lid closed except to check near the end. Check several thighs reach at least 165°F / 74°C and are tender; carrots should yield readily to a fork. Continue cooking if necessary. Transfer cooked chicken and vegetables to a serving dish and cover briefly while finishing the sauce.
5. **Thicken separately:** Pour cooking liquid into a saucepan and skim excessive fat. Bring to a simmer and reduce a little if very watery. Mix cornstarch with cold water, re-stir, and add half the slurry while stirring the simmering liquid. Simmer about 1 minute; add more slurry only as needed for a light coating consistency, simmering again after each addition.
6. **Serve:** Taste sauce before adding any extra salt. Pour over chicken and vegetables and serve with one starch, such as mashed potatoes. Wine remains an ingredient after cooking; this dish is not reliably alcohol-free.
