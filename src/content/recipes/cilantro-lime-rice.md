## Chef's Note

This Mexican-inspired bowl rice uses jasmine for its fragrance and tender texture. It is a different preparation from toasted tomato rice or rice cooked with a blended green sauce. The fresh finish belongs after the covered rest here: taste the rice with its main dish before deciding how much lime it needs.

## Directions

1. **Rinse and drain:** Rinse the rice in several changes of cool water until noticeably less cloudy, then drain well in a fine-mesh strainer. A little cloudiness is fine; do not soak it while preparing the other ingredients.
2. **Heat and cover:** Put drained rice, the measured water and salt in a heavy 2-quart saucepan. Stir once, bring to a gentle boil, then cover snugly and reduce to low. Cook at a gentle simmer for about fifteen minutes without repeatedly lifting the lid.
3. **Check the grains:** Briefly lift the lid and part the rice near the edge. There should be no pooled water, and a tasted grain should be tender through the center. If hard and dry, add a little hot water as described in troubleshooting. If firm with water still present, cover and cook a few minutes longer.
4. **Rest covered:** Once the rice is tender and the free water is absorbed, move the pan off the hot burner. Leave covered for ten minutes. Prepare cilantro, zest and juice during the cooking or resting time.
5. **Fluff and season:** Uncover, fluff gently, and fold in oil, cilantro, zest and the first tablespoon of lime juice. Taste with the food it will accompany. Add the optional juice a teaspoon at a time only if it needs more acidity. Divide into four side portions.

## With a rice cooker

Use the appliance's instructions for this quantity of white jasmine rice and its measuring cup. Do not assume its marked water line is calibrated for every rice variety. Keep the cilantro, oil and lime for the finish after the machine's rest; the stovetop time above does not describe every cooker.
