## Chef's Note

Thin fennel ribbons bring anise flavor and crunch, while citrus adds juice and sweetness. A short ice bath can refresh the shaved fennel, but dry it well afterward. Add dressing gradually and taste with the olives and cheese before using all the salt.

## Directions

1. **Shave fennel:** Wash the bulbs and fronds. Trim stalks and damaged outer layers, halve the bulbs lengthwise and cut out the hard core. Shave thinly with a sharp knife or a guarded mandoline. If the ribbons need crisping, immerse in ice water for **5–10 minutes** while preparing the citrus; drain and spin or pat thoroughly dry.
2. **Segment citrus:** Cut the tops and bottoms off the oranges and optional grapefruit. Set on a flat cut end and slice away peel and white pith. Over a bowl, cut between membranes to release segments. Squeeze the remaining membranes to collect juice, and measure the listed amount for the dressing. Reserve any excess juice for another use, rather than pouring it all over the salad.
3. **Whisk dressing:** Whisk olive oil, lemon juice, **the measured reserved citrus juice**, honey or agave, pepper and **half the salt**. Taste with a fennel ribbon. The dressing should be bright but not harsh; the cheese and optional olives will add more salt.
4. **Assemble and taste:** Spread dried fennel on a broad platter and arrange citrus segments over it. Add optional olives. Drizzle about **half the dressing**, turn gently and taste a complete bite with a little Parmesan if using. Add more dressing and the remaining salt only as needed. Offer unused dressing separately. Finish with the Parmesan and reserved tender fronds and serve promptly.
