## Chef's Note

Dissolving the sugar before whipping helps build a smooth foam. Keep all the listed whites and sugar; measure the whites by volume rather than assuming a count of eggs. The exterior should set while the baked center stays soft. Use pasteurized whites suitable for whipping because a soft center and an unmeasured heating stage do not establish a safety temperature.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Heat the oven to 250°F and line two baking sheets with parchment. Keep the measured pasteurized whites refrigerated until ready to heat; use pasteurized shell-egg whites or a pasteurized product labeled suitable for whipping, measured to the full listed volume.

2. **Dissolve:** Combine all the whites, sugar and salt in a heat-proof bowl over simmering water, with the bowl bottom above the water. Whisk gently until the sugar is dissolved; check for remaining grains with a clean spoon rather than touching the hot mixture or tasting it. Use the pasteurized whites selected in the preparation step; sugar dissolution is not an egg-temperature check.

3. **Whip:** Transfer the complete mixture to a stand mixer. Add all the cream of tartar and whip on high until stiff, glossy peaks hold and the bowl is no longer warm, starting checks around 8 minutes; mixer and egg product affect the clock.

4. **Fold:** Gently fold in all the chocolate chips until dispersed.

5. **Portion:** Scoop large spoonfuls onto the sheets, leaving about 1 inch between them. The original 36-cookie yield is a planning count; keep portions similar in size when making more or fewer cookies, using additional sheets or oven rounds as needed.

6. **Bake:** Bake about 45–50 minutes for the original portion size, rotating the sheets halfway through. The exterior should be firm. Break a sample open: the center should be cooked through but still soft, without flowing egg foam. Keep the pasteurized-white precaution even when the center looks set.

7. **Cool:** Cool completely on the sheets before lifting carefully into an airtight container.
