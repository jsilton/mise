## Chef's Note

The roasted peppers and smoked paprika give this generous cream sauce its color and sweetness. Cook the pasta and sauce alongside one another, then finish with Parmesan off the heat. Keep the reserved pasta water for adjustment rather than automatically pouring it all in. Adapted from [Food Network Kitchen’s Creamy, Garlicky Shrimp Skillet](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/creamy-garlicky-shrimp-skillet-3875851).

## Directions

1. **Prepare and boil:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it. Drain and slice the jarred peppers, slice garlic, grate cheese and dry the prepared shrimp. Cook fettuccine until about 1 minute short of its package al dente time. Before draining, reserve the listed pasta water. Begin the sauce while the pasta cooks.

2. **Warm the peppers:** Melt butter in the deep skillet over medium heat. Add peppers and cook about 2 minutes. Add garlic and paprika and stir about 30–45 seconds, until fragrant without darkening the garlic.

3. **Simmer the cream:** Add heavy cream and bring just to a simmer. Reduce to medium-low and cook gently for about 3–4 minutes, stirring, until it lightly coats a spoon. Avoid a hard boil.

4. **Cook the shrimp:** Add shrimp, separating them in the sauce. Simmer gently about 3–5 minutes, stirring so all pieces cook evenly, until firm, pearly and opaque through the center; a fine-tip thermometer inserted into a thick shrimp should read 145°F / 63°C. If the pasta is not ready, lift cooked shrimp onto a clean plate and turn down the sauce rather than leaving them simmering.

5. **Combine off heat:** Add drained hot pasta to the sauce and turn off the heat. Add Parmesan gradually while tossing. If the coating becomes too tight, add reserved pasta water a small splash at a time; you may not need all of it. Return any removed shrimp and toss briefly to warm.

6. **Season and serve:** Taste before adding salt, because the cheese and pasta water already supply it. Add black pepper and parsley, and serve promptly. The sauce thickens further as the pasta stands.
