## Chef's Note

Whole-berry cranberry sauce brings tart fruit to a rich holiday plate. This version cooks part of the fruit first, then adds the rest briefly so some tender pieces remain. Orange peel adds aroma rather than replacing the cranberries' acidity. Expect a spoonable sauce after chilling, not the firm shape of canned cranberry jelly.

## Directions

1. **Sort and divide:** Rinse and drain fresh cranberries, discarding spoiled berries. Set aside 85 g of the total for the later addition; the other 255 g go in first. Keep frozen berries frozen until each addition. Prepare a thin orange-peel strip with no thick white pith.
2. **Dissolve the sugar:** In a 2-quart saucepan, combine water, sugar, orange peel and salt. Heat over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves and the liquid begins to simmer.
3. **Cook the first berries:** Add the 255 g cranberries, return to a gentle boil and reduce heat to a steady simmer. Cook about eight to ten minutes, stirring occasionally, until most berries have split. Keep clear of hot splashes; do not crush every berry smooth.
4. **Tenderize the remaining berries:** Add the reserved 85 g and simmer another three to five minutes, or until those berries are tender when pressed with a spoon. Some may retain their shape. The hot sauce should still flow; it will thicken as it cools. Remove the orange-peel strip.
5. **Cool and chill:** Put a small spoonful on a cool plate to assess flavor and body once cool enough to taste. Transfer the main batch to a shallow heatproof container and refrigerate promptly, loosely covered while cooling and covered fully once cold. Allow about two and a half hours to chill, or refrigerate overnight. Stir gently before serving two to three tablespoons per person.

## Cranberry-orange variation

Replace the 180 ml water with **120 ml water and 60 ml orange juice**. Omit the peel strip, then stir **one teaspoon finely grated orange zest** into the finished sauce after taking it off heat. Keep the same fruit and sugar quantities. This retains the citrus-forward option in one recipe; two tablespoons of zest are unnecessary for this batch.

## A tarter variation

Use **100 g sugar** instead of 150 g if the meal needs a sharper condiment. Expect a different sweetness and possibly looser body, and judge a chilled spoonful. Do not assume removing sugar leaves identical texture or turns this into a shelf-stable preserve.
