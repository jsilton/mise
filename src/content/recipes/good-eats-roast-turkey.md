## Chef's Note

This adaptation retains the aromatic wet-brine idea from Alton Brown’s Good Eats turkey. It uses weighed salt, unsalted stock, a fully refrigerated schedule and a steady 325°F oven, with measured doneness rather than a predicted temperature rise. It is a separately developed variation, not a claim to reproduce his exact method.

Salt changes how meat proteins interact with water and seasons the bird; brining is not simply water being driven inward by osmosis, and it cannot prevent all moisture loss from overcooking. The spices mainly flavor the brine and surface. Dry the skin after brining, and give the meat enough time without using skin color as a thermometer.

## Directions

1. **Make and chill the brine concentrate:** Put 1 L of the measured stock in a saucepan with all the weighed salt, sugar, peppercorns, allspice and ginger. Bring to a simmer and stir until dissolved, then remove from heat. Cool the saucepan in an ice-water bath, stirring the brine without getting bath water into it, then refrigerate until 40°F / 4°C or below. Keep the remaining 2.8 L stock and 3.8 L water cold. Budget up to two hours for cooling and use a thermometer rather than a clock to confirm readiness.
2. **Brine for twelve hours:** Remove packaging, neck and giblet bags from the fully thawed turkey. Reserve neck/giblets separately under appropriate refrigerated storage or discard. Combine the cold concentrate, remaining cold stock and measured water in a food-grade brining container. Submerge the turkey breast-side down, cover, and refrigerate for twelve hours at 40°F / 4°C or below. If necessary use a close-fitting food-grade brining bag supported in a deep leak-catching container; do not top up with plain water or leave the setup outside the refrigerator. The turkey’s original raw-storage limit still applies.
3. **Drain and dry:** Lift the bird carefully, letting cavity liquid drain into the container. Discard all used brine. Do not rinse the turkey in the sink. Pat dry with disposable towels, remove clinging spice pieces, and set on a rack over a rimmed tray, uncovered in the refrigerator, for four hours. Keep it below and separate from ready-to-eat foods. Clean raw-poultry contact surfaces.
4. **Prepare for roasting:** Heat the oven to 325°F / 163°C. Put the apple, onion, cinnamon, rosemary, sage and initial cup of pan water below a rack in a shallow roasting pan. Set turkey breast-side up on the rack, leaving its cavity empty; cook any dressing separately. Tuck wing tips underneath and brush the skin with the measured oil. Place a probe in the thickest breast meat without touching bone if using a leave-in thermometer.
5. **Roast and check the whole bird:** Roast for roughly 3 3/4–4 1/4 hours, beginning checks around three hours. Keep a shallow film of liquid in the pan, adding the listed hot water in small amounts if it dries; do not pour water over the skin. Shield areas that brown early loosely with foil. Before removing the bird, use an instant-read thermometer to verify at least 165°F / 74°C in both thick breast sections, the innermost part of each thigh and the innermost wing areas, away from bone. Continue cooking if any site is below that endpoint. The time is a planning range, not a finish guarantee.
6. **Rest and carve:** Transfer to a clean carving board, cover loosely and rest thirty minutes. Remove breast meat from the breastbone and slice across its grain; separate leg and thigh joints for serving. Offer about twelve portions with sides, distributing light and dark meat as desired. Pan aromatics are for flavoring drippings rather than a required side; discard them after straining drippings if used.

## Planning notes

This is an unstuffed, conventionally raised whole turkey of the stated size. A different size, a pre-brined bird, a breast-only roast or a tightly stuffed cavity requires a different plan. It remains distinct from the smaller dry-brined [Roast Turkey](/mise/recipes/simple-roast-turkey) and the reviewed [Herb-Roasted Turkey Breast](/mise/recipes/roast-turkey-breast). Brined drippings can be salty; gravy is a separate preparation and should not receive automatic extra salt.
