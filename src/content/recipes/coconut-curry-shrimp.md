## Chef's Note

Cook the shrimp first and lift them out while the vegetables soften. That separates the brief seafood cooking from the slower sauce preparation. Honey gives this coconut curry its sweet character; lime and salt balance it, while basil goes in just before serving. Adapted from [Ree Drummond’s Coconut Curry Shrimp](https://www.thepioneerwoman.com/food-cooking/recipes/a11767/coconut-curry-shrimp/), with zucchini and mushrooms.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Chop the vegetables, garlic and basil and measure the sauce ingredients. Juice the lime. Pat the thawed shrimp dry; season lightly with salt and pepper.

2. **Cook the shrimp:** Melt half the butter in the skillet over medium to medium-high heat without browning it darkly. Add shrimp in a single layer, using batches if crowded and sharing this butter allocation between batches. Cook about 2 minutes on the first side, turn and continue about 1–3 minutes, checking early. They should be firm, pearly and opaque through the center; a fine-tip thermometer inserted into a thick shrimp should read 145°F / 63°C. Transfer cooked shrimp to a clean plate.

3. **Soften the vegetables:** Reduce to medium and add the remaining butter, onion and mushrooms. Stir for about 5–7 minutes until the onion is softened and the mushrooms have released moisture. Add garlic and curry powder; stir for 30–45 seconds until fragrant. Lower the heat if the spices begin sticking or darkening.

4. **Build the sauce:** Stir in coconut milk and honey, scraping the pan. Bring to a gentle simmer, add zucchini and cook about 4–6 minutes, until tender with a little bite. Keep the sauce fluid; add a little water if it reduces too far.

5. **Finish:** Return shrimp and their juices only long enough to warm through, about 30–60 seconds. Turn off the heat. Stir in basil and half the lime juice. Taste with a piece of vegetable, then add remaining lime, salt, pepper and optional hot sauce as needed.

6. **Serve:** Spoon over separately prepared rice and divide shrimp evenly among the bowls. Plain [Basmati Rice](/mise/recipes/basmati-rice) balances the rich sauce; coconut rice makes a richer meal.
