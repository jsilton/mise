## Chef's Note

This spring pasta gets its creaminess from ricotta, Parmesan and starchy pasta water, with no cream at all. Crisp pancetta goes on top so it stays crunchy, and lemon keeps the sauce bright. Take the pan off the heat before the cheese goes in, so the ricotta stays smooth instead of turning grainy.

## Directions

1. **Boil the water:** Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil for the pasta.
2. **Render the pancetta:** Put the pancetta in a cold 12-inch skillet and set it over medium heat. Cook, stirring now and then, until the fat has melted out and the pancetta is crisp and golden, 8 to 10 minutes. Lift it onto paper towels with a slotted spoon. Pour off all but a thin coating of fat.
3. **Soften the aromatics:** Lower the heat to medium-low. Add the shallot and cook until soft and translucent, 2 to 3 minutes. Add the garlic and stir until fragrant, about 30 seconds, without letting it brown.
4. **Reduce the wine:** Pour in the wine and scrape up the browned bits. Simmer until reduced by half, about 2 minutes, then turn off the heat.
5. **Cook the pasta and peas:** Cook the orecchiette until al dente, adding the peas for the last 2 minutes. Scoop out the pasta water, then drain.
6. **Toss off the heat:** Add the pasta and peas to the skillet and toss in the pan sauce. With the skillet off the heat and no longer sizzling, add the ricotta, Parmesan, lemon zest, lemon juice and a good splash of pasta water. Toss hard until creamy and glossy, adding more pasta water as needed.
7. **Season and serve:** Season with black pepper and taste for salt, since the pancetta and cheese are salty. Serve topped with the crisp pancetta.
