## Chef's Note

These wontons pair a springy pork filling with distinct pieces of shrimp and the crunch of water chestnuts. Stir the cold, seasoned meat until it becomes sticky and holds together, then fold in the chunky ingredients so they keep their texture. Finely minced celery or Fuji apple can replace the water chestnuts; celery adds a fresh herbal note, while apple makes the filling a little sweeter.

Plan on 60–90 minutes for mixing, wrapping, and cooking the batch, depending on your wrapping speed and pot capacity. Wrapper size and filling scoops determine the final count. Divide the filling among the listed portions, and have extra wrappers available rather than relying on an unspecified package size.

## Directions

Work with small batches: keep unused filling and waiting uncooked wontons refrigerated throughout mixing, wrapping, and cooking. Use clean utensils for serving.

1.  **The Paste:** In a large bowl, combine the pork, the **finely chopped** half of the shrimp, ginger, garlic, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sesame oil, sugar, white pepper, salt, and egg.
2.  **The Texture:** Stir the mixture **vigorously in one direction** for 3-5 minutes until it becomes pale, sticky, and forms a cohesive paste. Do not skip this!
3.  **Add Texture:** Fold in the water chestnuts (or the finely minced celery or Fuji apple alternative), green onions, and the remaining **shrimp chunks**.
4.  **The Test:** Cook a small flattened portion of filling in a small nonstick skillet until the center reaches 165°F / 74°C. Taste only after it is fully cooked, then adjust the remaining filling with a clean utensil if needed.
5.  **Assemble:** Place 1 tsp of filling in a wrapper. Moisten edges, fold into a triangle, and bring the two side corners together to form a "nurse's cap." Press out all air.

6.  **Cook:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add a batch of wontons with enough space to move freely and stir gently to keep them from sticking. Once the water returns to a boil, reduce the heat to maintain a gentle boil. Fresh wontons of this size often take about 4–5 minutes, but check several larger ones with a thin thermometer and continue cooking until the center reaches 165°F / 74°C; floating alone does not establish doneness. Lift out with a slotted spoon and serve promptly in hot broth or with dipping sauce. Repeat with the remaining batches.
7.  **Freeze Extras:** Freeze uncooked wontons promptly in a single layer, then transfer them to a freezer container once firm.
