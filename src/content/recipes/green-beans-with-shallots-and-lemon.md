## Chef's Note

Cook the beans to a texture you enjoy, then dress them. Shallots bring a gentle sweetness; a teaspoon of optional honey is enough to soften the lemon edge without turning this into candied vegetables. Lemon goes in at the end because prolonged heating with acid dulls green color. An ice bath slows further cooking when holding beans ahead, but does not permanently protect their color or texture.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the vegetables and water:** Trim the beans, rinse and drain. Dice the shallots, zest the lemon and measure its juice. Bring two liters of water with ten grams salt to a boil in a 4-quart pot. Begin the shallots in step three while the water heats if you can watch both pans.
2. **Cook to tenderness:** Add beans and return to a gentle boil. Begin tasting after three minutes; many beans need four to six minutes, and very thick ones can need longer. A cooled piece should bite cleanly with a little resistance. Drain thoroughly. For immediate serving, keep them warm and move straight to the skillet finish; do not chill them first.
3. **Soften shallots:** Melt butter in a 12-inch skillet over medium-low heat. Add shallots and cook five to seven minutes, stirring, until softened and translucent. Lower heat if they begin browning sharply. Use a small splash from the listed finishing water if needed to help them soften.
4. **Warm and coat:** Add the drained beans and optional teaspoon of honey. Toss gently over medium heat for two to three minutes until hot and lightly coated. If the pan is dry, add a little of the remaining finishing water. For refrigerated prepared-ahead beans, allow about four to six minutes, cover briefly with the water as needed, and check they reach 165°F / 74°C before proceeding; then uncover so excess water evaporates.
5. **Finish off heat:** Remove from heat. Add lemon zest, the first tablespoon of juice and pepper. Toss, taste a bean and add the optional finishing salt or extra teaspoon of juice only if needed. Divide among four plates and serve promptly.

## Preparing ahead

After step two, chill beans in a bowl of ice water until cold, then drain thoroughly and refrigerate as described above. This makes the final shallot-and-bean step easier to coordinate with a roast, at the cost of another heating cycle. Use fresh lemon at service. Do not leave a bowl of cooked beans at room temperature for the afternoon.
