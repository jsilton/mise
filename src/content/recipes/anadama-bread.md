## Chef's Note

Molasses and a cooked cornmeal porridge give this New England bread its distinctive flavor and texture. Cool the porridge before adding it to the yeast, and let both rises reach their volume cues. A cooled loaf slices more cleanly than one cut straight from the oven.

## Directions

1. **Cook the porridge:** In a small saucepan, whisk the first listed water portion and cornmeal. Bring to a boil over medium heat, stirring, and cook for about 5 minutes until thickened. Remove from the heat.

2. **Cool:** Stir in the measured porridge butter and molasses. Let the mixture cool until lukewarm before it meets the yeast.

3. **Activate the yeast:** In a large bowl, dissolve the active dry yeast in the listed warm water at 110°F. Let stand for about 10 minutes; look for a creamy, foamy surface.

4. **Mix the dough:** Stir the cooled cornmeal mixture into the yeast mixture. Mix in two thirds of the measured flour and all the salt, then gradually add the remaining measured flour until a shaggy dough forms.

5. **Knead:** Turn out onto a lightly floured surface. Knead for about 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic, using only enough dusting flour to keep it manageable. The dough should remain slightly tacky.

6. **First rise:** Lightly oil a bowl, place the dough inside and turn to coat it. Cover and leave in a warm spot until doubled in volume, about 1 hour.

7. **Shape and heat the oven:** Deflate and shape the dough. The original batch makes one loaf in a butter-greased 9 x 5-inch loaf pan. For larger batches, divide into original-size loaves in separate pans. For a smaller or partial loaf, choose a suitable smaller pan with similar dough depth and room to rise, and begin bake checks earlier. Begin preheating the oven to 375°F while the loaves rise.

8. **Second rise:** Cover and let the loaf double again, about 45 minutes. Wait until the oven has reached 375°F before loading.

9. **Bake and cool:** For original-size loaves, begin checking around 30 minutes. Bake until the crust is deep golden and firm, the sides are baked and the bottom sounds hollow when tapped. Carefully turn the loaves out onto a rack and let them cool completely before slicing.
