## Chef's Note

Anadama is an old New England loaf made with cornmeal and molasses. Cooking the cornmeal into a porridge first keeps the crumb soft and moist instead of gritty, and the molasses gives it a deep, bittersweet flavor. It makes wonderful toast with salted butter.

## Directions

1. **Cook the porridge:** In a small saucepan, whisk the cornmeal into the water for the porridge. Bring to a boil over medium heat and cook, stirring constantly, until thick, about 5 minutes. Take it off the heat, stir in the butter and molasses, and let cool until lukewarm, about 20 minutes.
2. **Proof the yeast:** In a large bowl, sprinkle the yeast over the warm water. Let it stand until creamy and foamy, about 10 minutes.
3. **Mix the dough:** Stir the cooled porridge into the yeast. Mix in two-thirds of the flour and the salt, then add the rest of the flour a little at a time until a shaggy dough forms.
4. **Knead:** Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface. Knead until smooth and elastic but still slightly tacky, 8 to 10 minutes.
5. **First rise:** Put the dough in an oiled bowl and turn it to coat. Cover and let rise in a warm spot until doubled, about 1 hour.
6. **Shape:** Butter a 9x5-inch loaf pan. Punch down the dough, shape it into a loaf and set it in the pan seam side down.
7. **Second rise:** Cover and let rise until doubled again, about 45 minutes. Heat the oven to 375°F while it rises.
8. **Bake:** Bake until the crust is deep golden brown and the bottom sounds hollow when tapped, about 30 minutes. The center should read 190°F.
9. **Cool:** Turn the loaf out onto a rack and cool completely before slicing, about 1 hour.
