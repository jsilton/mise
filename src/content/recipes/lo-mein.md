## Chef's Note

Lo mein means "scooped noodles": the noodles are boiled first, then folded through the wok with sauce at the very end. Scallion-shallot oil gives this Cantonese-style version its fragrant base. Cooking the finish in two rounds keeps a home burner hot enough to glaze the noodles instead of steaming them.

## Directions

1. **Coat the chicken:** Toss the chicken with the cornstarch, neutral oil, soy sauce and white pepper for the chicken. Set it aside while you prepare everything else.
2. **Mix the sauce:** Whisk the light and dark soy sauces, oyster sauce, broth, sugar and white pepper in a small bowl. Divide it into two portions.
3. **Boil the noodles:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Cook the noodles until springy but still a little firm, about 1 minute less than the package says. Rinse briefly under cold water, shake very dry and toss with a spoonful of the scallion-shallot oil. Leave them at room temperature.
4. **Sear the chicken:** Heat a wok over high heat until it just smokes. Add a little of the scallion-shallot oil and spread half the chicken in one layer. Leave it for 1 minute to brown, then stir-fry until cooked through, 2 to 3 minutes. Move it to a plate and repeat with a little more oil and the rest of the chicken.
5. **Cook the vegetables:** Add a little more oil and half the mushrooms. Leave them for 2 minutes, then toss until browned, 1 to 2 minutes more. Add half the garlic, ginger and scallion whites for 20 seconds and splash half the wine around the edge of the wok. Add half the bok choy stems and stir-fry for 90 seconds, then half the leaves until just wilted, about 30 seconds.
6. **Toss the noodles:** Add half the noodles and pour one portion of sauce around the edge of the wok. Scoop under the noodles and turn them over, scraping the bottom, until evenly bronze, 1 to 2 minutes. Add a splash of hot water if they clump. Add half the chicken, sprouts and scallion greens and toss until the sprouts are hot, about 30 seconds.
7. **Finish the first batch:** Turn off the heat and fold in half the sesame oil and vinegar. Move the noodles to a warm platter.
8. **Cook the second batch:** Repeat the vegetable, noodle and finishing steps with the rest of the oil and ingredients. Top both platters with fried shallots and serve right away.
