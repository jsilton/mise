## Chef's Note

Honey, hoisin, molasses, ginger, garlic and chile give these ribs a full sweet-savory glaze. For the Cuisinart CPC-600, drain the thick marinade before pressure cooking the ribs above a separate thin liquid, then boil and reduce every bit of marinade with the cooking juices. Broiling concentrates the sticky coating; check tenderness before that dry finish.

## Directions

1. **Blend the marinade:** Use hoisin sauce, soy sauce, unsulfured molasses, fresh ginger, garlic cloves, sambal oelek or Chinese chile-garlic sauce, and 2/3 of the honey. Set aside the remaining one third of the listed honey in a clean container for the broiler finish. Blend the hoisin, soy, molasses, ginger, garlic and marinade honey until smooth, then pulse in the chile sauce so it is fully included.
2. **Marinate under refrigeration:** Put pork spareribs or baby back ribs in a covered food-safe container, add all the blended marinade and refrigerate for 45 minutes, turning occasionally. Ask the butcher to halve the racks lengthwise if needed to make the sections fit safely in the cooker. The rib size and thickness affect tenderness.
3. **Keep the pressure liquid thin:** Use a Cuisinart CPC-600 with a suitable pressure-cooker rack that holds the ribs above the liquid. Drain the ribs thoroughly over the marinade container, scrape off excess thick sauce, and keep all this raw-contact marinade covered in the refrigerator until the reduction stage. Add water to the clean cooker pot, place the rack over it and arrange the drained ribs on the rack. Do not pour the thick marinade into the pressure liquid. Keep food and liquid at or below 60% of the pot’s capacity and never above its marked limit; use additional complete loads if needed. The 1-cup water allowance applies separately to every load and does not scale down with the recipe. For more than one load, keep waiting raw ribs covered in the refrigerator and repeat the pressure-cook, full-release and tenderness stages for every load, with at least 1 cup fresh plain water each time. Retain all cooking juices for the sauce.
4. **Pressure-cook the ribs:** Fit the lid, lock it, set the pressure-limit valve to the closed • position and select High Pressure with MENU. Set TIME to 25 minutes and press START/CANCEL. This is a starting cook; the countdown begins only once pressure has built. Check tenderness after full natural release.
5. **Release fully naturally:** When the timer finishes, leave the CPC-600 on its automatic Keep Warm setting while pressure falls fully naturally. Wait until the red float is completely down and the lid unlocks; this commonly takes 12–30 minutes and can take longer. Then press START/CANCEL to turn off and open the lid away from your face. Do not force the lid or release steam manually for this recipe.
6. **Check tenderness before glazing:** Test a meaty section away from the bone. The meat should be easy to pierce and the bones should move readily; the pressure timer alone does not promise this. If still firm, remove the rack, return ribs to the cooking liquid and select Simmer with the lid off, turning occasionally, until tender. Keep liquid in the pot during this finishing stage, adding water only as needed to prevent it from drying out. Check the meat reaches at least 145°F / 63°C, away from bone. Transfer tender ribs to a foil-lined rimmed baking sheet and retain all cooking juices. While subsequent loads cook, keep the completed ribs loosely covered in a warm oven and the cooking juices covered on gentle heat, both at 140°F / 60°C or warmer; check with the thermometer. Retain the juices for the full sauce.
7. **Cook and reduce the full sauce:** Pour all the cooker juices and all reserved raw-contact marinade into a saucepan. Bring the entire sauce to a full boil, stirring, and keep it boiling for several minutes before it touches cooked meat or is served. Then reduce, stirring frequently, until glossy and thick enough to coat a spoon; use its consistency rather than a fixed final cup volume. All the hoisin, soy, molasses, ginger, garlic, chile and marinade honey stay in this sauce. Extra cooker loads add liquid and can extend reduction.
8. **Brush the ribs:** Preheat the broiler and position the rack about 6–8 inches from its heat, as the broiler permits. Use 1/3 of the honey. Brush the ribs with the fully boiled reduced sauce and the reserved clean honey. Keep serving sauce separate from the brush and working glaze. The original serving alternative is to brush with the reserved honey alone and serve all the cooked hoisin sauce alongside.
9. **Broil and serve:** Broil until the coating bubbles and browns at the edges, starting checks at 3 minutes; about 3–5 minutes is a guide, and different broilers vary. Turn once carefully and watch closely so the honey browns without burning. Confirm at least 145°F / 63°C in a meaty section away from bone, then rest at least 3 minutes before cutting into individual ribs and serving with the clean serving sauce.

## Cooking Notes

Grated ginger distributes through the sauce. For a coarsely chopped ginger preparation, blend it until smooth and use 3½ tbsp in the original 4-lb rib batch, adjusting that proportion at other scales. Baby back ribs and Chinese chile-garlic sauce are alternatives. For a honey-only coating, brush the ribs with the reserved honey and serve the fully cooked hoisin sauce alongside.
