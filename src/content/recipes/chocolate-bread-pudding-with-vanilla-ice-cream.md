## Chef's Note

Brioche absorbs a chocolate custard while bittersweet and white chocolate leave contrasting pockets in the crumb. Weigh the bread, let every cube soak through, and bake each center gently. Serve the warm pudding with cold vanilla ice cream.

## Directions

1. **Choose dishes with room for the full batch:** Use oven-safe individual soufflé cups with at least 12 fluid ounces (1½ cups) capacity each. Coat them with nonstick cooking spray and set them on a rimmed baking sheet. Use enough cups for the whole batch. Fill no cup above three-quarters of its capacity after soaking, and have an extra cup or small dish ready if needed. For a larger pudding, use a coated 2-quart baking dish only if the complete soaked mixture fits with space below its rim; use an extra dish for any excess.
2. **Combine the bread and chocolate pieces:** In a large bowl, toss brioche, 2/3 of the bittersweet chocolate (70% cacao), and white chocolate chips until the chocolate pieces are distributed through the brioche. The remaining third of the dark chocolate belongs to the chocolate custard.
3. **Make the chocolate cream:** In a saucepan over low heat, whisk 1/3 of the bittersweet chocolate (70% cacao), heavy cream, unsweetened cocoa powder, and granulated sugar until the chocolate has melted and the mixture is smooth. Stir frequently, scraping the bottom; do not scorch or boil the cream.
4. **Cool the chocolate mixture:** Transfer the chocolate cream to a large bowl. Whisk in whole milk until smooth; let it cool to lukewarm before adding the eggs.
5. **Add eggs and vanilla:** In a second bowl, whisk large eggs, fine salt, and vanilla extract just until blended, then whisk in the lukewarm chocolate dairy mixture until uniform. Use the full listed cream and milk in this custard.
6. **Soak the brioche fully:** Pour the complete chocolate custard over the bread mixture and fold gently to wet every cube. Let stand for about 30 minutes, folding once or twice, until the bread has absorbed custard and no dry centers remain. Keep total room-temperature handling of the raw egg mixture within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F; cover and refrigerate if the wait will extend. Heat the oven to 325°F during the soak.
7. **Divide the full mixture:** Stir gently before each scoop so every dish gets bread, chocolate and custard. Divide the entire mixture among the prepared cups, keeping the stated headroom. If it will not fit the cups, use the prepared extra dish. A larger-dish batch follows the same complete ingredient allocation; do not discard custard or chocolate to make a vessel fit.
8. **Bake by each center:** Bake cups at 325°F, starting the first center checks around 30 minutes; roughly 35–60 minutes is a planning range for these roomy cups. For the 2-quart dish, start checking around 45 minutes and plan roughly 45–60 minutes, continuing as needed. The middle must be set with no liquid custard wave and reach at least 160°F in the egg custard, away from a melted chocolate pocket and the dish. A moist bread crumb and melted chocolate can look wet, so use both the texture and the temperature. Check each dish independently and shield a darkening top loosely with foil.
9. **Rest and serve warm:** Rest on a rack for 10 minutes so the custard settles, then serve warm with vanilla bean ice cream, shared across the batch. Refrigerate leftover pudding within 2 hours of cooking, or 1 hour above 90°F; keep the ice cream in the freezer.
