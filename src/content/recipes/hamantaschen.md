## Chef's Note

Hamantaschen are triangular Jewish cookies for Purim. Keep the butter dough cool enough to handle, and give each flap one over-and-one-under corner. The 3-inch circle has room for no more than a teaspoon of filling. Pinching and repairing cracks improve the fold, but filling consistency and dough thickness still affect leakage.

## Directions

1. **Cream:** Beat unsalted butter and granulated sugar until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes.
2. **Add egg and flavorings:** Beat in large egg, pure vanilla extract, and orange zest until evenly combined, scraping the bowl.
3. **Mix the dough:** Sift in all-purpose flour and sea salt and mix on low until a crumbly dough forms.
4. **Bring the dough together:** Have water (if using) and all-purpose flour (if using) available only if needed. Knead the crumbles gently into a smooth, slightly tacky ball. Add the listed water slowly, a little at a time, only if the dough is too dry to join; in the original batch use 1–5 tsp total as needed, adding about 1 tsp at a time. Scale that total range with the dough, but continue adding small increments by feel. If it becomes sticky, knead in a little of the optional flour. Stop once the dough holds together and can be rolled; avoid overworking it.
5. **Chill:** Flatten into a disk, wrap and refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below for at least 3 hours or overnight.
6. **Prepare to assemble:** Choose and prepare the filling before rolling; follow its own recipe or product instructions. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Use all-purpose flour and butter or baking spray for handling: lightly flour the work surface and rolling pin, then lightly grease the sheets with the butter or spray.
7. **Roll and cut:** Roll to the main ⅛-inch thickness. If needed, begin around ¼ inch, lift the dough with a pastry scraper, lightly re-flour and flip it, then finish rolling. Repair cracks with your fingers. Cut 3-inch circles and gather and re-roll scraps gently until all usable dough is portioned; avoid adding unnecessary flour. Keep unused circles covered with a lightly damp towel while filling. The listed cookie count is a planning value; thickness changes yield. Larger batches need more sheets and oven loads, with the same circle size and thickness.
8. **Fill and fold:** Use apricot jam, poppy seed paste, or chocolate-hazelnut spread, placing no more than 1 tsp in each 3-inch circle. Fold the left side toward the center; fold the right side so its upper part overlaps the left flap. Fold the bottom up with its left corner under the left flap and its right corner over the right flap. Each flap then has one corner over and one under, leaving a small center of filling visible. Pinch the corners gently but firmly and repair cracks; Keep the filling away from the seams and make each fold secure.
9. **Bake:** Space the cookies on the prepared sheets and wait for the oven to reach 350°F. The main route is 20–25 minutes per oven load, until cooked through and lightly golden. Begin checking at 10 minutes when cookies are thin: thickness and filling can make them finish sooner. Keep unbaked dough refrigerated between loads.
10. **Cool and store:** Cool completely on a wire rack before storing airtight. Follow the selected filling’s storage requirements; refrigerate cookies with perishable filling at 40°F / 4°C or below promptly, within 2 hours or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C.

## Cooking Options

### Rolling thickness

The main route uses ⅛-inch dough. Tori Avey’s thinner, more delicate option rolls just under ⅛ inch while keeping the dough strong enough to hold the filling. Her thicker, more doughy option stays closer to ¼ inch and makes fewer cookies from the same dough. Keep the 3-inch cutter and the one-teaspoon maximum filling per circle for either option; do not force the listed count by changing thickness. Check thin cookies from 10 minutes and continue only until cooked through and lightly golden.

Apricot jam, poppy seed paste and chocolate-hazelnut spread are separate filling choices, not three additions to each cookie. Their preparation, total quantity and storage requirements depend on the selected filling; a homemade filling needs its own complete recipe.
