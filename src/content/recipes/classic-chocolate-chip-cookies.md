## Chef's Note

Brown the starting butter, including its milk solids, then let it return to a soft solid before creaming. Fold in the already toasted sunflower seeds and semisweet chocolate after the flour, and let the soft centers firm on the sheet before moving the cookies to a rack.

## Directions

1. **Brown and cool the butter:** Choose a saucepan with room for the butter to foam; use complete smaller butter loads and combine all their contents if needed for a larger batch. Melt unsalted butter over medium heat. Swirl and watch the milk solids beneath the foam; when they are deep golden and smell toasted, pour all the butter and browned solids into a mixing bowl. Cool until solid but still soft enough to beat. Use the entire browned amount and its solids.
2. **Cream:** Beat the cooled brown butter with light brown sugar and granulated white sugar until pale and fluffy, about 3–5 minutes. Scrape the bowl so the browned solids are distributed.
3. **Add eggs and vanilla:** Beat in large eggs and vanilla extract, adding the eggs one at a time and mixing until combined.
4. **Add soda and salt:** Use baking soda, hot water, and kosher salt: stir the baking soda with the listed hot water, then mix it and the salt into the butter mixture.
5. **Finish the dough:** Stir in all-purpose flour, semisweet chocolate chips, and sunflower seeds until no dry flour remains and the chips and seeds are evenly distributed. The seeds are already toasted.
6. **Chill:** Cover the dough and refrigerate promptly at 40°F / 4°C or below overnight. Plan to portion and bake within 24 hours of mixing; keep waiting dough refrigerated between oven loads.
7. **Prepare to bake:** After the chill, preheat the oven to 350°F. Line baking sheets with parchment. Portion the dough into evenly sized scoops, allowing about 2 inches between them. The listed count is an approximate cookie count; scoop size determines how many you get. Use additional sheets and oven loads for a larger batch, keeping scoop size and spacing consistent. Wait for the oven to reach temperature before loading.
8. **Bake:** Bake for 10–12 minutes per oven load, until the edges are golden and the centers have set but remain soft. Keep unbaked dough refrigerated between loads.
9. **Finish and cool:** Sprinkle flaky sea salt over the cookies immediately after baking. Leave them on the sheet until firm enough to lift without breaking, then transfer to a rack to cool completely before packing.

## Cooking Options

### Plain-butter walnut option

For the original 24-cookie planning batch, replace the entire dough formula with 1 cup softened butter, 1 cup white sugar, 1 cup packed brown sugar, 2 eggs, 2 tsp vanilla, 1 tsp baking soda dissolved in 2 tsp hot water, ½ tsp salt, 3 cups all-purpose flour, 2 cups semisweet chocolate chips and 1 cup chopped walnuts. Scale this complete formula proportionally as a separate batch; do not combine its flour or water with the brown-butter dough. This option contains nuts.

Preheat to 350°F before mixing. Cream the butter and sugars, beat in the eggs one at a time and the vanilla, mix in the dissolved soda and salt, then stir in the flour, chips and walnuts. Drop large spoonfuls onto ungreased sheets and bake about 10 minutes, until the edges are lightly browned and centers are set. Let the cookies firm on the sheet, then cool on a rack. This route does not require the brown-butter route’s overnight rest. Its count depends on spoon size.

For this plain-butter option, fully cooled cookies or portioned dough can be frozen for up to two months as a quality plan: first freeze them separately on a covered tray until firm, then package airtight. Thaw frozen dough in the refrigerator before baking. The different dough does not establish an identical storage life or baking result for the brown-butter version.
