## Chef's Note

This smaller holiday turkey keeps the original lemon, garlic, herbs and cider, with a measured dry brine instead of a bucket of liquid. Salt seasons and changes how meat proteins retain water; it cannot protect a breast from unlimited cooking. Lemon zest and herbs chiefly perfume the surface and drippings, rather than seasoning every part of the bird to the bone.

Keeping the skin dry helps it brown. The cider is a pan ingredient: manage its evaporation and taste the resulting drippings before using them. It does not make the meat automatically succulent or provide a finished gravy by itself.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the thawed bird:** Remove packaging, neck and giblet bags, checking both cavities. Reserve those parts separately under appropriate refrigerated storage or discard. Pat the turkey dry with disposable towels; do not wash it in the sink. Put breast-side up on a rack over a deep rimmed tray or roasting pan that fits in the refrigerator.
2. **Season twenty-four hours ahead:** Mix the weighed salt with black pepper and the lemon zest. Distribute it evenly over the bird, using some directly over the breast and upper leg meat under gently loosened skin; avoid tearing the skin or depositing the mixture in one pocket. Refrigerate uncovered for twenty-four hours at 40°F / 4°C or below, protected from contact with other foods. Refrigerate the zested lemon separately for the pan. Do not rinse off the seasoning before roasting.
3. **Prepare the oven and pan:** Heat the oven to 325°F / 163°C with space for the turkey on a lower rack. Keep the bird refrigerated while the oven heats. Put garlic, onion, lemon wedges, thyme, rosemary and sage in the roasting pan beneath the rack. Add the cider, wine and initial cup of water. Set the turkey on the rack breast-side up, keep its cavity empty, tuck the wing tips underneath and brush the skin with all the melted butter in a thin coat. Use a clean brush; no repeated basting is needed.
4. **Roast and manage the drippings:** Roast at 325°F / 163°C. Begin checking the pan after about an hour, then periodically; carefully add a little of the listed hot water before the bottom dries out. Keep a shallow layer rather than submerging the bird. If skin colors strongly before the meat is ready, shield that area loosely with foil. Do not repeatedly open the oven just to wet the skin.
5. **Measure every required site:** Begin temperature checks after about 2 1/2 hours; plan roughly 2 3/4–3 1/4 hours total roasting, with additional time possible. A leave-in breast probe helps track progress, but use an instant-read thermometer to check the thickest part of each breast, the innermost part of each thigh and the innermost wing areas, clear of bone. Continue roasting until all reach at least 165°F / 74°C. Check again in a nearby spot if a reading seems implausible. A single thigh reading, clear juices and browned skin are insufficient.
6. **Rest and carve:** Move the bird to a clean carving board and rest thirty minutes, loosely tented if needed. Carve breasts from the bone and slice across the grain, then separate legs and thighs. Divide among about ten diners with sides; this is not a promise of ten identical meat weights or abundant leftovers. Strain the pan liquid, discard spent aromatics and taste before deciding whether to use it in a separate gravy. Do not serve burned or excessively salty pan deposits.

## Choosing between turkey recipes

This dry-brined 10–12 lb bird uses less refrigerator volume than the liquid-brined [Good Eats-inspired turkey](/mise/recipes/good-eats-roast-turkey), whose 14–16 lb bird and aromatic brine have their own schedule. The [roast turkey breast](/mise/recipes/roast-turkey-breast) is a separate smaller-cut option. Choose for actual bird size and available equipment; these are meaningful variations rather than interchangeable serving multipliers.
