## Chef's Note

This Sichuan-style chicken and eggplant uses spicy fermented broad-bean paste and Chinkiang black vinegar for a savory, tangy glaze. Brief covered cooking makes a full load of eggplant silky; uncovering then evaporates extra moisture before the sauce goes in. Cook the vegetables and chicken separately in the same pan, then fold them together gently.

## Directions

1. **Salt the eggplant:** Toss Chinese eggplant and kosher salt in a colander and leave for 15 minutes. Begin the chicken and sauce during this rest.
2. **Marinate the chicken:** Mix boneless skinless chicken thighs, light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch, toasted sesame oil, and ground white pepper until evenly coated and refrigerate for at least 15 minutes while finishing the prep. Keep the raw-chicken board, bowl and utensils separate from cooked food; wash hands after handling raw chicken.
3. **Prepare the scallions:** Thinly slice medium scallions, keeping the white and light-green parts separate from the dark-green tops. Set both portions aside for cooking and finishing respectively.
4. **Mix the sauce and finish prep:** Whisk Chinkiang black vinegar, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, granulated sugar, low-sodium chicken broth or water, and cornstarch in a bowl until the sugar dissolves and the starch is evenly suspended. After its rest, briefly rinse the eggplant, drain and pat thoroughly dry with clean towels. Do not coat the eggplant in cornstarch for this covered-cooking method. Have all aromatics chopped before heating the pan.
5. **Color all the eggplant in one load:** Heat the roomy wok or deep skillet over medium-high heat and add neutral high-heat cooking oil. Add all the dried eggplant at once and turn to distribute the oil. Cook for 3–4 minutes, turning occasionally so some surfaces take on color.
6. **Cover until silky, then uncover:** Have water and extra water ready. Reduce to medium heat, carefully add the measured water down the side of the pan and cover immediately. Steam for 4–6 minutes, checking once and turning gently. Add extra water only if the pan is dry before the eggplant is tender. Uncover and cook for 2–4 minutes more to evaporate remaining water. A knife should slide through the centers easily; the flesh should be silky, not firm or spongy. Allow extra time if needed. Transfer all the eggplant to a clean plate. Expect some browned patches rather than uniformly crisp pieces.
7. **Cook all the chicken in one load:** Have neutral high-heat cooking oil and water ready. Let residual water in the pan evaporate, add the measured oil over medium-high heat, then add all the chicken at once, spreading it as widely as possible. Leave for about 2 minutes, then turn and separate the strips. Continue uncovered, stirring every minute or so, until several of the thickest strips reach 165°F / 74°C, usually 8–12 minutes total; insert the thermometer sideways into their centers. If liquid collects, keep cooking uncovered. Reduce heat if the marinade catches, using a little of the water only if needed. Transfer the fully cooked chicken and its juices to a second clean plate. Use clean utensils for the finish.
8. **Bloom the chili-bean paste:** Remove any scorched residue from the pan and evaporate residual liquid. Reduce to medium heat and let the pan cool briefly if smoking. Add neutral high-heat cooking oil and Pixian-style spicy doubanjiang, warming the oil before stirring in the paste. Stir gently for 20–30 seconds until fragrant and the oil takes on a red tint; lower the heat before the paste scorches.
9. **Bloom the aromatics:** Add the prepared scallion whites and light-green parts with garlic cloves, fresh ginger, and crushed red pepper flakes (if using). Stir for 20–30 seconds until fragrant, lowering the heat if the garlic darkens rapidly.
10. **Glaze gently:** Have Chinkiang black vinegar (if using), light soy sauce (if using), and water ready. Whisk the prepared sauce again to lift settled starch and pour it into the pan. Bring to a simmer over medium-high heat, stirring, until glossy and slightly thickened, about 30–60 seconds. Return the fully cooked chicken and its juices and stir to coat. Fold in all the eggplant gently and heat through, about 1–2 minutes. Add water only if the glaze becomes too thick; it should coat lightly. Taste first, then add a little of the optional soy sauce if needed for salt and a little of the optional black vinegar if needed for brightness, staying within the listed allowances. Avoid vigorous tossing that crushes the eggplant.
11. **Serve:** Turn off the heat and fold in the reserved dark-green scallion tops. Serve immediately with cooked steamed rice.
