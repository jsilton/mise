## Chef's Note

This Southern-inspired home-style chicken keeps the buttermilk soak and bakes a cornflake-and-Parmesan coating instead of deep-frying. Cultured buttermilk supplies tang and a wet layer for the crumbs to grip; the rack raises the chicken above its drippings. Aim for a crisp crumb coating, and do not expect the covered skin beneath it to resemble bare roast-chicken skin.

## Directions

1. **Marinate ahead:** Whisk buttermilk, hot sauce, lemon juice and the twelve grams salt in a food-safe container; add onion, smashed garlic and thyme sprigs. Add the chicken without rinsing it. Use a snug covered container or a food-safe resealable bag set in a dish to keep the marinade against the pieces. Refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below for three to twelve hours, turning the closed bag or pieces once if needed for coverage. Wash hands and clean raw-contact surfaces.
2. **Set up the oven:** Near the end of the soak, heat the oven to 400°F / 205°C with a shelf in the center. Line an 18-by-13-inch rimmed sheet pan with foil and place an oven-safe rack on top. Brush the rack with one teaspoon of the measured oil. Check the usable rack area before coating; have a second pan and oven-safe rack ready if all pieces cannot fit with the stated gaps. If using two pans, place shelves in the upper and lower thirds and plan to swap their positions halfway through baking.
3. **Prepare the crumbs:** Crush the weighed cornflakes in a bag with a rolling pin until mostly small crumbs, with some texture remaining. Mix in the remaining five teaspoons oil, rubbing lightly to distribute it, then add Parmesan, chopped thyme and pepper. Put half in a shallow dish and keep the other half in a clean bowl to add as needed. The marinade, cereal and cheese supply salt; do not automatically salt the coating as well.
4. **Coat and arrange:** Lift chicken from the marinade and remove clinging onion, garlic and thyme stems. Let excess buttermilk drip away, leaving a thin film. Press each piece into the crumb mixture on all sides, adding reserved crumbs to the dish as needed. Press on one thin, even coat and leave excess crumbs in the dish; do not heap them on just to use them up. Place skin-side up on the rack with roughly an inch between pieces. Use a second pan if they would touch. Discard remaining raw-contact marinade, aromatics and crumbs; wash hands and utensils before handling side dishes or cooked food.
5. **Bake and check each piece:** Bake without turning the chicken. After thirty minutes, check the breasts, wings and legs individually with a thermometer in the thickest meat, avoiding bone; check more than one spot in large breast halves. Continue baking and recheck as needed until every piece reaches at least 165°F / 74°C, commonly thirty-five to fifty-five minutes total. Remove finished pieces to a clean rack over a plate while the others continue. Thighs and drumsticks can stay longer if you prefer softer dark meat, but do not keep finished breasts in the oven for them. If the crust darkens too quickly, loosely shield that piece with foil. With two pans, swap their shelf positions and rotate front to back around halfway, allowing additional time if needed.
6. **Rest and portion:** Let the final pieces rest for five minutes, uncovered so steam can escape. Divide the meat among four diners, sharing breast meat and distributing thighs, drumsticks and wings by appetite; two pieces are not automatically equal portions. Serve the chicken beside the vegetables and mash rather than burying its crust under them. Mixed pieces may finish twenty minutes or more apart; a clean uncovered rack protects the crust but does not keep them hot indefinitely. Have the sides ready near the chicken finish. For closer finish times, use similarly sized bone-in thighs as described in the substitution.
