## Chef's Note

A whole cauliflower heats much more slowly than small florets. Give the center a head start under a cover, then dry and roast the outside. The spiced oil goes on after the covered stage so it spends less time exposed to intense dry heat. Browning adds flavor, but the knife test tells you whether the core is ready to eat.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Heat the oven to 425°F / 220°C. Remove cauliflower leaves and trim the base so it sits flat, leaving enough core to hold the head together. Cut a shallow cross about 1 inch deep into the exposed stem. Wash and drain. Rub with 1 tbsp oil and half the measured salt.
2. **Cook covered:** Place stem-side down in a baking dish. Add the hot water around it and cover the dish tightly with foil. Bake about 30–40 minutes, until a knife pushed toward the center meets some resistance but the head has begun to soften. Open foil away from your face to release steam.
3. **Dry and season:** Lift the head carefully onto a rimmed sheet pan and let surface steam escape for a few minutes. Mix the remaining 2 tbsp oil with cumin, paprika, turmeric, remaining salt, and pepper. Brush over the cauliflower.
4. **Roast uncovered:** Return to the oven for about 30–40 minutes, turning the pan once, until the florets are golden brown and a knife reaches the core with little resistance. Extend as needed; cover loosely if the surface darkens before the center is tender. Meanwhile, prepare tahini sauce and measure 1/2 cup for serving.
5. **Finish:** Rest for 5 minutes, then move to a platter. Drizzle with the measured tahini sauce and add parsley, optional pomegranate seeds, and optional Aleppo pepper. Cut into four wedges at the table, or portion before serving.
