## Chef's Note

Monique Volz’s carrot muffins use finely shredded carrots with maple syrup and applesauce, then a cream-cheese glaze. Fine shreds distribute through the batter without leaving large pieces. Stop stirring when the flour disappears, and let the muffins cool completely so the glaze stays on their tops.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 350°F. For the original batch, line a 12-cup muffin tin and coat the insides of the liners with nonstick cooking spray, or grease the unlined wells. Finely shred the carrots before measuring their listed volume. For a scaled batch use enough similar wells; mix-ins can change the amount of batter, so use another well or load rather than overflowing a cup.
2. **Mix dry ingredients:** Whisk whole-wheat pastry flour, ground cinnamon, baking soda, ground nutmeg, and sea salt in a medium bowl until evenly distributed.
3. **Mix the wet base:** In a large bowl, whisk carrots, pure maple syrup, unsweetened applesauce, large egg, avocado or olive oil, and pure vanilla extract until combined. The carrots remain finely shredded, not pureed.
4. **Combine:** Stir unsweetened milk of choice and the prepared flour mixture into the wet base just until no dry flour pockets remain. Stop mixing then; do not beat the batter smooth.
5. **Fold in optional mix-ins:** Fold any chosen unsweetened shredded coconut (if using), raisins (if using), and chopped walnuts or pecans (if using) through the batter. Each is optional; keep all the batter quantities whether using one, several or none.
6. **Fill and bake:** Divide the batter among the prepared wells without overflowing. Wait until the oven reaches 350°F, then bake for 18–20 minutes per load, until a center toothpick comes out clean. Check the center again if wet batter remains.
7. **Cool:** Rest the muffins in the pan for 10 minutes, then transfer to a rack and cool completely before glazing. Extra oven loads and rack cooling take additional time.
8. **Mix the glaze:** Once the muffins are cool, whisk light cream cheese, powdered sugar, milk, and vanilla extract until smooth. Use the full glaze milk here, separate from the batter milk.
9. **Adjust the glaze:** If the glaze is too thick, gradually stir in milk (if using) as needed, stopping when it will coat a muffin top without running straight off. This additional milk is optional; do not add it automatically.
10. **Glaze and finish:** Dip the cooled muffin tops into the glaze, then return them to the rack. Add shredded coconut (if using) and chopped walnuts or pecans (if using) only if desired. Refrigerate the glazed muffins promptly.

## Cooking Notes

### Batter and glaze choices

White whole-wheat flour or all-purpose flour may replace the listed pastry flour at the same listed volume. Replace all the applesauce with one mashed ripe banana for the original twelve-muffin batch; scale that banana count with the batch. Use the same listed oil amount of melted, cooled coconut oil, walnut oil or butter instead of the avocado or olive oil, and any milk of choice at the listed batter volume. Nuts and nut oils affect allergen suitability.

The optional coconut, raisins and chopped nuts have independent listed amounts; do not take them out of the measured batter ingredients. Keep any extra coconut or nuts for the glazed tops separate from those mix-ins. If softened cream cheese will not mix smoothly, the original glaze method warms the cheese alone in a microwave-safe bowl for a reference 10–20 seconds, just until somewhat warm and soft, before adding the other glaze ingredients. Follow the appliance instructions and texture rather than multiplying that clock.

### Plant-based option

For the original one-egg batch, the publisher describes replacing the egg with 1 tbsp ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tbsp water; let it gel before adding to the wet base. Scale both amounts with the listed egg count. Use nondairy milk, the oil choice rather than butter, and omit the entire cream-cheese glaze, its additional milk and the optional glaze finishes. A little cinnamon and coarse sugar may be sprinkled on top instead. The egg-free batter can bind and bake differently; use the center check rather than assuming the same clock. The main recipe keeps its full egg and glaze.

### Make ahead

Freeze cooled unglazed muffins airtight for up to three months, then thaw and add the glaze when serving. For plain unglazed muffins, the publisher’s plan is an airtight day at room temperature followed by refrigeration for up to 4–5 days. Glazed muffins need prompt refrigeration; keep them at 40°F / 4°C or below and use within 3–4 days. Get the glaze and glazed muffins refrigerated within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C. Do not treat the plain-muffin room-temperature plan as a glazed-muffin allowance.
