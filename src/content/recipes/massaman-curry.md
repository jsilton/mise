## Chef's Note

The beef needs time before the potatoes join it. Keep the covered curry at a gentle simmer until the meat is nearly tender, then add the vegetables so they hold their shape. Full-fat coconut milk, the cooking oil and whole peanuts give the sauce its richness; finish with tamarind and sugar after the sauce reaches the consistency you want.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the beef and aromatics:** Cut the trimmed chuck into **1-inch / 2.5 cm cubes** and pat dry. Chop garlic and ginger and measure the paste, milk and stock. Reserve **one-third of the oil for the paste**; use the remaining two-thirds for browning. Keep raw-meat tools separate from serving utensils and wash them after preparing the beef.
2. **Brown in batches:** Heat the browning oil in portions over medium-high in the heavy pot. Brown the beef in an uncrowded layer, about **two batches for the full recipe**, using an equal share of that oil for each batch. Leave two broad faces against the pot for about **2–3 minutes per face**, turning once browned. Transfer to a plate; it is still raw inside. Allow the pot to regain heat between batches and lower the heat if the coating starts to blacken.
3. **Build the curry:** Lower to medium-low. Add the reserved oil, optional cracked cardamom pods and whole dried chiles; stir for about **20 seconds**. Add massaman paste and cook **1–2 minutes**, stirring, until fragrant but not scorched. Stir in garlic and ginger for about **30 seconds**. Gradually stir in the coconut milk, then stock and **half the fish sauce**, scraping up brown residue. There is no need to wait for coconut oil to separate. Return the beef and all its plate juices to the pot.
4. **Braise the beef:** Bring to a gentle simmer. Liquid should come just about to the top of the beef in the pot; add a little hot water if needed. Cover, lower the heat and braise gently for about **1 1/2–2 1/2 hours**, beginning tenderness checks after **75 minutes**. Every 20–30 minutes, stir along the bottom and check the liquid, adding hot water as needed to keep the beef mostly surrounded by sauce. A fork should enter the meat with only slight resistance before you continue. Toward the end of this stage, peel and cut the potatoes and onion.
5. **Add potatoes and onion:** Stir in the vegetables, cover and simmer about **20–30 minutes**, stirring gently once or twice. The potatoes should be tender through the center and the beef should yield easily to a fork. Check a thick beef cube reaches at least **145°F / 63°C**; tenderness usually requires cooking beyond that minimum. The off-heat rest comes before serving. If either needs longer, keep cooking gently and maintain enough liquid to prevent sticking. Lift out any potatoes that finish before the beef and return them at the finish.
6. **Finish the sauce:** Stir in peanuts, the starting amount of sugar and the starting amount of tamarind purée. Simmer **3–5 minutes**. If the sauce is watery, simmer uncovered for another **5–10 minutes** before final seasoning; it should coat a spoon but still flow around the meat. Taste with a piece of potato, then add the remaining fish sauce and optional extra sugar or tamarind in small amounts as needed. If using lime instead of tamarind, add it only after turning off the heat. Remove the cardamom pods and whole chiles before serving.
7. **Rest and serve:** Rest off heat for **5 minutes**. Serve with a modest portion of separately prepared [Basmati Rice](/mise/recipes/basmati-rice). The potatoes already supply some starch. [Coconut Jasmine Rice](/mise/recipes/coconut-jasmine-rice) is a richer accompaniment. Add cilantro and lime wedges at the table.
