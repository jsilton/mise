## Chef's Note

Massaman is the mildest and richest of Thai curries, with cardamom and other warm spices that came to Thailand with Persian and Indian traders. The chuck braises slowly in coconut milk until tender before the potatoes go in. Peanuts, palm sugar and tamarind give the sauce its sweet, tangy finish.

## Directions

1. **Brown the beef:** Pat the beef dry. Heat most of the oil in a heavy 4-quart pot over medium-high heat. Brown the beef in two batches, 2 to 3 minutes on each of two sides, and move it to a plate.
2. **Fry the curry paste:** Lower the heat to medium-low and add the rest of the oil, the cardamom and the chiles. Stir for 20 seconds. Add the curry paste and cook, stirring, until fragrant and a shade darker, 1 to 2 minutes. Stir in the garlic and ginger for 30 seconds.
3. **Add the liquids:** Stir in the coconut milk, then the stock and half the fish sauce, scraping up the browned bits. Return the beef and its juices to the pot.
4. **Braise the beef:** Bring to a gentle simmer, cover and cook over low heat until a fork goes into the beef with only slight resistance, 1 1/2 to 2 1/2 hours. Stir every 30 minutes and add a splash of hot water if the beef is no longer mostly covered.
5. **Add the potatoes and onion:** Stir in the potatoes and onion. Cover and simmer until the potatoes are tender and the beef is fork-tender, 20 to 30 minutes.
6. **Finish the sauce:** Stir in the peanuts, palm sugar and tamarind and simmer for 3 to 5 minutes. If the sauce is thin, simmer uncovered until it coats a spoon, 5 to 10 minutes more. Taste and balance it with the rest of the fish sauce and more sugar or tamarind. Pick out the cardamom pods and chiles.
7. **Rest and serve:** Let the curry rest off the heat for 5 minutes. Serve with cilantro and lime wedges.
