## Chef's Note

These tenders use a flour-and-egg coating to hold the panko in place. A light spray of oil helps the crumbs brown, while space around each piece lets the air fryer work evenly. The dipping sauce can be mixed before handling raw chicken so it stays ready for serving.

## Directions

1. **Prepare the chicken:** Use chicken tenders, kosher salt, and freshly ground black pepper: pat the chicken dry with paper towels and season both sides. Keep the tenders a similar thickness; if using breast strips instead, cut them to match. Do not rinse raw chicken.
2. **Mix the honey mustard:** Whisk together mayonnaise, honey, Dijon mustard, hot sauce (if using), kosher salt, and freshly ground black pepper in a clean bowl before starting the breading, or use clean hands and utensils while the first batch cooks. Cover and refrigerate until serving.
3. **Set the breading station and coat:** Set out all-purpose flour, panko breadcrumbs, large eggs, and buttermilk. Put flour in one shallow dish, whisk eggs with buttermilk in a second, and put panko in a third. Coat each tender lightly in flour, shake off excess, dip in the egg mixture and let excess drip off, then press into the panko. Discard leftover flour, egg mixture and crumbs after raw-chicken contact; wash hands and utensils.
4. **Air-fry in spaced batches:** Follow the air fryer’s instructions for preheating to 400°F. Arrange chicken in a single layer with gaps; cook only what fits without overlapping. Apply cooking spray to the tops and cook 5 minutes. Flip, spray the second side and cook about 5 minutes more. Probe the thickest part of every tender from the side for 165°F / 74°C; continue cooking as needed. Repeat with remaining chicken. Keep finished pieces on a clean rack without stacking while the next batch cooks, and serve promptly.
5. **Serve:** Serve the hot tenders with the honey mustard. Check each batch independently; browned crumbs alone do not establish that the chicken is done.
