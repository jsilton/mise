## Chef's Note

A generous butter base and a little flour give this soup its body. Soften onions gently, coat them evenly with flour, then simmer the tomatoes while scraping the pot bottom. Half-and-half and honey finish the soup; blending smooths the softened vegetables into the thickened liquid.

## Directions

1. **Soften onions:** Melt butter in a heavy pot over medium-low. Add diced onions and cook about **20 minutes**, stirring regularly, until soft and translucent without browning.
2. **Cook flour:** Stir in flour until no dry pockets remain. Cook about **3 minutes**, stirring and scraping, until the buttery mixture is pale gold; do not let it darken.
3. **Add liquid gradually:** Stir in broth a little at a time to loosen the flour mixture, then add all listed tomatoes and their juices, sugar, celery salt and black pepper. Raise heat enough to bring to a gentle simmer, stirring so the thick base does not stick.
4. **Simmer:** Reduce heat and simmer gently for about **30 minutes**, scraping across the pot bottom frequently, until the onion and tomatoes are very soft. Avoid a rapid boil.
5. **Blend and finish:** Remove from heat and let active bubbling stop. Blend with an immersion blender, keeping its guard submerged and stopping before lifting it. Alternatively, cool to your countertop blender’s permitted temperature and blend in as many small batches as its hot-liquid fill limit requires, with the lid and steam vent arranged exactly as its instructions specify. Start low; do not use a sealed personal cup for hot soup. Stir in half-and-half and honey. Return to gentle heat just until hot, without a hard boil.
6. **Taste and serve:** Taste before adding salt, since celery salt and broth already season the soup. Divide into the intended soup portions and serve. Bread is a separate accompaniment; refrigerate surplus promptly rather than leaving the large pot out through the meal.
