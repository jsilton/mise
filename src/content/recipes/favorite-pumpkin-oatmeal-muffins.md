## Chef's Note

Blend rolled oats with the pumpkin and wet ingredients until the oats are finely broken down. These are moist oat muffins: check that the center is set, and move them from the pan to a rack before storing.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Preheat the oven to 375°F. Grease a standard 12-cup muffin tin with the pan allowance, or use silicone liners. Use plain pumpkin puree, not sweetened pie filling; homemade puree should be cooled before mixing with eggs or loading a blender.

2. **Blend:** Combine rolled oats, pumpkin puree, milk, maple syrup, eggs, pumpkin pie spice, baking soda, vanilla and salt in a blender or food processor. Keep the chocolate chips out for topping. Follow the appliance’s actual capacity and loading instructions; divide a larger batch into proportionate loads if needed.

3. **Continue blending:** Blend until the oats are finely broken down and the batter is smooth, stopping and scraping with the appliance off as needed. Stop when no coarse oat flakes remain; blending time varies with the appliance and load.

4. **Fill:** Fill the prepared cups no more than ¾ full and distribute all the listed chocolate chips over the tops. Bake excess batter in additional cups or a later batch.

5. **Bake:** Bake for 20–22 minutes, until the tops are firm, the edges lightly golden and a center tester comes out without wet batter.

6. **Cool:** Cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then transfer to a rack. Serve warm, at room temperature or chilled; cool completely before sealing for storage.

7. **Store:** Refrigerate cooled muffins promptly at 40°F / 4°C or below, within 2 hours of baking or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C; use within 3–4 days. Freeze cooled muffins airtight for up to 3 months for quality, and thaw portions in the refrigerator.

## Cooking Notes

For a honey variation, replace the listed maple syrup with the same volume of honey; do not serve honey-containing muffins to children under one year. For stronger spice, double the listed pumpkin pie spice (2 tsp in the original 12-muffin batch). Chocolate chips may be omitted for the separate plain version. For the dairy-free option, use plain unsweetened nondairy milk and dairy-free chocolate chips if including the topping; choose silicone liners or a dairy-free baking spray rather than butter for the pan. Use certified gluten-free oats when needed.

For mini muffins, start checking around 12–14 minutes and use the same center-doneness cues; the written 12 count refers to standard muffins, not mini yield. A store-bought egg replacer can be used according to its own instructions for the listed egg count, with a different crumb and checking time. Serve smaller pieces to younger children.
