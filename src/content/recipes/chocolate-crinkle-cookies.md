## Chef's Note

A firm, chilled dough is easier to portion and coat. Roll each ball in granulated sugar first and powdered sugar second, then bake until the coating cracks and the edges feel firm. Keep the centers soft after baking rather than drying them out to make the tops look done.

## Directions

1. **Melt:** Set a heatproof bowl above gently simmering water, with its bottom clear of the water. Melt the chopped chocolate and salted butter together, stirring until smooth. Remove the bowl from the pan and let the mixture cool slightly before adding it to the eggs.

2. **Dry mix:** Whisk the flour, cocoa, baking powder, baking soda and kosher salt until evenly mixed and free of lumps.

3. **Beat:** Beat the brown sugar, eggs, vanilla and one-third of the listed granulated sugar on medium-high until light and smooth, about 2–3 minutes. For the original batch, that sugar portion is 1/2 cup; reserve the other two-thirds for rolling.

4. **Combine:** Mix the slightly cooled chocolate-butter mixture into the egg mixture. On low speed, add the dry ingredients only until the dough is evenly combined. Cover and refrigerate for 2–4 hours, until firm enough to scoop and roll.

5. **Prepare and coat:** Near the end of chilling, preheat the oven to 350°F. Line baking sheets with parchment. Put the reserved two-thirds of the granulated sugar in one shallow bowl and all the powdered sugar in another. Scoop about 1 tablespoon of dough per cookie, form balls, and coat first with granulated sugar and then generously with powdered sugar. Leave about 2 inches between balls. Keep unportioned dough refrigerated while later trays wait.

6. **Bake:** Once the oven reaches 350°F, bake for 10–12 minutes per tray, until the tops have cracked and the edges feel firm. Use further loads as needed; do not crowd the sheets to force a particular count.

7. **Cool:** Leave the cookies on the sheet for 5 minutes, then move them to racks to cool completely. Let a hot sheet cool before loading another batch. Discard coating sugar that has contacted raw dough.
