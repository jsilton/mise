## Chef's Note

Cook the beans to the texture you want before the short garlic-butter finish. The blanched route keeps the garlic cooking brief; the direct route adds garlic only after the raw beans have softened. All the butter and toasted almonds stay in either route.

## Directions

1. **Prepare and blanch:** Wash and trim the beans. For the blanched route, bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it to taste separately from the measured finishing salt. Cook beans about 3–4 minutes until barely tender-crisp, checking a thick bean; drain well. To prepare ahead, cool and refrigerate promptly as described below. For the direct route, skip the boiling water and continue with raw trimmed beans.

2. **Prepare almonds:** Set aside the already-toasted sliced almonds. If starting with untoasted sliced almonds in the same listed amount, toast in the dry skillet over medium-low heat for about 2–3 minutes, stirring until fragrant and lightly golden; transfer to a plate.

3. **Cook the chosen route:** For blanched beans, melt all the cold butter with the sliced garlic over medium heat, stirring about 60–90 seconds until fragrant without deep browning. Add drained beans and toss about 1–2 minutes until hot. For raw beans, melt all the butter over medium heat, add the beans first and cook, tossing, about 7–8 minutes or longer until a thick bean is tender enough to eat. Reduce heat if the butter darkens; then add all the garlic and toss about 60–90 seconds until fragrant. Use more skillets or rounds rather than crowding; divide the same butter and garlic among them.

4. **Season:** Season the whole batch with all the measured kosher salt and black pepper. The separate blanching-water salt is an unmeasured preparation allowance, not part of this finishing amount.

5. **Finish:** Toss in all the toasted almonds and transfer to a serving dish. Add the listed unmeasured flaky finishing salt to taste and serve promptly.
