## Chef's Note

These oven wings pair a Korean gochujang glaze with rack roasting. Dry the skin thoroughly before cooking, then apply the sauce lightly at service. Honey gives sweetness, vinegar lifts the glaze and sesame oil finishes its aroma. This is an oven preparation with its own texture, not a promise of identical results to double-fried chicken.

## Directions

1. **Dry and season:** Pat fully thawed drumettes and flats dry. Lightly oil two oven-safe racks and set them over rimmed trays. Toss wings thoroughly with the baking powder and salt so there are no concentrated powder patches.
2. **Refrigerate to dry:** Arrange in a single spaced layer on the racks. Refrigerate uncovered for 8–24 hours at 40°F / 4°C or below, on a low shelf with the trays containing drips. Include this time within the usual 1–2-day refrigerated raw-poultry storage window.
3. **Roast and check:** During the last 15 minutes of refrigerated drying, heat the oven to 425°F / 220°C with racks in the upper and lower thirds. Roast about 25 minutes, then turn wings and swap tray positions. Roast another 20–30 minutes, checking until skin is browned and several large pieces reach at least 165°F / 74°C away from bone. Around 175°F / 79°C is a useful tenderness target for wings. Remove smaller finished pieces earlier if necessary.
4. **Make glaze:** During roasting, combine gochujang, honey, soy, vinegar, water, garlic and ginger in a small saucepan. Simmer gently about 3–4 minutes, stirring, until garlic smells mellow and the glaze flows from a spoon. Turn off heat and stir in sesame oil. Keep warm; do not reduce to a stiff paste.
5. **Coat at service:** Put cooked wings in a clean bowl and toss with about two-thirds of the glaze. Add more only if needed for a thin coating; reserve the rest in a separate serving bowl. Do not broil the honey-coated wings or coat them repeatedly for a long hold.
6. **Serve:** Finish with scallions, sesame seeds and optional measured gochugaru. Divide into four main portions with separately prepared rice and cucumbers, or offer eight smaller appetizer portions. The 1.35 kg weight includes bones, not that much edible meat.

## Offering both wing flavors

For a four-person main platter with this glaze and [lemon pepper butter](/mise/recipes/lemon-pepper-wings), roast one 1.35 kg batch using the shared wing preparation only once. Split the cooked wings evenly and make half of each recipe's sauce and garnishes. That provides two flavor choices within the same total wing quantity; making both full recipes doubles the food.
