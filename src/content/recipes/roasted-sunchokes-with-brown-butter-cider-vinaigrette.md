## Chef's Note

Roast the sunchokes until their centers are tender, then dress them with nutty brown butter, reduced cider vinegar, shallots and honey. Reduce the vinegar into a separate bowl and clean the saucepan before browning the butter; keeping those stages separate lets the milk solids brown instead of simmering in acid. Spinach and chives bring a fresh finish to the warm vegetables.

## Directions

1. **Prepare:** Heat the oven to 375°F. Wash the sunchokes under running water before cutting. Set out a rimmed baking sheet with room for a single layer, a small light-colored saucepan and a heatproof bowl for the vinegar mixture. Use additional sheets for a larger batch.
2. **Roast the sunchokes:** Toss sunchokes (Jerusalem artichokes), extra-virgin olive oil, fresh thyme sprigs, garlic cloves, and kosher salt on the baking sheet and spread without piling. Roast for about 1 hour, turning once and checking any unevenly browned areas, until golden and easily pierced through the center with a fork. Continue if the centers are firm. Discard the thyme stems and garlic after roasting, then transfer the hot sunchokes to a large bowl.
3. **Reduce the vinegar separately:** While the sunchokes roast, set out apple cider vinegar, shallots, and honey. Simmer the vinegar alone over moderate heat until reduced to half its starting volume: 2 tablespoons for the original ¼-cup batch, roughly 3 minutes in a small pan. Use the volume endpoint for scaled batches rather than multiplying the time. Transfer the reduction to the heatproof bowl; stir in the shallots and honey. Wipe the saucepan clean and dry before returning it to the heat.
4. **Brown the butter:** Set out unsalted butter and red pepper flakes. Melt the butter alone in the clean, dry saucepan over medium heat. Swirl and stir, watching the milk solids beneath the foam. As they turn golden brown and smell nutty, add the red pepper flakes briefly, then take the pan off the heat before the solids blacken. Let the vigorous bubbling subside and carefully whisk the browned butter, including the browned solids, into the vinegar mixture in the bowl. Do not attempt to brown butter in the vinegar. Re-whisk before dressing if it separates.
5. **Dress and serve warm:** Set out fresh spinach leaves, fresh chives, kosher salt, and black pepper. Add the spinach, chives and warm vinaigrette to the hot sunchokes and toss to coat. The spinach should soften against the warm vegetables, though it need not wilt completely. Taste, then season with the finishing salt and pepper. Serve warm with the chives distributed through the dish.
