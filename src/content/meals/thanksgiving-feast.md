## Meal Overview

For twelve diners, this menu uses a **14–16 lb aromatic wet-brined turkey**, a shallow sausage-kale stuffing, cream cheese mashed potatoes, whole-berry cranberry sauce with its orange variation, lemon-shallot green beans and a small portion of gravy. The stuffing and potatoes are deliberately modest holiday portions alongside each other; there is no extra bread course in this plan.

Allow about **twenty-six hours from a fully refrigerator-thawed bird**, with **three and a half hours hands-on across two days**. Thawing the frozen turkey needs additional refrigerator days. All component quantities below are for twelve; the bird stays at its specified size. A helper for carving and carrying hot dishes during the final half hour is useful, but the stovetop order below keeps one cook from needing three simultaneous finishing tasks.

## Amounts for twelve

- **Turkey:** one full batch of the linked 14–16 lb recipe, including its entire measured cold brine. Its yield is twelve with sides.
- **Stuffing:** one full eight-side-portion batch, intentionally divided into twelve smaller holiday portions, roughly half a cup each depending on actual yield. Do not automatically multiply its sausage and bread while also serving potatoes.
- **Potatoes:** one and a half batches, starting with **2040 g peeled potatoes**, to supply about nine cups. The exact working quantities are below.
- **Cranberry sauce:** one full batch using **120 ml water, 60 ml orange juice and one teaspoon zest** in place of the canonical water/peel strip. Use the regular **150 g sugar**. About two cups gives twelve modest 2–3-tablespoon portions; this is a condiment.
- **Green beans:** three batches, **1350 g trimmed beans**, prepared and cooled a day ahead, then finished in three skillet rounds. Their working quantities are below. Omit optional honey here; cranberry and the turkey brine already bring sweetness.
- **Gravy:** one and a half batches, about three cups, prepared with the recipe's water substitution before the turkey finishes. Use **45 g butter, 45 g flour, 675 ml good unsalted chicken or turkey stock, 45 ml water and about 3/16 tsp pepper**. Reserve **up to 3/8 tsp fine salt** for tasting and **up to 90 ml hot water** for loosening. The stock supplies the flavor in this version. Do not add a second measure of salty turkey drippings afterward.

The gravy and creamy potatoes are rich; portion gravy at roughly a quarter cup each. The green beans provide a full vegetable serving, while lemon and cranberry bring acidity in different forms.

## Potato quantities and pot size

Use an **8-quart pot**, with enough room for potatoes and water below the rim:

- 2040 g peeled Yukon Gold potatoes in even one-inch pieces (buy roughly 2.4 kg before peeling)
- About 4.5 L cold water, or enough to cover by an inch
- 18 g salt for the cooking water
- 113 g unsalted butter
- 173 g cream cheese
- 270 ml half-and-half, plus up to 90 ml more if needed
- 3/4 tsp fine salt for finishing, divided after tasting
- 3/4 tsp black pepper

Follow the potato recipe's tenderness, drying and gentle-mashing instructions. The butter and cream cheese are rounded to whole grams from one and a half batches. Heating this larger pot takes longer: allow about **seventy minutes** from peeling through mashing rather than copying the smaller recipe's time.

## Green-bean quantities and preparation

For three batches, use **1350 g trimmed beans, 180 g diced shallots, three tablespoons butter, three teaspoons lemon zest, three tablespoons lemon juice with up to one additional tablespoon after tasting, and 3/8 teaspoon black pepper**. Finishing salt is **up to 3/8 teaspoon fine salt**, only if needed. Set aside **six tablespoons water** for reheating. Omit honey.

Boil the beans in three separate 450 g batches. Use a fresh **two liters water and ten grams cooking-water salt for each batch** so the quantities and pot capacity are clear. Cool each batch promptly in ice water until cold, drain thoroughly and refrigerate in shallow covered containers. The ice and cooling water are additional service equipment, not sauce liquid. Do this a day ahead to free the stove on serving day.

For the final skillet work, soften all the shallots in the three tablespoons butter, then divide that mixture into three equal portions. Reheat one bean batch with one shallot portion and up to two tablespoons water at a time, following the recipe's refrigerated-bean instructions and checking **165°F / 74°C**. Keep the first batches briefly covered in a warm serving dish above **140°F / 60°C** while finishing the others. Add the measured lemon, pepper and optional finishing salt after the batches are reunited, just before serving. The extra holding will soften color and texture slightly; this is a practical twelve-person service choice.

## Oven and refrigerator check

The turkey/brine container must fit inside a refrigerator at **40°F / 4°C or below**, along with the chilled sides. Do not plan to store the brine on a porch or in an unmonitored cooler. The turkey must be unenhanced and fully refrigerator-thawed before its twelve-hour brine; the brine itself must be cold before adding the bird.

The default oven holds the turkey pan below the shallow 9-by-13-inch stuffing dish, with air space between and around them. Both use **325°F / 163°C** here. Use the stuffing recipe's forty-to-fifty-five-minute allowance at that temperature and check its center independently. If both pans do not fit, use a second oven or the fully baked, refrigerated stuffing reheat alternative described in [the smaller turkey dinner's oven plan](/mise/meals/thanksgiving-practice#equipment-and-oven-plan). That fallback extends the rest-to-service interval and must be scheduled; it is not permission to crowd the oven or leave the bird out indefinitely.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Plan backward from roasting:** The turkey needs twelve hours in a cold refrigerated brine, then four hours uncovered refrigerated drying before roasting. Making and cooling its brine adds up to two hours before the soak. Set those stages from the actual intended oven start, with roughly four to five hours for roasting, checking, resting and carving. Refrigerator thawing happens before this schedule and is additional.
2. **During the overnight preparation:** Make and chill the orange variation of cranberry sauce. Prepare, cool and refrigerate the bean batches. Chop stuffing vegetables and refrigerate them separately from dry bread. Do not assemble wet stuffing overnight. Keep raw turkey and its container below ready-to-eat food.
3. **Drain and dry the bird:** Follow the turkey recipe without rinsing raw poultry. Discard brine, pat dry and give the bird its four-hour refrigerated drying stage. Preheat the oven and prepare the roasting pan near the end of that stage. Roast the fixed-size bird according to its actual temperature readings, not a minutes-per-pound rule.
4. **About ninety minutes before expected turkey removal:** Start the stuffing's bread hydration and stovetop preparation. Bake immediately after combining, in its shallow dish above the turkey. It needs its own **165°F / 74°C** center check. If the bird or casserole is slow, shift service rather than lowering either endpoint.
5. **About seventy-five minutes before service:** Start the larger potato batch with the quantities above. While it simmers, make the one-and-a-half batch of gravy using water in place of drippings; it can be ready before the turkey leaves the oven. Keep the gravy covered and hot at or above **140°F / 60°C**, loosening if needed. It does not need to compete with bean finishing or carving for attention.
6. **As the turkey finishes:** Measure both breasts, inner thighs and wing areas as directed, then rest the cooked bird for thirty minutes. Once stuffing reaches its separate endpoint, cover it loosely. A short hold can use the now turkey-free oven at **200°F / 93°C**, checking food stays above **140°F / 60°C**. Finish mashing potatoes, with their short measured water-bath hold if needed. Avoid long holds that dry either side.
7. **During the rest:** Finish the shallots and reheat the three bean batches in order, as detailed above; allow about twenty-five to thirty minutes. Use the warm oven briefly for earlier batches, then finish all beans with lemon at service. Ask a helper to carve and carry dishes if available; otherwise carve after the final bean batch and serve promptly, keeping the hot sides covered during those few minutes.
8. **Serve twelve:** Portion turkey, modest stuffing, about three-quarters cup mash, a full share of beans, two to three tablespoons cranberry sauce and roughly a quarter cup gravy. Offer remaining food after everyone has a complete plate. Cranberry sauce stays chilled until it is brought to the table.

## Delays and leftovers

A temperature check can extend the schedule. If a delay exceeds the planned short rests and holds, maintain hot food at or above **140°F / 60°C** or cool and refrigerate it promptly, then reheat to **165°F / 74°C** before serving. An unmeasured slow-cooker setting is not evidence of hot holding.

Carve remaining turkey meat off the carcass and cool cooked components in separate shallow containers. Refrigerate within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Use within three to four days and reheat hot components to 165°F / 74°C. Keep stock-making as a separate planned recipe with its own handling and timing; saving a warm carcass on the counter is not part of this dinner plan. Follow each component's storage guidance and [FoodSafety.gov's handling advice](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety).
