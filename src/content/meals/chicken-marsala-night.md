## Meal Overview

For four diners, make **one full batch Chicken Marsala**, **half a batch Cream Cheese Mashed Potatoes** and **one full batch Green Beans with Shallots and Lemon**. The mash starts with 680 g peeled potatoes and supplies roughly three cups; it is the only starch. The chicken's mushroom sauce is already part of the main, so no additional gravy, pasta or bread is required.

The Marsala recipe uses 680 g opened-flat chicken thighs, prosciutto and fresh mushrooms. The potatoes provide a creamy base for its sauce, and 450 g beans give a vegetable portion alongside. Omit the green beans' optional honey here: softened shallots and Marsala already supply enough sweet notes, while the measured lemon finish gives contrast.

Allow **eighty minutes elapsed and about sixty hands-on** for one cook. The chicken's fifty-minute recipe does not include peeling potatoes, heating two water pots or making a separate bean finish. This dinner uses a chicken skillet, a bean skillet and two pots; it is more involved than a thirty-minute pan meal.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Set out and prepare:** Use the potato recipe's 1/2× ingredient setting, with 680 g peeled potatoes and all other listed quantities halved. Trim beans, dice shallots, prepare lemon and slice mushrooms before raw-chicken handling. Put the potato water on to heat and start the bean water in a separate pot. Reserve a clean board and platter for cooked chicken.
2. **Prepare and cook the chicken:** Follow the Marsala recipe's cutlet preparation and two browning batches. Clean raw-contact surfaces before returning to vegetables. Partially browned chicken waits only while the same cooking session continues with mushrooms and sauce; do not refrigerate it half-cooked for later.
3. **Cook potatoes and beans alongside:** Simmer potatoes until fully tender, warming their dairy separately as instructed. Boil beans to a bite you enjoy, then drain. They will get their shallot finish near service; if completing them much earlier, use the bean recipe's rapid cooling and refrigerated preparation route rather than leaving a bowl out through a long delay.
4. **Build the Marsala sauce:** Brown mushrooms and prosciutto, reduce wine, then add the stock. Return chicken and its juices to finish at **165°F / 74°C** in the thickest centers. The pan sauce needs its liquid; loosen with stock if it reduces too far while other components finish.
5. **Finish the sides:** Gently mash the tender potatoes with the half-batch warm dairy and taste before finishing salt. If needed, use the potato recipe's covered water-bath hold above **140°F / 60°C**, for no more than about thirty minutes for texture. Soften shallots in the second skillet, warm the beans, then add lemon off heat. Omit their optional honey and taste before adding any finishing salt.
6. **Mount the sauce and serve:** Transfer fully cooked chicken to its clean platter, take the Marsala pan off heat and swirl in cold butter as instructed. Do this once sides are ready, so the finished butter sauce is not held at a hard boil. Divide the mash and beans among four plates, add the chicken, then spoon its mushrooms and sauce over the chicken and some of the potato.

## Timing adjustments

The bean and chicken skillets require attention at the same time near service. Prepare the bean shallots before the final chicken simmer if cooking alone, then warm the beans while the chicken finishes. If the stove cannot accommodate all four vessels, boil beans first, drain and finish them within this continuous cooking session, using the freed burner for potatoes; allow extra time instead of crowding the stove. A short potato hold is more forgiving than a long hold of the finished butter sauce.

For diners avoiding alcohol, use the chicken recipe's measured stock-and-lemon alternative. It makes a different mushroom pan sauce; cooking Marsala does not guarantee an alcohol-free meal. The main also contains pork, wheat and dairy, and the mash contains dairy.

## Leftovers

Keep chicken and sauce, potatoes and beans in separate shallow containers. Refrigerate within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below; use within three to four days. Reheat cooked components to **165°F / 74°C**. The sauce may separate and the beans soften; loosen sauce with a little unsalted stock and stir gently after reheating.
