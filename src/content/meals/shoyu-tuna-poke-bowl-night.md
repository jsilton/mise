## Meal Overview

Make **one complete batch of Shoyu Tuna Poke Bowl** for three generous bowls. It includes 450 g prepared tuna, four cups lightly seasoned cooked rice, 300 g fresh cucumber, 150 g shelled edamame and one avocado. Each diner receives approximately 150 g tuna, 1 1/3 cups rice, 100 g cucumber, 50 g beans and a third of the avocado. The main supplies the rice, vegetables and dressing for this dinner.

Fresh cucumber and a mildly sweet shoyu dressing give this version a gentler flavor than the chile-and-cucumber-salad poke meal. Offer the main's measured optional sriracha mayonnaise separately. Begin with the fish's light coating of dressing; the rice has its own salt and vinegar seasoning.

Read the main's sourcing note before buying fish. Raw tuna remains a deliberate choice even with careful sourcing. FDA advises children, pregnant people, older adults and people with weakened immune systems to avoid raw seafood. For a fully cooked seafood dinner, choose [Shrimp and Grits Night](/mise/meals/shrimp-and-grits-night), which serves four and includes a five-person adjustment. This poke menu does not include a separate cooked portion.

## Cooking Strategy

Allow **about 85 minutes elapsed and 40 minutes hands-on** with thawed tuna, starting the rice from dry. The rice soak, heating, covered cooking, rest and brief cooling are included. A rice-cooker cycle or slower chilling may extend the schedule.

1. **Plan cold storage first:** Follow the main's species and supplier guidance; a “sushi-grade” label alone is insufficient. Keep tuna at 40°F / 4°C or below. If frozen, allow refrigerator thawing before this schedule, often overnight, and remove vacuum packaging before refrigerator thawing as directed. Set out the rice pot, a second small saucepan or the edamame package's microwave equipment, a wide rice bowl and separate vegetable/fish boards.
2. **Begin the plain rice:** Follow [Plain Short-Grain White Rice](/mise/recipes/steamed-white-rice), starting with 300 g dry rice and its measured water. Start its thirty-minute soak before preparing toppings. Its full process takes about 65 minutes and should provide roughly four cooked cups; measure the amount used and reserve any extra separately.
3. **Use the soak and cooking time:** Cook the main's 150 g shelled edamame according to its package early in the rice process. Drain and spread in a shallow container, then refrigerate promptly until cold. Wash and cut cucumber and scallion with clean equipment. Keep the vegetables refrigerated. Mix the fish dressing in a separate clean bowl; measure the rice's vinegar, sugar and salt into another bowl so the two mixtures stay distinct. Slice the avocado near final assembly.
4. **Season and cool the rice:** Once the rested rice is tender, fold the main's rice-seasoning mixture into four measured cooked cups. Spread gently in the wide bowl and let steam escape until barely warm, about ten minutes. Keep the unused rice separate and refrigerate it promptly. During this cooling period, carry out the fish preparation in the next step. A damp towel is only for the brief assembly period.
5. **Dress the cold tuna while rice cools:** Prepare the avocado before switching to the fish board. Cut tuna into the main's 1/2-inch cubes, coat with its dressing and refrigerate for about five to ten minutes while setting out bowls. Clean and sanitize the raw-fish work area. This short seasoning period is a texture choice; it does not cook the fish or establish safety.
6. **Portion and serve:** Divide rice, tuna, cold beans, fresh cucumber and avocado among three bowls. Lift fish from excess dressing, add the main's sesame seeds and offer its optional mayonnaise with a clean spoon. Add fish to rice only when each diner is ready to eat; serve promptly.

## Rice option and advance preparation

For the main's prepared-shari option, use four cups [Sushi Rice (Shari)](/mise/recipes/master-sushi-rice) and omit only the main's three Rice-section seasoning ingredients. Keep its tuna-dressing vinegar and sugar. The full shari batch makes extra and takes about eighty minutes from dry; allow about ninety minutes for this meal when using that fresh-rice route.

Safely refrigerated rice and cooked beans can shorten the day-of work. Allow roughly 35–45 minutes if reheating chilled rice to 165°F / 74°C and then spreading the serving portion to become barely warm before adding tuna; very cold or large portions may take longer. Keep the fish refrigerated during this work. Cooked rice, fresh vegetable preparation and raw-fish assembly remain separate tasks.

## Leftovers and serving limits

Plan raw tuna for same-day preparation and service, respecting any earlier supplier limit. Do not keep assembled raw-fish bowls for meal-prep lunches or reuse raw-contact dressing as table sauce. Refrigerate untouched rice, beans and vegetables separately in shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C; count preparation and service time together. Discard perishables beyond those limits.

Use separately refrigerated cooked rice and beans within three to four days at 40°F / 4°C or below; reheat cooked leftovers to 165°F / 74°C. Cucumber and avocado are best freshly cut. The main contains fish, soy and sesame; ordinary soy sauce adds wheat and optional mayonnaise may add egg. Follow its packaged-ingredient checks for substitutions.
