## Meal Overview

Slow-cooked ragù over fresh egg pasta, with garlic bread and an arugula salad. Allow about 3–4 hours from scratch, with pasta preparation and its one-hour rest fitted around the ragù's 2–3 hour simmer. Rolling pasta for the first time may take longer.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Start the ragù:** Follow the complete stovetop recipe through its initial cooking stages, then allow 2–3 hours of gentle simmering. Stir and check the liquid during the other tasks; add the milk halfway through this simmer as directed.
2. **Make and rest the dough:** Scale Fresh Egg Pasta to 1.5× for six portions. Mix and knead while the sauce simmers, then give the dough its full one-hour covered rest. If rolling will wait after that rest, refrigerate the tightly wrapped dough at 40°F / 4°C or below. Keep total room-temperature handling within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F / 32°C; in a hot kitchen, rest in the refrigerator as the linked recipe directs.
3. **Prepare the accompaniments:** Wash the salad greens and make the dressing for six side portions; hold them separately. Soften the butter for one batch of garlic bread.
4. **Roll and cut:** Start rolling about 45 minutes before the planned meal, allowing longer for unfamiliar equipment. Work with one dough piece at a time; keep waiting pieces covered and refrigerated when the room-temperature handling budget is running short. Cut ribbons to the linked recipe's thickness cue. Cook promptly, or refrigerate cut noodles if the ragù needs more time.
5. **Coordinate the final cooking:** Once the ragù is tender and nearly ready, heat the oven to 400°F / 205°C and bring a large pasta pot to a boil. Bake the garlic bread for about 12–15 minutes while keeping the ragù hot.
6. **Boil and coat the pasta:** Cook the fresh ribbons in uncrowded batches as needed, checking around 2 minutes and continuing until there is no floury center, usually 2–5 minutes. Reserve cooking water. Toss the drained ribbons with ragù in a wide pan, loosening only as needed with small splashes of that water.
7. **Serve:** Dress the salad, portion the bread and serve promptly with Parmigiano-Reggiano as wanted.

## Notes

- For another dinner, freeze cooked ragù promptly in shallow meal-sized containers. Doubling its ingredients requires a larger pot or separate batches; its simmer time does not double automatically.
- Dried pasta is a useful alternative: follow its package quantities and cooking cues for six diners rather than the fresh dough schedule.
- Chianti or Sangiovese can accompany the meal.
