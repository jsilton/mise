## Meal Overview

For four people, make one full batch each of Mongolian Beef with Ginger and Scallions, Plain Short-Grain White Rice and Bok Choy with Garlic and Oyster Sauce. That is 680 g raw beef, 300 g dry rice and 600 g trimmed bok choy. The beef supplies a sweet-savory glaze; plain rice takes up a little sauce, and the bok choy provides juicy stems and tender greens.

Allow about 75 minutes elapsed and 50 minutes active for one cook. The rice's thirty-minute soak and ten-minute rest are part of that time. Use the rice saucepan plus a skillet or wok; a separate lidded skillet for the bok choy makes the last stages easier. This is a generous weeknight cooking window, so start before the usual last-minute dinner rush.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Start the rice:** Rinse the full 300 g rice batch, drain and soak in its measured 400 ml water for thirty minutes. The linked recipe is short-grain rice with a soak; it is not a twenty-minute rice-cooker guarantee.
2. **Prepare the beef during the soak:** Slice all 680 g across the grain, measure the coating, divide into three pan batches, and prepare the separate sauce and slurry. Keep raw meat and its utensils away from washed vegetables. Prepare scallion whites and greens separately.
3. **Wash the bok choy and heat rice:** Thoroughly clean and drain 600 g bok choy, cut larger heads into quarters, and prepare its separate oyster-sauce mixture. After the full rice soak, start the rice's boil and gentle covered cook. Keep a timer for the rice's cooking and subsequent off-heat rest.
4. **Cook the bok choy first:** Follow both pan-steaming batches and the brief garlic finish. Transfer to a warmed serving dish, loosely covered while the beef cooks. The leaves will soften a little during this short wait. If using one skillet, wipe it dry before searing beef; never pour frying oil into a pan holding water.
5. **Finish the beef:** Sear its three batches with their allocated oil, then cook aromatics and thicken the sauce separately. Toss beef and scallion greens briefly. Check its measured beef endpoint and allow the full three-minute wait before serving. If using the chicken variation, follow its 165°F / 74°C checks and allow extra time as needed.
6. **Serve together:** Fluff rested rice, divide all three components into four portions, and spoon over only enough bok choy sauce to coat the greens. The beef already has its own glaze. Add the bok choy's optional measured fried garnish at the table, if using.

## Cool spinach variation

For a different green side, replace the bok choy with one full batch of [Sigeumchi Namul (Sesame Spinach)](/mise/recipes/garlic-sesame-spinach). Its 450 g raw spinach makes four small portions after blanching and gently squeezing; it is a smaller vegetable serving than the bok choy. Keep the beef and rice quantities unchanged.

Make the spinach during the rice soak: blanch in its two brief batches, cool, drain, gently press, then dress with its measured garlic and sesame seasoning. Refrigerate until serving. Continue the beef preparation and rice cooking, then serve the spinach cool beside the hot beef and rice. Keep the same 75-minute planning allowance so the blanching, cooling and squeezing are included. This variation uses a Korean banchan alongside a Chinese-American main; it is a practical mixed menu.

## Make-ahead and leftovers

Measure sauces and wash/drain bok choy ahead to shorten the dinner session. Keep prepared meat refrigerated and separate until cooking. A rice cooker can simplify attention, but use its own cycle guidance and plan around its actual finish time.

Chill cooked leftovers promptly in shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C. Refrigerate at 40°F / 4°C or below and use within 3–4 days. Reheat needed portions to 165°F / 74°C, adding a small splash of water where useful. Beef coating and bok choy soften in storage; fresh cooking gives the clearest texture contrast.
