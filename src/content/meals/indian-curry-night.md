## Meal Overview

Coconut-and-almond vegetable korma, a small mound of plain basmati and a soft naan for each person. The curry supplies the vegetables and sauce; rice and bread share the starch portion. Yogurt gives the korma a little tang, so it needs no extra cream or separate sauce.

Allow about **5 1/2 hours elapsed** and **90–110 minutes of hands-on work** when making everything from scratch. Slow-cooker tenderness and the dough rise set the pace; a fast batch can be ready earlier, and a slow cooker or cool room can push dinner later. This is a weekend meal with a mostly unattended curry and an active bread-making finish.

## Portions for Six

- **Korma:** Make one full [Slow Cooker Vegetable Korma](../recipes/slow-cooker-vegetable-korma) batch, including its beans, peas and yogurt finish. Serve about **1 1/4 cups each**, dividing the vegetables and sauce evenly.
- **Rice:** Make **half the [Basmati Rice](../recipes/basmati-rice) recipe**, plain: **1 cup (200 g) dry aged white basmati, 1 1/2 cups (360 ml) water, 1 1/2 tsp ghee or neutral oil, and 3/8 tsp fine salt**. Omit the optional whole spices. Reserve **1 tbsp hot water** for the recipe's dry-but-firm correction, using more sparingly if another check calls for it. Use a narrow **2-quart saucepan** with a tight lid; rinse and drain without soaking. This gives about **3 cups cooked**, or **1/2 cup per person**.
- **Naan:** Make **half the [Naan](../recipes/naan) dough** and divide into **six roughly 70 g balls**. Use **250 g flour, 1 tbsp oil in the dough plus 1/2 tsp for coating, 2 tbsp unstrained whole-milk yogurt, 3.5 g active dry yeast, 1 tsp sugar, 3.75 g fine salt, 1/2 tsp baking powder and 120 ml warm whole milk**. Keep **1 tbsp flour** for dusting and **1 tbsp milk** for correction separate. Keep the full recipe's milk temperature, rise cues, 5-by-8-inch size and pan method. Omit the optional garlic butter for these plain breads. Cook six rounds, allowing about **20–25 minutes** for rolling, pan recovery and cooking.

These are modest rice portions because everyone also gets a naan. For five diners, use the same batches and offer the extra rice, curry and sixth bread as seconds or refrigerate the curry and rice promptly for another meal. Do not squeeze a larger korma batch into the same cooker.

## Cooking Strategy

Use a conventional **6-quart slow cooker**, two burners, a **2-quart lidded rice saucepan**, and a **12-inch heavy skillet**. Keep the hot bread wrapped in a clean towel.

1. **About 5 1/2 hours before dinner — start the curry:** Allow 25 minutes to wash, trim, weigh and cut the vegetables and mix the coconut sauce. Load and start on **High immediately**, following the korma recipe. Refrigerate the prepared beans and yogurt; leave the peas frozen. Do not use a delayed start.
2. **About 3 hours before dinner — make the naan dough:** Follow the half-batch quantities above. Hydrate the yeast, mix, knead and cover for the **60–90-minute rise**. The dough needs to roughly double; the smaller batch still needs its rise. Wash the mixing tools and set the table during this wait.
3. **After 3 hours of curry cooking — check before planning the finish:** Test a carrot and a thick cauliflower stem. Add the beans only when both are nearly tender, allowing about **45–60 minutes total** more on High. Begin checking the beans after **30–45 minutes** and add the peas when the beans are nearly tender, so their **10–15 minutes** of cooking falls within the end of that window, as the main recipe directs. If the carrots are still hard, keep cooking and check again in 20–30 minutes. Move the rice and bread finish later if necessary. If the curry finishes early, keep it covered on Warm at **140°F / 60°C or above**, with the yogurt still separate. Aim to keep this hold within an hour by bringing the last steps forward when possible; the vegetables soften further during a longer hold.
4. **About 45–50 minutes before serving — set up the final round:** When the dough has doubled, divide into six balls and cover for their ten-minute rest. Rinse and start the rice on the first burner, using the half-batch water and salt above. Once it reaches a gentle boil, cover, reduce to a gentle simmer and set a timer for its **15–18-minute** check. If the dough rises early, cook the naan earlier and keep the stack wrapped; warm it briefly in the skillet just before serving if it has cooled.
5. **Cook the breads while the rice simmers and rests:** Heat the dry skillet on the second burner during the last five minutes of the dough-ball rest. Roll two balls, then cook them one at a time. Take the pan off the burner while rolling the next pair or checking the rice, and let it recover before the next bread. Check the first naan's thick center for cooked dough and wrap each finished bread promptly. Check the rice at its timer, correct any hard centers, then turn off its heat for the **ten-minute covered rest**. Neither the naan's size nor the rice's simmer and rest times are halved.
6. **Finish the curry and serve:** Make sure the peas are hot throughout and all the vegetables are tender before switching off the slow cooker. If their final cooking overlaps the breads, add and check them between naan rounds with the skillet off the heat. Let the curry stop bubbling for about five minutes, temper and fold in its yogurt, then make the final salt adjustment. Fluff the rice and bring all six breads to the table with the curry.

## Make Ahead and Leftovers

For less bread work on the day, make and freeze the six plain naan ahead, then thaw and warm in a covered skillet. With ready naan and curry made from raw vegetables that day, allow the same curry window and roughly **45–60 minutes hands-on**, including rice and reheating the breads.

The curry can also be cooked ahead **without its yogurt finish**, divided into shallow containers and refrigerated promptly. With refrigerated curry and already-thawed plain naan, allow about **45–60 minutes elapsed and 30–40 minutes hands-on**: start the rice, reheat the curry in a separate **4-quart saucepan** on the other burner to **165°F / 74°C throughout**, then keep it covered at **140°F / 60°C or above** while using that burner to warm the naan. Temper in the yogurt just before serving. Thaw frozen breads before this service window. A slow cooker is for holding the reheated curry hot, not for reheating it from cold.

Refrigerate curry and rice separately in shallow containers within **2 hours** of leaving hot holding, or **1 hour above 90°F / 32°C**. Keep at **40°F / 4°C or below**, use within **3–4 days**, and reheat to **165°F / 74°C**. Do not leave the rice pot or full crock cooling on the counter. Cool untouched plain naan completely and wrap for up to two days at room temperature or freeze; any bread that has touched curry follows the curry's refrigeration rules.
