## Meal Overview

Chef Kriger’s sweet-savory brisket, garlic-and-Parmesan mashed red potatoes and butter-glazed carrots. Cook the brisket a day ahead so it can be sliced cold, then reheat it with every bit of its sauce.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **The day before:** Make one full brisket batch. Follow its covered braise to fork tenderness; it does not need a preliminary sear. Transfer meat, sauce and vegetables to shallow containers and refrigerate promptly for the overnight rest. Keep its carrots and onion with the sauce.
2. **About 75–100 minutes before dinner:** Heat the oven to 325°F. Slice the cold brisket across the grain. Bring all its sauce to a boil, stirring against scorching, and pour it and the vegetables over the meat. Cover and reheat, checking around 45 minutes, until the meat reaches 165°F throughout. Allow longer if needed.
3. **While the brisket reheats:** Start a double batch of the mashed-potato recipe: 16 medium red potatoes, 6 large garlic cloves, 4 tbsp butter, 1 cup whole milk and ½ cup Parmesan, plus its salt and pepper allowance. Use a pot with expansion/stirring room or two complete pots. The recipe’s doubled planned yield is twelve standard side portions, so eight diners can have generous helpings with some left over. Start about an hour before serving to allow for the larger water load. Keep the skins on, simmer quarters and garlic until both crush easily, drain, steam-dry briefly and hand-mash with all the butter, warmed milk and cheese.
4. **About 35 minutes before dinner:** Prepare one full glazed-carrot recipe. Simmer its matchsticks on the stove to tenderness, drain without rinsing, then toss with the full butter, brown sugar, glaze salt and white pepper; thyme is optional. It is a stovetop glaze, so the oven remains available for the brisket.
5. **Finish and serve:** Check the brisket’s measured reheat endpoint before plating. Spoon its sauce over the slices; it needs neither straining nor a separate gravy reduction. Finish the mash and carrots close to serving so they arrive hot. If any component finishes early, hold it at 140°F or above, or refrigerate promptly and reheat to 165°F before serving.

## Notes

- The full brisket batch and carrot batch each plan eight portions. The larger potato batch intentionally provides generous sides and possible leftovers; those portions are planning estimates, not a measured service yield.
- The brisket’s overnight rest counts against its 3–4-day refrigerated storage period. Cool meal leftovers in shallow containers promptly rather than leaving deep pots on the counter.
