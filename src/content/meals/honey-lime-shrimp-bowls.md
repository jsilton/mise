## Meal Overview

Honey-lime shrimp over rich coconut jasmine rice, with juicy bok choy in garlic and oyster sauce. Make **one full batch of each component for four bowls**: one pound of shrimp, rice starting with 360 g dry jasmine rice and 400 ml coconut milk, and 600 g trimmed bok choy. The rice recipe's six side portions become four generous bowl bases; the shrimp are a modest topping, not four large seafood entrées.

Keep the rice's sugar optional and omit it here: the shrimp already bring a deliberately sweet honey glaze. Preserve the coconut milk, butter and oil in their recipes. Bok choy adds a savory vegetable contrast; use only enough of its sauce to coat, so the bowl does not become overly salty or flooded. Do not add another batch of honey glaze or extra oyster sauce at assembly.

Allow about **one hour elapsed and 55 minutes hands-on** for one cook, with peeled, deveined and thawed shrimp. Use a heavy lidded rice saucepan, a lidded 12-inch vegetable skillet and a separate 12-inch shrimp skillet. A separate shrimp pan avoids washing a hot pan between the final two dishes. Thawing or peeling shrimp adds time.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Start the rice:** Rinse and drain, combine the measured coconut milk, water and seasonings, and bring to a gentle boil. Omit the optional sugar; pandan remains optional. Cover and simmer according to the rice recipe, allowing its full cooking time and ten-minute covered rest before serving.
2. **Prepare everything while rice simmers:** Wash and drain bok choy, cut thick bases consistently, and measure its oyster-soy sauce and three-part oil allocation. Prepare the shrimp garlic, herbs, honey and divided lime juice. Handle and dry raw shrimp last, keep them cold, and clean raw-contact tools before returning to the vegetables. No baking-soda soak or rinse is needed.
3. **Check rice before starting the attended pans:** Around the end of its 18–22-minute simmer, check grain tenderness and absorption and make any correction in the rice recipe. Start its covered rest once ready. Keep the rice hot for the brief wait described below if the remaining dishes need longer.
4. **Cook bok choy first:** Allow about fifteen minutes for both pan-steaming batches and the separate garlic-sauce finish. Drain excess liquid between stages, finish with sesame oil, then transfer to a warm serving dish. Keep the optional crisp garnish separate until assembly. Do not try to sear shrimp while attending the bok choy batches.
5. **Cook shrimp last:** With everything else ready, follow the main's moderate-heat garlic, shrimp and honey-glaze sequence. Allow about ten minutes, judging the thickest shrimp by their firm, pearly, opaque centers and **145°F / 63°C** endpoint rather than a fixed two-minutes-per-side rule. If the glaze needs more reduction, remove cooked shrimp first. Add the remaining lime off heat.
6. **Build bowls immediately:** Fluff the rice and divide the full batch among four bowls. Share bok choy and the actual shrimp count evenly, spoon the shrimp's existing glaze over them and add herbs and any optional crisp vegetable garnish. Serve as soon as the shrimp finish.

## Timing and leftovers

Rice may finish before the final pans. For this short service wait, keep it covered and hot at **140°F / 60°C or above**; do not treat a covered pot on the counter as hot holding. Keep the bok choy warm during the shrimp's brief cooking, and serve promptly because longer holding softens its leaves. If rice needs extra cooking, finish that correction before beginning the shrimp. Additional shrimp batches extend the schedule.

Refrigerate leftover components in separate shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Use within three to four days and reheat to **165°F / 74°C** throughout. Shrimp firm and bok choy softens on reheating; keep any crisp garnish separate. Cool rice promptly in shallow portions rather than leaving the full pot to cool slowly.
