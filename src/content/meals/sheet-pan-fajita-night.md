## Meal Overview

Chicken and three colors of peppers in warm tortillas, with cilantro-lime rice, lightly thickened black beans and chunky guacamole. Make **one full batch of each recipe** for six diners. The main supplies two pounds of chicken and twelve tortillas; share the rice and beans as six smaller sides than their four-portion recipe labels. Serve guacamole as a topping instead of the main recipe's optional sliced avocado.

This is a generous dinner with several components. The rice starts with 180 g dry rice, the beans use two undrained cans, and the guacamole starts with three avocados. Put some filling inside each tortilla and serve the sides alongside, rather than forcing everything into the wraps. Guacamole adds its own onion, tomato, lime and chile; taste a complete bite before adding optional hot sauce or more lime.

Allow about **90 minutes elapsed and 75 minutes hands-on** for one cook, including the guacamole's one-hour refrigerated rest. Use two rimmed half-sheet pans in an oven that can hold both, separate rice and bean saucepans, a mixing bowl and a microwave for tortillas. If only one sheet pan fits, cook in two rounds and allow another 20–30 minutes.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Make guacamole first:** Prepare it through the covered refrigerator rest. Keep its remaining lime and salt for the final taste check. Wash and prepare other produce and cilantro before handling raw chicken.
2. **Start the fajitas:** Heat the oven to **375°F / 190°C**, following the main recipe's two-pan arrangement. Slice chicken and vegetables consistently, mix the measured seasoning and distribute the filling across both pans. Keep uncooked filling refrigerated if cooking in rounds, and clean the raw-contact board, bowl and utensils before handling ready-to-eat food.
3. **Start rice as the chicken goes into the oven:** Allow its full forty minutes, including heating and covered rest. If the fajitas finish earlier, use the brief hot-holding guidance below; do not skip the rice rest to match a timer.
4. **Simmer the beans while the pans roast:** Use all the canning liquid. Warm garlic and cumin, add beans and oregano, then simmer and mash a few beans until the sauce lightly coats them. This needs about twenty-five minutes including preparation, rather than a quick warming. Add lime and assess salt at the end.
5. **Check both pans:** Several thick chicken strips on each pan must reach **165°F / 74°C**. Remove finished chicken to a clean covered bowl if vegetables need longer. Optional broiling is one vegetable pan at a time; keep watching it and avoid starting another attended task during those few minutes.
6. **Finish and serve:** Add the main's remaining lime, fluff and finish the rice, and taste the rested guacamole with a piece of tortilla. Warm tortillas in small stacks using the main recipe's microwave guidance. Share filling, rice, beans and guacamole among six diners, with two tortillas each. Offer the main's optional salsa or hot sauce at the table.

## Timing adjustments

Keep the broiler stage optional: it adds color but competes with finishing rice and tortillas. For a short wait, keep rice, beans and cooked chicken hot at **140°F / 60°C or above**, covered to limit drying; loosen beans with a splash of water if needed. Keep guacamole refrigerated until service. Long holds soften vegetables and dry chicken, so coordinate service around the rice's full rest and the chicken's measured doneness.

For a faster schedule, use the guacamole recipe's immediate-service option; the complete dinner still needs vegetable cutting, two-pan roasting and the rice stage. Omit the guacamole's jalapeño and cayenne for a milder topping and offer hot sauce separately. The chicken seasoning has its own chile character, so this is not a promise of a completely heat-free meal.

## Leftovers

Refrigerate chicken and vegetables, rice and beans in separate shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Use the cooked components within three to four days and reheat to **165°F / 74°C** throughout. Keep tortillas separate and follow their package storage instructions. Cover guacamole against the surface and use the next day for quality; lime does not make it shelf-stable.
