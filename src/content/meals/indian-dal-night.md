## Meal Overview

Creamy mung dal with an onion-and-spice tadka, saffron-cardamom rice, tender potatoes and cauliflower in a clinging tomato masala, and cool cucumber raita. Rice and potatoes share the starch portion; the dal anchors the plate, while yogurt and fresh herbs give a tangy contrast.

Allow about **1 hour 50 minutes elapsed** and **80–90 minutes hands-on** for one cook starting with dry rice and dal and uncut vegetables. Use three burners, a lidded rice saucepan, a dal saucepan, a deep lidded vegetable pan and a small skillet. The short spice stages need your attention; the dal and covered vegetables provide the useful preparation windows.

## Portions for Four

- **Dal:** Make one full [Dal Tadka](/mise/recipes/dal-tadka), starting with **200 g split hulled yellow mung dal and 720 ml water**, plus its consistency adjustments. Keep all its onion, three tablespoons of ghee and complete spice finish. Divide the finished pot among four plates or small bowls, about a cup each depending on final thickness.
- **Rice:** Make **half the [Saffron Cardamom Rice](/mise/recipes/saffron-cardamom-rice)**: **150 g dry aged white basmati, 300 ml water, 30 ml whole milk, 1/4 tsp loosely measured saffron, 1 1/2 tbsp ghee, three green cardamom pods, one clove, a 3/4-inch cinnamon piece, half an Indian bay leaf, 75 g sliced onion and 1/4 tsp fine salt**. Keep **30 ml hot correction water** separate. Finish with **1 1/2 tsp cilantro** and optionally **1 tbsp already toasted nuts**. Omit the optional black cardamom here so saffron and green cardamom stay prominent beside the two spiced dishes. Use a narrow **1 1/2–2-quart saucepan**; this gives about **2 1/4–2 1/2 cups cooked rice**, or a little over half a cup each.
- **Aloo gobi:** Make **half the [Aloo Gobi](/mise/recipes/aloo-gobi-potato-cauliflower-curry)** with **340 g trimmed potatoes and 340 g cauliflower florets**, halving its remaining ingredients. Use **1 1/2 tbsp oil, 1/2 tsp initial fine salt and up to 1/4 tsp finishing salt**. Its cooking water is **30 ml with potatoes plus 30 ml with cauliflower**, with a separate **30 ml hot correction reserve**. A deep **10-inch, 3–4-quart pan with a tight lid** suits this amount. Divide all the finished vegetables among four; they are a substantial side to the dal.
- **Raita:** Make **half the [Cucumber Raita](/mise/recipes/cucumber-raita)**: **240 g plain unstrained whole-milk yogurt, 150 g cucumber, 1/2 tsp cumin seeds, 2 tbsp chopped cilantro or mint, 1/8 tsp fine salt and optionally 1/8 tsp sugar**. The finished **1 1/4–1 1/2 cups** gives about **five to six tablespoons per person**. Use mint for a fresh contrast if you like; retain the raita's toasted cumin and loose yogurt texture.

All four components are included. Serve the rice in a small mound with dal beside or partly over it, aloo gobi alongside, and raita in a cool dish. No additional bread is needed for these portions.

## Cooking Strategy

Have a **2–3-quart dal saucepan**, the small rice saucepan, the deep lidded vegetable pan and a **small skillet for cumin and the dal's onion/tadka** ready. The skillet can reuse a burner after the dal is cooked. Keep cooked pots covered during brief waits.

1. **0–10 minutes — start dal and saffron:** Rinse and start the dal on burner one, using the specified mung, full water amount, turmeric and initial salt. Bring to a boil, then simmer gently. Warm the rice's 30 ml milk in a small heatproof bowl, crumble in its saffron and set aside to steep. Measure the separate spice groups so cumin for raita, spices for rice, aloo gobi spices and dal tadka stay distinct.
2. **10–30 minutes — prepare the vegetables and rice:** Cut the aloo gobi potatoes into 3/4-inch cubes and cauliflower into 1-inch florets; chop its onion, garlic, ginger and tomato. Prepare the dal's tomato, onion and garlic and slice the rice onion. Rinse and drain the rice without soaking. Check the dal while preparing: once its lentils crush easily, add its tomato and optional green chile and simmer another 5–8 minutes. Adjust to spoonable, cover and set aside briefly when ready. If dal is still firm when you start aloo gobi, continue its simmer and check between the longer onion and tomato stages; do not leave hot spice fat unattended for a dal check.
3. **About 30–48 minutes — begin aloo gobi:** On burner two, follow its sequence of whole spices, onion, short garlic/ginger stage and tomato masala. Give the pan your attention while spices fry. Add potatoes and the first 30 ml cooking water; bring to a gentle simmer and cover. Set a timer for the potato check. They need their full **12–18-minute head start**, even though this is a half batch.
4. **About 48–65 minutes — start rice while potatoes cook:** On burner three, cook the rice's whole spices in ghee and soften its onion to light gold, then fold in rice. Add all its 300 ml water, saffron milk and salt, bring to a gentle boil and cover for the **15–20-minute simmer**. Pause rice stirring briefly when the potato timer sounds. Add cauliflower and its 30 ml cooking water only when potatoes have softened outside with some resistance in the center; re-cover and cook until both vegetables are tender, checking every five minutes as directed.
5. **About 65–85 minutes — make raita and check both covered pans:** The dal can remain covered off the burner during this short interval. Use its freed burner to toast the raita cumin in the small skillet, then transfer the seeds to cool. Grate and drain cucumber, crush cumin, mix the measured raita and refrigerate for its brief chill. Check rice at its timer; correct firm grains if needed, then move off heat for its **ten-minute covered rest**. Finish the aloo gobi's uncovered drying, garam masala, lemon and cilantro once both vegetables are tender. Turn it off and cover loosely while preparing the tadka.
6. **About 85–105 minutes — finish the dal without competing spice work:** Return dal to a gentle simmer, adding hot water as needed because it thickens while standing. In the small skillet on a free burner, cook its onion in **two tablespoons ghee for 7–10 minutes**. Add its remaining tablespoon ghee and finish the mustard, cumin, dried chile, curry leaf and garlic sequence. Switch off the skillet before adding hing and ground chile. Pour this fragrant mixture into the hot dal, add lemon gradually and finish with cilantro. If the vegetable or rice checks need more time, do them before heating the final spice fat.
7. **About 105–110 minutes — serve:** Remove visible whole spices from the rice and fluff gently. Make sure dal and aloo gobi are hot; briefly warm the vegetable pan if it cooled during the tadka. Bring the raita straight from the refrigerator and divide all four components evenly. Tenderness controls the finish: allow longer for old dal, firm potatoes or rice needing more hydration. If a delay becomes prolonged, keep finished hot dishes at 140°F / 60°C or above, or refrigerate them promptly for later reheating; do not leave completed pots standing out while waiting indefinitely.

## For Five Diners

Follow the same cooking sequence, using the amounts below in place of the four-person quantities in the timeline. Use these measured amounts with the same component methods. They enlarge all four components together; small seasonings are rounded for practical measuring.

- **Dal:** **250 g split hulled yellow mung, 900 ml water plus hot water for consistency, 5/8 tsp turmeric, 5/8 tsp fine salt plus more to taste, 190 g diced tomato, one optional slit green serrano, one large diced yellow onion, 4 tbsp ghee, 1 1/4 tsp each cumin and black mustard seeds, ten optional curry leaves, two to three dried red chiles, five sliced garlic cloves, 1/4 tsp optional hing, 1 1/4 tsp Kashmiri chile powder or paprika, 2 1/2 tbsp cilantro and 1 1/4 tbsp lemon juice to taste**. Divide the ghee into **2 1/2 tbsp for onion and 1 1/2 tbsp for the final spices**.
- **Rice:** **190 g dry aged basmati, 375 ml cooking water, 2 1/2 tbsp (37.5 ml) whole milk, 3/8 tsp loosely measured saffron, 2 tbsp ghee, four green cardamom pods, one clove, a 1-inch cinnamon piece, half an Indian bay leaf, 95 g sliced onion, 3/8 tsp fine salt and 2 tsp cilantro**. Keep **2 1/2 tbsp hot correction water** separate and add it a tablespoon at a time only if needed. Optional nuts are **1 1/4 tbsp already toasted**; omit black cardamom.
- **Aloo gobi:** **425 g each trimmed potato and cauliflower, 2 tbsp oil, 5/8 tsp each cumin seeds, lightly crushed coriander seeds, turmeric and ground coriander; 65 g diced onion, three garlic cloves, 2 tsp grated ginger, one slit serrano, 95 g diced tomato, 5/8 tsp initial fine salt and up to 3/8 tsp reserved salt**. Use **2 1/2 tbsp water with potatoes and another 2 1/2 tbsp with cauliflower**, plus a separate **2 1/2 tbsp hot correction reserve**. Finish with **3/8 tsp garam masala, 1/8 tsp optional cayenne, 2 tsp lemon juice to taste and 1 1/4 tbsp cilantro**. For the very mild version, omit the serrano and cayenne.
- **Raita:** **300 g plain unstrained whole-milk yogurt, 190 g cucumber, 5/8 tsp cumin seeds, 2 1/2 tbsp cilantro or mint, 1/8 tsp fine salt and optionally 1/8 tsp sugar**. Follow the same toasting, draining, mixing and brief chill.

A **5/8 tsp** measure is **1/2 tsp plus 1/8 tsp**; **3/8 tsp** is **1/4 tsp plus 1/8 tsp**. Use a **2-quart rice saucepan**, a **3-quart dal saucepan**, and a **4-quart deep lidded vegetable pan**. Allow a further **10–15 minutes** for the larger preparation; grain and vegetable cooking still follow their texture checks. Divide each finished component five ways.

## Make Ahead and Leftovers

For a shorter cooking day, prepare dal and aloo gobi ahead, cool promptly in shallow containers and refrigerate separately. With those two components prepared, allow about **65–75 minutes elapsed and 35–45 minutes hands-on**: make the saffron rice, mix fresh raita during its simmer, and reheat dal and aloo gobi separately to **165°F / 74°C** during the rice's cooking and rest. Loosen dal with hot water; turn the vegetables gently so the florets stay intact. The prepared dal already includes its tadka—do not add a second complete dose of ghee and spices.

Refrigerate cooked rice, dal and vegetables separately in shallow containers within **2 hours**, or **1 hour above 90°F / 32°C**, and keep at **40°F / 4°C or below**. Use within **3–4 days**, counting from each component's original cooking date, and reheat to **165°F / 74°C**. Keep raita cold and use within one day for best texture; it becomes looser with time. Rice must not cool slowly in its full covered pot, and a fresh batch of raita does not extend the older cooked food's storage time.
