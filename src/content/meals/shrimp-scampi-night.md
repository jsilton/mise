## Meal Overview

Linguine and shrimp in a generous lemon-butter and white-wine sauce, with peppery arugula and a small portion of garlic bread. Make **one full batch of scampi, one full batch of salad and half a batch of garlic bread for four diners**. The main supplies one pound each of dry pasta and shrimp; the salad uses 5 oz / 142 g arugula. For the bread, use 200 g bread and half of every topping ingredient, then cut into four portions.

The smaller bread portion accompanies the pasta without becoming another large starch serving. Taste the scampi before adding the remaining lemon juice, and dress the salad lightly; both dishes already have their own measured citrus and cheese. Offer the main's Parmesan at the table rather than adding an extra blanket automatically.

Allow about **one hour elapsed and 50 minutes hands-on** for one cook, with thawed, peeled shrimp and softened bread butter. Use a large pasta pot, a deep 12-inch sauté pan, a rimmed baking sheet and a salad bowl. Finish the bread before the final pasta toss so the last few minutes can focus on the butter sauce and salad. Peeling or thawing shrimp and softening cold butter add time.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Start heating and prepare the bread:** Heat the oven to **400°F / 205°C** and put pasta water on to boil. Prepare the half-batch garlic bread with its topping spread evenly over the cut surfaces. Bake for about **12–15 minutes**, judging the warm center and golden edges rather than halving the full recipe's time. Use the baking-only finish for this schedule; broiling needs separate, undivided attention.
2. **Prepare the salad and main:** While the oven heats and bread bakes, wash and dry arugula if needed, shave its cheese and mix its dressing separately. Prepare the scampi shallots, garlic, parsley, lemon juice and butter pieces. Handle and dry the raw shrimp last, then clean the raw-contact tools. Remove the finished bread before beginning the final attended pan sequence; cover loosely for its short wait.
3. **Cook shrimp separately:** Follow the main's oil allocation and moderate to medium-high heat. Cook in a single layer, using batches if crowded, until the thick centers are firm, pearly and opaque and reach **145°F / 63°C**. Transfer to a clean plate. Do not leave the shrimp cooking while the wine reduces.
4. **Coordinate sauce and linguine:** Soften the shallots, add garlic and pepper flakes briefly, then reduce the wine. Start linguine according to its package duration so it is about one minute short of al dente as the sauce is ready. Reserve the main's cooking-water allowance before draining. If the sauce gets ahead, lower its heat briefly; do not overcook pasta to wait for it.
5. **Finish off heat:** Toss pasta with the wine base and a little reserved water until tender with a slight bite. Take off heat to add cold butter and finishing oil as directed, then fold in shrimp, their juices, parsley and the first portion of lemon juice. Taste before adding the remaining lemon, salt or pepper. Use further pasta water only if the coating needs loosening.
6. **Dress and serve:** As soon as the pasta is ready, toss arugula with its dressing and add its Parmesan. Divide pasta and shrimp evenly among four plates, serve salad alongside and cut the bread into four portions. Serve promptly; prolonged heat or holding can tighten the shrimp and separate the butter coating.

## Leftovers

Refrigerate pasta, salad and bread separately within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Use the pasta within three to four days and reheat gently to **165°F / 74°C** throughout, adding a little water as needed; shrimp firm on reheating. Use dressed salad by the next day and garlic bread within two to three days for quality, following their component storage instructions. Keep undressed greens and any unused dressing separate when possible.
