## Meal Overview

For four diners, make **one full batch Buttermilk Cornflake Baked Chicken**, **half a batch Cream Cheese Mashed Potatoes** and **one full batch Green Beans with Shallots and Lemon**. That means 1360 g bone-in chicken pieces, 680 g peeled potatoes and 450 g trimmed beans. The mash supplies roughly three cups, or three-quarters of a cup per plate. Share breast meat and distribute the dark-meat pieces by appetite; a wing is not a main portion.

For the half-batch mash, use **680 g peeled potatoes, about 1.5 L cold water** or enough to cover by an inch, **6 g cooking-water salt, 37.5 g butter, 57.5 g cream cheese and 90 ml half-and-half**, with up to **30 ml more** if needed. Allow **1/4 teaspoon fine salt** for finishing, adding half first and tasting before the rest; use **1/4 teaspoon black pepper**. The linked method text retains full-batch quantities, so use these amounts while following its tenderness and mashing cues.

The cornflake-and-Parmesan crust gives crunch beside soft mash, and lemon-finished beans balance the rich chicken and dairy. Omit the beans' optional honey here. This meal includes its starch and vegetable; it does not need extra bread or a pan-dripping gravy. Keep the chicken beside the mash so the coating stays exposed.

Allow **at least four hours forty minutes elapsed**, including ten minutes to assemble the marinade, the chicken's minimum three-hour refrigerated soak and up to about ninety minutes for the cooking session. Preparing vegetables during the final twenty minutes of marination uses part of that work earlier; it does not shorten the chicken's soak. An overnight soak is convenient but adds to the stated total. Hands-on work across both sessions is about seventy-five minutes, including approximately ten minutes before marinating. This is a planned comfort-food dinner, with enough moving parts to suit an unhurried evening.

## Cooking Strategy

1. **Start the marinade early:** Follow the chicken recipe's measured marinade and refrigerate for three to twelve hours. A thirty-minute soak is not this recipe's stated preparation. Keep within the raw chicken's original refrigerated storage window. Use the mash recipe's 1/2× ingredient setting; halve all its ingredients, not just the potatoes.
2. **Prepare vegetables before handling the chicken:** During the final twenty minutes of the refrigerated soak, peel and cut the half-batch potatoes and put them in their cooking pot with cold water and measured cooking salt; leave the heat off. Trim beans, dice shallots and measure the lemon and potato dairy. Set out the sheet pan and oven-safe rack, separate clean serving rack and tray, thermometer, potato pot, bean pot, dairy pan or microwave-safe bowl, 12-inch skillet and masher. Preheat the oven to 400°F / 205°C. Keep chicken refrigerated until its full three-hour minimum soak is complete.
3. **Coat the chicken and start potatoes:** Prepare the chicken crumbs, drain excess marinade, coat and arrange the pieces, and put them in the oven. Clean raw-contact hands, utensils and surfaces. Bring the prepared potato pot to a boil, then simmer until the one-inch pieces are completely tender; the smaller batch does not halve their cooking time. Warm measured dairy during the simmer, using the microwave option if the stove is crowded.
4. **Prepare the bean finish and check chicken:** While potatoes simmer, soften the shallots in the skillet for the bean recipe's five to seven minutes, then remove the skillet from heat. Heat bean water in its separate pot, but wait to add beans until close to service. Begin checking individual chicken pieces at thirty minutes in the oven and remove each only after it reaches 165°F / 74°C. Drain, briefly dry and mash tender potatoes with warm dairy. If they finish early, use their covered water-bath hold above 140°F / 60°C for up to about thirty minutes.
5. **Cook beans near service:** Once potatoes are mashed and remaining chicken pieces are nearing their endpoint, allow about ten minutes to boil beans until they bite cleanly, drain and warm with the already-softened shallots. Omit honey. Add lemon off heat immediately before plating. If chicken takes longer, keep beans uncooked a little longer instead of leaving finished beans in the hot skillet. Give the last chicken pieces their five-minute rest while finishing beans and dividing mash.

6. **Rest, divide and serve:** Rest the last cooked chicken pieces for five minutes on their rack, uncovered. Divide mash and beans among four plates, then portion the chicken alongside. Mixed pieces may finish twenty minutes or more apart, so early pieces will lose heat. The clean uncovered rack protects the crust but does not keep them hot indefinitely. For closer finish times, use the chicken recipe's similarly sized bone-in thigh option. Keep the crumb coating above any wet mash or bean juices.

## Timing and equipment adjustments

The potato pot, bean pot and shallot skillet can occupy three burners at once. Warm potato dairy before the bean skillet goes on, or use a microwave-safe bowl in short bursts, stirring until warm and the dairy softens without boiling. If the stove cannot safely fit those vessels, prepare the beans a day ahead using their rapid-cooling and refrigerated-storage instructions; reheat them to 165°F / 74°C in the shallot skillet before the lemon finish. Count that earlier bean work separately rather than treating it as free time.

Do not promise a five-person meal from four listed portions. For five diners, increase component ingredients appropriately and buy enough substantial chicken pieces for everyone. A larger chicken batch may require a second sheet pan and oven-safe rack; preserve spacing, swap shelf positions during baking and allow additional elapsed time. The ninety-minute cooking session describes the four-person batch, not every larger batch.

## Leftovers

Store chicken, mash and beans in separate shallow containers. Refrigerate within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below; use within three to four days. Reheat each cooked component to 165°F / 74°C following its recipe. The chicken coating and green beans soften in storage, while the mash may need a little additional dairy. The meal contains dairy, and ordinary cornflakes may contain barley malt; use the chicken recipe's verified gluten-free cereal option when needed.
