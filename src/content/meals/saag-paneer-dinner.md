## Meal Overview

Lightly browned paneer and chopped spinach in a creamy fenugreek sauce, with plain basmati rice and cool cucumber raita. The spinach is the vegetable as well as the sauce; the yogurt, cucumber and fresh herbs give a tangy contrast to the ghee and cream.

Allow about **90–95 minutes elapsed and 70–80 minutes hands-on** for one cook starting with fresh spinach, dry rice and uncut vegetables. Use two burners, a 6-quart blanching pot, a deep 12-inch nonstick skillet, a narrow 1 1/2–2-quart rice saucepan and a small pan for cumin and optional chile ghee. A kettle is convenient for the paneer soaking water. Washing sandy bunches can add time.

## Portions for Four

- **Saag paneer:** Make the full [Saag Paneer](../recipes/saag-paneer): **397 g paneer and 680 g trimmed fresh spinach**, with all the recipe's onion, three tablespoons ghee, 80 ml cream, fenugreek and spices. Divide the cheese and greens evenly among four. The separate tablespoon of chile ghee is optional and can be offered at the table.
- **Rice:** Make **half the [Basmati Rice](../recipes/basmati-rice)** using **200 g dry aged white basmati, 360 ml water, 1 1/2 tsp ghee or butter and 3/8 tsp fine salt**, with **1 tbsp hot correction water** reserved. Omit the optional cumin, cinnamon and bay for plain rice. This gives roughly **3 cups cooked rice**, about **3/4 cup each**, depending on the grain and pot.
- **Raita:** Make **half the [Cucumber Raita](../recipes/cucumber-raita)**: **240 g plain unstrained whole-milk yogurt, 150 g cucumber, 1/2 tsp cumin seeds, 2 tbsp chopped cilantro or mint, 1/8 tsp fine salt and optionally 1/8 tsp sugar**. The **1 1/4–1 1/2 cups** supplies about **five to six tablespoons per person**. Keep the raita cold until serving.

## Cooking Strategy

The times are approximate; finish each step by its texture cues. The rice simmers while you prepare raita and brown paneer, then rests while the spinach sauce finishes.

1. **0–20 minutes — prepare the main:** Put the saag's blanching water on to boil and set up its ice bath. Wash and trim spinach if needed, dice the onion and prepare ginger, garlic and chile. Measure the saag spices, cream, lemon, salt and ghee allocations. Cube and pat dry the paneer. Keep the separate soaking salt and final-seasoning reserve clearly apart.
2. **20–35 minutes — blanch, cool and chop:** Blanch spinach in about four batches, letting the pot return to a boil between them. Cool in the ice bath, drain, squeeze out freely dripping liquid and chop. Leave the greens moist. Clear the large pot off the stove.
3. **35–43 minutes — start rice:** Rinse and drain the rice. Heat its measured ghee in the rice saucepan, coat the grains, add the 360 ml water and 3/8 tsp salt, and bring to a gentle boil. Cover and lower to a gentle simmer for about 15–18 minutes. Check before resting; use the separate tablespoon of hot water only if grains are hard and the pot is dry.
4. **43–53 minutes — make raita:** Toast its cumin in the small dry pan for 1–2 minutes and let cool. Grate cucumber and let drain for about five minutes while measuring yogurt and chopping herbs. Gently squeeze, mix with the cooled crushed cumin, salt and optional sugar, and refrigerate. Wash and dry the small pan if using it for chile ghee later.
5. **53–67 minutes — brown paneer:** Prepare its hot salted soaking water. Brown paneer in two batches using the main recipe's allocated browning ghee and transfer to the soaking bowl. Check the rice near the end of its simmer; briefly take the paneer skillet off heat if it needs your attention. Give the rice its full ten-minute covered rest, then leave covered off heat until service.
6. **67–82 minutes — build the spinach sauce:** Cook cumin and onion in the remaining masala ghee, allowing 8–10 minutes for the onion. Add ginger, garlic and chile, then the ground spices and spinach with its initial salt. Cook the greens until hot and evenly coated, evaporating a puddle or adding a little of the measured sauce-water reserve as needed. The paneer soaks during this work.
7. **82–95 minutes — finish and serve:** Add cream and fenugreek, drain the paneer and heat it gently in the sauce until hot in the center. Finish off heat with garam masala and the measured lemon and seasoning reserve to taste. Make the optional chile ghee while the finished saag is off heat. Fluff the rice, stir the chilled raita, and divide all three components among four diners. For a prolonged delay, hold hot food at 140°F / 60°C or above, or promptly chill it and reheat later; keep the raita refrigerated.

## For Five Diners

Follow the same sequence using these quantities in place of the four-person amounts. They enlarge all three components together, with small seasoning measures rounded for practical spoons. Allow **10–15 extra minutes** for the additional spinach batch, cutting and pan handling. Use a 2-quart rice saucepan and the same deep 12-inch skillet, cooking the paneer in two uncrowded batches of about 250 g; use a third round if the cubes cannot lie flat with space between them.

- **Saag:** **500 g paneer, 850 g trimmed fresh spinach, 190 g diced onion, 3 3/4 tbsp ghee** split **1 1/4 tbsp for browning and 2 1/2 tbsp for masala**, **1 1/4 tsp cumin seeds, 1 1/4 tbsp grated ginger, five minced garlic cloves, 1 1/4 tbsp optional diced jalapeño, 5/8 tsp turmeric, 1 1/4 tsp ground coriander, 5/8 tsp initial fine salt, up to 3/8 tsp reserved finishing salt, 100 ml heavy cream, 1 1/4 tbsp loosely measured dried fenugreek leaves, 1 1/4 tsp garam masala and 1 1/4 tbsp lemon juice**. Reserve **75 ml sauce water**. Use the same **3 litres blanching water and 2 litres cold water with 500 g ice**, working in five small spinach batches; add fresh cold water or ice if the cooling bath warms. For paneer, use **900 ml hot drinking water and 5/8 tsp fine salt**, then discard after soaking. Optional chile ghee is **1 1/4 tbsp ghee with 5/8 tsp Kashmiri chile powder**.
- **Rice:** **250 g dry aged white basmati, 450 ml water, 2 tsp ghee or butter and 1/2 tsp fine salt**, with **1 tbsp plus 3/4 tsp hot correction water** reserved. Leave out all optional whole spices. Divide the finished pot into five similar portions.
- **Raita:** **300 g plain unstrained whole-milk yogurt, 190 g cucumber, 5/8 tsp cumin seeds, 2 1/2 tbsp chopped cilantro or mint, 1/8 tsp fine salt and optionally 1/8 tsp sugar**. Toast, grate, drain and mix as written, then chill.

For the spoon measures, **5/8 tsp is 1/2 plus 1/8 tsp**, and **3/8 tsp is 1/4 plus 1/8 tsp**.

## Prepare Ahead

With a **fully cooked, refrigerated batch of saag paneer**, allow about **45–50 minutes elapsed and 25–35 minutes hands-on** for fresh rice and raita plus reheating. Start the rice, prepare raita during its simmer, and reheat the saag gently on the other burner until 165°F / 74°C throughout. Use a little water to loosen it if necessary. The existing cream and ghee are already in the main; no second full dose is needed.

The main recipe also offers a frozen-spinach version. Follow its full package-cooking and draining steps; this removes blanching batches but does not make thawing alone a cooking step.

## Leftovers

Keep rice, saag and raita separate. Refrigerate cooked food in shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C, at 40°F / 4°C or below. Use cooked rice and saag within three to four days, counting from their original cooking dates, and reheat to 165°F / 74°C. Raita is best within one day; serving fresh raita does not extend the older components' storage time.
